## Questions and source-first findings

1. **June-labelled July show and Hell as homebrew (~00:00–02:45).** Scott interrupts his own introduction: despite calling it the July bonus, he says it is **for June** and late because they wanted the full crew. The first question concerns whether the structured society, souls and bargains of **Brett/Asmodeus's Hell** leave anyone at peace. **Beau explicitly rejects treating this particular Hell as definitive D&D cosmology**: a GM may adapt species, moral assumptions, geography and social structure while maintaining agreed game mechanics and player agency. Cast jokes about Brett as a mundane unpleasant host. **No new Hell visit, confirmed afterlife rule or Devil bargain is played.**
2. **Possible confrontation of original heroes, Hope's crew and Dunebreaker guards (~02:45–03:45).** Asked about a crossover “battle royale,” Beau says **future fighting depends on player choices** and refuses to disclose plans. Jon's guard party (Hack, Ramilda, Al) is **not depicted crossing into the current party's timeframe**. The cast jokes that they could merely pass by each other, *Shaun of the Dead*-style. **No crossover, battle or promised canon encounter occurs**; the prequel's fictional date remains **long before E001**, although this Q&A mentions it alongside present-day characters.
3. **Would Scott or Kristen GM? (~03:45–08:40).** Both have been intimidated by the preparation and responsibility but are **open to trying**, particularly within the comfortable existing group, maybe a one-off. Scott imagines either **classic fantasy or science-fiction/cyberpunk**, possibly under a system other than D&D; these are **private preferences, not announced campaigns**. The others describe GM cognitive load and encourage experimentation. Kyle discusses **Star Wars: Edge of the Empire** as collaborative dice-guided narrative.
4. **Finding a first D&D group (~08:40–14:55).** The cast suggests friends, online groups/Discord, Roll20, local shops/adventurers' tables when practical, and being willing to leave an incompatible group. They explain that episodes can be written around absent players (including during Kyle/Kristen's childbirth period), and mention starter-set one-on-one play through the **Essentials Kit / Dragon of Icespire Peak** and sidekick-style helpers. Some prefer **odd numbers** to break voting stalemates, or offering to DM to find players. **These are situational opinions circa 2020, not a rule for the fiction or a new campaign announcement.**
5. **Dipshit, sand squid ancestry and intentional style (~14:55–16:35).** A question wonders if the clumsy gnome cook **Dipshit** from [Dunebreaker E109](E109.md) created the ancestor of later **delicious sand squids**. **Jon answers no / he doesn't think so**, then calls the gnome his favorite prequel NPC and says the deliberately silly name is a nod to the sort of minor NPC Beau might name in the Wastes. **Do not establish jelly/squid evolutionary ancestry or a future important role** from this question. The playable gelatin larder incident belongs to [E109](E109.md), not this bonus.
6. **The dragon-metal heart and Nash's body (~16:35–19:10).** Asked whether Nash's E082–E089 heart-based rebuild allowed replacement of missing anatomy or new scales, **Scott says he did not request such a change and Beau declines to reveal why the experimental dragon-metal heart worked**. They note other bodies rejected comparable material, and **Nash's draconic nature is black, not green**. Very subtle scales are recalled, but **he has not become a dragonborn or had limbs restored on this Q&A alone**. Their mechanics discussion says draconic ancestry can alter appearance without a racial reclassification. **The successful implant's causal explanation remains intentionally withheld**.
7. **Possible returning guests (~19:10–23:00).** Suggestions mention **Brian Ibbott and other FrogPants personalities**, but Scott/Beau express logistical and tonal uncertainty; **no guest appearance or new character is booked/played by this discussion**.
8. **Stanley's early spell knowledge and the brief aliases (~23:00–25:15).** Jon says Stanley knew ***Vicious Mockery* from the beginning**, so its damage potential was not an in-story discovery made when a goblin first died. The group attempts to reconstruct pseudonyms from a strip-club/alter-ego scene, including approximate **Toyota Stump (Vorel?), Lady Despair, Nuts Phenom, Brill Darrow**; speakers and spelling are contested by hurried joking recollection. **These are retrospective recalled aliases, not fresh identities or a guaranteed written canonical spelling**.
9. **Inverse's Nothing and Stanley's hypothetical artifact (~25:15–27:45).** Jon says he **regrets choosing the assassin/action-oriented Nothing for parts of the separate [Inverse](../inverse/README.md) story**, particularly the M-sports-heavy run, **not primarily because Nothing couldn't speak**. Do not reinterpret the independent Inverse timeline as part of the Wastes. Asked about Stanley's unrecovered artifact / “elf side,” Jon says he would enjoy reuniting them, and Beau says it has **long been planned**, but **does not promise that it actually happens**. No artifact is received and no elven counterpart rejoins Stanley here.
10. **Scott's inspiration for Nash (~27:45–30:05).** Scott rejects direct borrowing from the suggested **Spawn, Deadpool, Darkman or Doctor Doom** appearances. Instead he cites an **older personal comic-book hero “the Feeler,” empathically drawn to victims' terror and compelled to stop harm**, as an influence on **Nash's personality**. The scarred/tumorous, physically rough appearance comes from **his Solar Mines history and the devastated Mad Max-like world**. **This is an authorial character-design explanation**, not evidence that “the Feeler” exists in Wastes canon, a retcon of Nash's origin, or objective confirmation of every Solar Mines memory.
11. **Vorel's prismatic-scales precision (~30:05–31:45).** Asked whether Vorel could produce lettering or living-pixel advertisements on his color-shifting skin rather than hire a cape, **Beau says individual-scale mastery is conceivable with a teacher and practice**, perhaps even moving images. He also stresses **Vorel does not currently command that precision intuitively**, and this unique effect is a **mutation**, not already an unlocked player ability or demonstrated spell. Record as **GM conditional future mechanic**, not a performed advertisement.
12. **Cast's affectionate relationship tally and Nash's fidelity (~31:45–37:00).** A jocular comparison of **Stanley and Hope's romantic histories** includes **contested counts**, offscreen/backstory ambiguity, deliberate improvised jokes (e.g. a supposed orc guard is immediately retracted) and private knowledge the cast may have only heard in recaps. **Do not convert the changing “four,” “five,” or ties into a reliable partner census** or infer that every reference was depicted. The cast explicitly recalls **Tedna, Celibus/Stump (?) and a Weir courtesan**, but spellings and relationships should be checked against contemporary played evidence; **Tedna's fate and Hope's relationship are not reopened here**. Scott explains that **Nash was sexually inexperienced before Angelashi** and is intensely faithful to her; that helps explain **his immature teasing of Stanley**. This is **Scott's retrospective character/backstory statement**, not a newly played intimacy scene or a direct observation by other characters.
13. **Outro (~37:00–37:20).** Thanks to subscribers and monthly-question invitation. **Parakeet final end 37:20.470, Qwen 37:20.160**; neither export contains a separately confirmed later Q&A segment. Transcription punctuation, proper names and rushed joking require selective listening.
