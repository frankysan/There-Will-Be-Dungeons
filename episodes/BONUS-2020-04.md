## Reliable retrospective and creator-level findings

1. **Walnut is human and male.** Asked explicitly about the young White Tower attendant's race and gender (~03:50–05:35), **Beau directly answers “human male”**; several players had pictured a gnome due to his stature. Beau clarifies he is intended to represent **an inexperienced junior worker/acolyte in the Euphemius-associated order**, alongside senior knights, craftspeople and apprentices. **This is later GM confirmation**, not a detail the E094 PCs established by testing his species. Beau's remarks about Vorel and Nash helping Walnut's curing efforts are consistent with **E096's actually played full ritual and tower-wide cure** ([E096](E096.md)). This bonus **retrospectively confirms his junior status**; it is not a second cure scene and must not be backdated to E094. The exact spelling of the order (“Pelagric/Palatric/Pelagic” in competing ASR/context) still requires corroboration.
2. **Vorel has not removed the inert Eye of Groxl remnant from his chest.** During character-drawing discussion (~24:45–26:20), the cast says that **the Eye is dead/inactive, yet something remains physically embedded**. When one player asks if the dead eye stares up or down, Vorel's player says he imagines a **scarred, crater-like, sunken area**; another says the material **has not actually been removed**. **Beau explains it is a living-type artifact rather than normal organic flesh and does not follow ordinary rotting rules.** These are **later player visual interpretation and GM artifact explanation**, not proof its power has returned or that it is a separate second Eye. Reconcile the E081 destruction / E082 rupture and membrane description as **functional destruction versus persisting inert physical remains**; **Groxl is singular and owner-confirmed canon spelling**.
3. **The friendly-NPC contrast was deliberate.** Asked why many people in Sigil appear more helpful than the deeply exploitative personalities of Earth (~28:55–32:12), **Beau confirms a conscious writing choice** to highlight Earth/Wastes' deprivation and the adventurers' deep mistrust. Sigil is a broad, diverse city capable of real goodness, **not a city of exclusively benevolent NPCs**. He describes the **White Tower's defenders as genuinely good-aligned in his conception**, despite the party's suspicion, while explicitly acknowledging rogues and predators in Sigil. He cites **Tietkalla as a scarce example of organized hope on Earth**, while also mentioning existing **paladinlike orders on Earth, including the Order of Paxus led by Melgor (spelling needs verification)**. **These are GM retrospective world-building claims**, not new visits to Tietkalla or proof each knight's decisions are faultless.
4. **Nash's “future selves” remain partly open.** During a joke about character visual iterations (~26:20–28:50), the cast recalls **at least two time-displaced Nashes with robes**, and suggests a version that **joined the fleshy hivemind in Kervalon/Caravalon** could still be alive **if that system survived its explosion**. The GM/player discussion is conditional and vague. **Do not invent a currently living extra Nash, a confirmed return journey, or a solved time-loop** based on this bonus.
5. **Production methods are discussed, not new campaign lore:** A listener asks how to run remotely during isolation (~00:40–03:50). The cast suggests **Discord audio/video and chat**, optionally **Roll20**, **Fantasy Grounds**, **D&D Beyond**, online dice and maps; they emphasize that **theater of the mind needs very little equipment**. These are **period-specific recommendations and production advice**, not evidence of the precise proprietary broadcast pipeline, which tools the show used in every session, or a historical player location.
6. **Fantasy casting and real-world magic-item wishlists are hypothetical:** Nash's player favors **Michael Wincott** in an imaginary animated adaptation; Jon says Stanley visually resembles **MCU Loki**, others propose **Patrick Warburton** for Vorel and **Evangeline Lilly/Jennifer Hale** for Hope. Many more celebrity names and film-production jokes follow. **No adaptation deal, character appearance retcon or actual cast list exists in this Q&A.** Similarly a question about possessing one fictional item in real life prompts wishes for a **portal gun, Diplomacy sword, immovable rod, bag of holding, Hope's transforming prosthetic arm and Nash's flying/cooling underwear**. **No new items arrive in the campaign**.
7. **Possible books and bonus games are ideas, not releases.** Scott says he would consider drawings of Nash's changing appearances and imagines **a coffee-table book** with campaign art, maps and notes, perhaps **standalone M-sports rules**, featuring community art. **No finished book, license, commercial release, commission or product announcement is established.**
8. **Favourite moments are player reactions, not replayed scenes.** Asked for favourites from **“the first hundred episodes”**, participants praise **Vorel's lizardfolk family/clan conflicts**, **early Tietkalla**, **Stanley/Vorel's complicated evolving relationship**, **the vampire adventure**, and **Hope's cinematic rocket-launcher escape that sent Vorel and Orp through a window**. These are retrospective impressions. A remark about saying the vampire's name three times is **a remembered possible summoning instruction, not an actual performed summoning now**. Favourite-cliffhanger discussion explicitly recalls **E098's Nash/banished-foe overlap** ([E098](E098.md), ~02:45–02:48). Beau adds that he had kept the banished creature at its original spot on the **GM layer**, and Nash independently moved into that spot before releasing Banishment. The GM calls the entity **a demon / creature of evil destruction** in this later conversation; that is retrospective GM characterization, **not a mechanically verified species or a demonstrated permanent union**. The cast speculates about instant death, a changed body, and tower-ward purification, but **none is an adjudicated aftermath here**.
