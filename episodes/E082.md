# E082 — Binch Better Have My Money

> **The Wastes · Arc 4 opening · provisional source dossier.** Based on [the processed Parakeet SRT](../sources/The%20Wastes%20S1E082.%20Binch%20Better%20Have%20My%20Money.parakeet.processed.srt), about **2 h 34 m including recap and outro**. Recording offsets below are approximate. This is **not independently audio-verified**. Full spoilers for the [E081 finale](E081.md). *Inverse* is a **separate fictional reality** and does not contribute evidence here.

## Source cutoff: the beam's aftermath is not a normal return

**At E081's cutoff the GM explicitly declared all four heroes dead after they dissolved into white light.** E082 now demonstrates four **different immediate consequences** without erasing that prior played death declaration:

| Person | E082 directly shows | Do not infer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Vorel** | Consciousness undergoes a disembodied cosmic journey, then **returns to a living, exhausted lizardfolk body** amid Sigil's refuse-filled sewer, without clothes or previous equipment | A routine resurrection, that Earth/Drive is safe, or that all his former magic/items survive |
| **Nash Maggard** | His conscious perspective returns **inside a floating dark stone/metal heart**, with first an eye, then a finger, no ordinary speech or body; can float and ram | A restored human or dragon form, ordinary spellcasting, or an unrelated pet called Speckle |
| **Hope** | Found **motionless** in the sewer with **metal arm and leg missing**, transported by Vorel on a slab | Alive, restored, or confirmed permanently irretrievable |
| **Stanley** | Found **motionless, cold, blue/bloated and GM-described as dead**, later carried with Hope | Revived during E082, a later afterlife revealed, or Diontalus recovered |

**Chance remains missing; Sira and Eddie, alive outside the beam at E081, do not appear; the Principle, Earth, the ultimate Drive state and demonic bargains remain unobserved here.** A corpse in Sigil is **not evidence of the precise mechanism of interplanar displacement**. The original E081 declaration and these new E082 observations must both remain available with their respective episode cutoffs.

## The played episode

### The star-filled experience and living arrival (~00:03–00:17)

The GM begins with **Vorel's point of view** as white light from the machine expands to fill his sight. Time and his body appear to separate: Vorel sees **his own form drift away**, experiences a falling, thought-like perspective with **360-degree awareness**, and observes a **vast unmoving white-haired, bearded humanoid face** resembling a planet. He cannot identify whether it is a god, body, celestial object or vision; **no voice, name or demand** comes from it. A small black spinning speck accompanies him. An apparently endless span of subjective time passes, but **no external millennium is dated**.

He is pulled back through a whirl of stars into his own form and wakes **extremely exhausted and momentarily paralyzed** in an unfamiliar brick sewer: green slime/moss, insects, garbage, shallow water, northern and southern exits. The black speck hits his snout, doing **two damage**, then drops between his knees. It has a small shifting **yellow/green gauge** and protruding pipes, one of which creates red/pink tissue and an **eyeball**. Vorel initially has no explanation of what it is.

### Nash is the stone heart called Speckle (~00:17–00:36)

The perspective moves to **Nash**, whose consciousness occupies the new eye. What looked like a speck is a **dark, heart-shaped metal/stone object**. Nash can see and think but has **no mouth, hand, face or legs**; he cannot make speech despite trying, nor move normally. Vorel says they must rest and receives **the benefit of a long rest**; Nash closes the eye and likewise receives **a long rest**.

A **one-foot-tall hooded ratlike humanoid** climbs onto sleeping Vorel with bib, fork and knife, preparing to **eat Vorel's arm**. Nash finds by Wisdom checks that his object can scrape across the floor, then **rise into the air**; he attempts to drive off the attacker but misses. The ratman cuts Vorel and deals **four damage**, waking him. The ratman flees into the garbage. Vorel digs out the eyeball-bearing heart, calls it **“Speckle the Cosmic Speck,”** and takes it along. Nash communicates by **blinking**, gradually learning to float in indicated directions. **Vorel does not initially recognize Speckle as Nash**, but later registers the object by Nash's actual name at the Welcome Center.

Vorel inventories what remains: **none of his ordinary clothing, gear or weapons**. The **Eye of Groxl** (canon spelling confirmed by the project owner; ASR variably renders Grojil) is **cracked open like an egg**. Its contents have formed **a protective membrane over Vorel's scales**, now shedding. The GM says **whatever was in Vorel's chest is dead and saved him**. Vorel can no longer sense the Eye's presence. This is powerful evidence of **an Eye-associated protective sacrifice**, not proof of every detail of the item's metaphysical origin or future recoverability. His **prismatic scale camouflage** still functions later.

### Jarver, the corpse collectors and the two lost friends (~00:36–01:15)

The diminutive hooded ratman identifies himself as **Jarver** (ASR varies Java/Jarvar). Hungry, he asks Vorel for food, payment, scales or even **Speckle's eye**; Vorel declines the last request. After an argument about which way to travel, he follows Jarver **north toward promised food** instead of Nash's earlier indication to go south.

They find a larger sewer junction where **four short collectors, subsequently described as halflings**, wear protective masks/goggles and retrieve apparently valuable bodies using a **hovering concrete transport slab with a mechanical claw**. One operates a **special multi-lens light scanner**; the team says the bodies have exceptional disposition or payout marks. Jarver wants the collectors driven away so he can **eat the bodies**. Vorel approaches stealthily with his working **prismatic camouflage**, knocks one collector **Boff/Buff** (uncertain spelling) unconscious with nonlethal force and borrows his goggles, which grant Vorel **darkvision**.

Vorel discovers the bodies are **Hope and Stanley**. Hope lies on the slab **without her artificial metal arm and leg**, and Stanley is dragged toward it, **cold, blue/bloated and unmoving**. The GM calls Stanley dead. Vorel charges to stop their removal. A collector uses an **electric two-pronged device** in the shallow water, stunning Vorel briefly and catching nearby coworkers. **Jarver slices a collector's throat**; Vorel punches the electrical operator unconscious **without killing him**, by subsequent GM ruling. Nash attempts **Fire Bolt** but fails because his present form cannot supply **verbal and somatic spell components**. Instead he **physically rams a collector**, knocking him down. Vorel **grapples the last active halfling**, stopping the removal of his companions.

The survivor explains the **Dead Book**: those recorded as dead can be collected and delivered to the **Dustmen** for a fee. Vorel refuses to give up Hope or Stanley, while Jarver openly feeds from one fallen collector. Eventually the survivor offers **ham sandwiches**, and they negotiate. This violence and bargaining are **played actions**, not an impartial resolution of lawful ownership of corpses.

### Binch Lory identifies Sigil and lends the slab (~01:15–01:41)

The surviving collector names himself **Binch Lory**, part of a **Collectors' League** (orthography provisional). He claims **100 gold pieces for each identified body**, with payment from the Dustmen, and wants some finder fee. Vorel refuses payment terms that involve surrendering his friends but allows Binch to inspect **the garbage pile where he arrived** in exchange for information. Binch lends the **hovering transport cart/slab**, takes out **a tripod and a detector** to study the arrival site, and admits places Vorel lists—**Tietkalla, Razak Val, Dusthill, Slavetown, the Wastes**—are unfamiliar to him.

Binch says they are in **Sigil**, also called the **City of Doors** or **the Cage**, a city linked by doors to **many worlds and planes**. He says even gods/powers have no purchase here and offers **a hand-drawn sewer route to the market district's Welcome Center**. This is **local testimony about planar geography and law**; E082 does not demonstrate a particular door back to the Wastes. Binch says there was **no third found body** of the kind Vorel asks about. Vorel retains **the two bodies**, accompanied by floating Nash, and leaves with the cart; Binch continues the investigation of the arrival site.

### Sigil's curving sky, Nash's finger, and refugee registration (~01:41–02:05)

Vorel pushes the cart through twisting sewers for roughly **thirty minutes of narrated travel**, climbs stairs, and emerges into a **crowded city whose buildings and streets curve up the horizon and continue overhead in place of an open sky**. This architectural impossibility is **direct GM narration**. The streets are full of diverse people, traders, and strange travelers. Vorel finds the crowded city deeply disorienting.

Nash experiences another painful mutation: **a human finger grows from a hole in the black heart**, with blood and tissue. He can now **wiggle it and create faint vibrations/noises**, but remains without a normal mouth or humanoid body. While trying to communicate he is still the same conscious Nash.

At the **Welcome Center**, an indifferent **tiefling clerk** registers Vorel as an impoverished newcomer/refugee. She asks his god (ASR sounds like **Fushi**), diet, work skills and how he arrived. Vorel describes strength, hunting and making shields from bone, but has **no money, imports or clothing**. She issues **three days of meals and accommodation** and proposes a **factorum** who can explain the city and factions. Crucially, Vorel identifies Speckle as **“Nash Maggard of the Solar Mines”**; she registers the stone-heart person separately, uncertain what species to list. This **corrects Vorel's earlier failure to recognize Nash**, but does not mean Nash has regained his old form.

The clerk describes the motionless **Hope and Stanley as belonging in the Dead Book** and suggests the **Dustmen**; Vorel instead asks about restoring their lives. She gives **an address sounding like “50 Gums Road”** for **a small church/cleric**, warning such work may cost money. **No resurrection occurs, and the cleric is not visited in this episode.** She sends them to **the Soggy Feather** nearby with their meal/lodging vouchers.

### The Soggy Feather and the new faces (~02:05–02:32)

Vorel pushes the slab holding Hope and Stanley into the **Soggy Feather**, a small inn/bar. The human proprietor **Bill** accepts the voucher; a woman named **Pam** serves food. Bill offers **coffee with blue sparkling “arcane spice”** and **drake meat**, and sympathizes with the strange situation, though he jokes about Nash's finger and eye. Vorel mentions his previous inn, **the Golden Scales**. Bill discusses finding local money—**pit fighting, gambling or craft work**—to pay a cleric; these are **suggestions, not jobs performed or money earned**.

**Two new playable characters appear for the first time in Arc 4**:

- **Jon's kenku**: a small, lithe black-feathered figure in worn dark garments and a **modified plague-doctor-style beaked mask**. Uses mimicry, repeating phrases and words spoken to him. When asked his name he appears to answer **“Nothing”**, but this could be mimicry or a provisional name; **no firm source-verified personal name is established**. Bill proposes he act as a paid **tout** (guide) for the newcomers.
- **Kristen's kobold**: about **two and a half feet tall**, wearing chainmail assembled from **safety pins** and carrying **oversized human-style dice**. The creature struggles with full-sized utensils, sits in the inn and watches the bodies; **no reliable personal name or class is established in E082**. The kobold is **not Hope**, whose body remains on the cart.

Bill introduces lore about **the Lady of Pain**, factions, and why Sigil is supposedly beyond the reach of gods. These are **statements by Bill**, not feats of the Lady directly witnessed. He cautions Vorel that factions have interests, suggests an independent guide may be more trustworthy than official newcomers' assistance, and offers a local contact for pit fighting. **No actual appointment is completed.**

The kenku extends a hand and mimics Vorel. Vorel attempts to introduce himself; the kobold continues eating nearby. **Hope and Stanley have not awakened; Nash remains heart-shaped; the cleric, the Dustmen, and a way home are all still future questions.** The GM zooms out from the Soggy Feather to show **Sigil's curved, city-filled overhead** and ends the episode.
