# E083 — Trash Monkey and The Bird

> **The Wastes · Arc 3 · provisional source dossier.** Based on [the processed Parakeet SRT](../sources/The%20Wastes%20S1E083.%20Trash%20Monkey%20and%20The%20Bird.parakeet.processed.srt), approximately **2 h 59 m including recap, break and outro**. Approximate offsets follow the source recording. **No independent listening or audio verification** was performed; names with ambiguous recognition remain provisional. Full spoilers for [E082](E082.md). *Inverse* is a separate fictional reality, not a source for this story.

## What changes in E083

At [E082's cutoff](E082.md), Vorel had brought Hope and Stanley's inert bodies to the Soggy Feather while Nash occupied a floating, nearly speechless metal/stone heart; the kenku and kobold had just appeared. **E083 identifies the two new characters, moves the bodies into the custody of a trickster church and establishes a conditional proposal for bringing their petitioners back.** Neither friend returns to life. Nash **regrows a mouth and one hand** after a further rest and can talk, but still has no ordinary humanoid body. The group then pursues an unusual, nonlethal fighting tournament as a possible source of money. **Registration is completed; no match or payout happens yet.**

| **Squirts** | Kristen's **kobold** identifies herself, leads the group to a church of trickster gods, reveals her **child Chunks**, opposes Pelor's priests | A new individual, not Hope in another form; has not registered for tournament competition |

**Wider cutoff:** Sira, Eddie and Chance do not appear. No witnessed update to the Traversal Drive, Earth, the Principle, the Eye's sacrificed presence or the former world's politics occurs here. E081's four-person death declaration remains a real E081 GM statement, followed by the different demonstrated E082–E083 fates.

## Played events

### Soggy Feather: the new companions introduce themselves (~00:03–00:15)

The session resumes at the **Soggy Feather**, where Bill serves Vorel; Nash/Speckle floats nearby, and the cart still holds Hope and Stanley. Bill has offered Jon's kenku as a guide, but the bird's mimicry makes answers difficult. When Vorel asks how to get rich enough to help his friends, Bill offers no easy solution and mentions that the Welcome Center's **factorum** should visit the following morning.

Kristen's kobold, previously unnamed in E082, speaks up when Vorel mentions a priest. **She calls herself Squirts**, says she has seen the dead brought to life, and offers to guide them to a church she knows. She wears improvised **safety-pin chainmail**, a **colander hat**, and a **crossbow assembled from utensils and rubber bands**. A little floating stone circles her head. She carries a **baby kobold named Chunks in a shoe** strapped to her outfit; she identifies herself as the child's mother. The group later consistently refers to Jon's kenku as **Nothing**. Squirts indicates allegiance to a trickster god whose name the ASR variously approximates as **Uldemara/Old Mara**; the canonical spelling and exact religious affiliation require checking.

The four conscious travelers—**Vorel, heart-form Nash, Squirts and Nothing**—leave with the floating slab. Squirts takes them through prosperous and then increasingly decayed districts over **hours of narrated travel**, to a shabby, otherwise empty alley ending in a concrete church. This is **not established to be the Welcome Center's separately suggested address, “50 Gums Road.”**

### The hidden trickster church and the priest's petitioner diagnosis (~00:16–00:42)

The church has disturbing carved faces and appears dilapidated outside, but much larger within. **Squirts opens the door with a deliberately comic bodily-noise gesture**, consistent with its trickster nature. They pass a bald human doorkeeper (later called something like **Helios**, uncertain), and a goblin receptionist, then enter a study where a **birdlike, feathered cleric** receives them.

The cleric initially assesses **Stanley's motionless body**, questions how the friends died and whether they died in Sigil, and requests a **crystal viewing device attached to a helmet** from an assistant whose name sounds like **Susu**. Looking through the crystal, he says Stanley is **not an ordinary corpse** but a **petitioner**, a personification of the friend's soul. He explains that souls may become petitioners of a god or power and assumes Vorel could someday become one for **Fushi**, whom Vorel identifies as a **fire dragon and guide to strength**. These explanations are **attributed to the priest**; the played scene shows the crystal examination and a stated diagnosis, not an independently tested general law of the planes.

The priest sees **Infernal writing and a mark read as “the Wyr/Weir”** around Stanley's neck in the special view. The spoken name resembles **Weir**, and the connection to the earlier [Château Weir / Soulbroker agreement](../world/places/chateau-weir.md) is **strongly suggested but requires source/audio reconciliation**, particularly the difference between the priest's **“Far Realm” assertion** and other characters' accounts of demons and outer planes. The cleric treats **both Hope and Stanley** as dangerous and unusually valuable **petitioners to the Weir**, and considers their destruction before listening to Vorel. Do not substitute a universal planar rule for this specific examination and claim.

The cleric suddenly accuses Squirts of bringing enemies to seize his church. Her **successful Persuasion check** calms him. He lets Squirts wear the viewing device; she sees **Draconic, not Infernal, script** associated with Vorel, reflecting his claimed devotion to Fushi. She reports no analogous evil mark on him. Vorel argues that his friends acted heroically despite the bargain and insists they deserve a chance to return. The cleric says **returning a petitioner to life is difficult**, especially two petitioners, and cannot simply undo their commitments.

### The conditional bargain and custody of Hope and Stanley (~00:41–00:53)

The cleric says his church prizes **cosmic humor and humiliating a powerful person in front of peers and subjects**, rather than gold. He has not yet received a vision identifying the target or exact act. Vorel **agrees in principle to assist once details are provided**; the priest promises to acquire materials and **attempt a resurrection ritual later**, not to perform an immediate one. The cleric gives his name as something sounding like **Kidigree** (spelling provisional); Vorel gives his name and introduces Nash and Nothing. The priest says the bodies would be tempting merchandise for unscrupulous collectors and offers secure, magically confusing crypts.

The assistant **Susu takes the hovering cart carrying Hope and Stanley through a rear doorway into the church's care**. The companions leave the bodies behind, having seen **neither ritual nor reanimation**. Vorel provides the **Soggy Feather** as their contact address. The priest will send word **when he knows the humiliation target**, not at a fixed hour. A doorkeeper speaks briefly with Squirts on the way out.

After the party exits, **the alley and church are no longer visible** when they turn back: houses occupy the space. Squirts insists the way can still be found. The **disappearance of the visible street is played observation**, but the mechanics of the church's concealment are not established. They return to the inn at night, under Sigil's overhead buildings whose lit windows resemble artificial stars.

### Another rest: Nash regains speech (~00:54–01:07)

Bill provides an **executive suite** with two beds and facilities. **Nothing** removes her beaked mask and cloak, sets down gear, plants a sword in the floor and sleeps in a chair. **Squirts and Chunks curl up in a tipped-over wastebasket**; Vorel sleeps under a bed, and **Nash floats under a blanket on a bed**. Bill pays Nothing **one gold coin** for continuing to guide Vorel. This is **Nothing's payment**, not Vorel's income.

After the long rest, the GM offers Nash **two regrowth points**. Scott chooses **a hand and a mouth** rather than more limbs. Nash wakes with **the earlier eye and finger, plus a protruding speaking mouth and a new grasping hand**; he can yawn, speak to Vorel and gesture, while still being a floating heart. His **previous failure to cast Fire Bolt in E082 was due to absent components**; E083 **does not independently demonstrate spellcasting in the new form**, even if the change could help.

### Welcome Center factorum: Pelor's three visitors (~01:07–01:38)

A trio in white robes brings **baskets of fruit and vegetables**: an older human priestess whose name sounds like **Mereen/Marene**, a green lizardfolk acolyte **Frail/Frael**, and a shorter human acolyte with a mole whose name sounds like **Belthuzad/Belthezood**. They serve **Pelor** (recognized in the dialogue and by Vorel's memory of the **Gray Socks'** earlier sun-god affiliation). They say they represent the **Welcome Center's charitable newcomer orientation**, not a police force.

**Squirts vehemently distrusts Pelor's clergy**, threatening them with a fork from her improvised weapon. Vorel persuades her to play along as if excessive politeness were a trick; she remains suspicious. The priestess describes Sigil as a gateway between mortal and outer/afterlife planes, claims the **Lady of Pain keeps divine powers from manifesting**, warns against faction warfare and advises Vorel to settle into work rather than immediately pursue heroic combat. This is **her religious and political account**, not a witnessed appearance or decree by the Lady.

The visitors propose **accounting supervision**, **leatherworking apprenticeship**, **pit fighting in the Hive**, or **bounties** as possible income. Nothing is arranged. The acolyte Frail attempts a calming, hands-on intervention and makes patronizing claims about lizardfolk nature; **Vorel does not consent to the offered touch/healing**. Vorel says he is lonely and wants to return to his world rather than live as an apprentice. The trio departs after offering future assistance. Vorel and Nash discuss **burning Sigil and challenging the Lady of Pain**, but Nothing and Squirts strongly discourage it; **no violence, fire or challenge is actually carried out**. They decide to pursue employment while Nash regenerates.

### Bill's business lead: bounty work versus marionette fighting (~01:38–02:13)

Nothing tries to explain the city's **factions** through gestures, but her limited kenku speech causes much confusion. They consider going to a guard post, then Bill suggests **the Fiend's Hoof**, an **underground meeting/drinking venue for dubious business** that Nothing recognizes. They **do not visit it in E083**.

A man named **Terrence** posts an advertisement for an annual **Big Time Smack'em Brothers / BMF (Basher Marionette Fighting)** tournament. Bill says **modrons** make magically capable fighting marionettes operated from a control stick and buttons. Since the puppets battle rather than people, he says, public competition avoids the Lady's restriction on open violence. The poster promises **1,000 gold pieces to the winner**, with **free entry and qualifiers that weekend**. Bill identifies the in-story day as **Friday**; specific upcoming Saturday/Sunday match scheduling is somewhat inconsistent in the dialogue. Characters such as **Bland, Rubles/Rubbles and Kang** are discussed, but their rankings are player/NPC opinions and the ASR forms are provisional.

At **76 Mulberry Lane**, **Vorel, Nash and Nothing each actually register** with the M-sports league, supply the **Soggy Feather's executive suite** as an address, and receive consecutive identifiers. The organiser assigns **Royce Gymnasium** as their qualifying-match site, saying **9 a.m. the next day** during Nash's registration. Nothing is explicitly recorded under the name **Nothing** after the clerk initially mistakes it for an evasion.

### Royce Gymnasium and the M-sports library (~02:28–02:57)

Nash ascends to survey the city but feels **an upward limiting force** and cannot float indefinitely above the buildings. This is a **played local constraint**, not proof the Lady herself cast it. Nothing leads them into a neighborhood of athletic facilities, then **Royce Gymnasium**, where the group sees **paired controller booths, idle marionette rigs and small square mechanical workers**. Terrence offers **practice for one gold per hour per person**, then **one gold for the group**, but they **cannot afford it and do not practice**. Instead he supplies a **free pamphlet describing 16 marionettes and their special moves**.

Terrence mentions a popular strategy treatise by a competitor whose name the ASR renders variably **Kenja/Kanja/Conja**. The party visits the **great libraries district** and learns from a young reader that a smaller, discreet **M-sports library** exists nearby. An odd **pale, upright noodle-shaped attendant** asks for quiet. Its copy of the famous treatise is **unavailable**; the party finds a beginners' guide instead.

Three other patrons are studying fighting books. **Nash openly bumps into the first reader** and attempts to look at the page, disrupting the quiet. A successful Perception check lets him see **the desired author/title name in a running page header**, despite the reader covering the book. **The episode cuts off here**: the party has **not obtained the sought-after treatise**, stolen a book, practiced, qualified or fought in the tournament.
