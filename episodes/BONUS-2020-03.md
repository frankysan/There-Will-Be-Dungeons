## Boundaries and findings worth carrying into reference pages

- **Campaign-created lore versus licensed D&D:** Beau explicitly says **the Weir and its inhabitants—including Kitobalpraith—are original to this campaign**; he cites the published **Far Realm** as a possible inspiration/connection that has been **alluded to**, not **proof the Weir actually is the Far Realm in the played fiction**. **Sigil and the planes** are drawn from **Planescape** and older **D&D/TSR** settings, and he mentions **Manual of the Planes**, *Planescape: Torment*, the Prime Material and phlogiston as the source-material cosmology he is adapting. **Do not claim Sigil originated with Beau, or apply every tabletop-cosmology explanation as a verified character discovery.** See [Sigil](../world/places/sigil.md) and [world portal](../world/README.md).
- **M-sports/BMF production origin:** Beau says the M-sports name/poster was **originally a gag about esports** with **no prewritten fighting system**. Only after the party, especially Kyle/Vorel, showed interest **did he develop M-fighting and its tournament structure**. This **production origin does not undo the already played sport, Vorel's title or E087's prize**. A weird blue “noodle” at the sports library is cited as another **made-up local comedic being**, not published D&D canon.
- **The CHIDS/Kervalon end offered a possible party fracture:** Beau says he **deliberately planted a premonition of a choice to abandon saving the world and go elsewhere** to create the possibility of interpersonal conflict or PvP. The players instead **entered the beam together in E081**. **This was an unchosen branching possibility**, not an unseen combat, betrayal, an alternate canonical timeline or evidence that E081's sacrifice was forced by the GM.
- **Resurrection had cost and geographical contingency:** Jon says **Stanley is emotionally changed by dying/returning**, questioning whether he belongs in the Wastes/Earth at all. Beau says that **resurrection was available after the group found themselves in Sigil**, a hub of extraordinary magic, and **required player effort and choice**; if the characters had died in Caravalon/Daemonvold without nearby resurrection practitioners, **there would not have been the same revival opportunity**. The cast discusses how **Nothing and Squirts/Chunks** temporarily became beloved replacement PCs and that choosing to restore Hope/Stanley was not automatic. **None of this establishes the Weir marks were removed or that resurrection is always available wherever the party goes.**
- **Game adjudication philosophy, not rulebook errata:** Beau and players prefer discretionary **DM rulings for collective storytelling** rather than rigid rules-as-written, while Beau readily admits **missed triggers/status effects**. **Vorel breaking through the deck grating in E093** is cited as an example of allowing an exciting physical action to work. This helps interpret **episode-era house rulings**, but it **does not certify any particular spell interaction as RAW** or retroactively change actions already adjudicated.
- **Speculative homecomings:** Asked what they might do after returning to Earth, **Nash's player prioritizes finding his wife and child**, who he **does not know to be born yet**; **Jon says Stanley might not want to return**, though he may follow friends who persuade him; **Hope's player humorously proposes godhood and revenge**, not something Hope actually accomplishes; **Vorel's player wants to check on his kingdom, Razak Val/Omnom and Golden Sands**. These are **conditional player preferences, not future events**, and none supplies proof of the Principle's defeat or the state of Earth.
- **Lady of Pain hypothetical is a joke:** Asked if he *could* turn the Lady of Pain into a final boss, Beau says **a GM has creative freedom to do so** if the table accepts it. The teasing reply that the **“Lady of Pain boss fight” is confirmed** is banter, **not an announced future encounter** or revision of E085's in-world GM identification.
- **Diontalus status aside is NOT secure:** Amid jokes about whether pre-sacrifice Stanley would have entered the beam, the speakers banter that **Diontalus may still be alive somewhere**. The two exports disagree sharply on the final noun at ~20:30–20:45 (**Parakeet “just in a cat”; Qwen “just in a capsule”**). This is **neither reliable in-world evidence of a cat/capsule** nor grounds to pronounce Diontalus definitively alive/dead. Cross-check earlier crystal/soul material and listen before using.
- **No solo-Vorel side story was scheduled here:** Asked whether he might create an entire Vorel solo episode to balance player spotlight, Beau says **no such plan exists**, although he likes the hypothetical comic-book spinoff idea. No hidden solo adventure is inserted between actual episodes.

## Continuity decisions / audio review queue

1. **Cross-index campaign authorship:** Treat **Weir and Kitobalpraith as GM-declared original creations**; keep Far Realm connection as **suggestion/allusion**, not established cosmology. Cite bonus as retrospective, not a new planar trip.
2. **Separate production history from fiction:** The M-sports **poster started without mechanics** but the later fighting league and champion title are still **real played history**. Likewise an E081 PvP temptation is a **discarded possibility**.
3. **Respect distinct resurrection layers:** E081 deaths were played/GM declared; Sigil made eventual E088 resurrection **possible but conditional**, and **Jon states Stanley was changed by death**. Neither an afterlife nor guaranteed resurrection elsewhere is proved. E094's conflict with Vorel reflects a later *played* continuation of Stanley's doubts, not an extra bonus-episode scene.
4. **Keep player predictions out of the chronology:** Family reunions, Hope's ascension, final Lady of Pain boss, solo Vorel adventure and alternate PvP are **hypothetical**.
