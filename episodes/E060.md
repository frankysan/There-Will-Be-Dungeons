# E060 — What's for dinner

> **Corrected Wastes S1E060**, provisional [processed Parakeet SRT](../sources/The%20Wastes%20S1E060.%20What's%20for%20dinner.parakeet.processed.srt), **not audio-verified**. ## Episode outcome

Vorel finds his own expectations of direct, uncomplicated lizardfolk honor confounded by **a political system of 478 clans (stated in dialogue), priestly hierarchy and elaborate rules of succession**. The reigning **Great Fang, Orp**, refuses to fight a friend, even as his captives face being eaten. Vorel issues the formal **“scaling”** challenge. **Diptak, furious at Orp's refusal and Vorel's hesitation, is killed instantly by Orp with one colossal blow**. This is a **directly played, confirmed death**, not a reported injury or later disappearance.

A diminutive but powerful adviser, **Mirion/Mirian Fengar (“the Morsel”)**, intervenes and invites the party to **Cave Fengar**. He says his father died challenging Orp, and explains how **his clan adopted the cyclops as Great Fang** while retaining political control. The city honors a **Council of Twelve** dragon gods through a **Tailway** shrine and emissaries; **Typalla**, associated with **Fuxi/Fushi**, and a possible Vorel betrothal are discussed but not settled. Other influential lizardfolk, including **Bramie/Braimie Fengar the Tyrant Slayer**, his sister/partner **Zercil/Zircal**, and the **Mekatek** warriors (a general called **Toxar** and another called **Skrot/Scrot**), frame rivalry and military strength. Their historical/cosmological explanations are **local testimony**, not automatically universal fact.

By the feast, Orp **accepts the public scaling duel** to occur **the following morning in the Razak Duar arena**. **Neither Orp nor Vorel has fought that duel by the episode ending**. Dinner includes dangerous **live snake appetizers** and mostly raw, rock-seasoned **“chicken lord”** from the Demon Vold. Near the final minutes **Nash alone discovers a cloaked figure in the latrine** with a face resembling Nash's without his scars and a red eye; the figure **claims to be Nash Maggard “of the future.”** This is an **in-session private revelation/claim**, not proof of time travel or character replacement. The GM asks other players to remove headphones, and the public party cannot be treated as having heard it.

## Character and world implications

**Diptak is dead as of E060**, despite earlier pledges/warrior bonds. **Orp remains alive**, with living human captives and violent control of Razak Val at this source cutoff. His overt affection for the PCs does **not** excuse the killings; the party has not liberated the captive population.

**Succession is not a simple rule of “the strongest lizard leads.”** The **scaling**, Great Fang, public challenge, **Council of Twelve**, Fengar family, many clans and existing offices establish concrete institutions; the truth of each priest's visions and gods' favor is not proven by those political structures.

**Nash's mysterious double is private character information** and currently could be deception, projection, time traveler or something else. Do not merge it with Nash's E055 nightmare, or report the stranger's future origin as verified. The audience can inspect the recorded private segment, but **Hope, Stanley and Vorel have not been told in the played scene**.
