## Cast, character-design and retrospective findings

1. **Deliberately avoiding secret-player knowledge (~00:35–02:25).** Asked whether the cast had listened to Beau and Jon's separate [Diontalus](../world/characters/diontalus.md)-origin episode, at least two participants say **they chose not to**, preferring discoveries to surprise their own characters. Scott says that even when assembling the finished podcast he **avoids listening to segments during which players were asked to remove headphones**, especially private Hope knowledge. **This is an editing/player-knowledge policy, not proof the characters already knew another PC's secrets** or that all individual cast members avoided every recording.
2. **Temporary characters for the separated Hope story (~02:25–08:25).** Jon traces the **minotaur “Steerious Business”** (a user-confirmed pun on **steer** and **serious**) to a joking private group chat that escalated into an agreed character. His suit and large sword draw explicitly from *Final Fantasy VII Remake*, including Cloud's weapon and the Turks' formal clothes. **Scott describes a gleefully sadistic, clown-like demonic ranger/archer** conceived as a contrast to Nash's personally grubby but fundamentally decent personality; he says this is **not** his prepared Nash-death replacement. His actual separate backup **“Hack Loman”** has an existing drawing and imagined voice/personality, while class details remained unsettled in this discussion; **Hack Loman is not played or recruited in this Q&A**, but **Scott actually plays him in [E107](E107.md)**, a Jon-GMed, same-world **pre-Dusthill Dunebreaker side story** released after this bonus. The earlier backup-character remarks do not establish that he ever replaced Nash in the main Arc Five party. Kyle describes **“Dr. Spider”**, a gentlemanly, science-obsessed spider healer/warlock-like figure built around caring for a baby while Kristen played the main Hope passages; influences mentioned include *World of Warcraft* nerubians and *Ghostbusters*/*Men in Black* voices. An interviewer also suggests *Clone Wars*' Trench and *Monsters, Inc.*' Mr. Waternoose as likenesses; those audience/cast suggestions are **not all confirmed design sources**. Name “Dr. Spider” is spoken clearly enough across ASR but a question also yields “Dr. Strand/Stranded”; precise in-fiction styled title needs later source confirmation. **These are character-origin comments, not extra played scenes**.
3. **Playing evil and the White Tower paladin (~08:25–10:15).** Jon says he struggles with roleplaying cruelty. He retrospectively says Stanley might have attacked the paladins in the earlier White Tower dispute, but a **natural-one incident in which a paladin injured his ally** swayed his next action toward restraint. Beau emphasizes the die roll was not scripted. **This is player explanation of an earlier decision**, not a second battle, a demonstrated alternative attack or evidence Beau secretly forced a predetermined result.
4. **Spatulo's fear and uncertain relationship to Hope (~10:15–13:15).** A player asks whether Beau was protecting an obviously frightened rival during **the day's play**. Beau says he was **roleplaying Spatulo's desire to survive** while using him as **a potential foil for Hope**: someone who believed the power belonged to himself, whose jealousy might create conflict. Whether Hope killed him, kept him, formed a relationship or otherwise used him depended on play. **The possibilities Beau lists are creative alternatives, not independently performed outcomes; [E106](E106.md) now directly shows Steerious wounding Spatulo before Dr. Spider's *Mental Prison* kills him and Blood Skunk later shoots the bottled brain.** Kristen describes the moral difference between Hope's treatment of close companions/people and strangers; the cast frames Hope's intent and growing darkness as *character perspective*, not an adjudicated alignment rewrite or a prediction already fulfilled.
5. **An unused solution to the Tungatung moon (~14:15–16:20).** Asked about the disappearing **Tungatung/Tunga Tunga** moon, Beau confirms **there was a potential non-divine solution involving the huge mechas**, possibly **player-controlled** and powerful enough for a planet-destroying strike. The cast suggests a *Voltron*-like combination; Beau says the cockpit/piloting route **was a solution, not the only solution** and admits the party had not reached a point of desperation prompting it. **No piloted mecha, combination attack, or moon destruction by mechas took place solely on the basis of this Q&A.** The actual earlier wish/vanishing result and spelling need original episode review. The GM explicitly declines to reveal all unused future-reusable designs.
6. **Stanley's mount and why flight was unavailable (~16:25–18:20).** The listener's repeat question about flying away from White Tower reprises [May's Q&A](BONUS-2020-05.md): Stanley had acquired a mount-summoning ability **before the fight**, but he could not use its roughly five-minute casting time amid battle, and he lacked command of Hope's broom. Jon recalls repeated circumstances in which a flying mount might have helped. The caster's regret is not evidence a Pegasus was summoned, mounted or used in those encounters.
7. **Vorel's distant home and older setting (~18:20–22:20).** Asked about **Razak Val** and the lizard king's eventual return, the cast jokes about fallen home institutions and recalls the **Chicken Lords** and pixie hulks. They discuss their affection for the **Weir**, the imposing **Principle** and later **Sigil**, and Beau's preference for original versus classic D&D cosmology. **No homecoming, new kingdom state or cosmological revelation** is played here. The question about whether the **Voice** is a disguised classical D&D character is explicitly treated as something Beau is unlikely to answer; **no identity is confirmed**.
8. **Body-swap hypothetical and player comfort (~22:25–26:55).** A listener proposes a *Freaky Friday* swap (especially Stanley and Nash). The cast enjoys the idea but Beau distinguishes **characters inhabiting each other's bodies** from **players portraying someone else's established character**. He worries about hurt feelings or perceived comparisons and notes combat would complicate high-level sheets. **No body swap, new magical feature or accepted future scenario** occurs; this is a table-safety/roleplay hypothetical.
9. **Counterfactual die rolls and the moon wish (~27:00–30:35).** Examples include Hope's rocket strike and subsequent survival/surgery, earlier petrification, Vorel's Eye of Groxl blade critical and **Squirts' moon-disappearing wish**. The GM/player reflections establish their sense of contingency, not alternate timelines as canonical events. An approximate **“seven percent”** likelihood is casually suggested for the wish, **not verified probability mathematics**. Nash's player recalls anxiety about expending dragon form at the wrong time, and the live chat's encouragement to transform; the other interpretations remain commentaries, not new rolls.
10. **The Nash/demon conjoinment and possible re-Banishment (~30:35–32:20).** Asked if [E098's Banishment overlap](E098.md) could have been undone by **banishing the attached demon again**, Beau says he would need an improvised ruling because the two were **distinct creatures physically joined**, and **would probably have banished both**. The cast agrees that surgically/partially excluding only demon flesh would be dubious. This is **a speculative GM counterfactual**, not a spell cast or a universal published D&D rule. Actual survival/separation belongs to [E099](E099.md)–[E100](E100.md).
11. **Stanley's hypothetical Earth return (~32:20–34:00).** Jon says Stanley **probably would have accompanied Nash and Vorel** through an Earth portal if given a choice, despite the Knight Commander's insight that he could give up adventuring and pursue happiness. His need for danger is **Jon's later interpretation**; he says circumstances **never provided that choice then**. No portal trip or departure occurred in this Q&A.
12. **Fang's Inferno, Angel Slayer and Eye of Groxl (~34:00–34:50).** A short listener inventory check elicits **Vorel's player's claim that the flaming greatsword “Fang's Inferno” was left on the tower ground** once the Eye of Groxl was restored; someone separately suggests another sword (**“Angel Slayer”** in Qwen) may have been returned to its heir. **These are retrospective custody statements of unequal clarity**, now partly resolved by [E106](E106.md): **Vorel actually returns borrowed Shatter/Angel Slayer to Jalver**. Fang's Inferno's separate custody still needs the [E101](E101.md) recording/played inventory checked. Do not collapse both swords or silently assume their exact ownership; “Groxl” is the one owner-confirmed Eye spelling.
13. **Hope's power and threatened kingdom (~34:55–37:05).** Asked whether tieflings might reject her ties to **Asmodeus** and her infernal-looking **Hand of the Voice**, Kristen says that, **from Hope's perspective**, seeing her kingdom devastated and people endangered has loosened her attachments; she remains motivated to help but may no longer care about some consequences. The question and her answer **allude to events after E101**, but the statement that people were “probably slaughtered or enslaved” is **her speculation, not a verified final census**. She and other cast members discuss whether a redemptive path is possible, without guaranteeing one. Scott says **Nash feels protective toward Hope but also hurt and angry that she left the group**, and he anticipates emotionally complicated future reunions. **No reunion, uprising, redemption or final choice is performed in this Q&A**.
14. **Outro (~37:05–end).** The host invites future monthly questions and says the next one may discuss several intervening episodes. **Parakeet stops at ~37:29.230** with the farewell; **Qwen continues to ~37:35.770**, including a short final **“Okay”** cue after the farewell. **Listen to confirm** whether this is speech in an edited/outro segment, a different audio trim or recognizer artifact; do not assume a confirmed six-second omission in Parakeet.
