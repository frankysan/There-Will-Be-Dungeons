With the adjacent April 2020 main episodes, this *suggests* **26 April 2020**, but spoken year, actual recording metadata and public posting day were not independently verified. The host says members receive it immediately and others one week later; do not treat the filename as a recording date.

**Spoiler boundary:** Through at least corrected [Wastes S1E101 — A New Hope](E101.md): the White Tower siege, the changed Eye of Groxl, the party's level-up and **Hope's separation from Stanley, Nash and Vorel** are discussed as past events. The host/cast refer to *today's* played session and Scott discusses sound effects used for the GM's dialogue with Hope; **a same-day recording after E101 is strongly indicated, not externally verified**. The remarks do not constitute additional played actions, secret retroactive party knowledge or the start of a new fictional scene.

## Retrospective and production findings

1. **Rules-as-written versus improvisation (~00:45–04:50).** The cast enjoys different tables and settings; one player describes a more conventional high-fantasy game, while others prefer Beau's permissive, story-driven rulings. Vorel's player deliberately tries unusual approaches; another player says she sometimes defaults to rules-as-written thinking. **Kristen** describes limited rule-preparation time while caring for a baby. Suggestions of playing an official module (e.g. *Curse of Strahd*) are **hypothetical**, not an announced campaign.
2. **Character choices versus players (~04:50–10:55).** Cast members distinguish character agency from their own judgments: Stanley's player still finds the earlier staged-accident killing morally upsetting; Nash's player describes his character's impulsive, unhygienic experimentation as wish-fulfillment and says the missing-genitals backstory was partly a way of avoiding awkward sexual roleplay. Vorel's player enjoys his character's lack of sexual interest. References to finding a victim's soul and apologizing are **joking suggestions**, not quests undertaken.
3. **Why the White Tower disease plot became short (~11:00–13:00).** Beau discusses consent, discomfort and depiction limits. He states that the disease plot took inspiration from the emerging real-world COVID-19 pandemic but that he changed direction quickly after considering how distressing a lengthy similar storyline could be for listeners who had lost someone. He says it **might otherwise have run for many episodes** (he mentions a possible ten-episode arc); other cast members felt the move toward hope helped. This is **GM writing/production intent** about the previously played [E095](E095.md)–[E096](E096.md) disease-and-cure material, **not a newly occurring cure, a fictional disease rule or evidence the GM's original plan was played**.
4. **Unused White Tower aerial battle (~13:00–15:10).** A listener asks why the party did not evacuate by flying broom, Nash's dragon form and mounts. Stanley's player says the battle began earlier than expected, leaving no five minutes to summon a Pegasus. Beau confirms **he had prepared a possible second phase of mounted aerial combat during a challenged relic handoff toward the boats/heaven**, but the session unfolded differently. **This is an abandoned GM contingency**, not the actual outcome of [E098](E098.md)–[E101](E101.md) or proof of a later aerial escape.
5. **Character leveling and mechanics (~15:10–20:20).** Players describe reactive rather than fully mapped character builds. Stanley's player says he **originally imagined a College of Swords melee bard** but chose another path; he looks forward to a prospective **level-14 Unbreakable Majesty** feature, **not yet acquired here**. A fighter player likes expanded critical-hit range. Vorel's player discusses **Relentless Rage at newly gained level 11** and a hoped-for future Constitution increase; the cast wonders how a zero-HP saving throw might interact with **the Eye of Groxl**, without a demonstrated ruling. Beau alludes to **someone encountered in the day's episode who knows about, and may have created, the artifact**; consult [E101](E101.md) and source audio before promoting that short allusion into a settled artifact genealogy. The owner's canon item spelling remains **Groxl**, though both ASRs approximate it differently.
6. **Future Nashes (~20:25–22:15).** Beau says the future visitors' warning was about **the world-destroying device**, and Nash **prevented that catastrophe**: in his retrospective explanation, their original mission is complete. The cast fondly hopes the future selves may return, but **a return is not promised or played**. They also revisit the **Nash left with the Earth-side hivemind**: Beau/cast say that instance **could still be reachable unless the place exploded**. Do not assert either that the copy survived or that the time-loop's entire history is resolved. Cross-reference the prior [April bonus](BONUS-2020-04.md) and [Nash's profile](../world/characters/nash-maggard.md).
7. **Sound design provenance (~23:00–25:50).** Scott describes building a personal audio library over many years, and using **Tabletop Audio**, old clips, web-found sounds, a previously recorded Hope gunshot, and an engine clip captured from the *Mad Max* (2015) game. He says he layered a quiet voice texture behind Beau's dialogue with Hope in **the session played earlier that day**. These are **Scott's production claims**, not a verified per-file audio provenance/licensing audit; his general assertion about free usage of a source is not an independently checked reuse license.
8. **Diontalus and Stanley after Hope's exit (~25:55–28:15).** Jon says Diontalus's long-standing warnings that goodness would get Stanley killed and that his friends would eventually betray him feel newly relevant **after the latest episode**. He recalls unrecorded/private character conversations with Diontalus; they are **player retrospective claims**, not verbatim missing episode scenes. Jon says Stanley *might* begin thinking Diontalus was right, but he has **not committed a new in-character decision**. The joking proposal to **free Diontalus** is not an accepted quest. This reflects Stanley's possible interpretation of [E101's departure](E101.md), not proof Hope knowingly betrayed him or that Diontalus was factually right.
9. **Froghemoth, eggs and Nash's hand (~28:15–30:15).** Beau stresses that the **giant froghemoth was not confirmed killed** in [E053](E053.md): part separated and went skyward, while the remainder stayed on Earth. Its subsequent whereabouts and survival are not established. Nash no longer has the previously mentioned **egg**; the actual disposal action requires primary-episode reconciliation. For the **extra baby hand on Nash's back**, Beau clarifies that **Nash controls it and it is *not sentient***. Its apparent replies could be staged by Nash as a joke; **no independent familiar, companion, autonomous hand intelligence or new egg acquisition** is established.
10. **Bloodball ideas and outro (~30:15–33:33).** Players lament never playing **Bloodball**: the earlier field is **now a park**, while future play in another city is only imagined. Beau describes a brutal *unplayed* rule he had envisioned: an eliminated player must absorb a baseball-bat hit without flinching or lose points; a player imagines cheating with pixies. A remembered block-26 fall/death anecdote is retrospective banter, not new action. The show ends with an invitation for more listener questions, followed by the isolated **~33:30 “If you need anything, just yell Walnut”** cue **present in both ASRs**, whose audible placement after the farewell has not been checked.
