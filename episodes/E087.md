# E087 — We have a winner

> **The Wastes · Arc Three · processed-ASR first-pass dossier.** Sources: [Parakeet processed](../sources/The%20Wastes%20S1E087.%20We%20have%20a%20winner.parakeet.processed.srt) and [Qwen Q8 / Canary CTC / Nemotron-labelled ASR](../sources/The%20Wastes%20S1E087.%20We%20have%20a%20winner.qwen-q8.canary-ctc.nemotron.srt), both ~**2 h 55 m 52 s** including recap, break and outro. **Not listened to against audio**; model agreement does not prove word accuracy, true spelling, timing or speaker assignment. [ASR comparison](../reports/E087_ASR_COMPARISON_2026-10-22.md). The [E086](E086.md) cutoff was in the **Great Bazaar**, with Vorel qualified for the larger BMF finals, affected by illicit performance potion, and Nash unable to afford spell keys. *Inverse* is an independent reality.

## The consequential outcomes

- **Vorel wins the entire Big Time Smackeroo M-fighting tournament**, not merely Royce Gymnasium's local qualifier: defeats the halfling **Mind Master Gorbkin/Göbkin Schartlinger (?)** with **Kung** versus **Rubles**, receives a **bye into the overall final** after the dwarf ghost **Tubbs 9K/Tubby Flavorbeard** is **disqualified on a positive doping test**, then defeats undefeated ten-time celebrity **Kanja/Kenja/Conja (?)** with **Kung** versus **Bland**. The organizers **award Vorel two sacks containing 5,000 gold and a championship trophy**; he physically receives them. This updates E085–E086's still-unpaid-prize cutoff.
- **Vorel wins while under the priest's illicit performance enhancement** from E086. It manifests during both played matches as an **intrusive, predictive voice telling Vorel the opponent's intended next move**, with disorientation at onset; the advantage is **real in play but not infallible**, and Vorel uses it to select countermoves. **The victory is achieved through cheating**, regardless of opponents' misconduct.
- The **doping test is defeated through specimen substitution**: the disguised trickster priest **Kidigree/Kidagri (?)** impersonates urine-test attendant **Jimmy (?)**, discards Vorel's actual sample and replaces it with the priest's own. The official testing mage sees **Vorel's cup pass** and **Tubbs 9K's cup turn red**, declaring the ghost disqualified for a suspected cognitive enhancer. **No independent basis shows the ghost's reading was planted or falsely positive**; Vorel's clearance is demonstrably fraudulent.
- **The Kidigree device is used after Kanja's defeat**. Vorel activates and slides a small disc near Kanja; its **purple glow visually exposes a miniature workforce moving around inside the champion's apparent body**. The announcer interprets this as **a team operating a false single competitor, in violation of M-sports rules**; Kanja retreats humiliated under the crowd's boos. The display and crowd's reaction are **played**, while its complete biological/magical mechanism and any later formal investigation are **not**. Kanja's losing match was played before the device was activated.
- **Vorel's young-lizardfolk “child killer” scandal spreads through Sigil** despite [E086](E086.md) establishing the child **survived**. The crowd pelts Vorel with vegetables and chants against him, then **cheers his victory and reversal of Kanja's reputation**. A subsequent **M Sports Weekly** interview attempts to clarify; Vorel says the boy survived and denies killing him. **This does not demonstrate that every resident now knows the truth**, nor erase Squirts' serious assault.
- **An opponent's companion** has eyes Vorel recognizes from **the E086 masked intruders**; the revelation **suggests** a link between halfling Mind Master and the expensive **Order of the Crimson Moon**, but **does not prove who hired them**, and the leader's earlier vanishing complicates identity. The handler also recognizes the insignia but finds **no evidence of a competitor's commission**.
- Vorel **gives Nothing 600 gold** for bodyguard/assassin-fighting services and **Nash 500 gold as money to buy a spell key later**. After an all-night victory celebration, **Nash earns another 86 gold (1 wage, 85 tips)** and **Nothing steals/earns additional money**. Vorel's prize purse is reduced to **3,900 gold after these two transfers**, and **3,800 after paying Bill 100 gold** for damage and a month of room/board; this is **prize-money accounting**, not a completed spell-key purchase or resurrection transaction.
- **A night's rest grants Nash a matching second forearm and one ear**, in addition to the prior two hands, one leg, mouth and eye. He is still a floating heart capable of movement; he **still has no purchased spell key**.
- **Bill charges 100 gold total**: 40 toward repairs and 60 toward **30 days of the Soggy Feather executive suite at 2 gold/night**, including basic food; Vorel **pays**. Bill and Pam consider retiring to the Feywild and **offer the prospect of Vorel buying or co-owning the inn**. **No ownership transaction occurs.**
- **Squirts cures Vorel's persistent assassin-dart poison** after the subsequent rest using **Protection from Poison**. The tournament physician's earlier tablets had only **suppressed symptoms for 8–10 hours**; their effect wears off during the party. After the cure, **the party heads toward Kidigree's temple to request Hope and Stanley's promised restoration**. **No resurrection, priest meeting or fulfillment is played** before the E087 cutoff; Nothing's separate Abyss kidnapping likewise remains unperformed.

## I. Back from the bazaar: another poison takes hold (~00:02–00:25)

The party leaves Sigil's crowded **Great Bazaar** with Vorel's **free red Soggy Feather cape**. People whisper and point as they pass. Bill, standing on a ladder, is **removing his old Arcane Spice sign** from outside the Soggy Feather because the endorsement was lost in E086's child incident. Vorel asks about a floating winged eye seen in the market; Bill calls the creatures **“floaties”**, saying they are often used for **journalism, watching and reporting**, but also other tasks. He does **not identify the actual eye's employer** or assert it was sent by the assassin client. The rumor that Squirts **killed the child** is spreading, even though Squirts treated him and he walked away.

Back inside, a **green-red swollen pocket** has formed on Vorel's shoulder, at the site where the female masked attacker struck him with a **dart** in E086. **Nash bites/punctures the pustule**, releasing liquid, and **Vorel develops or is adjudicated as having the Poisoned condition**: disadvantage on attack rolls and ability checks, devastating for a tournament where the prize may help reclaim Hope and Stanley. The GM calls the poison **slow acting**; the discussion varies on whether the status preceded the pop, but the condition is **played after the swelling bursts**. Nash's ingestion of pus produces no independently confirmed poisoning of Nash.

The handler **Pentheon/Pantheon (?)** returns to escort Vorel. Vorel accuses an M-fighting competitor of hiring the prior night's assassins, but his **rhyme-based speculation about Kanja and “ninja” is not evidence**. Pentheon inspects the costumes still scattered upstairs and independently recognizes the **Order of the Crimson Moon insignia**, observing that such costly assassins are unusual against a newly arrived outsider. He cannot link them to any opponent and advises Vorel to lodge an official report only with actual evidence; **no investigative ruling against Kanja, the Mind Master or anyone else happens**. Vorel presses for medical help, and Pentheon agrees to find **a physician at the Colosseum**.

The official warns that **magical items are forbidden** at the final venue; **Nothing deliberately leaves her enchanted warning sword embedded in the Soggy Feather floor**, and **Squirts sets aside her forehead stone** (in the trash). Their **ordinary weapons** are allowed as bodyguard equipment. Nash points out that his very **heart is magical**, so he cannot leave it behind. The handler says **a magically constituted being is permitted**, a distinction later recognized by tournament security. No magical item is successfully smuggled in by the party here.

## II. The arena and its contenders (~00:25–01:06)

An ornate **six-horse carriage** carries the four toward Sigil's enormous **Colosseum**, surrounded by banners, vendors, crowds and statues. Vorel's poisons leave him despondent; on the ride, the group discusses a backup plan to humiliate Kanja even if Vorel cannot win. Vorel half-jokingly checks whether the Colosseum could burn; it is **stone**, and **he does not attempt arson**. Their objective remains the trickster priest's promise to attempt **Hope and Stanley's resurrection** after a suitable humiliation.

At security, attendants pat down entrants and sweep them with a magic-detecting device. **Nash's body registers magic**, and he receives a visible **“M” marker** designating a magically constituted being; this is **not a spell key or permission to import enchanted gear**. Vorel asks for the promised physician, and the others proceed to an **eight-player competitors' lounge**.

The eight finalists include: celebrity ten-time champion **Kanja/Kenja**, a fashionable young man with mirrored shades and entourage; **the “Mind Master,” a boasting halfling surrounded by admirers**; **Tubby Flavorbeard**, a **dwarven ghost** whose gamer name is **Tubbs 9K**; **Fax/Faks (?)**, a talking **illithid/mind flayer with metal on his head**; a quiet human **paladin Arsenad/Asenad of the Exiles (?)**; an orc competitor **Gofolmunch (?)**; an opponent named **Brixter Blanca (?)**; and **Vorel**. The list of names, especially first/last spellings, has **never been audio- or scorecard-verified**. It is **not proof every entrant actually plays a fully presented match**, because the first rounds involving other competitors are announced and resolved mostly offscreen.

The other contestants and spectators have heard an **M Sports Emergency Bulletin** alleging Vorel or Squirts **killed the young lizardfolk admirer out of jealousy**. Tubbs and Fax, who invite Vorel to sit with them, ask whether it is true; the rumor includes a **false romantic/parentage interpretation** that Squirts and Vorel are lovers and Chunks is their child. Vorel and Squirts do not give a coherent public correction at this moment. **None of the rumor is established as fact**, and the child is **known from E086 to be alive**.

At the physician's examination, the elven doctor verifies a **slow-acting toxin** and would normally prescribe longer treatment and rest. He offers **pills to reduce the poison's disabling symptoms for approximately eight to ten hours**, with the warning that the Poisoned condition **will return**, and that Vorel needs longer-term care. Vorel distrusts the doctor as another possible plotter. Outside the room, **Nothing briefly eavesdrops**, hearing only the doctor **sing to himself**; **no paid-off conspirator, coded communication or additional hostile drugging is revealed**. Nash **chews one pill as a test**, feeling less discomfort but no sinister effect, then Vorel **swallows the physician's remaining tablets** and is **temporarily cleared of Poisoned status** for the finals. **This is symptomatic relief, not final cure**, as the end of the episode demonstrates.

## III. Vorel defeats the Mind Master; a familiar pair of eyes (~01:06–01:33)

The announced first-round bracket has **Kanja vs Brixter Blanca**, **Arsenad vs Fax**, **Gofolmunch vs Tubbs 9K**, and **Mind Master vs Vorel**. The GM **narrates** the earlier games: **Kanja, Arsenad and Tubbs return as winners**, without all three matches receiving the detailed controller-by-controller gameplay of Vorel's matches.

When Vorel approaches the stage for his pairing, a **dark-haired woman accompanying the Mind Master** kisses the halfling and looks toward Vorel. He recognizes **her eyes as those of a masked attacker in his Soggy Feather suite**. This is **an on-screen perception** and a potentially important link to the Order of the Crimson Moon, but **not a recovered contract, confession or independent proof the Mind Master hired them**. The earlier special female attacker disappeared into dust after Nash burned her; this encounter does **not resolve whether that disappearance was teleportation, death, illusion, replacement or something else**. Vorel needles the Mind Master by claiming the woman was in his bedroom last night. The opponent dismisses it.

Vorel emerges into a vast audience that **boos “child killer”** and throws vegetables. He briefly rages and deflects produce with fire; officials warn **magical tricks on stage could disqualify him**, and he stops. **The rumor affects the crowd**, but does **not mean a child died**.

At the massive marionette station, **Vorel chooses Kung** and Mind Master chooses **Rubles**. As the match begins, **the illicit enhancement drunk in E086 unmistakably activates**: Vorel sees doubled/trailing motion and **hears in his mind the opponent's intended move before execution**, allowing countermove planning. This is **not the normal rules of fair M-fighting**, not a natural divination talent demonstrated without the potion, and **not Vorel gaining automatic victories**; he is initially dazed, takes hits and responds tactically.

The actual contest features blocks, quick and heavy punches, a failed big-time Smackeroo, rush, combo breaking, and Vorel's increasingly precise **jump-heavy kicks**. The Mind Master nearly corners Vorel but fails to land decisive attacks. **Vorel drops the opponent's Rubles to zero health using a duck-heavy kick**; Kung wins the match. The halfling threatens to destroy Vorel's future career and implies vengeance, **but never in this played scene admits hiring the Crimson Moon**. Vorel advances, **with cheating assistance**, despite the crowd's continued boos.

## IV. The doping test that tests the wrong person (~01:33–01:52)

In the winners' lounge, **Kanja** praises the match's result superficially, then claims Vorel plays *too well* for a recent arrival and **must be cheating**. He pressures an attendant named **Belfoy/Bellfoy (?)** into testing Vorel, threatening to call the league illegitimate. **Squirts urinates on Kanja's leg**, and suggests **everyone** should be tested, rather than only Vorel. The officials accept a **group urine screen** for remaining contenders.

A test attendant called **Jimmy (?)** directs Vorel into a private latrine stall. While wearing special observation glasses, the attendant watches Vorel supply urine into a cup—then **reveals himself as Kidigree in disguise**. He **pours Vorel's urine away and substitutes his own**, labels that new sample as Vorel's and submits it. Vorel **knowingly accepts this deception**. This is **a second independently played cheating act**, not simply a suspicious test result or a speculative protective disguise.

The testing mage finds **Vorel's substituted sample normal** and **Tubbs 9K's sample red**, interpreted as a performance-enhancing substance, possibly named **Ubelax 4K (?)**. The ghost dwarf is **officially disqualified and suspended for at least a year**, protesting the difficulty of competing as a ghost. **No scene independently verifies which substance Tubbs actually used**, beyond the testing mage's interpretation; importantly **there is no observed poisoning of Tubbs's cup by the priest**, so do **not** present his failed test as another proved Kidigree trick.

Because **Tubbs was scheduled to be Vorel's next opponent**, Vorel receives **an automatic advance into the championship final**. **He does not defeat Tubbs in a played marionette match.** In the other semifinal, **Kanja defeats Arsenad** (narrated/offscreen) and thus meets Vorel. The timing/label of bracket stages can be simplified to **opening round, disqualification/bye, grand final**, with no invented extra match.

Kidigree privately gives Vorel **a small round disc with a button**. He instructs Vorel to **activate and throw/slide it at Kanja after beating him**, for **public humiliation greater than merely ending his unbeaten streak**. Kidigree warns the group that their victory involved irregular methods and recommends leaving M-fighting after the stunt. The priest repeats that he will **then see about getting Hope and Stanley back**, not that they are already restored.

## V. The final against Kanja and what the disc reveals (~01:52–02:16)

The great announcers introduce **Kanja as the unbeatable ten-time champion** and Vorel as the reviled outsider still dogged by the false child-murder story. **Kanja chooses Bland**; **Vorel chooses Kung**. For a second time the elixir produces **double vision and direct awareness of the rival's intended moves**, but Kanja is much more formidable than Mind Master. The match is fully played, with **Kanja's lonesome kick**, a dangerous slow but undodgeable strike, **Harmony Strike/dazing chains**, and Vorel's ducked counterpunches, jump-heavy kicks and timely **combo breaker**. Neither player is unhurt: late in the match **Vorel's marionette is reduced to about two HP**, with Kanja's fighter also nearly defeated.

With Kanja preparing a final **Big Time Smackeroo**, Vorel selects a **duck-heavy kick**, wins the action and reduces **Kanja's Bland to zero HP**. **Kung wins**, and Vorel has beaten the hitherto undefeated overall champion in an actual played match. **The earlier potion's prediction materially assisted him**, even though he still required decisions and good rolls. Kanja calls Vorel a cheater, the crowd boos and chaos threatens the marionette station; Vorel replies that Kanja lost to a novice.

Vorel then **presses Kidigree's button and slides the small disc to Kanja's feet**. Its purple glow illuminates what appears to be **floors, ladders, tiny people operating wheels and workstations inside Kanja's body**. The small beings notice that they are visible and scramble; **Kanja's apparent humanoid body moves to flee**. The announcer and crowd treat it as exposure of a **group working together under the disguise of a single champion**, which is against the competition's rules. The crowd's hostility swings from Vorel to Kanja, and Kanja **retreats humiliated**.

**The visual reveal is a played effect and receives an in-world official interpretation**; however, it is **the artifact of an interested trickster priest**, so the full identity, physiology, authenticity of each little inhabitant and future official inquiry are not independently corroborated. It would also be incorrect to suggest this was only a hypothetical prank or that Vorel lost. The recorded outcome is a **Vorel win, followed by a visible revelation and public humiliation**. Whether the priest's promise to revive Hope and Stanley can now be fulfilled **remains unplayed**.

## VI. Champion, five thousand gold, and actual spending (~02:10–02:49)

The referee publicly names **Vorel the new Big Time Smackeroo champion** and the crowd cheers; organizers **physically hand over two sacks containing the advertised 5,000 gold and a personalized Kung trophy**. Small **flying reporter-eyes/“floaties”** create portraits for **M Sports Weekly**. Vorel is interviewed by reporter **Ted Mundy/Monday (?)**. Asked about the child scandal, **Vorel says he did not kill a child and the boy lives**, though his evasive joking and the paper's competing incentives do not prove perfect correction of public belief. He chooses not to commit to additional weekend tournaments; **winning one championship does not make him a perpetual competitor or legal owner of the league**.

The companions return **by carriage** to the **Soggy Feather**, followed by an enormous celebration. Bill and Pam welcome the business. **Nash waits tables**, earning **one gold wage plus eighty-five gold tips (86)**. **Nothing works in the bar and focuses on stealing/swindling**, receiving **roughly one gold wage and one hundred gold from a purse/thefts**; exact spoken accounting overlaps with tips but there is **no second independent 100-gold tip award**. **Squirts picks up approximately twenty-five gold in small thefts/tricks**. Vorel stays wary of assassins, eats stew from his new trophy and refuses further unwanted intimate attention. **No sexual encounter or further homicide occurs**. The crowd disperses only around **4 a.m.**, and the physician's temporary toxin relief then expires: **Vorel is poisoned again**.

In the privacy of their battered room, **Nothing requests a bodyguard fee**. A suggestion of ten percent is debated, but the final acted transfer is **600 gold from Vorel to Nothing**. Vorel separately **gives Nash 500 gold toward a school-specific Sigil spell key**. Nash agrees to treat it as a loan/obligation in conversation, but **no written contract, bought key or repayment occurs yet**.

**Money summary at the explicit episode transactions:** Vorel **receives 5,000 gp**, **pays Nothing 600**, **gives Nash 500** and later **pays Bill 100**. **5,000 − 600 − 500 − 100 = 3,800 gp** remains from this prize before other unitemized spending. The GM explicitly states **3,900 gp after the first two transfers**; the **hundred to Bill** occurs later. This is **prize balance**, not a confirmation of the character's entire worldwide net worth or all household funds. Nash has **21 gp from E086 plus 86 earned tonight plus 500 gifted/loaned = 607 gp**, which the table voices. **That is money he now physically possesses**, unlike the former 25,000 gold kept in a Tietkalla vault; he has **not yet returned to purchase a spell key**.

After a long rest, Nash spends **two regrowth points** on **a second forearm**, bringing his two arm/hand protrusions to a similar state, and **one ear**. The new ear lets him hear more conventionally; his **mouth, single eye, one leg and heart body remain bizarre and incomplete**. Do not claim full humanoid recovery.

The Welcome Center's three-day free lodging has expired. **Bill charges two gold a night for the executive suite** with basic meals for four, plus repairs for two mattresses, windows and curtains. They settle **forty gold in damage and sixty for thirty prepaid nights: one hundred gold**. **Vorel actually pays**. The suite is **not instantly repaired**, and the group remains at the Soggy Feather for now. Bill and Pam discuss a possible **retirement to the Feywild**, and Bill **offers Vorel the possibility of buying the tavern or becoming a partner**. **Neither price nor purchase is agreed**.

The poison still manifests as swollen painful shoulders. **Squirts has adjusted her cleric spell preparation after the night's rest** and casts **Protection from Poison**, which the GM adjudicates as **neutralizing Vorel's existing poison**, returning him to health. This is **observed actual cure after the temporary tournament tablets**, not proof the earlier physician was poisoning him.

Finally, Vorel asks Squirts to fulfill the other half of Kidigree's bargain. **She guides the party out of the Soggy Feather and toward the hidden church**, past additional admirers. **The episode ends on that journey**, before a meeting with the priest or any **resurrection**.
