# E107 — The Handshaking Algorithm

Owner-provided archive catalogue: **publisher TWBD 109, 21 June 2020**, corrected Wastes S1E107; this catalogue metadata is not independently checked against the publisher. The owner catalogue calls [E108 — Save The Two](../sources/The%20Wastes%20S1E108.%20Save%20The%20Two.parakeet.processed.srt) the *second chapter* and E109 — *Sand Jell-O* the conclusion; **neither later session was analyzed here**.

**Continuity classification is explicit in the played introduction (~00:01–00:03):** **Jon Jagger GMs while Beau is absent**. Jon expressly excludes **Inverse**, places this adventure in **the same Wastes as Nash, Vorel, Hope and Stanley**, and dates it **long before those four set out from Dusthill**. The recording calls itself a “one-off” because of the temporary cast/GM, but the owner catalogue confirms a three-part Dunebreaker story. It is **a Wastes prequel, not a parallel-reality Inverse episode, not a new present-day Arc Five chapter, and not Nash's E106 asteroid-tower continuation**.

**E107 played cutoff:** In a rarely held **Dunebreaker guard selection arena**, three newly played characters—**Hack Loman (Scott), Ramilda Gloryfaded (?) (Kristen), and Al (Kyle)**—and associated dwarf **Strum** survive a battle against a grotesque **Chicken Lord**. **Bart Rogers** and **Killdeath the Darkheart (?)** are both **stabilized while unconscious** but are **not accepted as guards**. A mysterious woman **Fleece/Fleese (?)** offers to pay for confidential information about the traveling vessel; **Al prints a written contract and she signs**, but **no information has been disclosed or bounty paid**. The new guards actually board the **Dunebreaker**, learn its geography and restrictions, and receive a first assignment from **Ember Shalisper (?)** and guard captain **Lotho**: investigate an engineer's murder. They see a **dead engineering worker with four back wounds** and hear a witness point down the passage, but **do not identify the killer before the episode ends**.

## Team introduction and rare guard tournament (~00:00–00:18)

1. **The prequel is placed explicitly (~00:00–00:04).** Scott introduces **Jon Jagger as substitute GM** because Beau is away. Jon tells listeners not to expect *Inverse*: the story is in **the original Wastes world**, long before the main protagonists' Dusthill departure. The **Dunebreaker** is introduced as a roaming supply-bearing mechanical caravan. The game begins after **two previous days and nights of tournament qualifying**, not by playing those days; the third day's final challenge is performed.
2. **Ember's imposing authority (~00:04–00:08).** The final competitors stand in a scrap-metal arena before crowds drawn across the wasteland. **Ember Shalisper/Schalisper (?)** has orange-red skin, black hair that intermittently catches fire, a worn military parade uniform and a voice enhanced by an unexplained mechanism. He says the goal is to qualify as a team, not to slaughter fellow challengers; some may nevertheless fail or die. Ember's origins, elemental nature and supposed charity remain **open**, not deduced simply from visible flame.
3. **Three PCs and three arena NPCs (~00:07–00:16).** **Scott portrays Hack Loman**, a stern Southern-accented human ranger wearing a **see-through mesh blindfold** and carrying knives/bow; **Kristen portrays Ramilda Gloryfaded (orthography provisional)**, an unusually tall, muscular **halfling paladin** with wind-swept white hair, purple eyes and a makeshift hubcap shield and **morningstar** (the initial “sword” description is corrected aloud). **Kyle portrays Al**, a towering **warforged-like mechanical artificer** with camera eyes, an old tablet showing a breathing human face and jerky automated salutes. **Jon controls three other named competitors**: ordinary but competent **Bart Rogers**, knife-covered shadowy **Killdeath the Darkheart (?)** and exuberant **dwarf Strum**. Their total is **six competitors**; Strum is an NPC follower, not a fourth new player character.
4. **The Chicken Lord unleashed (~00:16–00:22).** Workers wheel in a battered metal box containing a **huge chicken-like monster**, with an extending fleshy neck, circular toothed opening and corrosive green bile. The GM explicitly calls it **a Chicken Lord**. The team's task is to **defeat/survive** it. The monster's species label connects to existing Wastes *Chicken Lords* terminology, but this does **not** prove this exact arena specimen was one of any separately named former leaders.

## A brutal test whose losers do not die (~00:22–01:06)

5. **Different fighting styles (~00:22–00:39).** **Ramilda casts Guiding Bolt** (14 initial damage; helps a subsequent attack) and strikes with her morningstar. **Hack** mixes two-weapon melee with ranged bow attacks and uses **Colossus Slayer** (one missed opportunity is retrospectively allowed) and later **Zephyr Strike** to escape danger. **Al** deploys a mechanical rifle, grenade-flavored **Thunderwave** and assistive tooling; his grenade causes only half damage after a successful save. **Strum** attempts **Vicious Mockery** and offers Bardic Inspiration. Their approaches contrast with the glory-seeking **Killdeath**, who charges the monster early and is bitten/acid-burned.
6. **Stabilizations and the GM's special death rule (~00:38–00:56).** **Killdeath falls unconscious** after the creature bites her; **Al actually casts Spare the Dying** using defibrillator-like paddles and stabilizes her. A subsequent acid spray knocks her **unconscious again**; Jon explicitly announces a **house rule** making successive knockdowns within one encounter start with progressively worse failed death saves. **Bart Rogers** is later critically bitten and also falls. This rule is **Jon's table-specific ruling for this prequel**, not a universal D&D rule or a retroactive reinterpretation of Beau's Wastes campaign fights.
7. **The Chicken Lord is killed; competitors are saved but rejected (~00:55–01:06).** After Al's rifle damage and Ramilda's shattering blow to a leg, the beast is prone and near death. **Hack closes and delivers the killing stab**, pinning its head down. **Al subsequently stabilizes both Killdeath and Bart** by *Spare the Dying*. They are **alive but incapacitated** as arena attendants drag them away; **Ember expressly declines to hire them**. The job offer goes only to **Hack, Ramilda, Al and Strum**. Do not miscount the two discarded contestants as dead or as Dunebreaker guards.

## Welcome, a secret information offer and celebration (~01:06–02:07)

8. **Gens the engineer and the literal handshake (~01:06–01:11).** A bespectacled **Goliath engineer and glassblower Gens/Genz (?)**, surprisingly less muscular than other Goliaths, congratulates them and offers to show their temporary tents. **Al activates a literal “handshaking algorithm”** to reciprocate Gens's greeting with both stiff mechanical hands; **the title describes this played action**, not a demonstrated network-security handshake, telepathic pact or secret automated discovery. Gens is curious how Al works. The arena/tent encampment lies roughly **between Slavetown and the Teet**. Rare food and water are provided freely at the festival, in contrast to everyday scarcity.
9. **Fleece's unaffiliated approach (~01:11–01:22).** A modestly dressed, platinum-haired woman called **Fleece/Fleese (?)** appears in the newly recruited guards' tent. She says an **unnamed employer** is intrigued by the Dunebreaker's almost unlimited food/water and its apparent immunity from raids despite minimal protection. She asks only for **information**, not sabotage, and proposes meeting **in the Teet market when the caravan next visits**. **Al actually manufactures/prints a small contract** that promises **100 gold for information plus 50 gold per pertinent fact**; **Fleece signs**, Hack also makes a threatening verbal commitment. Her employer's identity, entitlement to ship secrets, future meeting and actual payment are unknown. **They have not yet stolen a map or reported confidential engineering data to her.**
10. **Paper entrepreneurship stays talk (~01:22–01:30).** Al explains he can manufacture paper or small useful items by consuming/reprocessing scrap during rest. **Hack proposes monetizing this rare paper**; they talk about a business partnership in the Teet. Nothing is manufactured and sold to the public **during E107**, beyond the contract paper, and no revenue or permanent enterprise exists at this cutoff. Do not confuse Al's playful game-rule explanation with a verified unlimited paper factory.
11. **The champion's feast (~01:30–02:07).** The four celebrate among festival traders, speak to a bartender and other residents, speculate about Ember, and hear a merchant's **unverified account** that Ember found and restored an ancient machine and protects trade by clever bargaining. A passing gnome bears stew and is insultingly called **“Dipshit”** by the vendor; the group does not establish it as his official legal name. They toast themselves as **the Victorious Quartet** (a proposed group nickname, **not a formally granted title**). **Ramilda eats nine helpings of stew**, fails a Constitution check, vomits on a bystander and briefly makes a performance of it. They return to their tent and **actually sleep through the night**; festival jokes about drinking, the meat's unknown source and Al's physiology are not confirmations of a hidden atrocity.

## Boarding a glass-making moving city (~02:07–02:34)

12. **An ordinary van, an extraordinary transport (~02:07–02:12).** At dawn **Ember, his wife and their two half-orc guards** greet the winners and send them in a rusty but running van to the vessel. **Gens rides with them**, giving a technical introduction. It is a **new morning after their overnight rest**, not a continuation of the same arena afternoon.
13. **Physical design and operations (~02:12–02:24).** They **actually see and board the stationary Dunebreaker**, a roughly **400-foot**, **four-level**, segmented landship with **head + three major cars + tail**, connected by flexible passageways, with a continuous working deck when aligned. Massive heat under the hull **melts sand into a trail of glass** in motion; Gens says the crew tries to reuse established routes but sometimes diverts for storms or settlements. **Head:** piloting, communications and meetings. **Segment 1:** passenger quarters. **Segment 2:** workshops, labs, trading facilities, a **vault**, and a side that can deploy into a bazaar. **Segment 3:** main engineering, power and temperature facilities. **Tail:** freight loading/transport. Gens's claims about market operations and full load capacity are **NPC exposition**; the physical ship and visible heat are directly observed. Their van really ascends the tail loading ramp, and they move from it to a briefing room.
14. **The guard hierarchy and restrictions (~02:22–02:34).** Ember introduces the muscular lion-like **Captain Lotho (?)**, in charge of the new guards, and personal escorts **Argul and Gasper (?)**. **Their guard job pays no regular gold**: food, water, residence, security and a berth aboard are the benefits. Ember's welcome stresses family but demands obedience. He limits recruits to **segments 1–2**, except when ordered otherwise, and declares that **ship maps and knowledge must not be copied or distributed**. His suggestion that this secrecy protects their power is an **employer's instruction**, in obvious tension with the earlier information contract, but **no actual breach has occurred yet**.
15. **A murder interrupts initiation (~02:32–02:36).** **Horace (?)** announces that an engineer has been found **dead in segment 2's lower engineering level**. Captain Lotho orders **Hack, Ramilda, Al and Strum** to investigate and report their findings. The murder is a current plot, not retroactive proof Gens, Fleece or Ember committed it.

## First clues and the unresolved engineer murder (~02:36–02:52)

16. **Through the hot machinery (~02:36–02:42).** The quartet finds its way through the sealed bulkheads to the **fourth/bottom floor**, very hot and loud. **Al conjures improvised investigator's tools and prints little floor maps** as they travel. **Ramilda loudly leads** rather than attempting stealth; Hack follows with a ready bow. Al's **Investigation 16** notices **structural damage in segment 1** that may require repair within **4–6 weeks** and some engineers **pretending to be busy when watched**. Neither observation independently identifies a murder suspect nor proves deliberate sabotage.
17. **The actual body (~02:42–02:47).** In **segment 2 engineering** a worker lies in blood wearing attire similar to the other engineers, with **four separate puncture wounds in the back**. The party has no independent identification of his name. Al finds a **small notebook** among the tools and **actually picks it up/quickly flips through**, but his **Investigation 5** reveals only poor handwriting and diagrams; the notebook's contents remain **unread and unsolved**. The body is truly dead; don't call him stabilized or resurrected.
18. **A witness points toward danger; cutoff (~02:47–02:52).** An engineer says he heard **yelling, screams and running footsteps**, fled rather than intervening, and points **farther along segment 2** as the likely direction of someone involved. The four walk onward. **The culprit is not seen, confronted, identified or apprehended**, no secret of the Dunebreaker has been given to Fleece, and no ship departure is played. **Jon ends the episode at this investigative cliffhanger**; later cast discussion and credits are out of game.
