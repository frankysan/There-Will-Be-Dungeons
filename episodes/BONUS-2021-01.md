## Delvers — actual background versus live character invention

**~00:00:47–00:03:07.** Scott, Jon and Kristen describe choosing cleric, rogue and paladin largely on personal interest. The group considered the practical value of healing, toughness and damage roles but did **not** pre-assign a fixed trinity. Kyle is glad both Evelyn and Krandal can heal: in a three-person party one can restore the other after an incapacitating blow, letting him attempt challenging encounters. This explains the [S1E02](../delvers/episodes/S1E02.md) combat rhythm, but does not itself grant anyone an extra spell, hit point or class feature. The suggested *all-wizard* party is only an illustration.

**~00:03:07–00:05:38.** Kyle refers to the **poltergeist** fought in the latest Delvers job, acknowledging the limitations of encounter challenge ratings. His **skeletal minotaur** and poison-spider alternatives are examples of attacks he could have run, **not inhabitants or encounters confirmed for Kord's crypt**. He says the party's two divine casters are driving a more divine-oriented adventure. His line that he might otherwise design a world without gods is **GM shorthand about a hypothetical different campaign**, not confirmation that faith depends upon the adventurers or that Delvers deities only exist because of their classes.

**~00:05:38–00:07:35.** Asked about the Colossus comparison, Scott explicitly says **Krandal was not based on the Deadpool portrayal**, although he likes the resemblance. His intended characterization is a strong, largely courteous, socially obtuse man: someone who does not always read a room, but **is not therefore unintelligent**. Scott cites Vorel's blunt social approach as inspiration and says he consciously drops grammatical joining words from Krandal's speech. The broad East-European-sounding accent is a performance choice, not a geographical location proven to exist in Delvers.

**~00:07:35–00:09:49.** Asked which parent gave Krandal the accent, Scott **decides “mother” on the spot**. He imagines a pragmatic, kind-hearted mother who largely raised Krandal and served him potatoes, with a warlike, frequently absent father and a less close paternal relationship; he speculates this upbringing could inform his devotion and choice to become a cleric. A proposed division of half-orc communities into differently accented/culturally uneasy groups is still **Scott's idea in discussion**, not a played map, ethnography, clan history or Kyle's confirmed setting decree. The supported editorial value is *a cast-proposed family and motivation lead*, not definitive backdated memory of Krandal's childhood.

## Inverse and Wastes — separate interpretations, no new played scene

**~00:09:49–00:12:32.** Jon says he has **a possible conclusion** in mind for Inverse, whose scale had expanded beyond a short one-shot format. The planned ending depends significantly on **Baffo (Beau's character)**, and Jon wants the full ensemble to participate. The needed attendance has not coincided with opportunities for him to DM; **the proposed conclusion has not been played**. Scott's wish for **Garpoon** to return and Jon's idea of a portal-dropping entrance are light speculation, not a scheduled story or occurrence. Scott separately asserts that an ASR-rendered **“Hackloman”** belongs to the Wastes world of Nash; the speaker appears to be recalling a *different one-shot*. The spelling and exact referent await checking against that one-shot. This is **not** an assertion that *Inverse* and *The Wastes* inhabit one timeline. An imagined interdimensional arena is likewise hypothetical.

**~00:12:32–00:14:22.** A question pits Wastes, Inverse, Delvers and another one-shot's characters against one another at equal level. Vorel, Nash, Stanley and **Squirts** are suggested, with joking arguments about Nash copies, Vorel's vulnerability to mental effects and Squirts's ability to survive. **No tournament, death, crossover, resurrection or character-power ranking is established.** No Vorel title is bestowed, and no Nash spellcasting phrase is performed.

**~00:14:22–00:15:15.** Kristen answers that **Hope would still seek to prevent the Scorching by travelling back in time if she had a way**. Her perspective follows enormous personal losses in the Wastes. Scott recalls the hopeful mechanic who first set out in the Fate Four and speculates about the story returning to her old objective. This is a meaningful **player-authored statement of Hope's potential enduring motivation**, but not proof a working method exists, that Hope has decided on a specific action in a played scene, or that changing history succeeds. It may be linked as *retrospective context* in her Wastes biography; it must not overwrite any later independently played change of mind.

## Delvers — grick, rules and production decisions

**~00:15:15–00:18:10.** Kyle confirms that the creature Koh saw **at the farm's Underdark fissure** was a **grick**, consistent with Cassius's identification in [S1E01](../delvers/episodes/S1E01.md). The grick was a dangerous candidate for the party's level. Kyle says that **if** it had caught Koh, he might have been grabbed and dragged deeper, leaving Evelyn and Krandal an opportunity to damage or break its hold; damage could have frightened the creature away. **None of this happened**. The survivors' return and the sealed cave remain as played. Kyle describes the grick as a beaked snake with tentacles. His offhand comparisons with piercer/darkmantle/Grell are explanatory D&D creature discussion, not additional encounters.

**~00:18:10–00:22:49.** Kyle chooses **experience points** rather than milestones partly to leave himself uncertain when player power will rise and which spells they will select, letting each dungeon respond to the group's development. He describes a **planned twelve-episode shape** as GM preparation, not an already-played twelve-session ending or a promise that every proposed outcome will occur. The cast knowingly selected **level one over level three**, favoring gradual understanding and growth; Kyle cites the tactical testing value of low-level adventures and the Shadow near the fig tree as an experiment in vulnerabilities and enemy hit rates. His statements about encounter CR/XP are retrospective design views, not a replacement rules reference.

**~00:22:49–00:26:29.** Kyle manages initiative rolls to help the **audio-show pacing**. The group discusses a reader-suggested **popcorn initiative**, in which the current combatant nominates the next; they find it interesting but do **not** adopt it. Kyle instead imagines more automation in encounter software and even a new roll order each round, **as a wish**, not a change that occurred. Do not rewrite past combat initiative from this unperformed suggestion.

**~00:26:29–end.** The cast suggests the existing **Discord** as a place to request character sheet links. Scott and Kyle brainstorm a read-only, external campaign viewer, and Scott mentions Twitch-linked APIs; neither demonstrates a finished public viewer, an open campaign API, nor current character-sheet access. Future sharing is a proposal only. The final spoken exchange thanks supporters, repeats the **planned** make-up bonus and ends the show.
