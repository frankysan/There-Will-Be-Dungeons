# E085 — Always High Kick

> **The Wastes, Arc Three · Sigil · first-pass research dossier.** Source: [processed Parakeet](../sources/The%20Wastes%20S1E085.%20Always%20High%20Kick.parakeet.processed.srt) and [Qwen Q8 / Canary CTC / Nemotron-labelled ASR](../sources/The%20Wastes%20S1E085.%20Always%20High%20Kick.qwen-q8.canary-ctc.nemotron.srt), both approximately **2 h 46 m** with recaps, break and outro. **No audio independently checked**, and recognition-specific speaker labels or proper nouns are not verified spellings. [E084](E084.md) ended after two opening qualifier wins and Nothing's double forfeit; [Arc Three narrative](../narrative/ARC_THREE.md) is ongoing. This is **not Inverse**.

## What actually changes

- **Vorel becomes the winner/champion of the Royce Gymnasium qualifying event**, earning a place in the **next day's larger finals at the Great Colosseum**. He **has not won the full Big Time Smack'em Brothers championship, defeated overall champion Kanja/Kenja, received the advertised 5,000 gold or revived Hope/Stanley**.
- **Nash and Vorel are paired against each other in the last four**. They discuss friendship and sportsmanship; **Vorel defeats Nash's Bland with Kung** in a fully played marionette match. Nash is eliminated from the qualifier, not harmed physically.
- A bullying alligatorlike **local champion, Ventro (?)**, defeats a quiet elf called **Minno/Minnow (?)**; **Vorel beats Ventro in their marionette game** using Bland against Ventro's Kung, notably by chaining **high jumping kicks**. This is the actual match outcome, before Ventro turns violent.
- **Squirts tries to drug Nash with a sick-stick after the pairings, but Nash makes a Constitution save and is **not poisoned**. She **also pricks Ventro twice** in the competitor lounge; he catches her despite her becoming invisible. She has **used up her supply** by the late episode, per GM inventory accounting.
- **A huge, green, woman-shaped manifestation appears** in the arena during a violent brawl, imposes a silent warning, then **Ventro is pulled through an almost invisible pinhole in reality and vanishes**. This is **observed**, unlike E082–E084's secondhand explanations of the Lady of Pain. Whether the woman is **the Lady of Pain herself**, an avatar or another enforcement mechanism is **not explicitly independently resolved**. **Do not describe Ventro as confirmed dead**, or the party as personally summoned by the Lady.
- Vorel's unexpected fame brings **interviews, fans, unaccepted sponsorship offers**, and a large party at the **Soggy Feather**. **Nash and Nothing actually earn money working the party**; Squirts **acquires unnamed trinkets, potions and gems**. Vorel **signs no sponsorship contract** and refuses unwanted personal attention.
- After the group sleeps, **Nothing's warning sword alerts her to movement**, and **two sword-bearing intruders dressed like ninjas enter through a window and rush the group**. **The episode stops before their attack is adjudicated or anyone identifies them**. Nothing's separately accepted Abyss kidnapping remains unperformed.

## The tournament becomes a regional final (~00:03–00:10)

The cast returns after a Christmas production break; the recap repeats [E084](E084.md), though its alliterative shorthand is **not a fresh on-screen action**. The GM says the **1,000-gold original prize pool** was increased by **an additional 4,000 gold**, making **5,000 gold** advertised for the larger competition. That amount is a **claimed prize pool**, not gold in party inventory. The party's goal remains to **humiliate the overall best player, Kanja/Kenja (?)**, before his audience for the trickster priest's conditional aid with Hope and Stanley.

Rather than individually playing every round, Beau **narrates multiple intervening victories** for Vorel and Nash and advances them among the last four at **Royce Gymnasium**. Vorel notices **more opponents complaining of illness**, consistent with Squirts' covert interference, while Nash's opponents generally seem inexperienced. **Not every earlier pairing, injection, knockout or result is separately played**. The final four are **Vorel, Nash, a quiet gray-skinned hooded elf called Minno/Minnow (?), and a well-known local champion called Ventro (?)**. His seven-time title at **Royce** is a **local title**, distinct from the allegedly ten-time-winning **Kanja**, who is the future target of the priest's humiliation bargain. A later character line says seven titles; host/announcer naming and his team's attendance remain ASR/continuity review points.

The final four enter a special competitors' room for food during a meal break. **Vorel suspects the food is poisoned** because so many opponents have been sick, but **the buffet itself is not shown to be poisoned**. The hooded elf sits quietly and refuses conversation. **Ventro**, an enormous alligatorlike man, arrives with a threatening entourage, drives aside an usher and crowds the food table. He insults Nash's strange floating-heart appearance and confronts Vorel. Squirts, largely concealed by her size, taunts him. The companions debate whether provoking a violent act might cause Ventro to be **disqualified**, but **no disqualification is actually imposed during this lounge exchange**.

## Squirts' needles and the first physical confrontation (~00:22–00:40)

Squirts sneaks beneath the table and **sticks Ventro in the leg with two syringe-like “sick-sticks.”** The GM asks for Stealth; a poor roll lets Ventro **see her before her invisibility fully hides her**, and he grabs her even after she becomes invisible. Nothing imitates and challenges his voice. Ventro grows angry enough to draw his axes. The group takes defensive positions rather than opening with a lethal attack: Nothing **Dodges**, Vorel braces himself to take a blow and make disqualification likely, Squirts **cowers to distract** while holding Chunks, and Nash remains behind Vorel.

Ventro **swings his axes close enough to make Nothing flinch but does not strike the kenku**. He then **kicks Squirts and infant Chunks across the room**, dealing **two nonlethal damage** to Squirts. No severe injury to Chunks is established. He backs down and returns to his entourage; the organizers' failure to sanction this behaviour is conspicuous but **not evidence of written permission for violence**.

The usher finally announces the two pairings: **Ventro vs Minno** and **Vorel vs Nash**. Vorel recoils at the idea of fighting a friend, while Nash reassures him that **a controlled marionette match is not a real feud**. Squirts attempts to **inject Nash**, now Vorel's tournament opponent, in a heart ventricle. The **needle actually sticks into Nash**, but he succeeds on a **Constitution save of 20** and **is not poisoned**. Vorel and Nash proceed toward their match. **Do not retroactively record Nash as sickened or deliberately throwing the game**.

## Vorel versus Nash: a real match between friends (~00:41–01:05)

Only **Bland, Kung and Rubles** remain selectable, following the E084 qualifier restriction. Vorel chooses **Kung**, while Nash takes **Bland**. Both agree to choose moves simultaneously; their conversation emphasizes friendship even while the game requires one of them to be eliminated. Nash offers privately to lose deliberately if Vorel wants, but **the actually played match is not described as an arranged forfeit**. They repeatedly trade missed blows, blocks, rush chances, jumping and ducking attacks, and daze/combo rules. Beau corrects his handling of **Bland's reduced-stagger passive** along the way; **the spoken rule adjudications remain game-specific and occasionally revised**, not a stable independent handbook.

The deciding sequence is Vorel's **dazing blow**, followed by a **jump-heavy-kick chain**, briefly interrupted by Nash's successful **combo breaker** and counterattack. Nash then rolls a **natural one** on a quick punch, becomes vulnerable, and **Vorel lands the final jump-heavy-kick**. Kung's winning animation plays and **Nash's Bland is eliminated**. Nash is directed to the audience, congratulating Vorel. This outcome belongs to the **marionette game**, not physical injury to Nash, and is an actual game victory following earlier first-round wins.

On the feature stage, **Ventro wins the separate match against Minno**. He **kicks the defeated elf woman in the stomach**, knocking her from the stage. A tournament attendant dismisses the violence as part of the local champion's intimidating behavior and warns Vorel not to retaliate. This is **attendant testimony and visible inaction**, not proof that Sigil has no law or that the Lady of Pain cannot intervene.

## Vorel versus Ventro: the local title and public humiliation (~01:05–01:19)

Vorel proceeds to the **feature table** to face Ventro, the favorite of a large local crowd. He is mocked as an inexperienced outsider and at times misidentified as another alligatorlike fighter rather than lizardfolk. As **the last qualifier matchup**, this is the **Royce event championship**, not the entire BMF tournament. The GM now awards Vorel **full M-fighting proficiency**, adding the proficiency bonus and Dexterity modifier in the game; this is **later than E084's mere reading familiarization**.

Ventro deliberately **selects Kung before Vorel can**, expecting the lizardfolk novice to be helpless without his favored character. Vorel chooses **Bland**, the monk, and plays a strong series of **Harmony Strike dazes and repeated jump-heavy-kicks**. The GM resolves Ventro's last chance to break the combo; both saving throws fail. **Vorel defeats Ventro's Kung on the marionette field**. The opponent is enraged. Instead of conceding, Ventro lifts part of the marionette station and tries to smash it onto Vorel.

## The brawl and the silent green apparition (~01:19–01:49)

The fight spills out of the sport and into physical combat. **Nothing throws a small hammer into Ventro's arm** to interrupt the strike, causing the heavy stall to fall on his foot. **Nash tries Enlarge on Vorel**, but a mysterious external **suppressing force negates the spell** after he pays the slot. **Squirts successfully casts Command**; the GM has Ventro grovel at Vorel's feet and **praise Vorel against his will**. This is a played **Command effect**, not proof that Ventro sincerely changed allegiance or that the trickster priest's resurrection bargain has been fulfilled.

Ventro's entourage rushes the stage. Nothing scatters **ball bearings**, and several attackers tumble into one another. Squirts casts **Calm Emotions**, making **five of eight attackers indifferent** for the moment, and later readies her improvised cutlery crossbow. Vorel **rages** and endures attacks; Nothing **trips Ventro and strikes with nonlethal intent**, and Squirts drives a **steak knife into his foot**. Nash repeatedly tries to use **Minor Illusion** and **Ray of Frost**, but **both fail to manifest because of the same apparent outside negation**. At one point his fingers visibly begin a spell effect before suppression. This is **not the old E082 problem of lacking verbal/somatic components**, and no scene establishes that Nash is incapable of casting magic *in principle*. **Squirts' divine/trickster spells succeed in the same room**; whether different sources of magic explain the contrast is **unverified**. Frustrated, Nash ultimately **floats forward and physically punches an attacker for one damage**.

The gang continues to attack Vorel, who **swings one grappled thug into others** as an improvised weapon. When Ventro raises his axes over Nothing, **everyone blinks and a monumental green woman-shaped statue is suddenly in the arena**. It appears to face all viewers at once; the crowd is silent, and everyone understands a **warning without audible speech or telepathy**. Ventro protests that he did not start the trouble and drops his weapons. His body is then **drawn through a tiny pinhole in reality**, vanishing completely. The statue is gone when the onlookers blink again. **The removal is directly witnessed**, rather than only the NPC accounts of Lady of Pain authority from E082–E083, but **the statue is not explicitly self-identified as the Lady**, the mechanism is not explained, and **Ventro's death, afterlife or return are not witnessed**.

The rest of his entourage flees. An initially frightened crowd **erupts in celebration for Vorel**. The announcers name him **new Royce Gymnasium champion** and a **finals participant**, associating him with the **Mighty Squirts**. His achievement follows both a **genuinely played game win** and a **physical altercation and disappearance**; the crowd's version of the story need not include every piece of cheating and coercion. **Kanja remains unchallenged and the larger tournament is tomorrow.** No one receives the 5,000-gold pool.

## Sudden fame, offers that are not signed, and a crowded inn (~01:49–02:23)

A publication called **M Sports Weekly** wants an image and interview. Small floating creatures rapidly paint Vorel's portrait. He speaks enthusiastically about **Kung and jumping kicks**, declares **Mighty Squirts** his team and acknowledges **Squirts as its manager**. This is **public self-description and media coverage**, not proof of a formally filed esports organization or a prior team registration. Interviewers ask about Vorel's home and childhood; his personal account and the interviewer's enthusiasm are separate from verified off-screen history.

Two manufacturers offer sponsorships. **Arcane Spice**, represented by **Charles**, proposes **200 gold per month** to wear an identifiable logo and endorse its coffee flavoring. **Angry Dawgz/Dogs**, a controller company, proposes **500 gold per month** for using and praising its equipment. Vorel **declines to sign both contracts**, associating written pledges with his companions' disastrous dealings. Coaching opportunities and fees are **negotiated but no client hires him**. This is potential income, not earned recurring revenue, gear or a binding obligation.

Some fans want advice, affection or friendship. A young lizardfolk admirer asks Vorel to be his father; Vorel encourages him to seek pride in his own family and **hands him the strategy book** that was stolen from the M-sports library. The **later book custody is not tracked**. An increasingly intrusive group of admirers follows Vorel; some touch him and ignore his stated limits. **He does not consent to intimacy**. Eventually **a large crowd follows the party to the Soggy Feather** for a spontaneous victory celebration, creating an unexpected business opportunity for Bill and Pam.

Bill asks for help serving patrons. **Nash works as a waiter**, balancing a tray on his floating heart; Bill **removes the stuck syringe** from Nash's body and discards it, and pays him **one gold in wages plus 20 gold in tips**, though the GM immediately says he “now has twenty gold,” a **small spoken accounting discrepancy**. **Nothing also serves and separately swindles patrons for 24 gold**, then is given the same **one gold wage and 20 gold tips**. **Squirts instead pockets a sketchbook, two unidentified gems (yellow and green) and three unidentified elixirs (red, blue and purple)**, whose properties are **not observed**. **Vorel receives no contract cash or guaranteed fees**.

Vorel's persistent admirers continue to follow him even after he refuses their advances. Attempting to escape, he **sets a curtain on fire with his storm soul ability**, realizes it is dangerous to Bill's building, then **jumps through an upper window** to get away, raging to mitigate the fall. The glass/window is broken; Vorel suffers **four damage after mitigation**, but escapes the unwanted attention. He later returns when the crowd clears, and **Nothing lets him into the locked inn**. Nash tries to redirect the admirers to a **supposed “Stinky Weenie”** elsewhere, with **success not confirmed**; this place need not be added to the map. The party finally goes to sleep.

## Night alarm and cutoff (~02:39–02:45)

The companions have **not arranged a watch**. Nothing's sword, which has a **warning enchantment against surprise**, starts vibrating; she hears **creaking movement on the roof above the shared suite** and gives a sharp call to wake the others. Squirts flashes some light with a cantrip. They pause to listen. Suddenly **two sword-bearing, ninja-like intruders leap through a window into their room and run toward the party**.

**End of played E085 action.** The intruders' names, employer, connection to **Pelvis, the anonymous Abyss client, Ventro, the fans or anyone else** are **unknown**. The scene stops before any attack or injury is resolved. **Do not say the party repelled them, or that the kidnapping contract started**. The host later teases an “Ode to Hope and Stanley” for a future show; it is **out-of-character future programming**, not a scene in E085.
