**Played cutoff (~02:41–02:42):** In the desert beside the crashed Elhandar, **Nash, transformed into a black dragon, hits Henatar/Henitar Grape with a second acid breath**. The three-headed commander's floating disk gives way; he falls and lies limp in the sand. The cast discusses questioning him next session; **his death, interrogation, custody and the fate of Diontalus are not resolved in this recording**. The main household and rescued harbor tieflings have survived Elhandar's initial crash, though **several tieflings die during the subsequent violent impact**. The end of the episode is the combat cutoff, not a completed post-combat conversation.

## After Elhandar's first impact (~00:03–00:18)

1. **The protected household lives (~00:03–00:08).** The GM resumes at the instant of E125's crash: Villa Ornath's hidden third-floor chamber absorbs the shock, then quiets. Stanley, Hope, Vorel, Nash, Lyrielle, Angelashi and the four children are still present. Bok Bok is thrown from a seat; Chunks hangs by his diaper from a chandelier until **Lyrielle uses Mage Hand to lower him safely**. No household death is narrated at this point. Stanley learns that holding the study key lets him will the concealed doorway open or shut, after initially finding its hidden lock.
2. **Survivors were transported into the villa (~00:09–00:15).** The family finds injured harbor tieflings occupying the lower floors. One says elves gathered the harbor community before destruction and that the next thing they knew they were inside the building; **the precise transport mechanism and who directed it are not observed**. Stanley **casts Healing Word** on a badly injured tiefling (nine HP as played); Hope and Stanley help with medicine checks. Nash joins while **Angelashi remains with the four children**, who display their established shapeshifting-object forms. The home is heavily disordered within, but the outer structure has not collapsed.
3. **Buried inside the fallen station (~00:16–00:27).** Vorel tests the lower floor and finds stone, metal and broken station debris pressed against doors and windows, with no open route out. His tactile check indicates that they remain on a space-station structure rather than ordinary Earth ground. **Nobiery/Nobieri** and **Emenil/Emanil** are caring for other tieflings. Nobieri says his own attempted travel magic is blocked. Nash and the companions consider Teleportation Circle, but **no successful teleport or circle is performed** in this section; they lack a safe path outside and debate possible destinations and return arrangements.

## The secret of Villa Ornath (~00:27–00:51)

4. **Dalet's magical journal speaks (~00:28–00:31).** Stanley consults his father's book for the villa's defenses. A magically presented account from his father explains that the villa is linked to the hidden-room mask (name transcribed **Elton IV / Eltanfawr (?)**) and is part of a greater **spelljammer** capable of travelling through space. Its warded cottage form is unusually resistant to damage; with proper control it can shrink to the size of a dime **with occupants safely shrinking too**, fly and enter a vessel mode. This is new source-backed information, not proof that Stanley has already piloted it.
5. **Stanley actually dons the mask (~00:31–00:39).** He retrieves the near-weightless obsidian dragon-metal mask from his belongings, forms it into his preferred eye-mask shape and sees **eight movable chromatic gems**. It imparts knowledge of remote, mental flight/size controls. **His first attempted lift makes the house creak but meets resistance**: the mask is not attuned to him and is said to require **four consecutive nights** to develop the connection. The spelljammer mode reportedly supplies air; the inactive cottage mode does not. **Flight, shrinking and the mode change are not completed in E126.** The father's future “Prime” title and mask name need audio/orthography review.
6. **Other possible ways out (~00:39–00:50).** The group discusses Nash's circle magic and various risky destinations including the previously supplied **Mars platform and Elhandar**, while noting that re-entering the present villa by a permanent circle is not possible. Lyrielle checks the disordered pantry; Bok Bok brings Vorel a technological device which Lyrielle identifies as a **reusable beauty bomb**, intended to change nearby clothing to desired formal outfits. Lyrielle also changes her familiar from a ferret to a small **spider** to scout tiny exterior gaps; do not assert that it returned with a clear report.

## Light, a temporary opening and the second crash (~00:51–01:25)

7. **The beauty bomb actually detonates (~00:51–01:00).** Vorel activates it among the sheltered tieflings. Loud ceremonial music, streamers and magical/illusory glamorous costumes appear. Nash's ideal suit resembles *Miami Vice*; Hope adopts tough leather/armaments; Vorel sports bartender-style finery and headdress. The device **reassembles into a reusable state**. Vorel tries to lift morale through performance, though most refugees do not join in enthusiastically. The sudden shift of the cottage and falling debris immediately afterward is **observed, but its mechanical cause is not established as the dancing or the bomb**.
8. **A way up through the wreckage (~01:00–01:09).** The house tilts and stone moves, exposing daylight near an upstairs window. Opening it drops rocks into the room but leaves a narrow route. **Vorel climbs through**, sees Earth's desert, scattered Elhandar wreckage and objects burning in the atmosphere, then **returns and reports a passable way to the sand**. An apparently Elhandari ship briefly passes overhead. The party considers sheltering and allowing the injured to recover rather than immediately revealing themselves to enemies.
9. **Another falling catastrophe (~01:10–01:22).** A brilliant blue, enormous burning object and the hostile **castle ship** come down toward the crash site. Hope warns the other occupants, who move to the hidden upper chamber. The impact sets the villa spinning through the rubble. Several tieflings **actually die** after being thrown against walls; the GM does not supply a reliable total. All four of Nash's children and Chunks are **shielded by adults** rather than reported dead. Nash initially fails to grasp Angelashi but then, with help, **successfully pulls her into a safer floating position**; Vorel, Hope, Stanley and others suffer repeated bludgeoning damage. Stanley subsequently expends healing spells on **five gravely injured tieflings**; many more remain hurt. The surviving household is not thereby fully healed.
10. **A battle outside (~01:24–01:30).** Villa Ornath has been knocked clear and is again upright on desert sand. Outside, **Diontalus in blue ethereal form** is fighting the three-headed **Henatar/Henitar Grape**, now playing an immense magical **electric guitar** on his hovering disk. Sound-wave attacks drive Diontalus down. The castle ship crashes into Elhandar and triggers a huge explosion; the exact damage to the station and whether any other evacuees survive are not known. Diontalus's spectral form becomes **motionless in a crater**, visibly defeated; **death, banishment or permanent destruction is not verified**.

## Henatar's assault and the party's fight (~01:30–02:42)

11. **The bounty hunter approaches (~01:30–01:45).** With resources depleted, the party debates shelter versus defense. Nobiery and Emenil **heal the combatants**, narrated as restoring the party's HP; this is not a rest restoring spent spells. Henatar's guitar chords shake the apparently damage-resistant villa and loosen fastenings. His **six blue orbiting spheres** intercept incoming magic and later return/recharge; these are not ordinary permanent ammunition. Stanley's **Eldritch Blast** is absorbed, and other initial probes deplete individual spheres. Henatar threatens the building.
12. **Escape teleport actually fails (~01:45–01:49).** Hope and Vorel emerge, Vorel carrying the rearmed beauty bomb. Hope **casts Teleport** to close directly with the enemy; the attempt **does not transport them**, consuming the use/charges under the table's adjudication. Henatar claims to be responsible for blocking teleportation. Hope and Vorel charge on foot; the others attack from farther back. A successful later teleport must not be inferred.
13. **The orbs and hostile music (~01:49–02:04).** Stanley's **Hex** is absorbed; he grants allies **Mantle of Inspiration** and repeatedly attacks with his animated rapier **Diplomacy**. Henatar's spheres can emit incapacitating/stunning energy near him: **Vorel fails and is stunned**, while Hope resists an earlier burst. Henatar's guitar imposes a **compulsion to attack the nearest person** on both Hope and Vorel, and sonic force knocks Nash prone and deals **psychic “shredding” damage**. As played, the compulsion leads to **Hope and Vorel physically fighting each other** despite their intentions. Separate the table-specific combat effects from generic D&D spell rules.
14. **Nash casts Haste, then becomes a dragon (~02:02–02:20).** Nash **actually casts Haste on Stanley** and later transforms into a **black dragon**. Hope climbs up to Henatar's disk and disrupts his guitar-playing briefly; Vorel, compelled by the music, kicks her off and grapples her. Stanley's **Vicious Mockery** hits a sphere rather than the commander; **Diplomacy** repeatedly penetrates and wounds him. The sphere recharge timing is described by the GM as its own recurring ability, **not a reaction caused by repeating a particular player spell**.
15. **Nash's Shield rhyme is performed (~02:21–02:24).** Henatar uses the guitar to fire numerous **Magic Missiles** at dragon-form Nash. Nash actually **casts Shield** as a reaction, narrating an improvised couplet approximating “Hey buddy, turn and cough / These things you're shooting are going to bounce right off” (exact words audio-unverified). This performed phrase already has a provisional source-indexed entry **NSP-012** in [Nash's phrase register](../world/characters/nash-spellcasting-phrases.md); **do not create a duplicate**.
16. **Acid breath bypasses the orbiting spheres (~02:24–02:30).** In dragon form, Nash uses **acid breath**, which the GM specifically rules is **not intercepted** by the magic-absorbing spheres. Henatar saves and suffers half of the rolled damage, but his clothing and body are visibly burned. Hope and Vorel are still fighting under the music's compulsion.
17. **Stanley takes the guitar (~02:29–02:37).** Stanley **casts Telekinesis on himself** and successfully wins an **Arcana contest** against the guitar's magical connection, **pulling Henatar's floating instrument into his own hands**. Stanley plays it experimentally; it **fires a Fireball** but a sphere consumes the magical blast. This is an **actual temporary seizure/control of the weapon** during battle, not a proven permanent acquisition, attunement or safe mastery. Henatar fails to wrest control back and begins retreating into the sky. The hostile compulsion on Hope and Vorel ends when the guitar is no longer under his control.
18. **The cliffhanger (~02:38–02:42).** Nash's dragon breath is ruled recharged on a die roll. He flies above the fleeing Henatar, lands a **second acid-breath attack** and burns through the floating disk, sending Henatar to the ground. The GM additionally applies fall damage. Henatar is lying **limp in the desert** at the played cutoff; the cast states an intention to **talk to him next session**, not that they have captured, killed, searched or questioned him already. Credits conclude around **02:44:10**.
