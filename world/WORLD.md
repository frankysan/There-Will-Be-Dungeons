## Present-day world

- **Earth, long after human civilization as we know it:** The setting is an Earth covered in wasteland; the GM describes no oceans, no winter and pervasive dust, with scarce resources and pockets of surviving civilization. **Source:** E001 00:04:17–00:05:12 (GM exposition).
- **Dust Hill as a surviving settlement:** One of the remaining centers of cooperative habitation, built into a rocky, defensible site. **Source:** E001 00:05:12–00:08:13 (GM exposition).
- **Scavenged technology and magic coexist:** Residents repair machines and use old mechanical technology. Magic is present, discussed in the introduction in connection with engines made from dragon remains. **Source:** E000 00:02:42–00:03:25 (GM prologue); E001 00:07:14–00:07:30 (GM exposition).

## Ancient history — GM's dramatic prologue

- Long ages ago, humanity inhabited a more flourishing Earth and reached for the skies/other realms; alien beings called **dragons** arrived through gates and devastated the world. **Source:** E000 00:01:26–00:02:09.
- Humans developed battle machines, and made dangerous pacts involving demons and monsters in their fight against dragons. **Source:** E000 00:02:09–00:02:42.
- Humans made enduring **dragon engines** using the remains of slain dragons. The narration associates the engines with mana waves and extraordinary magical power. **Source:** E000 00:02:42–00:03:07.
- A prolonged war between humanity and dragons further ruined the world, and the dragons eventually vanished after scorching the land. The engines survived as valuable power sources. **Source:** E000 00:03:07–00:03:49. **Text caution:** words around 00:03:07 are visibly mistranscribed; confirm exact length of war before standardizing it.

## Cultural and metaphysical concepts

- **Dragon engines:** Rare, ancient engines associated with the dragon war and with powerful magical effects. Hope seeks one; the introduction presents them as technologically and metaphysically significant. **Sources:** E000 00:02:42–00:03:49 (GM); E000 00:09:59–00:10:17 (character aspiration); E001 00:16:02–00:16:17 (character activity).
- **Tieflings and the dark pact:** Kristen's introductory narration describes her people as emerging from a dark pact connected to the ancient conflict. This is a **character's account**, not independently established universal history. **Source:** E000 00:08:46–00:09:44.
- **Scarcity and mutual aid:** Dust Hill's economy, water supply, agricultural facilities and governance demonstrate the importance of cooperation and protected resources. **Source:** E001 00:05:55–00:08:13.

## Statements not yet promoted to established fact

- Hope believes finding a dragon engine will let her return to the past and restore the world. Her objective is explicit, but **the feasibility of time travel is not yet verified**. **Source:** E000 00:09:44–00:10:17.
- The exact chronology of the ancient war and human history contains potential recognition errors and dramatic stylization; defer precise dates.
- The Hillmasters say an old dwarven pump system feeds Dust Hill's well, but in the first episode this is presented partly as transmitted local knowledge; avoid claiming the engineering history has been independently established. **Source:** E001 00:07:30–00:08:09.

## E009–E012: facility collapse, Slavetown and contested histories (ASR-only)

**Source standard:** These notes come from the [E009](../episodes/E009.md), [E010](../episodes/E010.md), [E011](../episodes/E011.md) and [E012](../episodes/E012.md) **unverified processed Parakeet SRT dossiers**. They introduce **spoilers through E012**. Keep GM-narrated events distinct from statements by the imprisoned tree, Leopold, the Jeweler and Slavetown officials.

- **Ancient underground facility (E009 ~00:50–03:12):** The desert antenna leads to an underground complex with a large living tree, security robots, laboratories, control systems and a `warden AI protocol` menu that can alter Leopold-associated functions. The tree *claims* it was an elf transformed/imprisoned thousands of years ago during human anti-dragon experiments. The party authorizes a facility self-destruct and escapes during collapse; these do not independently establish the tree's complete origin or Leopold's nature.
- **Black dragon observation (E009 ~03:10–03:12):** After the underground collapse, a huge black dragon **emerges visibly and flies upward out of sight**. Do not assume the animal's age, intentions, affiliation or exact method of imprisonment; chronological proximity to the facility collapse does not identify a precise mechanism. The E010 party observes that it has flown away.
- **Fate of guest characters (E009 ~03:08–03:13):** Edison "Eddy/Eddie" Manchester III and Chance are **lost beneath falling sand** while Nash and Stanley escape. The GM/host explicitly says *out of character* they are **not dead**; their in-world location and rescue remain unresolved.
- **Slavetown society (E010 ~02:01–03:19; E011–E012):** The town houses slaves (including people from Dusthill), an official magistrate, the **Rusty Cage** combat-court, an orc warrior order (working transcription `Koromukishi`/`Kuromukishi`) and figures invoking **the Principle**. The party wins its trial in E010 and is released in E011. **This releases the adventurers, not the enslaved townsfolk.**
- **Government, faith and commerce:** The Principle is invoked by legal/military figures; the **Oilbeards** are a mining/slaving family; **Celibus Stump** runs a commercial hospitality/gambling concern; **the Jeweler** operates the Sapphire and speaks against Principle interference. Their interests overlap and conflict; do not collapse them into a single faction.
- **Disputed origins of Slavetown:** In **E012 ~03:02–03:09** the Jeweler *claims* the settlement was once called **Pristine**, transformed by **Trentard Oilbeard**'s mines and the growing slave trade with the Principle. This is **NPC testimony**, not independently established universal history.
- **Dusthill betrayal allegation (E012 ~02:55–02:59):** The Jeweler alleges a Hillmaster and a gnome resembling **Softhead** came to Slavetown and compromised Dusthill's safety. The speaker's confidence is not evidence of actual intent or a complete timeline. Preserve as an **unresolved allegation**; compare to E001–E006 and other evidence.
- **Rescue/complicity problem:** Talia, Bruce Bane's mother, is recognized among the enslaved in **E011 ~02:00–02:11**; E012 offers temporary accommodation without guaranteed freedom. Stump and the Jeweler bargain over a mine deed in connection with releasing Dusthill citizens and/or displacing slaving influence. None of the various offers equals a completed rescue.
- **Mine deed:** E012 uses **copper mine** and **gold mine** in close succession, while also discussing other mining property. Use *mine deed* as the current neutral descriptor, with a logged discrepancy; missing holders and intermediaries remain third-party statements.

These discoveries extend the world reference but do not alter earlier-episode character knowledge or establish a reliable legal/cosmological interpretation of the Principle, the dragon, or the tree.

## E013–E014 and the E013.5 supplement: political background, character cosmology and ritual danger

**Sources:** [E013](../episodes/E013.md), [E013.5](../episodes/E013.5.md), [E014](../episodes/E014.md). These are navigational findings from **processed Parakeet SRTs; no audio has been checked**. Release order is **E013 → E013.5 → E014**, but the supplemental's Hope/Vorel dialogue belongs **in-story around E008–E009**.

- **Pristine-to-Slavetown transition: now explicitly GM-explained in E013 ~00:05–00:12.** The GM says that over roughly **10–20 years** the **Oilbeards** cut a supply and slavery deal with representatives of the **Principle**, surrendered broader sovereignty and saw Pristine renamed **Slavetown**. The **Toyota Host** serves as the larger military force; **Koromukishi** are comparatively few honor-code warriors/officers; the town retains a **sheriff** and some frontier self-government even under the Principle's magistrate. This **out-of-character GM exposition** is a stronger setting source than E012's interested Jeweler account, though timing/terminology remain approximate.
- **Rival mine-deed stakes (E013 ~00:10–00:16):** The GM distinguishes **Celibus Stump's** desire to destroy/implicate the Jeweler and consolidate influence from **the Jeweler's** wish to use the deed against **Oilbeard** control. Dialogue here often says **gold mine**, while E012 includes **copper**; do not erase either recognition in the absence of a document.
- **Lactid/Lacted Cartel (E013 ~00:19–02:12):** An armed cartel from/associated with **Principle City** appears at the Sapphire and wounds the Jeweler. Stump later portrays it as vying to supplant the Oilbeards. Its purpose, client and precise demanded item remain **partly unclear**, and **Red Scarlet** should not be treated as an alias for the cartel.
- **Supplemental character cosmology, not GM law (E013.5 ~00:12–00:22):** **Vorel** tells Hope stories about a red astral figure named `Exantos` and lizardfolk once raised into armies by dragons; **Hope** describes a dragon-origin **portal constellation**, an unfinished **dark pact** and **her goal to travel into the past to complete it**, apparently via dragon engines. These are **in-character cultural claims and intentions**; the supplemental was **released after E013** but the conversation is set around **E008–E009**. No demonstrated chronomancy or cosmic history can be inferred from their conversation alone.
- **The cyclops (E013 ending → E014 opening):** An enormous one-eyed humanoid demands its stolen magical **shiny**, believes a man called `Thailander`/variant has it and is fascinated by magic tricks. The party discovers a **five-bead necklace** further into E014 that appears relevant. Avoid deducing the cyclops's proper name, trustworthiness or the necklace's actual powers from his claims.
- **New dangerous underground site (E014 ~01:40–03:00):** A residence on **Thorn Row** conceals magical traps and ritual chambers; **Randall Owen** is found mutilated and freshly dead. The final antagonist says he serves/summons **Catoblepraithe / the Soulbroker** and animates corpses. **Invoked ≠ manifested**: the ending is a confrontation with the ritualist and newly animated dead, not the observed presence of the named demon.
- **Rebel movements and agency:** A **Liberators recruitment brochure** and **Red Scarlet** message emerge in E014. Their origin and alliances require independent work. They cannot be presumed identical to the **Laborer's Accord**, **Cartel**, **Jeweler**, **Stump**, or **Principle**, merely because the same story arc mentions them.

## E019–E021 — the deep Black Mine, Tylander's diary and competing testimony

**Primary evidence:** [E019](../episodes/E019.md), [E020](../episodes/E020.md), [E021](../episodes/E021.md) (**unverified processed Parakeet SRT**, original files preserved; spoiler horizon E021). - **Black Mine's deep shaft (E019):** An elevator battle pits the four PCs against **large numbers of undead miners** while the lift initially ascends. **Scrolly Oilbeard's** guard contingent reverses the mechanism rather than admitting the undead. **Tickles**, the coerced human made to behave as a dog, jumps aboard and descends with the group. A chain subsequently snaps; after a long free fall **Stanley casts Feather Fall**, saving the party; the falling corpses become a horrible mass of remains at the bottom. The **exact floor reached is unnumbered**, not the magistrate's alleged level **999**.
- **Death of Tickles (E019–E020):** Tickles survives the elevator plunge but is crushed in a **rockfall trap** when Hope operates an isolated lever in E019's final scene. In **E020 ~00:14**, he is uncovered with a dented helmet and fails to respond; the party says **he is dead**. Distinguish E019's *unresolved end* from E020's *confirmation*.
- **Ancient/old workings (E020):** A correctly operated **second safety lever** opens a narrow passage to a preserved study containing **Tylander Sullivan's handwritten journal**. The GM explicitly identifies the journal's name/author. Its **day 245** entry addresses **Mariosa**, invokes **Daisy**, **Tobyl/Tobel** and **Lily**, describes a **voice behind a door deep in the Black Pit**, blames **the Principle** for family loss and says a **woman in Slavetown** must be won over to advance vengeance. **Written, self-interested testimony**, not independently confirmed ancient history. The male skeleton beside it is **not identified** as Tylander.
- **Tainted ecology / glyphs (E020–E021):** An old chasm holds **mushroom growth**, **magical glyphs**, and a wounded **displacer beast**; the group defeats the beast in E020 by the pit. An experiment with the corpse produces **opaque magical darkness**, entered at E020's ending. E021 begins with **Hope privately hearing “find me”** while inside this cloud. A separate group of **hostile animate tentacled mushrooms** attacks, and further glyphs obstruct the path. These are different kinds of **fungi, magical objects and creature encounters**, not a single homogeneous “Mushroom Kingdom” population.
- **Dorothy (E021):** The group converses with an apparently **talking metal-faced door** called **Dorothy**, answers/negotiates around a riddle and access restrictions, and ultimately passes. Her claims of feelings, a maker or “mother,” and lethal security measures are **partly voice testimony**, not a verified ontology for enchanted objects.
- **Tylander and Pristine (E021 ~02:05–02:20):** Tylander **claims he formerly served as mayor of Pristine**, refused the **Toyota Host/Principle's** demand for slaves, watched **Oilbeard-backed agents** kill his family in a public square, and was thrown into the Black Mine. He says a **demonic patron gave him a new body and powers**. This detailed antagonist account can be compared with **E013 GM exposition** and the **E020 journal**; neither agreement nor physical undead makes every autobiographical detail objectively proven.
- **Undead as manufactured force (E021):** Tylander **admits turning people enslaved in the mines and town into undead** because they would not fight for him. This **speaker confession** is consistent with the direct physical zombie encounters in E018–E019, but does not confirm the fate of every miner or innocence of all parties. His stated goal is **destruction of Pristine/Slavetown and his enemies**, not indiscriminate liberation by peaceful methods.
- **Fingers of Catoblepraithe (E021):** Eight human-like followers attend the underground chapel. Tylander names them his **Fingers**, orders them to transform and attack the party; **the party defeats them** after the negotiation breaks down.
