## The world and its ancient catastrophe

### The surviving Earth

In the opening GM description, the Wastes are **a far-future Earth**, mostly dry, dusty and resource-poor, with no oceans or winter as described in the first episode. Isolated protected settlements depend on **scavenged machinery, limited farming, military force, organized mutual aid and magic**. The scarcity of working water systems drives the first adventure, and competition over the conversion or transport of scarce resources later drives politics in Tietkalla. This is **setting exposition**, not a claim that the explorers have surveyed the entire planet. [E000](../episodes/E000.md) · [E001](../episodes/E001.md).

The dramatic prologue describes **dragons arriving through gates** in an earlier, more prosperous age. Humanity resisted with advanced machines and dangerous **pacts with monsters or demonic forces**; a protracted conflict ravaged the world. Surviving societies use **dragon engines**, powerful devices associated with dragon remains and **mana emissions**, even though the fate of the dragons themselves is not fully reconstructed from the first-year episodes. The exact dates, length of the war, identity of the gates' builders and the accuracy of poetic prologue language **remain open to audio and source review**. [E000](../episodes/E000.md).

### Uneven records of the past

Different civilizations remember the ancient age differently. **Hope's introductory tiefling account** connects her ancestry to a dark pact; **Nash** remembers the Solar Mines through experiences that later seem inconsistent; an apparition of **Peter** disputes Dusthill's own inherited leadership; **Tylander Sullivan** offers his own account of past rule and forced labor; and Tietkalla's **Nobiery, Kefeli and Monsoon** disagree over the kingdom's destruction and who deserves to rule it. Their stories should remain **speaker-attributed historical claims** where the party cannot verify them. An ancient-looking skeleton, artifact or wall inscription is not by itself proof of the narrative supplied by the person describing it. [E000](../episodes/E000.md) · [E005](../episodes/E005.md) · [E013](../episodes/E013.md) · [E021](../episodes/E021.md) · [E048](../episodes/E048.md) · [E051](../episodes/E051.md).

## Settlements, societies and political power

### Dusthill — shared survival and disputed inheritance

The party meets in **Dusthill**, a defensible settlement enclosed by rock, with farms, a garage, public water distribution and a succession of **Hillmasters**. Its reliance on a **subterranean pump and dragon-engine complex** demonstrates a society organized around a finite shared resource. When the current Hillmaster dies during the expedition, a returning apparition called **Peter** alleges that later leaders were impostors; this is **his testimony**, not an automatic retroactive invalidation of every previous ruler. A goblin raid kills and abducts residents, and an emergency meeting appoints **Shun'ti** to a temporary civic role while **Tetsuo and Jimster** help organize defense. By E006 the repaired water outlet is **tested flowing**, distinct from the earlier moment at which it remained untested. [Dusthill reference](places/dusthill.md) · [E002](../episodes/E002.md) · [E005](../episodes/E005.md) · [E006](../episodes/E006.md).

### Pristine, Slavetown and the Principle

**Slavetown**, the slaveholding city where the adventurers become captives, grew from a settlement that the GM in an E013 **out-of-character briefing** calls **Pristine**. He describes a shift over roughly **10–20 years** involving the **Oilbeards**, slave traffic and arrangements with agents of the **Principle**. This is stronger evidence for the briefing's intended setting context than a dealer's or magistrate's sales pitch, though the numerical interval should still be checked against audio. The name **Principle** appears in source dialogue alongside `Principal`; neither spelling choice establishes a different person or institution without referent review. In the [June 2019 bonus](../episodes/BONUS-2019-06.md), **Beau retrospectively says** he conceived the Principle in episode one, describes him as **over one thousand years old** and familiar with life and technology **before the Scorching**, and warns that **Quizquaz's lich comparison is insufficient**. This is **GM creator testimony with deliberately withheld details**, not the Principle's independently proven species or the party's knowledge from E013. [E013](../episodes/E013.md) · [Slavetown reference](places/slavetown.md).

Slavetown's political economy includes **coercive trials, slave labor, competing government and military agents, the Koromukishi, Oilbeard interests, the Lactid Cartel, criminal middlemen and rival claims to a mine deed**. The protagonists gain their own freedom but **do not free every Dusthill captive**. A supposed agreement or bank heist is not a directly witnessed fact merely because a faction spokesman describes it. The city suffers a substantial **real fire in E017–E018** while Nash is also experiencing **Pixie Blush hallucinations**; those are two different kinds of evidence. [E010](../episodes/E010.md)–[E018](../episodes/E018.md).

### Tietkalla, Trashburg and the keep

**Tietkalla** is the project display for a historic tiefling city/kingdom supported by **Jon's cast-written spelling**, with ASR aliases `Tiat Cala` and `Tiat Kala`. **Teet/Teet keep** often refers to its fortified seat; **Trashburg** is the poorer, heavily governed settlement associated with it. These labels are **related places**, not necessarily identical administrative boundaries in every scene. By Year One, the fortress is ruled through the **Guzzle Gang/Suck Master Din**, opposed or exploited by **Monsoon Cabbage's Twenty-Sixers**, **Toe Rippers** and other local interests. Jolt's planned **royal wedding** promises political advantage; a **Blood Ball** match is prepared and trained for but **not actually played**. [Tietkalla reference](places/tietkalla.md) · [E031](../episodes/E031.md)–[E039](../episodes/E039.md).

In E054, after ruin and siege, **Nobiery actually arrives with hundreds of armed tieflings**, **publicly crowns Hope Akmenos**, and brings house representatives into a war council. The returned troops and the acclamation are **observed played outcomes**; they do **not independently establish** the earlier exact claim of **one thousand soldiers from twelve houses** or the legality of an ancient royal succession. **Vorel executes captive Suck Master Din at Hope's signal**. Named figures such as **Melgor of Poxis** (security), **Fula Dentipilius** (food/farms) and **Relium Vanch** (construction) organize or propose work; those orders are **not evidence** that the sweeps, harvests, clinics, citizenship protections or city rebuilding have already succeeded. Some officers openly distrust non-tieflings; Hope admits Nash, Stanley and Vorel to her council despite opposition. **Monsoon escapes as mist** and the city's future remains contested. [E054](../episodes/E054.md).

The **Golden Land** and **Diptak** connect Vorel to lizardfolk politics. ## Dragon engines, machinery and altered bodies

**Dragon engines** link Old-World craft with present-day magic and resource economics. Beneath Dusthill the party distinguishes an **engine**, pumping machinery and a **much lower river intake**; separate work on those components precedes the successful **E006 outlet test**. A specter suggests that dragon engines are **blood-fueled**—a specific supernatural witness claim, not a confirmed universal engineering law. Hope's ambition to use a dragon engine to change the past is **her objective**, not proof that a practical time machine has been built. [E000](../episodes/E000.md) · [E002](../episodes/E002.md)–[E006](../episodes/E006.md).

**Nash's heart** is a metal dragon-engine-like organ revealed during the **Château Weir** encounters. It can emit powerful mana and helps explain his transformation into first a **black dragon wyrmling** and later a **young black dragon**; the transformations carry costs such as **exhaustion**. The [Year One Q&A](../episodes/BONUS-2019-03.md) adds Beau's **retrospective design explanation**: he developed the heart concept after Scott improvised uncertainty about Nash having a normal heart. This tells us how the fiction was constructed, not which Solar Mines memories were literally true. The identity of Nash's former betrayer is also **partially unshared player backstory**, not a settled NPC in Year One.

**Fate 4**, Hope's repaired and improved vehicle, holds the companions together on the road; it is **not already a successfully time-traveling dragon engine**. The hidden **Leopold laboratory** uses access badges, surveillance, programmable containment and a powerful imprisoned tree. The subterranean dwarven complex beneath Dusthill includes **animated machinery such as C4N-D13**. These installations are different places with different functions; the E008–E009 facility should not be confused with the **Black Mine** because both are deep underground. In the [June bonus](../episodes/BONUS-2019-06.md) ~00:00:33–00:02:43, Beau recalls an **unclaimed magic staff** he placed among the laboratory loot as **tree- or druid-associated**, but says he **cannot recall its name or exact powers**. Do not invent a staff inventory entry, assign it to Nash or conflate it with Stanley's **Immovable Rod**. [E006](../episodes/E006.md) · [E008](../episodes/E008.md)–[E009](../episodes/E009.md).

**Body alteration and medical technology** range from Vorel's lizardfolk ritual experience and Nash's engineered heart to **Hope's amputations and mechanical prostheses** after the rebounded rocket in E038–E039. These are actual character-altering consequences, not simply costume changes. Hope later refuses a proposed **vampire transformation**. A separate homebrew procedure using a previously killed **basilisk** and old preserved eyes **restores the petrified Hope and Vorel in E051**; the scene does **not validate a universal alchemical recipe** or create remaining stock. [E038](../episodes/E038.md)–[E039](../episodes/E039.md) · [E051](../episodes/E051.md).

## Otherworldly powers, bargains and artifact provenance

### Soulbroker and Château Weir

In E023, after the **Black Mine beholder** confrontation, the party enters **Château Weir** and meets **Catoblepraithe/Catobelpraithe** (spelling unresolved), the **Soulbroker**. **Hope, Nash and Stanley willingly sign** a soul-related compact obliging action against the **Principle** within a stipulated **one thousand days/nights**; **Vorel refuses**. Hospitality waivers, signed bargains and descriptions of the Weir are **different source types**. A claimed afterlife consequence is part of a supernatural contract and is not automatically witnessed later. The freed ancient scholar **Nobiery/Nobieri** departs through it in E048, reportedly seeking his army, and **returns with armed soldiers in E054**. [E048](../episodes/E048.md) · [E054](../episodes/E054.md).

### Drug and pixie exploitation

**Pixie Blush** is a drug associated with **pixies' exploitation and crushed bodies**, powerful hallucinations, and in large concentrations **involuntary monstrous enlargement or other unstable effects**. **P0D**, a pixie close to Nash, describes captivity/harvesting around Trashburg. **Monsoon's Order 26** causes her monstrous transformation in E049, followed by **Diontalus killing her E050**. Other enormous pixies later appear and some return to small size; those are not resurrected P0D. In E050 Hope experiences intense **drug-induced imagery**, including a vision of Tedna rising toward a **steel heaven**; the death is real, but the imagery is **not observed metaphysical geography**. The [Q&A](../episodes/BONUS-2019-03.md) confirms an **unchosen pixie-rescue route** and GM preparation of Monsoon's transformed troops, not a liberation already carried out. [E031](../episodes/E031.md) · [E049](../episodes/E049.md)–[E054](../episodes/E054.md).

## Religions, souls, curses and uncertain realms

The **Weir** is the Soulbroker's domain or claimed destination for souls, but the precise metaphysical nature of the place, its rules and its relationship to other realms is **not fully verified** by an actual-play journey alone. The ancient scholar claims to be a **Dead Tongue** guiding tiefling souls to a **Night Pasture** rather than the Weir. That description, the alleged royal line, and claims about a vampire-cursed council should be kept as **the scholar's testimony** unless independently supported. The direct arrival of his armed forces in E054 validates **some of his concrete promises**, not every cosmological or historical explanation. [E048](../episodes/E048.md) · [E054](../episodes/E054.md).

**Kefeli**, the serpent-haired figure encountered under Tietkalla, truly **petrifies Hope and Vorel**. She says **Andreeste/the Nourisher** enabled her transformation to defend tieflings against a curse; **Monsoon** tells a partially competing story, later warning of Andreeste's coming claim when Kefeli is killed. Kefeli is destroyed as a statue in E051 and Monsoon escapes E054. **Andreeste does not arrive within the documented Year One ending**; claims about her divine authority and future intervention remain **NPC forecasts**. [E039](../episodes/E039.md) · [E051](../episodes/E051.md) · [E054](../episodes/E054.md).

The **Fuxi** traditions of Vorel's people, claims of **tiefling savior gods**, Hope's privately experienced maternal voice or vision, and Nash's sense of a **“Mother”** near dragon activity are all important to how characters understand themselves. They should not be flattened into a single confirmed pantheon, family relationship or afterlife. In the Year One Q&A, **Beau explicitly says he left the setting's gods and the “steel heaven” uncertain**. No later encyclopedia should promote Hope's pixie-blush imagery to a GM-confirmed excursion into heaven. [E015](../episodes/E015.md)–[E016](../episodes/E016.md) · [E023](../episodes/E023.md) · [Q&A](../episodes/BONUS-2019-03.md).

## The three-month transition and new political order (E055–E056)

The **three-month interval first becomes accomplished fictional time in E055**, not E054. The new government **abolishes Guzbucks and houseless serfdom**, orders toilets and merit-based employment, and reports **repaired walls and vehicle fleet**. Distinct families still **contest access to work**, and chief engineer Vanch/Relium reports a **shortage of building materials**. The **Golden Sands inn and adventurers' guild** is now a working local institution under **Vorel and Bok Bok**. **Stanley** helps with a hospital and studies the **Tidebearers**, learning to produce water with the Fishbird mantle. The **church of Malhya/Malhaia** and its priestess **Emenil/Eminel** provide **ritual and practical healing**; the priestess's doctrine that the **“savior gods”** oppose all Weirlords is *her belief*, not proof that the earlier god ambiguity is resolved. [E055](../episodes/E055.md) · [E056](../episodes/E056.md) · [April Q&A](../episodes/BONUS-2019-04.md).

At E055 court, a formerly missing gnome **Softhead** claims to represent the **Principle**, denies that his master personally ordered the Dusthill attack and **offers release of Dusthill captives and negotiation**. These are **terms proposed by an interested envoy**, not the observed return of any prisoners. Hope **orders Softhead's tongue cut out**, keeps him alive and indicates willingness to meet. A captured Twenty-Sixer is ordered executed, and the young queen's command of punishment creates questions that cannot be glossed over as merely benevolent reform. A **poisoned assailant invokes a name approximately “Dollo Poppio”** and attacks Hope in her private chamber; palace guards kill him. The entity named and principal architect of the attempt are not confirmed just from the attack. [E055](../episodes/E055.md).

An **enormous object burns through the sky and falling stones kill civilians**. The party directs residents toward **subterranean shelters**, but rescues do not cancel the deaths already witnessed. In **Nobiery's ritual**, Hope enters an **ethereal landscape**, apparently faces tiefling ancestors, and encounters **Quizquaz**. He alleges **Andreeste** has hired a contractor whose name sounds like `Dollo Poppio`, that the Soulbroker is litigating at a **Grim Council**, and that a **dragon was destroyed entering the world**. He offers a contract involving **Melgor and fifty tieflings**; **Hope refuses**. These are **the oil-demon's interested claims**, not independently checked cosmology, astronomy or a confirmed dragon death. [E056](../episodes/E056.md).

The ritual shows **angry spirits with real effects**: **George Miller appears as a ghost** without undoing E045 murder; an apparition of **Martha Miller** follows; **P0D speaks to Nash in spectral form**, without returning to mortal life. Ghostly aging leaves **Vorel elderly**, but **Emenil performs Greater Restoration and he is fully returned to normal within E056**. At the episode's final cliffhanger, **Nobiery commands a reanimated Carnage to attack Emenil** when she objects to the royal family's hidden **demonic dealings**. Her **death is not confirmed**. The claimed provenance/number of that corpse `47/49 Carnage` needs reconciliation with E055's captured/executed gang member. [E055](../episodes/E055.md), [E056](../episodes/E056.md).

**External geography and development:** At the Golden Sands a trading captain `Joderty Strongnee` (ASR) of the **Diamond Stroke** reports supplies and routes through **Esophageal Pass**, **Mogen Frogan**, supposed dwarven ruins and three spires of **Principle City**. ## The Principle's personal diplomacy and crystallized archfey (E058–E059)

The **Principle's metallic/blue-etched appearance with ten red rings** is physically described when he comes to Tietkalla court. He offers to **return captives**, trade oil/water for materials, establish a **rail connection**, permit reciprocal embassies, and build a **small place of Steel God/Jesus Chrome worship**. He alleges the **Slavetown magistrate Ethanelia** caused the Dusthill raid without his order, claims ancient personal service to the Steel God and argues that coerced labor serves his anti-Weirlord war. The party challenges his slavery ethic but **provisionally accepts political/economic cooperation**, requiring paid voluntary labor and security precautions. **The railroad and embassy are negotiated projects, not completed infrastructure by E060.** [E058](../episodes/E058.md) · [E059](../episodes/E059.md).

The Principle **actually extracts Diontalus's active presence from Stanley into a glowing crystal in E059**, and **takes the crystal away**. He proposes confinement and possible launch into space, neither yet shown. This is not the same thing as proving that Diontalus has ceased to exist. His claim of a destroyed pre-catastrophe city and of relying on **Buttons' intelligence** is **the ruler's explanation**, not independently checked historical truth. **Some Dusthill people actually return alive in E059**, including **Talia, Bruce Bane, Jimster and Shun'ti**, while other former prisoners describe forced labor in **Salivating Fields**; a general account of living conditions does **not** vindicate slavery. The fate of all originally abducted people remains open. [E059](../episodes/E059.md).

## Razak Val — lizardfolk political power and competing dragon traditions (E059–E063)

Vorel goes with **Diptak** to the mountain/valley settlement provisionally written **Razak Val** (also `Razak Fal/Razakvale`). **At the E059–E060 cutoff the Great Fang is Orp the cyclops**, who keeps living people tethered as food; one is killed in front of the party. **Diptak is killed by Orp in E060**, and Vorel formally challenges Orp to **a single-combat scaling duel** to remove him, scheduled for the next morning **after E060**, not already resolved. An influential small lizardfolk court adviser **Mirion/Mirian of clan Fengar** explains that **Orp was adopted by his family**, following the death of an earlier Great Fang who fought Orp. Leadership is therefore shaped by **clan statecraft as well as public warrior reputation**, contradicting Vorel's assumption that simple strength is the whole governing mechanism. The exact number **478 clans** is stated in dialogue, not independently enumerated. [E059](../episodes/E059.md) · [E060](../episodes/E060.md) · [Razak Val place guide](places/razak-val.md).

The city has a **Tailway shrine, public arena (Razak Duar), Cave Fengar** and many hunting clans. The religious **Council of Twelve** and its emissaries (including **Typalla**, associated with the dragon **Fuxi/Fushi**) represent **local belief and institutional ritual**; no one has demonstrated that all named dragon gods are physically manifest or that the local priesthood is unbiased. **Bramie/Braimie (“Tyrant Slayer”)** and **Skrot/Toxar** discuss the nearby **Demon Vold**, from which exotic prey including a grotesque **“chicken lord”** is said to come. ### The Solar Mines and deliberate artifact design — August bonus creator testimony

In the [August public-release Q&A](../episodes/BONUS-2019-08.md), Scott describes the **Solar Mines** as an exploitative industrial operation where **Nash and other workers were compelled to harvest solar power from old panels under dangerous, often fatal radiation exposure**. Supervisors or a privileged class avoided the risk; workers might be allowed books and old entertainment as a means of keeping them. This is **player backstory testimony**, not a scene played in E068, and **Nash's specific breaking point/escape trigger is intentionally withheld**. It enriches the setting's social and technological history while remaining speaker-attributed.

Beau says he **conceived bespoke, identity-building magic artifacts for all main PCs**: Nash's dragon-engine heart and **Vorel's Eye of Groxl** are integrated into their bodies and can be developed beyond conventional leveling. **Diontalus** entered Stanley's life accidentally in an unrelated adventure; **he has not yet received the preplanned custom artifact**. Kyle's hope for an **Eye shield that becomes a buzzsaw, hammer or sword** is **future player imagination**, not a currently available combat rule. The party favors **milestone leveling**, and its limited loot-gathering partly reflects **the impoverished Mad Max-style economy**, Beau's limited loot-table preparation and Stanley's dislike of modern firearms. These are **cast/GM explanations**, not proof firearms are universally useless or that E068's rifles were looted.

### Secure portal apparatus versus the claimed Traversal Drive (E075)

In CHIDS's lower laboratory weapons section the party physically operates **a sealed, anechoic chamber-within-chamber** by coordinating **buttons, valves, closed main door and a control computer**. Firing an endpoint at an incompatible target can **remove that aperture while leaving the other inert**. **Vorel gives the sole device to Hope**. Nash recognizes a similar concept from an ancient game; his suggested long-range escapes are **plans**, not demonstrations of unlimited teleportation.
