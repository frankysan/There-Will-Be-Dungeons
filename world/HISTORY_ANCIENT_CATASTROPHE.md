# Ancient catastrophe — dragons, gates and the Scorching

Long before the four companions crossed the Wastes, Earth was a different world. The GM's opening account evokes an older human civilization whose machines and extraordinary power could not prevent a ruinous conflict: **dragons came through gates**, people made weapons and dangerous bargains, and devastation left a scarred, thirsty planet. This is the broad historical frame for the campaign, **not a dated record** identifying every participant, the original gate-builders or the precise beginning and end of the **Scorching**.[^prologue]

The traces of that age remain close to everyday life. Desert communities depend on salvaged machinery, including dragon engines, while ancient laboratories and sealed installations still contain functioning devices. Some devices help people survive; others bear evidence of coercive experimentation. Their survival connects the present to a destructive past without proving that any one surviving storyteller fully understands that past.

## Opening account

The [E000 prologue](../episodes/E000.md) places the arrival of dragons, gate travel, human technology and catastrophic war before the main adventures. The companions themselves do not witness this ancient invasion. Nor does the prologue establish an exact calendar, comprehensive military history or a single confirmed explanation for all later magic, demons and planetary damage. The [world overview](WORLD.md) follows the effects of this inherited landscape on settlements, power and ordinary survival.[^prologue]

Stories handed down inside the Wastes are not interchangeable with the opening narration. A remembered **Solar Mines** uprising, the contested lineage of **T'ietkalla**, and **the Principal's** accounts of earlier research concern particular people and institutions, not independent confirmation of how the first gates opened. The Dunebreaker adventures are a **played historical prequel** before the central party's journey, but are not thereby scenes from the Scorching itself. ## Surviving evidence versus claims

The travelers encounter real remnants of older engineering beneath [Dusthill](places/dusthill.md), in the [Leopold laboratory](../episodes/E008.md), in T'ietkalla's ruins and especially under [Khaervilon](places/khaervilon.md) at [CHIDS](places/chids.md). The CHIDS complex contains physical bodies, biological containment, conditioning controls and the enormous [Traversal Drive](technology/traversal-drive.md). These discoveries establish **experiments performed on particular subjects**. They do **not** prove that CHIDS created all lizardfolk, every dragon or the Principal, nor that the Drive once successfully moved Earth.[^chids]

Later witnesses offer different explanations. A spectral dragon in E067 says an ancient human machine beneath Khaervilon was intended to move the planet away from dragons. A CHIDS hive council says it preserved human minds, engineered soldiers and sought weapons against an invasion; its E078 explanation even distinguishes its recruited lizardfolk genetic material from creating their whole people. **Quizquaz** describes the Drive's power as coming from the Void, while Delit Or'Nath's E118 journal proposes time-adjacent histories involving the Weir and a Nash-like anomaly. The party **hears or reads** these accounts, but their common reference to spectacular machinery does not independently prove their ancient chronology or cosmology.[^rival-accounts]

A dying black dragon later presents another history: human colonists allegedly entered a strange portal, made a Soulbroker bargain and exposed Earth to the Weir. Its suggested remedy using the dragon's **natural heart** later inspires the [Rubistic Triangulosphere](technology/rubistic-triangulosphere.md). The heart's actual extraction and Ruby's eventual effect do not verify every assertion about the dragonflights, the bargain, or who originally caused the Scorching. Nash's **separate engineered heart** must not be substituted for that recovered natural heart.[^dragon]

## Lasting legacy and unanswered history

The Scorching leaves more than broken structures: scarcity, forced labor, contested monarchies, efforts at sanctuary and competing claims of divine or scientific authority shape the lives of the Wastes' peoples. By the played **S3E18 finale**, the heroes have banished the Worldbreaker and authorized Ruby's activation. The GM visibly restores rain and vegetation to Earth, while ordinary Earthbound mana-wave spellcasting ends. That witnessed renewal is a **later event**, not retrospective proof of the precise mechanism, date or full responsibility for the ancient catastrophe.[^legacy]

The unresolved questions remain substantial: **who built and controlled the first gates; how the wars and Scorching unfolded; what CHIDS and the Drive were originally intended to accomplish; whether particular dragon, hive and Weir accounts correspond to a common history; and which ancient institutions survived into the protagonists' era**. The [campaign chronology](CHRONOLOGY.md) and [historical events](HISTORICAL_EVENTS.md) provide the sequence of known episodes; the [CHIDS crisis history](HISTORY_CHIDS_DRIVE.md) records its particular research, and the [consistency ledger](CONSISTENCY_LEDGER.md) retains disagreements rather than treating theories as settled facts.
