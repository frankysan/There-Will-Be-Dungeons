# Places and locations

## Dusthill (ASR alias: Dust Hill)

**Type:** Wasteland settlement. **Spelling status:** `Dusthill` is documented in the [wiki](https://twbd.fandom.com/wiki/There_Will_Be_Dungeons_Wiki). `Dust Hill` is retained as a transcription/search alias; speech has not been independently proofread.

**Description:** Nestled in a partly enclosed rocky setting, with giant red rocks forming a natural protective barrier and winding entrance; has gates and defensive positions. Its residents include farmers, workers, mechanics, defenders and people from different ancestries. The community is governed by a lineage of Hillmasters, with established traditions of cooperation and accepting outsiders after vetting. **Source:** E001 00:05:12–00:06:45 (GM exposition).

**Facilities:** Mud huts; long-standing barns used for meals/storage; farms in caves; garage/mechanical workshop; centralized water delivery described as a well or pump/valve. **Source:** E001 00:06:45–00:08:13.

**Episode 1 significance:** The party assembles here, receives an invitation to the Hillmaster's feast, and becomes involved in a crisis when the water system stops working. **Sources:** E001 00:08:51–00:50:00, 02:08:09–02:12:00.

## Dusthill's well / water system

**Type:** Infrastructure, probably connected to ancient subterranean pumps.

**Description:** The 'well' is a valve and plumbing arrangement drawing water from below ground; water is traditionally distributed by a designated attendant. Local accounts credit an ancient dwarven installation buried under fallen rock. **Sources:** E001 00:07:27–00:08:09 and 00:41:01–00:41:48 (GM).

**Episode 1 development:** The water stops flowing; the Hillmaster says reserves may last only weeks, subject to rationing, and seeks help investigating underground. **Source:** E001 02:08:00–02:12:00.

**Reference warning:** Whether the installation is literally a 'well' or a pump-fed water outlet is clarified in the session; use `water system` for neutral references.

## Dusthill garage

**Type:** Settlement workshop.

**Description:** A mechanically oriented work area cut into/near the mountain; accommodates scavenged machinery and repair material. The GM describes a very small car and a small RV with enormous wheels, as well as guarded weapons and parts. **Source:** E001 00:16:17–00:18:23.

**Character connection:** Hope works here, adjusting a vehicle intended in her view to be able to hold a dragon engine. Mechanic `Gimster`/`Gymster` is mentioned. **Source:** E001 00:16:02–00:18:23.

## The underground complex and concealed door

**Type:** Restricted subterranean area linked to the water crisis.

**Description:** The Hillmaster takes the party into a hidden route; traps, a cave passage, a gap with a bridge, and a locked door become relevant in the first expedition. **Sources:** E001 02:11:37–02:31:30, 02:58:30–03:01:25.

**Plot note:** The episode ends shortly after the door opens, in a dramatic armed confrontation; the exact final action is held for careful audio review. **Source:** E001 03:01:20–03:02:10.

## Underground infrastructure and ruins revealed in E002–E005

**Dragon-engine chamber:** Concealed beyond the bridged chasm and locked door beneath the Hillmaster's tent. E002 ~00:50–01:20 ties an **obsidian engine** to Dusthill's water pumps, shows a crowned/guarding skeleton described as the first Hillmaster, and leads to the deeper intake. E005 ~00:03–00:40 adds the spectral **Peter** and attempted engine restart. **Source:** processed E002/E005 Parakeet SRTs; see [E002](../episodes/E002.md), [E005](../episodes/E005.md). Do not assume the first Hillmaster's identity was spoken in E002.

**The shaft and underground river:** The multi-mile, narrow descending stairwell passes traps and spiders (E002 ~01:22–02:36), ending near a lower pump, river and damaged inlet (E003 ~00:20–01:38). Softhead is seen falling into the river after an attack; neither his death nor his destination is thereby established. **Sources:** [E002](../episodes/E002.md), [E003](../episodes/E003.md).

**Moag Ferhain (later-wiki form; provisional):** An ancient dwarven complex accessible via the river-side opening, explored in E003 and E004. Processed SRTs variously produce `Mog for Hain`, `Mog Farain`, `Mog Forain`; the [fan wiki's Episode 6 summary](https://twbd.fandom.com/wiki/Episode_6) writes `Moag Ferhain`. The ruins include animated skeleton inhabitants, medical rooms with an automaton and a throne room with a queen linked to Quizquaz. **Sources:** E003 ~01:40–03:09 and E004 ~00:05–02:30. **Do not treat later wiki spelling as audio verification.**

## Travel route and new sites in E006–E008

**Dusthill meeting circle and defenses (E006 ~01:19–01:53):** The public meeting occurs in front of the Hillmaster's tent. The town also has **Scoutmasters** who discreetly vet newcomers, ordinary Hill Guard, volunteer wall defenders and a militia whose leadership is disputed and clarified. The gathering mentions **45 raid dead** and **25 taken**, reported counts rather than independently counted names. [E006](../episodes/E006.md).

**The Fate 4 / Fate Four (mobile location and vehicle):** Hope's giant-wheeled modified RV, unveiled in E006 ~02:15–02:30, with room for travel, equipment and living space. Source drawings and its original rules sheet were said to be shown to listeners/players and are potentially recoverable creator resources. The party drives southeast into the wastes following the goblin raiders; there is no guarantee that direct-line travel to **Slavetown** would intersect their actual trail. Its overheating and repair delays matter in E007 ~01:16–01:50. **Do not infer fixed specs from gameplay discussion without the contemporary document.**

**Desert pursuit and beetle habitat (E006 ~02:30–03:10; E007 ~00:06–00:46):** Roadless sand southeast of Dusthill; a disoriented man is encountered and the RV camps near three blinking lights. E007 identifies the lights as **luminous, large beetles**. Afterward the trail bends more northerly relative to the assumed Slavetown route; whether the map's named place is `Slavetown` or another location is a spelling/source-map issue.

**Goblin intercept location (E007 ~00:47–01:53):** A bus equipped with a catapult and two pickup trucks intercept the Fate 4. Crash/combat disables the RV. **This is the site of a delaying-party fight, not a verified arrival at the entire raider camp, a rescue of the abductees, or capture of the goblin ruler.**

**Desert antenna and sand aperture (E007 ~01:54–02:16):** Lone structure Nash approaches away from the disabled RV, described as an antenna, or `metal tree` by later arrivals. Reactive ground swallows Nash and then Stanley. A message/voice is perceived via the vehicle/radio. The exact operating mechanism is not determined.

**Underground containment complex (E008 ~00:10–03:00):** Distinct from the Dusthill tunnels and dwarven ruins; access is via the desert metal structure/sand. Includes wired wall panels, an elevator marked **B7**, illuminated hallways, crew quarters with human remains, laboratories with a chamber containing **two floating metal pieces**, a projection/simulation space, and controlled power/doors. **Leopold** claims to manage this secret containment/security installation, refuses egress and threatens intruders. Neither his nature nor the complex's deeper chronology, geography or relationship to earlier ruins is proven. [E008](../episodes/E008.md).

**Elhandar (claimed celestial place):** E006 ~02:00–02:27 has Zorenthelas describe this as elves' origin/home **in the sky**. The word's relation to elven afterlife/religion and whether it is physically reachable remains the NPC's account. `El Hondar`, `Elhandar` and possibly `El Handar` are aliases needing audio checks; spelling `Elhandar` appears in the [E006 wiki recap](https://twbd.fandom.com/wiki/Episode_6).

No precise coordinates, distances or map hex locations have been independently verified. Refer to the original show map before committing an exact route.


## E009–E012 new localities and travel context

**Antenna-linked subterranean facility (E009):** Expanded from E008's sealed corridors into the chambers of an immense **living tree**, cold equipment rooms, heavy doors, security-robot routes, power controls and a facility-destruct mechanism. The tree's claimed ancient elven identity is reported history. The complex collapses as Nash/Stanley reach the surface and a black dragon emerges (~E009 03:10–03:12). It has **not** been shown to be connected physically to Dusthill's dragon-engine shaft or Moag Ferhain. [E009](../episodes/E009.md).

**Desert road to Slavetown (E010):** After reunion at **Fate 4**, the party is pursued by an orc-led armed caravan, surrenders and is transported prisoner for several days. Distinguish E007's goblin road ambush and E010's caravan/military encounter. E009's final vehicles are subsequently clarified as a much larger organized force. [E010](../episodes/E010.md).

**Slavetown (aliases `Slave Town`, `Slavertown`):** A commercially active wasteland settlement with an extensive parking area, shops, saloons, hotel, market and official detention/arena facilities. Its connections to the slave trade and the **Principle** are demonstrated in E010–E012. Do not treat E005's map's uncertain orthography as a direct spoken correction. A local figure in E012 says the town was previously named **Pristine**; this is the **Jeweler's historical claim**, pending corroboration.

**Prison/holding barracks:** In E010 ~02:06–02:25, the party is led into confinement among other prisoners, including recognizably kidnapped people from **Dusthill**. Seeing the captives is not freeing them.

**Rusty Cage:** A Slavetown arena serving as a **trial-by-combat court** under the Principle's local magistrate and **Koromukishi** initiates. The party wins in E010; the court ceremony/release follows in E011. The exact spelling of the warrior order and officials' titles require audio and creator-asset review.

**Slavetown map and streets:** E011 ~00:15–00:44 a map-seller offers a **street map**. Candidate labels include **Silver Trail**, **Heaven Street**, **Arena Row**, and a derogatorily named poor quarter rendered `Shit Eaters Row` in the SRT. Confirm written labels from a contemporary in-show **town map**, distinct from the E005 Wastes map.

**Buxom Confederate:** Tavern/gambling and entertainment house where the party meets local entrepreneur **Celibus/Selylus Stump** in E011 ~01:30–02:15, and where an enslaved **Talia** is recognized. A named **Madame Wild Tongue** appears linked to the coercive service/entertainment rooms in E012's recap; confirm layout and ownership before assuming they are identical establishments.

**Lattlebing's Hotel:** A multi-story hotel managed by **S. J. Lattlebing** (spelling unresolved; SRT `Ladlebing`, `Lattle Bing`). In E012 ~00:07–00:46, Vorel receives an upper-floor suite and Stanley arranges a different room for **Talia**. Lodging does not make her legally free.

**The Sapphire:** Establishment associated with a man known as **the Jeweler**. Nash fights/loses possessions following an E011 late challenge (E012 ~00:46–01:10); the party subsequently discusses local alliances here (E012 ~02:47–03:12). Do not mistake the Jeweler for Stump.

**Owen's garage, the foundry and general store:** Separate work/trade sites explored E011–E012. Hope takes repair work at **Owen's garage**, Stanley has weapons repaired at the **foundry**, Nash buys replacement clothes at the general store; each contributes to the town's economic and character context.

**Mines / deed property:** E012 ~02:28–03:10 refers to a disputed mine deed variably called **copper** or **gold**, against a background of mines controlled by the Oilbeards. Do not settle location, title, mineral or ownership from these inconsistent ASR phrases.

See [E011](../episodes/E011.md) and [E012](../episodes/E012.md) for timing and episode-boundary cautions; the show-referenced town map is an important missing primary spelling source.
