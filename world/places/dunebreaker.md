# The Dunebreaker — roaming landship of the Wastes (historical prequel)

> **First directly documented:** [E107 — The Handshaking Algorithm](../../episodes/E107.md) (corrected Wastes S1, **same setting, long before the Dusthill party departs**), as explicitly declared by guest GM Jon Jagger at ~00:01–00:03. [Full-export ASR comparison](../../reports/E107_ASR_COMPARISON_2026-10-23.md). **ASR-provisional; no audio listened to.** This article also covers [E108 — Save The Two](../../episodes/E108.md) and [E109 — Sand Jell-O](../../episodes/E109.md), the second and third episodes of the same historical prequel, while distinguishing what was known at each episode cutoff. This is **not a ship Nash's present-day Arc Five group has been shown to use**.

## What is actually seen

The **Dunebreaker** (also phonetically **Dune Breaker**, ASR occasionally “Doombreaker”) is a huge **mobile mechanical caravan/landship** moving along desert routes between wasteland settlements. GM narration describes **about 400 feet** of connected machinery, **four floors** and five main structural units: a **head**, **three full-sized articulated segments** and a trailing **tail**. A van is **shown driving up the lowered tail ramp** and onto the upper deck while the vessel itself is stationary. The extreme heat underneath apparently **melts sand into glass behind the vessel** when it travels; engineer **Gens/Genz (?)** says glass routes are reused where practical, with detours for weather/danger. The observed vessel has towers over two segments, an antenna/dish at the head, covered internal passages, enormous engine heat, red-lit engineering floors and compartments sufficiently large for residences and trade.

| **Segment 1** | Passenger accommodation, amenities; recruits initially assigned here |

This is a **mechanical vessel observed aboard**, unlike the origin tale heard from a bartender. The engineer gives an enthusiastic technical tour, but **the energy source, supply creation and uninterrupted ability to outrun raiders are not established**.

## Organization and surface claims

**Ember Shalisper/Shalaspur (?)** presides over the caravan, with **orange-red skin, black hair that can ignite in an apparent controlled flare**, a patched military-style coat and a forceful voice. He promises incoming guards **food, water, accommodation and protection but no gold wages**. **Guard captain Lotho (?)** is a large lion-like figure and explicitly their immediate superior; **Argul/Gasper (?)** serve as Ember's separate bodyguards. **Chief engineer and glassblower Gens/Genz (?)** is a bespectacled Goliath who escorts the new guards, shakes Al's hand and describes the ship. **Horace (?)** reports the engineer's murder.

Ember restricts new guards to **segments 1–2** unless assigned elsewhere and prohibits passing on **maps or knowledge of the vessel**; he presents the ship as a family. One bartender **claims** he restored an ancient vehicle and wins safe passage through a silver tongue, but this is a **local's story**. A woman **Fleece/Fleese (?)**, on behalf of a **nameless employer**, considers the ship's supply abundance and lack of attacks suspicious; her written offer of **100 gold plus 50 gold per valuable fact** is actually signed by the woman after **Al prints a contract**. **No secret is sold or money paid in E107**.

The caravan's selection event is held at a temporary arena **between Slavetown and the Teet**; a **Chicken Lord** is defeated and **Hack Loman, Ramilda Gloryfaded (?), Al and NPC Strum** win employment. **Bart Rogers and Killdeath the Darkheart** are **stabilized but not hired**. The festivity's food/water are plentiful, a striking contrast with normal scarcity, but nothing confirms they are unlimited in literal quantity.

## E107 homicide — open at cutoff

**A dead worker in segment 2 engineering has four puncture wounds in his back**. Investigators **Hack, Ramilda, Al and Strum** have been expressly ordered by Lotho to solve the case. Al notices **structural damage in segment 1 with a suggested 4–6-week repair window** and **workers apparently pretending to work**; **neither is proof of murder or sabotage**. **Al picks up a handwritten notebook by the body** but makes only a **failed cursory search**, so its diagrams are uninterpreted. An unnamed engineer says he heard yelling and footsteps, then indicates a direction **deeper into segment 2**. The team heads that way and **the episode cuts off before revealing a suspect or outcome**.

## E108 — solved murder, hidden daughters and rat-worker sentence

[E108 — Save The Two](../../episodes/E108.md) resumes the murder investigation without moving this side story into Arc Five. **Hack, Ramilda, Al and NPC Strum** follow **blood mixed with condensation** through lower engineering. A gaunt man **Lamulkin Farthing (?)** calls surrender, but Ramilda **casts damaging *Guiding Bolt***. The man initially says **he stabbed the engineer** during a confrontation after he and his daughters stowed away aboard the ship. **Two daughters**, roughly **16–17 and 10**, step out; the older confesses that **she stabbed the engineer**, and Lamulkin confirms he falsely claimed responsibility to protect her. The scene of the actual killing is **recounted after the fact**, not physically reenacted.

The guards debate **cutting off a limb as false death evidence** or jumping off under ***Feather Fall***; **neither happens**. Lamulkin elects to face punishment alone. Hack knowingly reports him as sole murderer (**Deception 17**) and **Al actually presents the surrendered dagger to Lotho**. **Ember spares his life** after an appeal (notably **Ramilda Persuasion 23**) but sends him to the **tumbler vents**, tight machinery tunnels under the ship cleaned by vulnerable workers nicknamed **“rats.”** The crew treats this as onerous, risky duty, not freedom or safe release. **The daughters remain undisclosed and secretly aboard**. Their later fate is open.

**Strum is transferred from guard party to engineering and remains alive**. After the hearing, **Strum, Hack and Ramilda return with food and drinks** for the hidden girls, who **actually eat**; **Al declines to come**, and the promised next-day food routine has **not yet occurred**. The three PCs obtain **separate rooms 3A–3C**, while the gnome known by the nickname **“Dipshit”** dispenses food and water; special treats apparently require approval or points. **Hack privately reveals an old circlet-like poorly healed facial scar**, with origin unknown. **No Fleece contract terms are fulfilled, no map sold and no ship secrets disclosed**. Earlier evidence of structural damage in segment 1 is **not yet repaired**, and the ship's vast supplies remain unexplained. ## E109 — the secret exchange and defeat of the giant dessert

At [E109](../../episodes/E109.md), guard **Fawkes/Fox (?)** issues insignia sashes bearing **three welded platinum coins** to **Hack, Ramilda and Al**. **Captain Lotho reopens suspicion over E108's falsified murder account**: four knife wounds in the engineer's **back** contradict the reported self-defense. Hack's **Deception 12** does not satisfy him; he **orders a later reinvestigation**, not performed before this mini-arc ends. **Lamulkin and his two daughters remain unresolved**, even though the story reaches its three-part closing scene.

The gnome cook **Dipshit**, overwhelmed by servicing hundreds alone, confesses that he **bought and prepared fruit gelatin against Ember's rules**. The jelly turns into a mobile **red, fruit-speckled ooze** that fights the party in the larder, escapes down a drain, then grows through the **Dunebreaker's sewage system** by absorbing sewage, debris and bodies. **Al blasts a waste collection vat with Thunderwave**, damaging plumbing and causing **sewage to flood back into the kitchen**. His action **does not destroy the ooze**. A faint fiend-like ranger detection is **not proof of a summoned fiend**; Jon later says this was the same dessert transformed by absorption.

A **mandatory lockdown** places the guards at **deck-mounted miniguns** as **Ember, Lotho, Fawkes, bodyguards and a tall slender negotiator** conduct a confidential **desert trade with armed marauders**. **The PCs see goods and documents exchanged, but not the actual terms**, so the ship's supply and safe-passage mysteries remain **unverified**, even after the exchange. The enormous gelatin rises on both sides of the ship, attacking the parties. **Hack and Ramilda actually fire deck guns**; **Al searches the head bridge and presses a “vent” switch** that releases superheated steam and devastates the monster. **Ramilda crushes its last cherry-bearing globule with her foot**: the gelatinous threat is **killed rather than temporarily dispersed**. The **Dunebreaker survives**, while **at least two guards and multiple marauders are killed**; Ember, Lotho and Fawkes survive, and a trailer of additional cargo is brought aboard. Repairs to Al's damaged plumbing and the precise cargo remain unknown.

**Jon ends the characters' second day as guards, awards level 4 and expressly closes this three-part prequel.** No main-party crossover is played; no outcome is shown for **the daughters, Strum's future, Fleece's information contract, Hack's scars or the ship's supply mechanism**.
