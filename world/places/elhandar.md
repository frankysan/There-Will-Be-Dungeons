# Elhandar — the forest among the stars

## Introduction

Elhandar was an inhabited elven sanctuary high above Earth, an immense technological station joined to a glass-enclosed sphere of living forest. Its woods were not illusions: trees, wildlife, lakes and brooks thrived within a great constructed habitat. Paths, hunting grounds and dwellings lay beneath a regulated succession of day and night. Beyond the dome stretched a long cylindrical complex, a reminder that this apparent wilderness depended upon a sophisticated union of engineering and magic.[^elhandar-first]

A luminous network called **Rainbow Road** linked the station's districts. Its inhabitants had access to science, magic, permanent teleportation facilities and care for the wounded. Admiral Chrysorius welcomed visiting travelers, while an elder known as the High Elf served as a spiritual leader of a society that professed cooperation rather than kingship. The High Elf spoke of his people's attempts to restore damaged ecosystems and of beliefs about elven souls; these remained the community's own accounts of its past.[^elhandar-society]

Within the forest stood a furnished lakeside cottage, offering a kitchen, bedrooms, a wharf and an open hearth to Nash, Angelashi, their children and their companions. To those arriving from the devastation of T'ietkalla, Elhandar appeared as both a home and a promise: a place where ordinary life might continue amid the stars.[^elhandar-cottage]

## Development by arc

### Arc Five — refuge, hidden dangers and destruction

#### Sanctuary and its limits

Elhandar's protection extended beyond the first rescued household. Its hosts provided rest, offered care and made room for visitors to explore the forest. News of other survivors reached the cottage, and the admiral promised to inquire after Stanley's missing father. The High Elf even offered to share memories, though Stanley refused. The station's hospitality was substantial, but neither its knowledge nor its assurances could resolve every wound carried aboard.[^elhandar-first-days]

Its defenses were formidable enough to destroy a threatening pirate vessel. They did not, however, isolate the forest from the powers contesting Earth. The Principal appeared before the visitors, displaying supernatural might and proclaiming his own divinity. His claims did not make him the station's ruler, and his appearance revealed how vulnerable even an apparently sheltered society was to intrusions from the wider world.[^elhandar-principle]

#### A refuge becomes a community

The forest was spacious enough for displaced tieflings to make a settlement of their own. In a clearing of cabins gathered around a well, Nobieri, Emenil and their neighbors tried to establish something more enduring than temporary shelter. Hope returned to Elhandar alongside Nash and later addressed these survivors, naming their new home **[the Harbor](the-harbor.md)**. Not all accepted the vision: one family departed. Elhandar could offer safety and ground on which to rebuild, but it could not erase T'ietkalla's rivalries, grief or conflicting loyalties.[^elhandar-hope][^elhandar-harbor]

The station also held more secrets than its welcoming surface suggested. [Omnom Cinderborn](../characters/omnom-cinderborn.md) remained bitter and grievously injured, and Stanley's inherited key revealed an impossible upper study within the lakeside cottage. Its charts, **Book of Adjacencies** and **Mask of Eltanfawr** connected the refuge to questions of planetary travel and the Weir. The father's explanations remained conjecture, but the discovery changed the cottage's significance: this modest family dwelling was not wholly what it seemed.[^elhandar-study]

#### An enchanted forest with hidden dangers

Elhandar's biodome held more than tame woodland. Lakes and northern hunting grounds supported unusual creatures, including speaking animals and a huge beast called **Chester**, believed to have escaped from earlier experimentation. The animal was killed during a hunt, but a more disturbing danger followed: hostile elves masquerading as friendly deer attacked the visitors and carried Hope into a mountain cave. The apparent tranquility of the forest could conceal malice, and not every threat came from outside the station.[^elhandar-hunt][^elhandar-chester]

The caves exposed still deeper mysteries. The archfey Diontalus appeared through an accomplice, Omnom died in the fighting, and an imprisoned black dragon intervened. The wounded creature claimed to recognize a maternal power within Nash's artificial heart. A golden draconic apparition appeared, yet the dragon's broader account of Nash's making remained uncertain. Before dying, it suggested a possible defense for Earth built around its **own natural metal heart**. That heart was extracted and concealed in the mountain, a hidden burden now bound to Elhandar's fate.[^elhandar-cavern][^elhandar-heart]

The **Gatherers' Lodge**, tended by spectral elven custodians, provided rest after the ordeal. Rumors that the hostile mages belonged to an elven council remained unproven, and nothing established corruption throughout the station. Near the Harbor, communion with Malhya brought relief from wounds inflicted by Diontalus. Elhandar remained a place of healing even as suspicion and danger complicated the sanctuary it promised.[^elhandar-return]

#### Leadership and the breaking of the refuge

The High Elf's audience chamber stood at the center of Elhandar's authority, where spiritual leadership met questions of magic, diplomacy and defense. There Stanley negotiated a tentative settlement with Diontalus; the archfey's future assistance was promised, not secured. The High Elf also offered a route toward Mars and a lead to the missing engineer [Tungsten Wheel](../characters/tungsten-wheel.md), who might build the proposed protection for Earth. Elhandar possessed extraordinary knowledge and connections, but could not itself deliver the completed device.[^elhandar-court][^elhandar-engineer]

That distinction between power and safety became catastrophic when an attacking castle and airship fleet descended upon the station. Mercenaries under [Henatar Grape](../characters/henitar-grape.md) breached the central chambers. The High Elf's defensive trees, shields and returning volleys showed how heavily Elhandar could resist; they could not hold it aloft. As the station fell toward Earth, its central transport routes became paths of escape through failing machinery and falling debris. The hidden rooms of [Villa Or'Nath](villa-ornath.md) offered a last refuge to the household fleeing the forest.[^elhandar-siege][^elhandar-flight]

#### From sanctuary to ruins

Elhandar struck the desert and ceased to function as an orbital home. Villa Or'Nath sheltered its occupants through the impact, and wounded Harborers found refuge among the wreckage. A second crash, caused by the attackers' falling castle, killed several tieflings who had survived the first catastrophe. The struggle with Henatar continued outside the cottage; its final violence belonged to the wreck of a place whose defenses and hospitality had failed to prevent great loss.[^elhandar-impact][^elhandar-aftershock]

The High Elf and Admiral Chrysorius remained alive to direct rescue work. Surviving Elhandari vessels gathered elves and tiefling refugees, while a recovery party retrieved the dragon's natural heart from the ruined mountain. Angelashi, Nash's children and other members of their household boarded ships bound for a terraforming platform on Mars. The fallen station could no longer shelter them in the sky, but its surviving people and their capacity to carry refugees onward preserved something of its original purpose.[^elhandar-exodus]

A final upheaval struck the same landscape when a violet rupture opened overhead and an enormous creature burrowed into Earth's desert. The resulting sandstorm swept across the wreckage. Villa Or'Nath remained standing after the wave passed, while the creature vanished beneath the surface. Elhandar's ruins had become part of a world facing dangers much larger than the station itself.[^elhandar-storm]

## Current status

Elhandar is no longer a functioning orbital sanctuary. Its shattered halls, machinery and forest habitat lie across Earth's desert, and the full number of those killed remains unknown. The High Elf and Admiral Chrysorius survived long enough to oversee rescue efforts, but the future of the wreck and the people left behind is uncertain.[^elhandar-final]

Ships carrying rescued tieflings, elves and Nash's family have departed toward Mars with the recovered natural dragon heart; their safe arrival is not yet known. [Villa Or'Nath](villa-ornath.md) remains on Earth with Stanley, Nash, Hope and Vorel. The proposed planetary protection has not been built, and the immense burrowing creature remains beneath the desert. What survives most clearly of Elhandar is not its machinery or forest, but the community of refugees it sheltered and helped carry toward another possible home.[^elhandar-status]
