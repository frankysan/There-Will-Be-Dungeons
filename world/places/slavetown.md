# Slavetown

**Slavetown** was a fortified commercial center and slaveholding city in the Wastes, where crowded streets, hotels, foundries, merchants and magistrates operated alongside guards and coercive labor. Under the authority claimed in the name of **the Principal**, its institutions could offer formal legal procedures while denying freedom to people brought there by force. The city's trading connections reached toward [Dusthill](dusthill.md), the [Black Mine](black-mine.md) and the wider desert routes.[^arrival]

## Entry under coercion

After the collapse of the underground facility, a mounted, orc-led force captured the travelers aboard Fate 4 and escorted them across the desert. In Slavetown they found captives taken from Dusthill, confirming that some of the people they sought had survived but **not** that they had been freed. An elven magistrate accused the party of violating the Principal's laws and required a public trial by combat against initiates of the **Koromukishi**, an order distinct from orcs as a people. The companions won. At the subsequent ceremony, the authorities recognized **their** freedom and offered them a place in the martial order; enslaved residents remained in captivity.[^arrival]

The adventurers encountered the city's elaborate social divisions firsthand: affluent hospitality and gambling establishments, a foundry, jewelry trade, districts of conspicuous poverty and holding areas for enslaved people. Stanley recognized **Talia**, Bruce Bane's mother, among the captives. The party's search for Dusthill survivors became entangled with wealthy patron **Celibus Stump**, the **Jeweler**, the [Lactid Cartel](../FACTIONS_AND_POWERS.md), the cyclops **Orp** and conflicting accounts of a mine deed. A magistrate's promises of future prisoner releases and descriptions of disputed property were **negotiating claims**, not accomplished rescues or independently verified titles.[^city]

## Violence, mystery and fire

During the investigation of **Tylander Sullivan**, the travelers encountered murders, apparent returns from death, a stolen necklace and competing criminal and political interests. An attack on a local site and an animated severed arm were witnessed; stories that Tylander himself had returned alive or that a pact explained him remained matters of testimony. The city's authorities subsequently evacuated some residents and Dusthill captives to the **Salivating Fields**, where they remained under the Principal's forces rather than acquiring demonstrated freedom.[^conflict]

The party's departure ended in destructive violence. While under the influence of **Pixie Blush** and experiencing unreliable perceptions, Nash set off a large cache of explosives. The **blast and the fire spreading through Slavetown were real**, whatever the status of the voice or figure he believed had guided him. The foundry owner had also been killed in a separate confrontation, and Stanley was seriously injured. Hope secured a station wagon to carry the group away toward the Black Mine; this was not a fully repaired Fate 4.[^fire]

A fierce sandstorm overtook the fleeing travelers. They speculated that it might extinguish the blaze behind them, but never saw a verified account of the city's overall survival, its full casualties or the fate of everyone left inside. Later, a contact told Vorel that **Jolt's associates** had robbed a Slavetown Oilbeard safe during the distraction and gave him a share of the proceeds. The payment was witnessed; the earlier robbery was reported, not a second party visit to Slavetown.[^aftermath]

## Later claims and restitution

The Principal later visited the party in [T'ietkalla](tietkalla.md), asserted that a magistrate—whose name remains provisionally rendered **Ethanelia**—had ordered the Dusthill raid without his instructions, and proposed trade, a railway and paid voluntary construction work. He acknowledged or defended the broader coercive system in the conversation, so his denial of personal responsibility was not independent exoneration. A diplomatic arrangement was discussed and conditionally accepted, but proposed labor reform and future rail construction were not evidence that slavery in Slavetown had ceased.[^diplomacy]

The promised return of Dusthill captives was **partly realized**. Talia, Bruce Bane, Jimster, Shun'ti and other named survivors physically arrived in T'ietkalla and reunited with friends and family. Their accounts included compelled work in the Salivating Fields. The return was real, but the release of **every** person originally taken from Dusthill is not established.[^return]

## Current status

Slavetown's fate after the explosion, spreading fire, evacuation and sandstorm remains uncertain. Reports of withdrawal, criminal activity and changed control do not establish a fully abandoned city, an end to its coercive institutions or a verified transfer of government. Its surviving historical significance lies in the violence of its slave economy, the party's contested dealings with the Principal's officials, and the confirmed—but incomplete—return of Dusthill survivors.[^aftermath][^return]
