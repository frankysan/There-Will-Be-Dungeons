# Dusthill

Sheltered by rust-red stone and hidden approaches, **Dusthill** is the small wasteland settlement where **Hope, Nash, Stanley and Vorel** first join forces. Its people grow food in cave farms, repair salvaged machinery and depend on a central water outlet that looks like a well but draws on a much older **underground system of pumps and a dragon engine**. When that supply fails, the new companions descend beneath the village. Their success restores the water, but a goblin raid transforms the adventure into a search for abducted townspeople.

## Setting

Dusthill occupies a **naturally protected rocky alcove**. Large boulders narrow the approach, with guarded positions above; modest mud dwellings and communal wooden-and-metal barns stand inside. **Dark farms in rear caves** raise crops and animals with ancient irrigation. The settlement also contains **Jimster's garage** and a central **valve-operated public water outlet**, not simply a rope-and-bucket well. A gathering area beside the Hillmaster's tent is witnessed later in the opening arc. The exact layout, dates and measurements of surviving hand-drawn maps are **not established**. The separately preserved owner-selected illustration depicts the **E001 opening state as an artistic reconstruction, not a survey**; it is not currently embedded in this article.[^illustration]

In [E001](../../episodes/E001.md), the **Hillmaster** hosts a meal bringing together the four protagonists and asks them to investigate the failing water supply. The passage into the hill is guarded by traps and members of the **old Hill Guard**, who are distinct from the newly appointed companions. [E002](../../episodes/E002.md) leads them to old machinery and a **dragon engine** powering pumps, with a still-deeper **river intake** to examine. The serving Hillmaster **falls to his death** during the expedition; his statements about the engine and the skeleton of a first Hillmaster are character testimony.

In [E003](../../episodes/E003.md), **Hope repairs the lower intake**, while **Softhead is knocked into the underground river**—his death is **not witnessed**. Beyond the intake the party enters ancient dwarven rooms animated by eerie skeleton residents. A ruler wearing a head apparatus is connected to a dark being later named **Quizquaz**. In [E004](../../episodes/E004.md), Stanley severs their connection, the party defeats Quizquaz, and the queen and skeletal inhabitants collapse. The spectral **Peter**, claiming to be the first Hillmaster, then describes the engines and disputes the authority of later leaders. His alleged **blood-fueled workings** and historical interpretations are not automatically independent facts. [E005](../../episodes/E005.md).

The ancient ruin is sometimes associated in retrospective discussion with **Moag Ferhain**, an owner-confirmed geographic spelling from a later map. **The mapped surface location is not independently proved identical to the exact underground halls** the party enters in E003–E005.

## After the expedition

The party works to restart the machinery, but by **E005** the public outlet **has not yet been directly tested**. On returning to Dusthill they encounter a **goblin attack**. Raiders are fought off, civilians have died and others have been taken. At the next town meeting in [E006](../../episodes/E006.md), residents report **45 dead and 25 abducted**—reported emergency figures, not a verified final census. **Nash physically opens the town valve and confirms running water** in E006, resolving the earlier uncertainty about whether repairs reached the surface.

After the Hillmaster's death, **Shun'ti Stoutbarrel** steps forward as **interim townmaster**; **Tetsuo** remains involved in defense and **Jimster** supplies technical support. Vorel takes custody of the captive goblin **Bok Bok**, the beginning of a relationship that should not be backdated to the original feast. **Hope's modified Fate 4** is presented, and the four leave Dusthill to pursue the abductees. The journey through [Slavetown](slavetown.md) and [Black Mine](black-mine.md) does **not** immediately free every Dusthill captive.

## Important people and connected sites

**Shun'ti**, **Jimster**, **Tetsuo**, **Scoutmaster Zorenthelas**, **Talia** and **Bruce Bane** are part of the town's history, with additional family and witness identities still source-provisional. The deceased Hillmaster is separate from **Peter's claimed earlier office**. Key locations include **the garage and Fate 4**, **the central water valve**, **the pump and dragon-engine rooms**, **the deep shaft and river intake**, and **the ancient dwarven chambers**. These are connected by the played expedition, not proof that every historical map labels the same structure.

## Survivors finally return — E059

In [E058](../../episodes/E058.md), **the Principal promises** to release Dusthill captives, but the transfer has **not yet occurred**. In **[E059](../../episodes/E059.md)**, a group **actually arrives alive in T'ietkalla**, including **Talia, Bruce Bane, Jimster, Shun'ti** and other familiar Dusthill residents. Survivors speak of **forced labor in the Salivating Fields**; their arrival verifies a **partial return**, not the fate of all 25 reported missing people. They reach **T'ietkalla**, rather than being shown physically resettled in Dusthill. No later full survey proves the village's permanent condition, population, government or continued water security.
