# Villa Or'Nath — Delit Or'Nath's family cottage and concealed spelljammer

Villa Or'Nath began as a family home in Elhandar's forest biodome. For Stanley Billings, born **Valiir Or'Nath**, it offered a rare connection to his father **Delit Or'Nath**. The modest-looking cottage concealed an impossible upper floor and, as the companions discovered during Elhandar's destruction, a vessel that would eventually become **Fate Six**.[^identity]

## Earlier uses — ordinary home and impossible upper floor

The companions lived in the villa while visiting Elhandar. Its hidden nature surfaced when Stanley recalled his parents' poem and followed a levitating key to an invisible lock. An extra staircase opened into a third-floor study larger than the house's visible structure allowed. Stanley and Hope found star charts, an ornate mask and the **Book of Adjacencies**, a responsive collection of his father's writings. The room was physically accessible; Delit's theories about time, the Weir and Stanley's future were still his testimony.[^study]

## E125 — impact shelter and episode-bound uncertainty

When a mercenary fleet attacked Elhandar and the station fell toward Earth, Stanley led his companions and household to the cottage. Angelashi, the children, Lyrielle, Bok Bok and Chunks joined the four travelers in the hidden study. Seats and restraints appeared, and the occupants secured themselves as the crash began. E125 ended at impact, before their survival could be established.[^impact]

## E126 — occupants survive, the house is a spelljammer

The hidden room protected them from the initial crash. Below, injured Harbor refugees had appeared inside the house; a survivor said elves had gathered them beforehand, without explaining how the transfer occurred. Rubble trapped the building within Elhandar's wreckage.

Stanley consulted the journal and learned that the villa was said to be the shell of an interstellar ship. The **Mask of Eltanfawr** offered control of flight and transformation, but required four nights of attunement. Wearing it, Stanley could make the house creak, not fly. Further impacts eventually left the villa upright in Earth's desert. Several tieflings died, although the household survived. The house resisted tremendous force, but absolute invulnerability was never proved.[^survival]

## E128 — the season-finale refuge on Earth's surface

After the mercenary's defeat, surviving Elhandari took Angelashi's family and other Harbor refugees toward Mars. Four noble speeders were attached to the villa for the remaining companions. Stanley, Nash, Hope and Vorel stayed beside the cottage on Earth. It endured a later sandstorm, and they rested there before continuing the mask's attunement.[^finale]

## Historical E126 cutoff and open questions

At E126's end the villa had **not** flown, shrunk or changed into ship form. Its occupants had not traveled to Mars. The father's explanation was more complete than Stanley's demonstrated control; the later voyage does not retroactively make an unperformed E126 action occur.[^survival]

## Season Two — a ship called Fate Six, actual Earth-to-Mars travel

On the fourth day, **the transformation happened**: the villa rose as the captain's quarters of an elven airship, worked by a ghostly crew. First mate Sever introduced himself as Stanley's uncle. Beneath the engine, a carved image of Delit spoke as Stanley's father and said his remaining essence powered the ship, a history told by the figure rather than independently replayed.

The companions flew over damaged T'ietkalla, landed at Razak Val to take lizardfolk survivors aboard, and crossed space to a terraformed region of Mars. There Stanley formally christened the ship **Fate Six**. Flight and landfall were witnessed; the proposed shrinking and the mask's dangerous plurality power were not.[^flight]

## Current status

At the S2E02 cutoff, **Villa Or'Nath is no longer a stationary Earth cottage**. Its living structure forms part of Fate Six, which has landed on Mars. The ship's subsequent adventures are tracked elsewhere, distinct from the original home's uncertain magical origins.[^flight]
