# Château Weir

Château Weir is the otherworldly seat and guest-house of **Catoblepraithe, the Soulbroker**. The travelers first reach it through a rift beneath the [Black Mine](black-mine.md) in E023. What appears to be lavish hospitality becomes a bargain over souls and the fate of the Principal, and the château later becomes the setting of the party's final confrontation with its host. The realm's appearance and the events witnessed there are distinct from the Soulbroker's claims about cosmic law, death and his own power.[^arrival]

## The first visit

After a beholder is defeated beneath the Black Mine, **Quizquaz** uses a gem to open a tear in reality. The Soulbroker, a large goat-like figure, appears and invites Nash, Stanley, Hope and Vorel to visit his domain. They cross a bridge through a strange, star-filled darkness toward a great complex. A whirlpool of struggling figures can be seen below; the host calls them souls, but their exact condition is not independently verified. The guests travel by carriage to comfortable quarters, where **Baba** and other staff offer meals, bathing and rest. They complete a night there. Hospitality forms and assurances of safety should not be mistaken for the later soul bargain.[^arrival]

Hope experiences an intimate **dream or vision of her mother**, who gives an account of Hope's missing father. The vision is experienced by Hope, but its story is not corroborated simply because it happens within the Weir. The next day, the company meets the Soulbroker for a more consequential offer.[^arrival]

The Soulbroker demands that the adventurers destroy **the Principal** within **1,000 days and 1,000 nights**, threatening the signatories' souls if they fail. He promises power and information in return. **Hope, Stanley and Nash sign; Vorel expressly refuses.** The bargain does not bind all four equally, and its exact legal mechanics remain subject to the original contract or handout, which this account has not independently verified.[^pact]

## Powers and limitations

In E024, Nash receives private visions and revelations involving the **Solar Mines** and his **dragon-metal heart**, then visibly takes the form of a black dragon. The Soulbroker offers leads about the revolvers **Fox and Scully**, a place transcribed as **Elhendar/Elhandar**, an alleged lost mine deed and possible action against the Principal. These are information supplied by an interested supernatural actor, not independently proven histories or locations.[^pact]

Nash also receives access to a **key for Château Weir**. Its reported use requires activation from sufficiently far underground; an E024 account suggests at least 100 feet, but the complete handout and general mechanical limits have not been checked. A trip in the realm lasting nearly a day is later compared with roughly two weeks passing outside; another visitor's shorter trip appears to behave differently. This is no established universal time-conversion rate.[^pact][^retrospective]

The key becomes important in E048. An elderly tiefling, provisionally **Nobiery/Nobieri**, claims that a concealed army of loyal tieflings has been held within the Weir, at a terrible cost involving twenty sacrificed children. The party has not yet seen that army or verified the elder's historical bargain. **Nash activates the key; the elder actually enters the Weir and is greeted by Baba.** In E054 he **actually returns with a substantial armed tiefling contingent**, who recognize and crown Hope. Their visible arrival confirms the return and the soldiers, not every detail of the elder's claimed past or exact original headcount.[^elder]

## December 2019 GM retrospective — undelivered petitioners and contested protection

A December 2019 GM Q&A, discussing events through E083, explains that the souls of Hope and Stanley had been promised to the Weir under their contract. Their passage toward that destination was **interrupted**, and their petitioner-like manifestations were found in Sigil. This is a creator's later explanation of E081–E083, not a newly played delivery of their souls or a resurrection. The Q&A also reports disputes in the Weir court over protecting Hope and a contract involving **Dolo Popio** and **Andreeste**. It does not demonstrate a court verdict, the fall of T'ietkalla or an ultimate disposition of the political conflict.[^retrospective]

The same conversation places the special key's loss in the E081 beam and leaves unanswered whether it would have worked from Sigil. It also compares the party's earlier near-day stay with a longer period outside the realm, while noting that Nobieri's journey differed. A separate Year One Q&A describes unchosen chrome-related possibilities and artifacts; it does **not** retrospectively grant Vorel powers that he declined.[^retrospective]

## Played S3E11 return — the five confront Soulbroker; Château torn into storm

Much later, Hope uses the charged [Hollow Ring](../technology/hollow-ring.md) to open a modified *Gate* to **Château Weir's courtyard**, not directly into the throne room. **Hope, Nash, Stanley, Vorel and the temporarily allied Principal all cross.** They observe the high castle walls, grass, lilies, cold wind and dark cycling clouds, then enter a seemingly vacant throne hall lit by jade-green torches.[^return]

**Quizquaz appears again** from oily shadows and demands that the three contract signers answer for arriving with the Principal rather than killing him. He explicitly acknowledges **Vorel's exemption** because Vorel never signed. Stanley briefly sits on the Soulbroker's throne to provoke Quizquaz, but does not thereby become the ruler of the realm. The Soulbroker himself then appears in goat-robed form and takes the throne.[^return]

The Soulbroker says a **Worldbreaker seed has already been planted on Earth** and claims he could remove it but will not while the Principal blocks the Weir's ambitions. His asserted ability to extract the seed is not demonstrated. After an exchange of threats and arguments, the Soulbroker grows to impossible size; the château begins breaking apart into a vast tornado and lightning lashes the group. **S3E11 ends at the onset of this violence. The battle, the Soulbroker's defeat and the party's escape have not yet occurred at that episode cutoff.**[^return]

## Played S3E12 — Soulbroker is actually defeated; reality collapses around the party

S3E12 resumes in that storm and **plays the battle**. The Principal creates a protective chrome dome. Nash touches its lightning, producing **51 additional Nash copies**; subsequent lightning effects increase the number dramatically, reaching **3,280 copies** during the fight. Nash chooses an eighth-level *Fireball* rather than the briefly discussed *Sunburst*. The GM allows the thousands of copies to cast the spell together and adjudicates **58,950 fire damage**. The Soulbroker's physical manifestation is **defeated and disintegrates**. This is an observed outcome, not merely the threatened fight from S3E11.[^defeat]

As the Weir fractures into glass-like rifts, the number of Nash duplicates grows as high as **95,120** in the GM's narration. Many are drawn into unknown adjacencies; their separate fates and the identity of every surviving copy are not settled. The Principal opens an exit, aided by dragon-form Nash copies rescuing companions, but closes it before the others can all escape. **The portal leads to Hell, not directly back to Earth.** Later explanation confirms that the Weir–Earth connection is severed, **but the Worldbreaker seed remains inside Earth**. The immediate defeat of the Soulbroker does not prove every building, resident or court of the Weir was permanently destroyed.[^defeat]

## Interpretive boundaries

The Soulbroker, Quizquaz, the beholder and other figures who appear near the rift are **distinct actors**. The Soulbroker's statements about other gods, Hope's family, the afterlife, the Principal's origins and the fate of souls are claims or visions unless supported by independently observed events. The name **Catoblepraithe/Catobelpraithe** and other forms remain subject to original-audio verification. Subsequent GM commentary may clarify intended narrative mechanics without turning an unplayed hypothetical into an event.[^arrival][^retrospective]

The E023 soul contract and ordinary hospitality paperwork are also separate. Vorel's refusal matters when Quizquaz raises the pact again in S3E11. Likewise, the elder's E048 promise and E054 witnessed return must not be merged into an uninterrupted visit or an independently confirmed account of the alleged sacrifices.[^pact][^elder][^return]

## Current status

**At the S3E12 cutoff**, the Soulbroker's manifested body has been destroyed in a played battle, and the Weir realm is violently coming apart. The party escapes through the Principal's portal to **Hell**. The game's later account treats the **connection from the Weir to Earth as broken**, not the Worldbreaker threat as solved. The château's lasting physical condition, the fate of its wider inhabitants and the status of any surviving Weir court are **unverified**; there is no later witnessed throne-hall audience that settles them.[^defeat]
