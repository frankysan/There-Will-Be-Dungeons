# Fate Six — Villa Or'Nath's revealed spelljammer

**The Wastes.** Once the Or'Nath family's seemingly ordinary [Villa Or'Nath](../places/villa-ornath.md), Fate Six awakens as a wooden elven spelljammer after Stanley Billings (Valiir Or'Nath) completes his bond with the [Mask of Eltanfawr](mask-of-eltanfawr.md). The cottage rises into ship form in [S2E01](../../episodes/S2E01.md); in [S2E02](../../episodes/S2E02.md), its ghost sailors carry Stanley and his companions from Earth's desert to a living pocket of Mars, where he christens it **Fate Six**.

**Stanley is its first demonstrated captain.** The ship goes on to ferry friends and armies between distant destinations, withstand battle, and suffer two genuine destructions before returning to flight. By the campaign's end, Hope Akmenos has piloted the restored vessel to Planet Nash and back; **only afterward** does Stanley give her the Mask and intended authority over its surviving ghost crew. Her own Mask attunement and first Mask-powered command remain unperformed.

## Transformation and first voyage

While it was a cottage in Elhandar's forest and later crashed to Earth's desert, the Villa concealed a spelljammer. In **S2E01** it **actually rises into a wooden elven flying ship after four days of attunement**, the former cottage forming the **rear captain's quarters**. In **S2E02**, Stanley and Hope **learn controls**, fly from Earth across the **Dead Fingers** region to **Razak Val** (via a no-landing visual pass of damaged T'ietkalla), **land** at the lizardfolk canyon, take on a **large contingent of willing lizardfolk**, ascend into the void and complete the ~**one-story-hour** flight to a **protected Mars jungle biosphere**, then **land**. This is the ship's first demonstrated interplanetary voyage in current review; there is **no confirmed later departure at the S2E02 cutoff**.

The ghost crew's first mate and Stanley's apparent **uncle Sever** introduces approximately **40–50 ghost elves**, magically bound to ship operation and unable to depart or touch mortals. Important roles include helmsman **Crooked Hand Row**, cook **Chef Durgonzola Mamsie**, ghost surgeon **Dr. Splenival Daffodil**, and an enormous one-eyed non-elven cabin boy **Gorgeous George(s)**. Full surnames and whimsical roster spellings remain audio/handout-unverified. **Twaddle, an 87-year-old elf**, agrees to serve as physical assistant to the ghost surgeon in S2E02, writes his farewell letter and travels by speeder with the party toward Mars council; **he has not yet boarded or worked as assistant**.

### Helm, protection and routes

Deck arcane ballistas, below-decks stores, captain's private lounge, engine controls, wheel, dials and elven glyph-keypad are observed on the tour. The helm can present an ethereal chart with only **Earth, Mars and Planet Nash** preprogrammed as actual destination coordinates in S2E02. The DM's briefly displayed additional red marker is an **interface mistake, not a fictional destination**. Arbitrary new destinations require correct coordinates; obtaining glyphs alone is **not proof of successful input or safe travel**. The ship's visible magical/rainbow exhaust and **blue protective shield** allow the crew and mortal passengers to breathe and survive void exposure. Sever's giant-space-hamster stories are explicitly **unwitnessed sailors' legends**, not an encounter.

### The father aboard

Beneath the engine, a physically strange chamber houses **a wooden carving resembling Delit Or'Nath** that speaks as Stanley's father. The voice says Delit gave up his corporeal body after a mortal wound, leaving **his life essence to power the vessel**, and that Stanley's mother died. Sever states Delit is **the ship, not merely the carved face**, and that face provides a comprehensible means of contact. The presence, conversation, and GM-supplied resemblance are **played**, but exact ancient circumstances and the ship's possible access to privacy are not independently witnessed.

### Name and cutoff

“Fate Six” is **discussed ~00:34** in reference to Fate Four / Fate Five, then Stanley **formally adopts the name aloud at ~01:25–01:28**, and the ghosts cheer its christening. The motivational explanation Stanley gives the ghost crew is improvised; it should not be used as an accurate historical inventory of exactly six prior ships. **Fate Six is the current name** from then on, not merely a joke or future plan. At the S2E02 cutoff, it is **landed on Mars** in the terraforming biosphere, with ghost crew aboard and the recruited lizardfolk on Mars exploring mud. No specific commitment to ferry Nash's family to Planet Nash has been made. [Dossier](../../episodes/S2E02.md).

## Villa, shipboard rooms and hidden brig — observed boundaries

The family home concealed spaces long before its first voyage. In E118, Stanley and Hope **actually unlock and enter an impossible third-floor study** by using Delit Or'Nath's key and family poem; the visible two-story cottage cannot contain that staircase and room by ordinary geometry. The study holds charts, globe, Book of Adjacencies and Mask of Eltanfawr. In **E126**, the father's responsive journal **describes** a warded spelljammer shell, a prospective **dime-sized shrinking of house *and occupants***, and flight after Mask attunement. **Neither shrinking nor flight is performed then**; after the intervening nights, **S2E01 actually transforms the cottage into a flying ship**, with the old house forming the aft captain's quarters. These are separate cutoffs, not three simultaneous powers demonstrated in E118.

**Interior compartments are real, but their capacities are not unlimited.** During **S2E02**, Uncle Sever's tour physically reveals **deck ballistas, galley, munitions and armory**, captain's rooms, machinery and a dimensionally unusual **approximately 20-by-20-foot wooden chamber beneath the engine**. The carved Delit-like face **actually speaks in that chamber**; its reported connection to Delit's sacrificed life essence is testimony, not an independently witnessed engineering experiment. The ordinary armory's contents and dimensions are **not inventoried**, and the under-engine room is **not proof that all cargo or the entire vessel exists in unrestricted extradimensional storage**.

**The concealed prison is a later, distinct performed feature.** In **S3E02**, Sever and Gorgeous George **actually take the captured, living Doctor Spider and Steerious Business to a magically warded extradimensional brig**; its doorway **disappears into the ship's wood**. On Spider's request and with Stanley's permission, the **dead remains of Squirts and Blood Skunk** are placed inside as well, not raised from the dead. Stanley later has Spider **brought out under supervision for Union Wheel's actual emergency surgery**, then returned to the brig. **Spider and Steerious are physically released at Blast Belly Rock**, rather than left imprisoned through the voyage to Mars. These episodes demonstrate a functioning hidden holding space and controlled access, **not an independently tested unbreakable prison, indefinite detention, resurrection function or general-purpose interplanar portal**. Handling these particular prisoners also does not settle the ghost crew's otherwise stated limits on touching living mortals.

**The ship can take cottage form again in flight, without becoming invulnerable.** During **S3E07** Stanley **actually changes airborne Fate Six into a falling cottage and back into a ship**, temporarily removing visible ghost-crew manifestations; Vorel is briefly outside, then caught on the return transformation. The dragon and Paladins **track the maneuver**, so it fails as an evasive disguise. **No dime-sized miniaturization is witnessed in this encounter**. Groxl later **crushes the real ship**, despite its capacity to change shape; S3E13's and S3E18's later rematerializations do not demonstrate preservation of every pre-wreck room, object, prisoner or spirit.

**Destruction does not produce one uniform spirit status.** In [S3E07](../../episodes/S3E07.md), **some ghost deckhands visibly dissipate during the Paladin/dragon battle**, then **all visible ship-ghost manifestations vanish when Groxl crushes Fate Six**; the difference between visible dissipation and permanent soul-death is not personally resolved for each sailor. In [S3E13](../../episodes/S3E13.md), Stanley **actually rematerializes the ship** and **Sever and Gorgeous George visibly return**, alongside **most but not all** sailors. **Row remains absent**, and Sever mournfully reports that he passed into nothingness; **no identical proof of permanent destruction exists for every unnamed missing sailor**. [S3E14](../../episodes/S3E14.md)–[S3E15](../../episodes/S3E15.md) again show functioning crew **without Row**. In [S3E17](../../episodes/S3E17.md), the second crash **really destroys the vessel**, but no separate individual ghost-by-ghost end state is narrated. [S3E18](../../episodes/S3E18.md) restores operational Fate Six after four days and grants Hope **prospective command of the ship and ghost crew after her own new Mask attunement**; it provides **no complete post-second-wreck crew roll call**. The **wooden father-face and Delit Or'Nath's claimed ship-bound essence** are a separate, unverified consciousness history, not another ordinary sailor to count as returned merely because the ship works.

## Delit Or'Nath's ship-mind — a witnessed face, but no confirmed post-wreck voice

**S2E02 — actually encountered, claimed metaphysics.** In [S2E02](../../episodes/S2E02.md), Uncle Sever leads the crew into a spatially unusual **wooden chamber under Fate Six's engine**, where a **carved glowing face resembling Delit Or'Nath actually speaks** to Stanley and the party as his father. The **face, audible replies and GM-described resemblance are witnessed**, but Delit's report that his mortal body was fatally wounded, that he **gave his remaining life essence to power the vessel**, and that Stanley's mother died during their separation is **his own retrospective account**, not a performed ancient death/transference scene. Sever states the **ship is Delit rather than merely the carved face**, but the exact scope of consciousness, independent verification of personal identity and capacity to perceive private conversations remain untested. This ship-bound voice is **not the separate responsive [Book of Adjacencies](book-of-adjacencies.md)** or a freely travelling ghost sailor.

**S2E04–S3E05 — further actual conversations, still attributed claims.** In [S2E04](../../episodes/S2E04.md), Sever explains that Fate Six needs no ordinary fuel and **attributes its sustained fey-wood energy to Delit's essence**; the ship actually continues functioning, but there is **no depletion, extraction or isolated energy-source experiment**. In [S2E08](../../episodes/S2E08.md), **Hope physically consults the carved father alone**, and the speaker claims to have recruited Tungsten Wheel for Triangulosphere research, while **failing to disclose Tungsten's prison location**. In [S2E12](../../episodes/S2E12.md), Stanley and Vorel **actually confront the same father-voiced ship-mind** about Stanley's childhood, and the speaker offers alleged Weir/Principal history and Nash's engineered heart-regrowth mechanism, some of it reportedly learned from an older Nash; these claims are **not independently staged historical memories**. In [S3E05](../../episodes/S3E05.md), **Stanley actually speaks privately to the under-engine father face**, challenges the suffering his father justified as destiny, and **does not reconcile**. This is the **last directly documented active wooden-face conversation** among the reviewed maintained episode dossiers.

**S3E07–S3E18 — the hull returns, but Delit's individual fate is not demonstrated.** In [S3E07](../../episodes/S3E07.md), Groxl **really crushes the whole ship** and the enchanted ghost sailors **visibly dissipate**. **No distinct destruction, departure, death declaration or survival test for Delit's life essence or wooden face is performed.** In [S3E13](../../episodes/S3E13.md), Stanley's mask **actually rematerializes a functioning Fate Six**, and **Uncle Sever, Gorgeous George and most surviving ghost sailors are individually observed**, but Crooked Hand Row and others remain absent. The source **does not separately revisit the father chamber or record Delit speaking**; a working ship does not by itself prove Delit's consciousness **survived unchanged, was restored, or died and was resurrected**. During [S3E17](../../episodes/S3E17.md), the ship is **actually destroyed again in the deliberate crash**, and [S3E18](../../episodes/S3E18.md) later documents **a four-day physical reconstitution and actual voyages**. Stanley then **gives Hope the ship's Mask and intended command over its surviving ghost crew**, but there is **no final face conversation, Delit-to-Hope introduction, specific testimony from Sever about the father's condition or personal-essence transfer demonstrated**. The status of **Delit's individual consciousness after either wreck is *unverified***, even though the restored ship's operation is witnessed.
