# T'ietkalla — the lost kingdom and its contested future

Beneath T'ietkalla's crowded lower town and ruling keep lay the remains of an older tiefling kingdom. Its forgotten throne and conflicting accounts of royal power became part of a new struggle: first over who would rule the city, then over what sort of society could survive war, exile and return.[^origins]

<a id="before-the-party"></a>

## The lost kingdom beneath the keep

Buried chambers, a water throne, tiefling remains and old records testify to a former polity, but not to a complete or reliably dated royal history. The elder Nobieri, released from an enchanted book, speaks of an Akmenos dynasty, lost houses, concealed soldiers and a pact. Kefeli presents a different account of protecting the kingdom against vampires and Andreeste; Monsoon Cabbage brings his own interests to its history. Their accounts illuminate the competing claims without establishing which ancient bargains or rights of succession were genuine.[^ancient]

<a id="contest-for-the-keep-e031e054"></a>

## The struggle for the crown

The lower settlement, also called Trashburg, endures gang rule, severe inequality and the exploitation of pixies through the Pixie Blush trade. The Guzzle Gang, the Twenty-Sixers and other rivals struggle for the keep and the streets. Monsoon's Order 26 forces P0D into a giant form; amid the ensuing betrayals Vorel kills Buttons, Diontalus kills P0D, and Tedna dies. The companions recover the Gem of Tides, and Kefeli is destroyed. Even the giant froghemoth's apparent end is incomplete: its lower portion dies while the upper portion escapes.[^struggle]

After further devastation, the companions find Angelashi alive and free her from captivity. Monsoon escapes in mist rather than surrendering his designs. Armed tieflings return with the elder and publicly crown Hope Akmenos. Her new authority is real, but a coronation cannot undo the city's damaged institutions or settle the rival accounts of its older kingdom.[^coronation]

<a id="reforms-and-pressure-e055e059"></a>

## A fragile reforming court

Three months later, Hope's court has undertaken repairs and attempts to change the city's hierarchy, infrastructure and defenses. The effort contends with shortages, rival nobles and a population that has already suffered exploitation. A destructive fall of stone kills civilians, showing how precarious security remains. The Principal offers railway trade, supplies and the return of Dusthill prisoners; the railway arrangement is initially an agreement in principle, whereas several of the prisoners do later return. Promises of reform and diplomatic reconciliation must be distinguished from institutions that have actually taken hold.[^reforms]

<a id="arc-five-devastation-and-rescue"></a>

## Invasion, flight and displacement

Hope later returns to an inhabited city with an operating railway network and burns its temporarily closed, empty station. The destruction of that station is witnessed; the full consequences for trade and the city's politics are not. Soon afterward a demonic army attacks, shattering buildings and killing residents. Hope flees with several companions through a gate. Whether her choices drew the invaders or whether she deliberately endangered the city is contested rather than established by the flight itself.[^invasion]

When the travelers return, nycaloths still threaten the ruined streets. The Elhandari intervene, rescue people and capture General Carthax alive, although his attack kills Squirts and Melgor. Surviving tieflings disperse; some settle in Elhandar's forest and name their new home the Harbor. Omnom later accuses Hope of having brought disaster upon the city; she gives a different account of the invaders' pursuit. Neither their dispute nor the rescue supplies a complete casualty count or proof that the city was already liberated and rebuilt.[^rescue][^displacement]

## Lasting legacy

Earth's eventual restoration brings greenery and water back to the surrounding land, but does not rebuild T'ietkalla's ruined streets. The keep stands amid the damage. Monsoon dies by his own decision in the sunlight before that renewal, leaving Hope his helmet. Hope returns as queen with surviving tieflings, welcomes human refugees, dragonsons and some former Toyota Host members, and wears the Mask of Eltanfawr—handed over by Stanley—as a crown. She begins the work of reconstruction; its success and the eventual form of her inclusive government are not yet witnessed.[^finale]

The city's older genealogy, ancient pacts, full toll of its sieges and lasting political settlement remain unsettled. Its history is therefore neither a simple return to the lost monarchy nor an already completed rebirth.
