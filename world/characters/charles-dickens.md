# Charles Dickens — the illithid office dissenter

## Introduction

In an ancient conference room beneath Khaervilon, Nash put on a set of wired glasses and found himself in a pristine corporate office, apparently inhabiting an illithid body. There he met another illithid, casually dressed in shorts, sandals and spectacles, who called himself Charles Dickens. Charles offered snacks and a coin for a brain-vending machine. His workplace manners barely concealed his resentment: after fifty years without a promotion, he wanted to embarrass the superior who had kept him waiting.[^office]

## Rivalry inside the projected office

Charles described the headsets as a means of projecting visitors' ethereal selves into the Void Realm while their bodies stayed in the physical offices. He said that his rival Ovaxiad intended to use the human-built Traversal Drive to bring that realm into Earth's plane and exploit captured souls. The companions really experienced a second office environment, with changeable illithid avatars, but Charles's broader explanation of its nature and the device's purpose remained his own interested account.[^office]

His proposed sabotage served his grudge. He supplied Nash and Hope with a memory stick containing what he said were false reporting figures, and instructed them to place the data on an unattended office computer. He promised that the deception could set the operation back by months. Nash completed an apparent upload while Hope changed her projected form to distract the staff, but their intrusion triggered an alarm. The same alarm sounded in the physical facility. When the glasses clung to their heads like living membranes, both escaped them and returned fully to their bodies.[^sabotage]

## Current status

Later, an old computer in the physical offices accepted an unseen password and displayed a message signed by Charles. The sender reported that the false data had reached its destination and warned that further contact might be compromised. A burning drive component was ejected nearby. The message supports the reported transfer, but it does not establish that the Traversal Drive was delayed for months, that the sender could be independently authenticated, or that Charles had become a trustworthy ally. No later direct encounter or confirmed fate is recorded at this source cutoff.[^message]
