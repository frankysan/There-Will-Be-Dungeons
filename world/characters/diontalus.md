# Diontalus — self-described archfey of carnage

Diontalus enters the companions' story as a luminous, imperious presence in the oil sands. He calls himself an archfey of carnage, speaks with the certainty of someone accustomed to obedience, and makes **Stanley Billings** an unwilling vessel for his power. His cruelty repeatedly strains Stanley's loyalty to the others. Even after he is torn from Stanley, Diontalus remains a force they cannot easily trust: at times an enemy, at times a dangerous ally, and finally a fighter whose visible end leaves questions about what an archfey can truly lose.[^identity]

<a id="origin-and-intrusion--e029e034"></a>

## The unwanted companion

Following the strange song beyond the travelers' camp, Stanley finds a being who offers him an arrangement. Stanley refuses. Diontalus joins himself to Stanley anyway, using the same body while retaining a separate voice, will and capacity to seize control. Later exchanges make the distinction unmistakable: Stanley can resist or despair at the stranger's choices without being their author. Nor is this violation a new agreement signed on behalf of the Soulbroker.[^intrusion]

Where Stanley hopes that speech, healing and restraint might improve the world, Diontalus is drawn to force, humiliation and the taking of power. He introduces himself as a great being, but the magnitude of his abilities proves neither the full rank nor the ancient history he claims. Their shared life becomes an argument about responsibility that Stanley is never allowed to escape.[^relationships]

<a id="conduct-and-casualties--e045e057"></a>

## Harm done through Stanley's hands

Diontalus's willingness to hurt the weak is not merely a threat. In the chambers beneath T'ietkalla he deliberately kills the injured guard George Miller, despite Stanley's objections and the companions' attempts to intervene. Later, amid the struggle over the Gem of Tides, he takes part in a chaotic battle in which the transformed pixie P0D becomes a target. Nash tries desperately to save her; Diontalus destroys her instead. Dashington also dies by Diontalus's attacks. Buttons is killed in that same conflict, **but by Vorel's sword**, not by Diontalus.[^violence]

These killings draw a deep division between Stanley and his friends. Nash mourns P0D; Vorel mistrusts the body Diontalus shares with Stanley; and Stanley carries guilt without having chosen the violence. Their disagreements are rooted in real harm, not proof that host and intruder are one moral agent.

<a id="buttons-the-arcanum-and-relationships"></a>

<a id="november-2020-bonus--limited-player-form-and-independent-archfey"></a>

Buttons, the intelligent cat who had traveled with them, seems particularly invested in Stanley's fate. The possibility of removing Diontalus through the Arcanum never becomes an accomplished rescue. Stanley's attempt to live constructively under Hope's rule is no cure, either. After a long quiet spell, Diontalus acts again and kills the leprechaun Coopen, taking magical energy from him. Silence had not meant departure.[^relationships][^violence][^player]

<a id="extraction--e059"></a>

## The prisoner in the crystal

At Hope's court the Principal confronts the company and, with terrifying ease, **pulls Diontalus bodily free of Stanley and seals him inside a glowing crystal**. Stanley can at last act without his unwanted companion controlling him. The Principal takes custody and speaks of studying the prisoner, whom he blames for an ancient catastrophe; the terrible history is the Principal's account, while the extraction itself is witnessed. Schemes for a future exile or imprisonment do not establish what eventually happened to the crystal.[^crystal]

The separation matters to Stanley as liberation, not as reconciliation. His fear of becoming a vessel remains intelligible even after the immediate presence has gone. It also means that the being who later acts under Diontalus's name is no longer simply another voice inside Stanley.

<a id="creator-commentary-and-disputed-fate"></a>

## Signs of a return

Long afterward, the Principal hints privately to Stanley that a dangerous old acquaintance might have survived and come back. Stanley suspects Diontalus and wonders whether the eager young elf seeking his apprenticeship, Dilo, conceals the spirit. The suspicion is reasonable but not proof. Dilo joins the travelers, then betrays them and imprisons Hope; even that treachery does not by itself reveal how the supernatural enemy escaped the crystal.[^warnings]

In a mushroom-filled cave, the captive Hope's supposed keeper addresses Stanley as someone who knows him intimately. When Stanley strikes Dilo's body, **a separate blue spectral Diontalus emerges**. The young elf is left motionless and apparently not breathing. Whether Dilo was willing, how long he had been controlled, and whether the Principal helped Diontalus return are not established by Diontalus's claims. The spirit threatens the party, wields a reaper's scythe and kills Omnom Cinderborn before an injured black dragon intervenes. Diontalus escapes into the rock, alive and separate from Stanley.[^warnings]

<a id="e123--persistent-harm-and-fears-without-a-new-encounter"></a>

## Fear, threats and a precarious bargain

After the cave encounter, the companions fear that Diontalus can take other hosts and leave wounds that are not easily healed. They consider his vulnerability to radiant power and the possibility of finding him again. Their theories cannot be taken for a new demonstration of his powers or a second attack.[^harm]

<a id="e124--summoned-interlocutor-and-conditional-accord"></a>

At Elhandar a High Elf brings the spectral Diontalus before Stanley once more. His manner is no less dangerous: he demands that Stanley someday visit his own Feywild court, threatens the elves if Stanley breaks the promise, and holds out help against the Principal. Stanley persuades him to **await that visit and aid their fight**, rather than immediately take Stanley away. The agreement is negotiated, not an observed restoration of trust; nor is a journey to the Feywild or discharge of the bargain actually shown.[^accord]

<a id="e125--summoning-instrument-admissions-and-actual-fleet-intervention"></a>

## Help that does not erase the past

Diontalus supplies Stanley with a strange ocarina, passing it into his hands as a solid wooden instrument. He says its melody will summon him, though the promise is not tested. Pressed by Vorel, he admits having coerced disguised elves and claims to have released and directed ghostly pirates. His frankness about abuse offers little reassurance. Yet when mercenaries attack Elhandar, he genuinely fights them, reaping foes with his scythe and striking hostile vessels from the air. This is real help, not proof that every threat or hidden ambition has vanished.[^intervention]

<a id="e126--defeated-by-the-guitar-fate-unresolved"></a>

During the conflict with Henatar Grape, a magical guitar hurls Diontalus into the sand. He lies motionless, his fate uncertain to the others as the fighting continues. The silence resembles death but does not establish it.[^guitar]

<a id="e128--conscious-again-drains-the-mercenarys-corpse"></a>

Stanley later finds the fallen spirit and **restores him with healing magic**. Diontalus rises and speaks, still separate from his former host. He then draws blue, soul-like energy from the dead mercenary with his scythe and consumes it. Once more he has both survived and shown why assistance from him comes at a terrible moral cost. The consumed essence's final fate remains unknown.[^healing]

<a id="s3e05--physically-deploys-to-earth-toward-northwest-cannon-battle-result-pending"></a>

## A place in Earth's defense

When the defenders gather on Mars to prepare their assault on the Solar Mines, Diontalus is among them. He is assigned the northwestern anti-air position and descends to Earth with the coalition. Taking an Elhandari speeder, he goes toward his objective rather than rejoining Stanley. His participation is witnessed, but a successful destruction of that weapon is not shown at that stage.[^defense]

<a id="bonus-2022-07--beaus-alleged-ancient-connection-with-groxl-and-elhandari-imprisonment"></a>

## The older quarrel

At the Solar Mines, Diontalus encounters a mountain-sized lizardfolk whose appearance seems to revive an ancient enmity. The giant addresses him as **Dian** and threatens to kill him again; Diontalus recognizes the foe and says it has died before. Those declarations hint at a long and violent past, yet neither combatant offers an independently dependable history. Stories of a former company, Groxl and old Elhandari captivity cannot simply be treated as witnessed adventures or confused with the much later crystal imprisonment.[^ancient]

<a id="s3e08--manifested-ethereal-body-destroyed-by-groxls-eye-laser"></a>

## The visible end

Diontalus appears over the battlefield in his luminous blue form and meets the giant with his spectral scythe, but the blows fail against its armored scales. A terrible beam from the giant's remaining eye burns through the spirit's body. Its blue substance reddens, and **Diontalus visibly disintegrates**. Far away on the monster's back, Stanley feels a connection torn out of him. This is neither a false report nor another capture: the form fighting beside the defenders has been destroyed.[^fall]

Still, what destruction means for a being who claims the rank of archfey is less certain than what everyone saw. Even accounts from those who knew him, or later told stories about his ancient bonds, cannot prove that no aspect of him could ever exist again.

## Current status

Diontalus's **last witnessed manifestation is destroyed in the battle at the Solar Mines**, and Stanley feels their remaining connection break. No later return, resurrection, new host or visit to his court is shown. His promise to help against the Principal and his insistence on drawing Stanley back to the Feywild are left without a final reckoning. **Whether the spirit is annihilated beyond the loss of its manifested form remains unknown**; his violence, his uneasy assistance and the freedom Stanley gained from their separation are the lasting facts of his story.[^fall]
