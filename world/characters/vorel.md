# Vorel

## Introduction

Vorel comes to [Dusthill](../places/dusthill.md) looking for food and water. A powerful lizardfolk warrior with an elaborate way of speaking, he places great value on strength, kinship and the obligations people owe one another. His confidence can become grandiosity, but his search for a place in the Wastes begins with ordinary needs and the companions he finds while meeting them.[^vorel-introduction]

## Development by arc

### Arc One — Beginnings in Dusthill

The troubles of Dusthill bring Vorel into company with [Hope](hope-akmenos.md), [Nash](nash-maggard.md) and [Stanley](stanley-billings.md). His strength becomes part of a shared effort to survive and help the settlement. The association also brings him [Bok Bok](bok-bok.md), a goblin he captures while tracking raiders. Bok Bok initially submits to him as a lizard king or god; their relationship begins with captivity and deference, before developing into the enduring companionship that follows them across the Wastes.

That early distinction matters to Vorel's growing stature. Being addressed as a king gives him an admiring follower, but it does not give him sovereignty over every lizardfolk people. His readiness to accept a magnificent description of himself coexists with the much more immediate responsibility of deciding how to treat someone in his power.[^vorel-arc01]

### Arc Two — Chains and Bargains

Slavery and manipulation in [Slavetown](../places/slavetown.md) and the Black Mine place Vorel's independence under pressure. He is capable of violent and disruptive schemes, and his later code of decency cannot make every earlier choice harmless. Yet he draws a clear boundary when the [Soulbroker](the-soulbroker.md) offers power in exchange for a soul-bound obligation. Hope, Nash and Stanley sign; Vorel refuses, declaring that he wants strength without corruption and will rely on his own abilities.

His refusal does not end his friendship with the signatories. He remains beside them while withholding the consent that would bind him to their bargain. His emerging Pillars of Decency express a related ambition: honest speech, fair dealing and kinship should provide a basis for living together. They are principles he proposes, rather than a guarantee that he will always act justly. Hunting with Bok Bok and sharing provisions show the practical side of that outlook alongside his appetite for impressive deeds.[^vorel-arc02]

### Arc Three — Road to a Lost Kingdom

Vorel's claims to leadership begin to meet obligations beyond his immediate companions. A challenge from [Diptak](diptak.md) ends with a pledge from the Golden Land clan to Vorel's clan. He sees the promise as potential support against [the Principal](the-principal.md), although the oath has not yet put an army at his disposal. His desire to gather his people becomes more concrete without establishing universal lizardfolk allegiance.

He also stands up for companions whose Soulbroker bargain he rejected. When an armored paladin demands the signatories' execution, Vorel opposes surrender and fights for them. His independence from their contract does not mean indifference to their lives.

In the struggle around T'ietkalla, his boast of being Lizard King remains a claim contested by other lizardfolk. More tangible opportunities come through Charlemagne's inn keys and a great chainsaw-edged sword. These give him property to care for and a distinctive weapon, while his friendship with Bok Bok continues through dangerous dealings and upheaval.[^vorel-arc03]

### Arc Four — The Crown of T'ietkalla

The struggle for [T'ietkalla](../places/tietkalla.md) exposes the violence within Vorel's loyalties. He kills Buttons during the betrayal and warehouse fighting. The deaths caused by [Diontalus](diontalus.md), especially P0D's, draw his hostility toward the intruder sharing Stanley's body; Stanley's presence does not make those acts Stanley's willing choices. Vorel's friendships now require him to distinguish a companion from the dangerous separate being who can act through him.

His own survival also depends on others. Kefeli turns him to stone, and Bok Bok guards him while Nash and Stanley seek a remedy. Their successful restoration returns him to life. Soon afterward, inside the transformed froghemoth, he accepts the living [Eye of Groxl](../technology/eye-of-groxl.md), which embeds itself in his chest. It grants protection and fire, but has a will and reactions of its own. His separate ability to change his scales' colors comes from the strange contact and energies of that ordeal, rather than simply from possessing the Eye.

Vorel supports Hope's rise, but his role includes killing the captive Suck Master Din at her signal. His commitment to fellowship and fair dealing exists alongside participation in coercive power.[^vorel-arc04]

### Arc Five — Kings and Consequences

With Bok Bok, Vorel develops the Golden Sands inn and adventurers' guild. Here the Pillars of Decency become an attempt at everyday institution-building: rules for a shared place, rather than another title recited on the road. Helping civilians during the stonefall likewise gives his strength a purpose beyond winning challenges.

[Razak Val](../places/razak-val.md) offers a larger and more dangerous version of leadership. Vorel challenges Orp, loses Diptak to the cyclops's violence and survives a succession struggle in which a feast explosion kills leaders and bystanders. Defeating Orp brings recognition as Great Fang, but the Sun's Witnesses crisis immediately exposes how little a victory alone can secure. People are transformed into monstrous forms, and Vorel leaves while others, including [Omnom](omnom-cinderborn.md), remain amid the suffering.

His new standing therefore carries unfinished responsibilities. It does not mean Razak Val is safe or that its survivors have ceased to matter when the party moves on. Even the loss of Fate 4 calls on his practical resourcefulness: he builds a replacement rickshaw from wreckage and dinosaur bones, keeping his companions moving after another apparent security has failed.[^vorel-arc05]

### Arc Six — Beneath the World

At Khaervilon and [CHIDS](../places/chids.md), Vorel encounters powers that complicate his belief in strength as something personally possessed. Specialized armor and a psychic-dampening helmet help him confront engineered threats. When an attack severs parts of his hand, the Monster Mender procedure restores them with his companions' assistance. His formidable body is vulnerable, and survival can require technology and help as well as endurance.

The facility also offers disturbing explanations of lizardfolk history. A hive council describes the use of existing lizardfolk material to breed soldiers against dragons; the laboratories reveal particular experiments. Such claims and discoveries do not establish that all lizardfolk share one manufactured origin. The Eye's responses to marked vessels deepen the questions about what he carries without answering them completely.

At the Traversal Drive, Vorel opposes a proposed shortcut that would sacrifice Earth. He instead suggests that the four companions share the burden. They join hands in the crimson beam and are declared dead. His choice makes mutual obligation concrete at the moment of greatest danger: he asks them to face the cost together rather than preserve themselves by destroying their world.[^vorel-arc06]

### Arc Seven — The City of Doors

Vorel returns to an active living body in [Sigil](../places/sigil.md), separated from his former possessions and with the Eye damaged. Its inert remnants remain embedded in his chest, but its earlier powers cannot simply be assumed. He has to act with changed resources while the companions struggle to find one another and restore their lives.

The marionette fighting circuit gives him a genuinely public achievement: he wins the Big Time Smackeroo championship and its prize. He also accepts the trickster church's humiliating condition in the effort to restore Hope and Stanley. His appetite for recognition coexists with willingness to bear indignity for friends. At the [White Tower](../places/white-tower.md), preparing and administering curative broth with Walnut provides another kind of service, helping plague-stricken residents survive.

The hooded Voice eventually restores the Eye's function. The restoration does not restore unity to the company: Hope takes the living Hand associated with the separate Fist gauntlet and leaves. Vorel emerges with renewed power but a fractured fellowship, still responsible for distinguishing what his companions choose from what the powerful beings around them demand.[^vorel-arc07]

### Arc Eight — Separate Paths

After Hope's departure, Vorel continues with Nash and Stanley. Their custody of the closed Fist case is temporary; Stanley later opens it for Tobacho, who accepts the gauntlet. Vorel also returns the borrowed sword Shatter. These departures leave him with obligations fulfilled rather than a permanently expanding collection of other people's weapons and artifacts.

Returning to devastated T'ietkalla brings a more personal reckoning. He finds Bok Bok alive at the Golden Sands, but the battle against General Carthax kills Squirts and Melgor. Vorel helps capture Carthax alive with Elhandari pacification devices. Bok Bok survives an internal explosion at the cost of a hand, and Vorel carries Squirts's ashes away in a vial.

His ties to the city now include rescue, bereavement and the protection of survivors. The inn he helped build cannot insulate its people from war, and finding Bok Bok again does not undo the losses around them. Keeping company with the surviving refugees gives his earlier promises a harder, less triumphant meaning.[^vorel-arc08]

### Arc Nine — The Fall of Elhandar

At Elhandar, Vorel doubts what his leadership amounts to. He reports Squirts's ashes missing and calls himself the Fang of nothing. The words express grief and disillusionment, not a formal abdication or proof that his people have all died. He helps care for Chunks, takes part in the refugees' ceremony and is called Keeper of Stories: responsibility persists even while his confidence falters.

Finding Omnom alive makes that responsibility immediate. Vorel refuses Omnom's request to be killed and arranges a hunt with the companions. Omnom renews his recognition of Vorel as Great Fang, but the attempt at renewed fellowship ends in another loss when Diontalus kills him. Vorel recovers remains, works to save the injured Stanley and later carefully extracts a dying dragon's heart. His practical treatment of bodies accompanies, rather than removes, the weight of death.

He continues to protect people during Elhandar's fall, helping free the household from a collapsing lift. Afterward, anger at the repeated destruction of places they tried to help spills into threats of indiscriminate violence. He does not carry those threats out. Remaining with the original companions, and ultimately escaping with them on Nash's back, keeps him within a fellowship that his grief has strained but not ended.[^vorel-arc09]

### Arc Ten — Across the Stars

Returning to Razak Val answers part of Vorel's fear that his title has become empty. Survivors kneel and recognize their Great Fang, then ask to accompany him. Their actual voyage aboard Fate Six turns personal recognition into a following with a voice in its own future. At the Council of Earthly Defenders, he urges the Elhandari to approach Earth's settlements as neighbors rather than intimidating visitors in great ships.

His vulnerability remains visible. An alien lure traps him in visions of rule and abundance, and his companions' song frees him from that false life. Later, in the crypt, he protects Tungsten and entombs the Maggot Lord alive instead of granting the death the lich demands. When Nash's apparently dead body produces a living heart and eye, Vorel frees them from the chest and retrieves his belongings. He helps preserve his friend, then tends to him while Stanley's magic grows a new body.

The voyage also brings a tentative closeness with Union Wheel. He takes her offered hand and later considers what affection might mean for him. Their shared rest and gestures of care do not settle their relationship into a marriage or permanent commitment. His habitual certainty about strength and titles sits beside a more hesitant response to being wanted as a person.[^vorel-arc10]

### Arc Eleven — The Last War

Before the return to Earth, Vorel refuses the augmentation his three companions accept. He retains the Eye but declines another alteration, and takes part in the Courtship of the Fang among his people. Union teaches him a dance, then leaves in tears after hearing about the courtship. Their growing closeness is wounded; no reconciliation or final separation follows in that moment.

Leading the lizardfolk into war, Vorel tells them to spare enslaved people and entertains their hope for a home after victory. With the sword he names [Chrome's Bane](../technology/chromes-bane.md), he helps defeat the chrome dragon and brings down the enormous foe identified as Groxl. In the encounter that follows in the Endless Dunes, he rejects a proposed revenge crusade and voices a wish for a peaceful home. Groxl's stories about ancient origins and Vorel's body remain the speaker's claims.

Vorel also defends mortal life against the Principal's vision of chrome existence and refuses a new bargain in Hell. Conditional cooperation against a shared enemy does not erase his distrust. The confrontation with Worldbreaker costs allied lives, and the Principal's betrayal confirms the danger of their arrangement. Vorel grapples him through Stanley's prismatic barrier; the ruined chrome coats his scales. He survives and continues protecting his friends through the attack by further copies, without becoming their puppet.

When Earth is restored, Vorel is moved to tears. His hopes now concern living communities as well as personal victories. At the survivors' council he accepts Kuromokishi into the care of his people, and yields on harsher proposals that the council does not adopt. Leadership ends the war with an obligation to help former enemies find a place in society.[^vorel-arc11]

## Friendships, principles and strength

Vorel's enduring bonds complicate every simple account of him as a boastful warrior. Bok Bok begins as a captive and becomes a close companion. Hope is a friend and queen whose decisions Vorel can oppose. Stanley remains a friend despite the distinct cruelty of Diontalus, and Nash is both a fighting partner and someone Vorel repeatedly helps preserve when strength alone cannot repair him. His losses do not make those relationships painless or his own conduct uniformly merciful.

His rejection of soul bargains is consistent, but it is not a rejection of every gift, tool or act of assistance. He accepts the Eye, uses crafted weapons and armor, and depends on companions who heal and restore him. The Eye's will, his fire and his independently changing scales remain distinct aspects of his body and abilities. His many [titles](vorel-titles.md) likewise have different foundations: boast, greeting, recognized leadership, public victory and affection cannot all be treated as the same kind of authority.[^vorel-bonds]

## Current status

Vorel is alive on restored Earth. Chrome plating remains on his scales, the Eye of Groxl remains embedded, and Chrome's Bane is in his possession. He begins the overland journey toward rebuilding Razak Val with surviving lizardfolk and Kuromokishi entrusted to their care; their arrival and a completed reconstruction lie ahead.

He commissions a memorial to Nash while his friend is dead, then sees Stanley restore Nash to life away from Earth and embraces him before Nash stays with his family. At Vorel's own departure, Stanley grants him the title Friend on behalf of the companions. Vorel accepts it as Friend Till the End of His Days. Among all his grand claims, this is a distinction bestowed by people who have shared his life.[^vorel-status]
