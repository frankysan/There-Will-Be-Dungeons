# Emenil — Tidebearer priestess

## Introduction

Emenil was a priestess of the Tidebearers, devoted to Malhya, the Fishbird whom her church revered as a shepherd of the tides. In T'ietkalla she ministered to survivors, defended the value of healing and communal care, and taught Stanley prayers and the practice of water magic. Her faith offered an account of gods who might save a suffering world, though that account remained the teaching of her church rather than an established explanation of every supernatural power.[^early]

## Mercy and disagreement in T'ietkalla

When a hostile spirit aged Vorel, Emenil answered Stanley's request for help and restored the lizardfolk to his proper age. Her compassion soon brought her into conflict with the city's rulers. Learning of concealed bargains with demonic powers, she insisted that the citizens deserved the truth. Nobieri ordered two animated dead to attack her. One bit her before Stanley healed the wound and Hope commanded the assault to stop. Emenil survived, but her willingness to hear an explanation did not resolve the moral disagreement or make her party to the deception. She later offered the Tidebearers' hospital to examine captives returning from Dusthill.[^early]

When Hope returned to T'ietkalla, Emenil welcomed her at the Fishbird church and spoke of a troubled city. She described disputes over political and spiritual priorities, interrupted supplies, a new chancellor backed by the Principal and rumors of vanished opponents. These were her reports, not proof that she knew the perpetrators. Hope refused her hooded patron's demand to kill the priestess, leaving Emenil alive as the city's future grew less certain.[^regency]

## Refuge, loss and Stanley

After T'ietkalla's catastrophe, Emenil joined surviving tieflings sheltered by the elves of Elhandar. Nobieri first told Stanley that she had survived and asked after him; their subsequent reunion established that she was indeed among the refugees. At the gathering where Hope named their community the Harbor, Emenil recalled the fear that had driven her to prayer and argued that abundant water, not competition over scarcity, offered a path to peace.[^harbor]

Her relationship with Stanley proved more painful. In her cottage she confessed that she loved him and regretted how little religious reassurance had helped him. Stanley cared that she had survived, but could not return her love or promise a relationship while struggling with his own identity. After he departed, he heard a crash and understood that she had broken her shrine and offering bowls. Her anger and disappointment did not end her later devotion to Malhya.[^harbor]

## The lake communion

When Stanley warned that Diontalus might threaten the refugees, Emenil objected to abandoning their elven protectors. She proposed a communion at the Harbor lake instead. Joined by the four companions, she used sacred fish flakes said to have been entrusted to her by her late husband, a reverend killed during the old persecution. She admitted that she had never performed this rite before. The five breathed underwater and passed into a vast watery encounter.[^lake]

A kraken frightened her but swam away. A winged fishlike being answering to Malhya then appeared. Emenil petitioned it for her companions, and both Stanley and Vorel were visibly cured of debilitating injuries. The visitor's actions were witnessed, while its claims of divine identity and wider history remained its own testimony. Emenil returned to the Harbor lake with the others, still alive and still seeking meaning in her faith.[^lake]

## Current status

During the battle at the Solar Mines, Emenil fought beside the allied force. She and Union Wheel clung to the enormous lizardfolk's tail, were thrown off and attempted to flee. The giant struck them with a crushing blow, leaving visible remains. Both were physically killed in the witnessed battle; no subsequent resurrection of Emenil is established. Her soul's fate is unknown. Her remembered legacy is one of mercy, moral courage and a faith tested by grief, not a completed romance or an unbroken certainty.[^finale]
