# Stanley Billings

## Introduction

Stanley is a half-elf bard whose charm and command of a room serve an ambition larger than personal renown: he wants to shape a more orderly society. He begins among the people of Dusthill, where political ideals meet the practical needs of a settlement struggling to survive. His persuasive gifts make him a natural advocate, but his faith in institutions will be tested by what those institutions ask him to accept.[^introduction]

## Development by arc

### Arc One — The promise of civic order

Stanley travels with Nash, Hope and Vorel through communities where power is often enforced by force or custom. He tries to answer disorder with negotiation, performance and law, and accepts the Soulbroker's compact with Hope and Nash while Vorel refuses. The choice reflects Stanley's belief that powerful systems can be used for the common good, but it binds him to an authority whose aims he does not fully understand. His relations with his companions grow through disagreement as well as loyalty; with Nash especially, friction does not prevent the beginnings of a lasting bond. Stanley's confidence in civic order is real, but not yet uncomplicated.[^arc-one]

### Arc Two — The burden of another's acts

The forced joining with Diontalus tests Stanley's understanding of agency. He refuses the being's offer before Diontalus is joined to him against his will, yet the archfey can speak and act through Stanley's body. Diontalus's violence—including the deaths of George Miller, Buttons, P0D and Dashington—leaves Stanley grieving and ashamed, even though those acts are not his choices. His distress does not erase the distinction between host and intruder; it shows how deeply he feels responsible for harms he could not prevent.

Meanwhile, Stanley directs his public-minded instincts toward Tietkalla, helping establish medical and religious institutions during the city's reconstruction and taking part in Hope's coronation. His confidence in the Principle's order weakens as he sees how authority can preserve injustice. When the Principle finally extracts Diontalus into a crystal, Stanley continues without the presence that had violated his body, though the fate of the crystal and Diontalus's longer story remain separate from Stanley's own.[^arc-two]

### Arc Three — Choosing whom to save

Stanley keeps trying to make institutions serve people rather than the other way around. His work in Tietkalla's medical and religious life gives that ideal a practical form, while Razak Val forces him to question the morality of experiments and imprisonment. After Follow dies, Stanley promises to save Sira; the promise becomes a measure of his sense of duty, not a guarantee that he can control events.

At CHIDS, Stanley offers himself as a possible substitute for Sira and joins Nash, Hope and Vorel in sharing the crimson Drive beam rather than letting it destroy Earth. Sira escapes, but the GM declares the four companions dead. Stanley's choice is an act of solidarity shaped by the same protective instinct that once made him trust powerful arrangements. The sacrifice does not resolve his Soulbroker compact or settle what his death means for the promise attached to it.[^arc-three]

### Arc Four — Return, faith and separation

Stanley's body is found in Sigil, and after a period in which others interpret his condition for him, he chooses to leave the void and returns through a mirror rite. He is alive, but the restoration does not erase the death that preceded it or prove his compact has ended. His changing relationship with faith and civic authority continues as he helps the White Tower community and grows close to Alentris.

During the siege, Stanley is badly hurt but survives, then helps Alentris and the defenders. Hope's choice to attach the Hand of the Voice and leave the original companions unfolds in the same session: Stanley witnesses her departure, but not the private instructions behind it. Her absence leaves the company changed rather than neatly reconciled. Stanley continues with Nash and Vorel, carrying grief and uncertainty instead of treating the separation as a resolved promise of reunion.[^arc-four]

### Arc Five — A name chosen, a family found

Stanley's return to Tietkalla and Elhandar tests his trust in civic order. In an alternate tower he acquires counterparts of familiar possessions and uses *Modify Memory* on Alentris, troubling his companions and complicating his self-image as a principled advocate. He learns more about his Elhandari birth family but chooses to remain Stanley Billings. His father's hidden study offers clues; its journal remains hypothesis, not settled destiny.

Stanley permits Dilo to accompany the party as a provisional pupil, then faces the consequences when Dilo betrays them and Diontalus reappears as an independent spectral being. It renews an old fear without making Stanley responsible for the archfey's choices. Stanley helps free Hope, apologizes for his treatment of her and welcomes her back. He values Emenil but they do not commit romantically; he also accepts the Harborers' wish to stay. His accord with Diontalus promises the archfey's return to his own court, where he will wait and later aid them against the Principle. Neither that journey nor the aid has occurred, and Stanley's soul claim remains.

When Elhandar falls, Stanley fights to protect the people around him and escapes with the household to Villa Ornath. He later revives Diontalus after the archfey falls motionless, but does not rejoin with him. A private conversation with the Soulbroker gives Stanley new claims about the Weir, the Principle and Earth; he shares that conversation with his companions only after they have escaped the storm. The contrast between an immense witnessed threat and the Soulbroker's account of the past leaves Stanley with urgent questions, not answers.[^arc-five][^retrospective]

## Relationships and enduring traits

Stanley's closest bonds are also sources of friction. He loves Nash, Hope and Vorel, even as their choices challenge his judgments; his kinship-like closeness with Nash is not literal brotherhood. His relationship with Hope moves from public distrust to private apology and renewed trust, without making every disagreement disappear. Emenil matters to him, but a confession of love and their shared approach to communion do not become a romantic commitment. Alentris is a valued ally, though Stanley's use of magic to alter her memory remains part of their history.

Diontalus is neither Stanley's other name nor an extension of his agency. Their forced bodily connection, separation, later spectral encounters and negotiated accord belong to distinct stages of a difficult relationship. Stanley's identity remains his own, even as he considers what he owes the archfey and the compact that still claims his soul.[^relationships]

## Current status

Stanley survives the Season One finale on Earth with Nash, Hope and Vorel near Villa Ornath. He has not completed the Mask of Eltanfawr's attunement or made the villa fly. Diontalus is alive and separate from him; the archfey's promised return and aid remain in the future. Stanley's father is still unfound, the Soulbroker compact unresolved, and his next choices shaped by obligations he has accepted but not yet fulfilled.[^current-status]
