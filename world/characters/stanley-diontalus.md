# Stanley Billings and Diontalus

**Portrayed by:** Jon. **Identity model:** Two distinctly acting personalities/agents who **share Stanley's body**, with shifting control; not interchangeable aliases for one intention. **Spelling:** **Diontalus** is the creator- and owner-accepted canonical display ([D-026](../../project/DECISIONS.md)). `Dion Talus`, `Deontalus`, `Diantalus`, `Diontalis` are searchable source spellings, not equal official names. **Spoilers:** E000–E054 and Q&A.

## Stanley before the intrusion

Stanley introduces himself in Dusthill as a persuasive advocate for **order and government**. He is ambitious, socially nimble and capable of harsh conduct, while periodically becoming the party's conscience. He helps repair Dusthill's water crisis, participates in the dangerous Slavetown disputes and acts as one of the three characters who **voluntarily sign the Soulbroker pact** in E023. Later he tells companions **Stanley Billings** was a **name he chose to begin anew**; this should not be treated as a verified birth name.

Stanley is especially vulnerable to a change that happens **without his consent**. While the other characters are entranced in the oil sands, he investigates alongside Buttons and encounters a blue luminous being naming himself **Diontalus**. Stanley **refuses** the proposed arrangement; the entity nonetheless **forces magical entry into his body** in E029. The aftereffects are not merely a cooperative disguise. In E034 Diontalus openly takes charge and acknowledges the forced joining.

## Diontalus as a separate actor

Diontalus calls himself an **archfey of carnage**; this is his self-identification, not a guarantee every cosmic claim is correct. He can control the shared body when Stanley cannot, uses magic and impersonation, and tends toward cruelty in situations where Stanley attempts restraint. In the caves E045 he **deliberately kills the guard George Miller**, over Stanley's objections. In E050 Diontalus **kills P0D** despite Nash's effort to save her, then kills Dashington. Both acts worsen the party's internal fracture, especially Vorel's hostility toward the shared body.

Stanley later describes witnessing George's death while unable to prevent it, and **interprets the killing as an attempt to punish Stanley**. That interpretation belongs to Stanley, distinct from the observed murder. Stanley reassumes control after the warehouse conflict, **casts Speak with Dead on Buttons**, grieves P0D, helps reverse the petrification of **Hope and Vorel** in E051 and participates in Hope's arrival at the Tietkalla throne. No successful expulsion of Diontalus has been performed by E054.

## Buttons and the later revelation

Buttons, the intelligent talking cat, takes a particular interest in Stanley from E028 onward. His later betrayal helps spark the E049 ambush in which Vorel kills him. Only **after the arc**, in the [March 2019 Q&A](../../episodes/BONUS-2019-03.md), does Beau reveal that **Buttons loved Stanley romantically** and sought to take him to the **Arcanum to remove Diontalus**. This is a **GM retrospective disclosure of intended motive**, not a line Stanley heard in E049 and not proof Buttons' attacks caused no harm.

Jon also says Stanley's name takes inspiration from **Stan in Monkey Island**, with “Billings” chosen for a politician-like association. He speculates that Stanley, rather than Diontalus, would have **waited instead of killing P0D**; that is a hypothetical, not an alternative E050 event.

## Status and remaining questions

By the close of E054 **Stanley and Diontalus are still bound to the same body**, and their capacity to know/impede each other's actions is situation-dependent. His self-chosen name, the original ancient text he carries, the Soulbroker contract and the proposed separation method are distinct threads. Future Arcanum travel, a completed cure, or a returned book are **not already achieved**.

## After Year One — healer, believer and still a divided body (E055–E056)

**Stanley** takes part in building a **hospital and medical library** and studies with the **Tidebearers** during the three-month interval. **Diontalus remains in his body but unusually dormant**. In the [April roundtable](../../episodes/BONUS-2019-04.md), **Jon explains** that Stanley and Diontalus may take control when the other sleeps and that Stanley **privately intends his medical research also to seek separation**; that private player motive is **not a revealed in-world conspiracy to every party member**. No exorcism or second body occurs.

E056 sends Stanley into a storm evacuation and then into Nobiery's laboratory, where **the angry ghost of George Miller whom Diontalus killed** attacks. Stanley fights with **Dissonant Whispers** and his magical sword **Diplomacy**, and later seeks **Emenil's Greater Restoration** to undo Vorel's magical aging. Stanley **visibly creates water through his Tidebearer/Fishbird training**. When Emenil discovers the party's demonic dealings, Stanley argues for redemption and unity; **Nobiery orders his undead to attack her** despite the attempt at persuasion. ## June bonus — guilt, civic order and atonement (player interpretation)

In the [June 2019 bonus Q&A](../../episodes/BONUS-2019-06.md) (~00:02:43–00:05:37 and ~00:19:29–00:21:51), **Jon describes a crisis in Stanley's original mission**. Stanley left Dusthill wanting a world with laws and institutions; encountering the Principle's order, including **slavery and indenture**, makes him question whether the institutions he once desired would really help. Jon sees the Principle as both **an intriguing fulfillment of that ambition and a cautionary example**. He says Stanley **carries guilt for people killed through his body even when Diontalus acted**, and is seeking **atonement**. The continued **Weir/Soulbroker obligation** gives him a reason to remain with his companions. He is curious about Principle City but **does not seek redemption through submission to the Principle or Steel God**. These are Jon's **retrospective player readings and private intentions**, not conversations automatically heard by other characters, and do not independently prove Diontalus's removal is permanent.

Jon also explains his progression from **dark/manipulation-themed spells** toward **weather, thunder, lightning and shelter** (bonus ~00:12:42–00:14:55), including the inspiration behind *Tiny Hut* and *Polymorph*. Spell-selection rationale is not another played casting.

## Crystallization under the Principle (E057–E060)

**E057:** Diontalus still **takes control of Stanley's body**, murders **the unlucky leprechaun Coopen/Coupon** and consumes magical essence. **E059:** The visiting **Principle performs a visible separation**, pulling Diontalus into **a glowing crystal**, which he keeps and promises to imprison or eventually launch into space. **Stanley survives and appears free of the intrusive acting presence through E060**. This is a **played magical extraction**, not independent proof of permanent extinction, secure captivity or successful space exile. The Principle alleges that the archfey destroyed an ancient metropolis; **this is the Principle's interested account**. [E057](../../episodes/E057.md) · [E059](../../episodes/E059.md).

Stanley **heals Emenil's bite in E057**, searches old magic with his reference book, and uses **Augury** during the Principle negotiation; the omen is **woe** for a contemplated violent attack. After the prisoner return, his old acquaintances and responsibilities resurface. At Razak Val he is disturbed by the court's logic and **tries but fails to punch** the diminutive **Mirion**, a mistake the adviser reframes as dishonor against a much smaller opponent. [E057](../../episodes/E057.md) · [E059](../../episodes/E059.md) · [E060](../../episodes/E060.md).

## Razak Val — the cost of saving friends (E061–E063)

Stanley becomes suspicious when the guards have been placed under magical **Sleep** and questions Nash's long absence. He helps reason through **Bramie's murder versus the separate Fengar egg theft**, but neither the E061 investigation nor E062 questioning identifies the killer. His attempted **megaphone-feedback test** does not reveal a brain worm; Vorel instead confronts Zercil. [E061](../../episodes/E061.md) · [E062](../../episodes/E062.md).

During **Hope's E062 rocket detonation** Stanley survives the mass-casualty blast and fights the remaining guards, using **Mantle of Inspiration** and **Booming Blade**. In **E063**, he **secretly casts Healing Word on the critically injured Vorel** during the fight against Zercil, prioritizing his friend's life over the lizardfolk's expectation of unassisted combat. He also provides **temporary hit points through Mantle of Inspiration**. **Stanley alone then sees a young human girl standing among the lizardfolk who makes eye contact and literally disappears**; the figure's nature is not resolved and cannot be assigned to all companions' knowledge. [E062](../../episodes/E062.md) · [E063](../../episodes/E063.md).

Stanley uses **Mage Hand to retrieve Orp's Necklace of Fireballs** with **five beads/charges**. Under Vorel's new rule, he organizes **medical screening of 200–300 starving captives**, separating examined individuals rather than claiming a completed release. He senses **unexplained arcane significance** around a calmer-than-expected legless prisoner calling himself **Follow/Follo**, who claims to be a displaced touring performer, and **has him held separately** for further questions. No worm is directly seen inside the man in this episode. [E063](../../episodes/E063.md).

## Mind-reader, collapse and old song (E064–E065)

In [E064](../../episodes/E064.md), Stanley resists Omnom's suggestions of lethal dissection or deliberately infecting a prisoner during screening of the **Sun's Witnesses**. A captive designated by **Follow's telepathy** is searched without a clear visible worm; Vorel provokes Dollo Poppio and a mass transformation follows. Stanley fights during the flight from Razak Val. **The Necklace of Fireballs he recovered E063 is given to Omnom E064**, not retained in Stanley's active inventory.

In [E065](../../episodes/E065.md), Stanley **speaks Elvish** to Follow's daughter **Sira/Sera**, who understands, and **hums an old remembered melody** for her in the shelter. This evokes incomplete personal memories, **not a fully recovered biography or known elf homeland**. He maintains **Tiny Hut** in a rare downpour while Nash works on drainage. His **Augury to Malhya** yields **both weal and woe**, a mixed result rather than evidence of a particular storm creator.

The [July-archived bonus](../../episodes/BONUS-2019-07.md) adds Jon's **player explanation** for letting Vorel and Hope lead their storylines. Jon considered **cleric multiclassing but did not take the class**, preferring a gradual exploration of Stanley's faith. Beau loosely says the **1,000-day Soulbroker deal has 80–90% remaining**, without calculating an exact day count.
