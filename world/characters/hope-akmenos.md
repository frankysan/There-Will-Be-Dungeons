# Hope Akmenos

## Introduction

Hope Akmenos was a tiefling engineer among the ruins of Dusthill, a survivor who could make damaged machines move again and imagined that older, more powerful technology might also undo a personal loss. She relied on tools, improvisation and a refusal to surrender when a mechanism failed. Traveling with Nash, Stanley and Vorel brought her into dangers that no repair alone could solve. Hope's determination gave the companions a practical anchor, but also exposed her to bargains that promised to remake the world at a cost she did not always understand. Her journey would test the difference between making something work and deciding what it ought to do.[^introduction]

## Journey through the eleven arcs

### Arc 01 — Beginnings in Dusthill

Hope's engineering offered the newly formed company a means of surviving the Wastes rather than merely crossing them. In Dusthill she helped confront the failing waterworks and began turning salvaged technology into equipment the group could use. Her instinct was to approach a problem as something that could be understood, altered and repaired. Nash's unpredictable sorcery, Stanley's magic and Vorel's physical courage demanded a different kind of trust: she could not direct every companion like a machine. The early journeys made her both an indispensable maker and a friend whose choices affected other people. She still looked toward ancient technology for a way to change her own past, but increasingly had companions with whom she would have to face the consequences.[^hope-eleven-01]

### Arc 02 — Chains and Bargains

The dangers around Slavetown and the mines showed Hope that ingenuity could be turned toward confinement as readily as freedom. She continued to build and improvise under pressure, while the group's encounters with coercive authorities demanded decisions that could not be solved by finding the correct tool. The Soulbroker's compact bound Hope, Nash and Stanley to a hazardous promise; Vorel did not sign. Hope's willingness to accept such a bargain reflected both determination and the danger of treating a powerful offer as a route to repair what had been lost. She helped the companions escape captivity and recognize themselves as a company, but their freedom did not erase the bargain. The question of who deserved protection began to compete with the dream of remaking her own life.[^hope-eleven-02]

### Arc 03 — Road to a Lost Kingdom

On the road toward T'ietkalla, Hope faced the cost of relying on forceful devices. A rocket turned back upon her caused devastating injuries, and she later awoke with a mechanical arm and leg. Rather than cease working, she learned the possibilities and limits of a rebuilt body: a grasping metal hand and built-in weapon were tools, not a return to the body she had lost. The companions' struggle through factions, captivity and threatened lives also demanded decisions about whom to trust. Her new limbs could answer some physical dangers, but could not settle her ambitions or protect everyone she cared about. The engineer who sought to reverse the past was herself being changed, and adaptation became as important as restoration.[^hope-eleven-03]

### Arc 04 — The Crown of T'ietkalla

In T'ietkalla, Hope's practical intelligence became a form of public responsibility. The struggle to free prisoners and challenge the old order placed her among people whose lives would continue after her companions moved on. Nobieri and the assembled tieflings offered her a crown, and she accepted the role of queen. She set about changing infrastructure, sanitation and a harsh social hierarchy, treating a damaged city as more than a collection of machines to repair. Her rule was also marked by severe decisions, including authorized executions and Softhead's mutilation. Hope could oppose exploitation and still employ cruelty, a contradiction her title did not absolve. Recovering the Gem of Tides and helping release Angelashi gave the kingdom possibilities, not proof that its water supply, politics or injustices were permanently resolved.[^hope-eleven-04]

### Arc 05 — Kings and Consequences

Hope left T'ietkalla carrying both its obligations and the consequences of the choices she had made there. At Razak Val, a redirected rocket blast killed leaders and bystanders. She offered an account of her purpose while Omnom accused her of intent; neither account alone established all that she had meant to do. Her losses and her attempt to shelter others tested the idea that a strong ruler could keep people safe by deciding quickly. She remained capable of inventiveness and daring, but saw how a device meant for one purpose could become a disaster in someone else's hands. Sira's abduction drew the companions toward another difficult rescue. Hope's authority did not make her free of accountability, and her practical gifts could not guarantee that the future would follow her plans.[^hope-eleven-05]

### Arc 06 — Beneath the World

The journey through Khaervilon and CHIDS brought Hope among technologies whose scale rivaled her most dangerous hopes. She used equipment and portals to keep moving, while discovering again that powerful mechanisms imposed costs beyond their operators' intentions. The confrontation with the Traversal Drive threatened to destroy Earth. Hope joined Nash, Stanley and Vorel in sharing the crimson beam rather than allowing one companion to bear the catastrophe alone. The act brought an apparently fatal end to their journey, but it also showed a change in what she valued: she chose solidarity over mastery of the device. She was later restored to life, not proved invulnerable. Her allegiance to the group had become something she could act upon even when every tool had failed.[^hope-eleven-06]

### Arc 07 — The City of Doors

Hope's restoration in Sigil returned her with natural arms and legs rather than her old mechanical limbs. The change was a recovery of living flesh, not the secret persistence of her prosthetic weapons. With her friends, she searched for a way back to Earth and encountered powers that offered transformations more dangerous than mechanical repair. At White Tower, the hooded Voice privately urged her to surrender her ordinary right hand and bind the living Hand of the Voice in its place. Hope agreed, gaining abilities while taking on a new dependency she could not fully measure. She pursued the possibility of becoming something greater, but the patron's claims offered no proof of godhood. When sent away, she refused to bring her original companions and walked through the portal alone. Her departure left the fellowship divided, and the others did not know the full private terms of her choice.[^hope-eleven-07]

### Arc 08 — Separate Paths

Away from Nash, Stanley and Vorel, Hope found that the Hand's power did not give her a secure command over her own story. Her return to T'ietkalla and dealings with dangerous allies exposed her again to accusations of causing suffering while trying to prevent it. She helped burn an empty railway station, then fled as another assault threatened the city; whether the Hand's presence caused the attack remained contested. She signed new bargains and sought powers and answers that were not simply granted in exchange. Her title as queen did not guarantee the kingdom's safety or her own moral certainty. The isolation she had chosen changed her relationship to her former friends: reconciliation would require more than crossing back to the same place, and obedience to the Voice remained a decision rather than an inevitable fate.[^hope-eleven-08]

### Arc 09 — The Fall of Elhandar

Hope found her way back to Nash and then to Elhandar, rejoining the companions she had left. Stanley did not immediately trust her, but private acknowledgement and apology began repairing their relationship. She helped establish the Harbor for T'ietkalla's refugees and respected the people's choice to remain there, rather than assuming a queen should decide every family's fate. When the Voice privately directed her to kill Diontalus, she refused. That act did not erase her bargains, but it showed the limits she was willing to place on the patron's commands. Elhandar's fall drove the survivors toward Mars-bound ships; Hope helped direct the evacuation while the original four stayed on Earth. She entered the next stage of the struggle with relationships restored enough for cooperation, though her responsibilities as queen and her ties to the Voice remained unresolved.[^hope-eleven-09]

### Arc 10 — Across the Stars

Hope remained the company's engineer and increasingly its navigator as Fate Six carried them across space. Reuniting with the survivors on Mars was an uncertain hope before the voyage was completed, and the prison at Pentel Weiss tested their ability to act without ordinary magic. Hope discovered that the supernatural Hand could still help there, but repeatedly chose her friends' safety over private directions to pursue more power. When Nash died and survived as a metal heart and eyestalk, she helped bring the company away from the prison. A later encounter with the Principal cost her the living Hand: he severed and took it, and Stanley restored an ordinary biological arm in its place. She had lost the power she once accepted at White Tower without losing her place among the companions. On Mars she received Stanley's enchanted sword *Diplomacy*, while uncertain claims about Nash's origins remained contested rather than binding the group's next choices.[^hope-eleven-10]

### Arc 11 — The Last War

Hope's leadership during the assault on the Solar Mines combined reckless courage with a growing willingness to protect and listen. She fought beside Vorel and the other companions, helped move the unfinished and completed defenses toward their intended purpose, and eventually entered the Mines again. There she discovered the animated, severed head of her father among the imprisoned workers. After speaking tenderly to him, she ended the suffering of that visible remnant. The encounter did not reveal her mother's fate or resolve every question about his death. Hope's own earlier losses did not make this farewell less difficult.

When she met the Principal, Hope proposed a temporary alliance against the Soulbroker rather than immediate battle. She used the Hollow Ring to open a passage to the Weir, then resisted the temptation to perform fresh evil merely to recharge it. During the fight against Worldbreaker, she carried and shielded an invisible Nash so that his banishment could endure. After Earth's renewal and Monsoon Cabbage's witnessed death in sunlight, Hope returned to a different responsibility. She helped the survivors agree to resettle rather than punish whole peoples collectively, traveled with Stanley and Vorel to see Nash resurrected, and went back to the damaged T'ietkalla that awaited rebuilding. Her final crown was made from a different artifact—the mask Stanley entrusted to her—not from the lost Hand of the Voice.[^hope-eleven-11]

## Relationships and abilities

Hope's practical imagination and willingness to intervene remained constant through changes of body, allegiance and office. She was a builder whose inventions could save lives and harm them; a queen capable of both reform and cruelty; and a friend who left her companions but learned to work with them again. Her restored natural limbs were not the mechanical replacements she had used earlier. The living Hand of the Voice, her own arm, the Hollow Ring, her enchanted sword *Diplomacy*, the separate firearms *Fox* and *Scully*, and the final mask-crown belonged to distinct episodes of that history, with different powers and custody. Her relationship with the Voice, the nature of several bargains and the eventual fate of some older equipment remain incompletely understood.[^arc-three][^arc-four][^arc-five]

## Current status

At the end of the played Wastes campaign, Hope is **alive and queen of T'ietkalla**, where her keep survives but the city still needs rebuilding. She welcomes a future including tieflings and other surviving peoples rather than claiming their resettlement has already been completed everywhere. She retains the protective helmet of Monsoon Cabbage, who died in sunlight, and receives the **Mask of Eltanfawr** from Stanley. Hope shapes the mask into a crown and wears it; the transfer offers **future control of Fate Six and its ghost crew after attunement**, not proof that she has already mastered those powers. Her old supernatural Hand has not returned, and her living arm remains distinct from it. She proposes future reunions with her friends, but those gatherings have not yet occurred. Hope's immediate work is a kingdom to rebuild, not a kingdom already restored.[^hope-eleven-end]

## Earlier five-arc introduction

Hope is a tiefling engineer whose instinct is to make broken things useful again. In Dusthill she brings that practical ingenuity to the journeys of Nash, Stanley and Vorel, while hoping that ancient technology might one day change what has already been lost.[^introduction]

**Related people:** [Tieflings and their communities](../races/tieflings.md).

## Development by arc

### Arc One — A mechanic among the ruins

Hope begins as a resourceful builder, shaping old machinery into something her companions can depend on. She puts that ingenuity into Fate 4, the group's modified vehicle and mobile home, as well as into the practical problems of their journeys. Her ambition reaches beyond survival: she looks to the world's remnants for a way to undo a personal tragedy. That hope gives her a reason to keep moving, but it also makes powerful technology and bargains difficult to refuse. As she travels with Nash, Stanley and Vorel, she becomes part of a company whose members answer hardship in very different ways. The Soulbroker's compact binds some of them to a dangerous promise; Hope accepts it alongside Nash and Stanley, while Vorel declines. Her early ingenuity and willingness to risk herself establish both her strength and the danger in her belief that a sufficiently powerful device can repair the past.[^arc-one]

### Arc Two — A queen remakes Tietkalla

The cost of Hope's ambitions becomes physical when a rocket rebounds and leaves her dependent on mechanical limbs. Rather than retreat from the work that defines her, she adapts, builds and continues to act. In Tietkalla she turns that resourcefulness toward a wider struggle: she challenges an entrenched order and, after the old power structure collapses, accepts the crown offered by Nobieri and the assembled tieflings. Her rule brings reforms to sanitation and infrastructure and attempts to loosen the city's harsh social hierarchy. It is not a spotless reign. Hope authorizes executions and the removal of Softhead's tongue, and violence, shortages and rival claims persist. Her choices show a ruler prepared to use power decisively, even when the means sit uneasily beside her opposition to exploitation. The recovery of the Gem of Tides and Angelashi's release offer reasons for hope, but neither proves that Tietkalla's water supply or its political wounds are permanently healed.[^arc-two]

### Arc Three — Responsibility and sacrifice

Hope's growing authority does not protect her from the consequences of her decisions. At Razak Val, a redirected rocket blast kills leaders and bystanders; her account of why she fired it and Omnom's accusation of deliberate intent remain distinct. The loss of Follow and Rorf, and Sira's abduction before she can reach the refuge Hope offered, deepen the cost of her attempts to protect others. Her engineering skill remains a source of agency: she uses a dangerous portal device to escape threats, but its destructive setting and spent rockets underline that every tool carries limits. At CHIDS, the original companions choose to share the crimson Drive beam rather than let it destroy Earth. Their choice saves the world from that immediate destruction at the cost of their lives. Hope is declared dead with them, then restored in Sigil through a mirror rite. She returns alive, but survival does not erase the sacrifice or settle what it means for her obligations to the Weir.[^arc-three]

### Arc Four — The hand and the departure

After her return, Hope seeks a way back to Earth and is drawn into the conflict at White Tower. There, the hooded Voice privately directs her to abandon her right hand and take the living Hand of the Voice. Hope chooses to cut away her own hand and bind the new one to herself, gaining formidable power and a changed body at a serious cost. The Hand is not the separate Fist gauntlet, and the patron removes that relic from her before she leaves. Hope later says she wants to become a god, but neither that wish nor the Voice's claims settle what she will become. The patron sends her away alone. She refuses to bring Nash, Stanley or Vorel with her and walks through the portal, leaving the original company divided. The others witness her departure and changed form, but not the private commands and promises that shaped her choice.[^arc-four]

### Arc Five — A divided path, then a return

Hope's new companions and bargains carry her back to Tietkalla, where she helps set the empty railway station alight and later flees the attack by General Carthax. Omnom blames her for inviting the demons; Hope says the Hand drew them and that she hoped to lead them away. The fire and flight are real, but neither account establishes the whole cause of the invasion. She signs Tobacho's compact and agrees to work with the Principle, yet the Eyes and larger goals she seeks remain out of reach. Her journeys through the planes eventually bring her to Nash and then back to Elhandar, where she reunites with her former companions and founds the Harbor with Tietkalla's refugees. Stanley's distrust does not vanish at once: after Hope acknowledges her choices, their relationship begins to mend through a private apology and conversation. When the Voice later orders her to kill Diontalus, Hope refuses in a private exchange, showing that the Hand's power has not made her simply obedient.

Hope remains a queen to people who recognize her, but she does not treat that title as a claim to decide every refugee's future. She supports the Harborers' choice to stay, then, after Elhandar's fall, directs the surviving community toward the ships for Mars. When an attempt to feed her ring by desecrating bodies fails, she buries and mourns the refugee she disturbed. Hope and her original companions remain on Earth. She shares with them the separate quests and offers she had kept to herself; Stanley's private conversation with the Soulbroker becomes common knowledge only after he tells the group. Her loyalty to her companions has survived the separation, even as her relationship with the Voice and the consequences of her rule remain unsettled.[^arc-five][^family-retrospective]
