# Hope Akmenos

## Performer retrospective before the announced Wastes return (BONUS-2021-11-13)

Kristen says in [post-Delvers Q&A ~29–31min](../../episodes/BONUS-2021-11-13.md) that Hope was originally devised as a **Road Warrior/Mad Max-style serious suffering protagonist**. The actual Wastes accumulated heavy personal loss and responsibility, and Kristen found staying in that register exhausting over such a long campaign. She **INTENDED TO INFUSE MORE LEVITY into Hope** on returning from Delvers, to avoid making her a perpetual “wet blanket.” This is **out-of-game performance intention recorded when the Delvers campaign had just ended**, not proof of a performed personality change in any particular later Wastes episode; later played sources determine whether that happened. [Exact text-comparison report](../../reports/BONUS_2021_11_13_ASR_COMPARISON_2026-10-26.md).

## Introduction

Hope is a tiefling engineer whose instinct is to make broken things useful again. In Dusthill she brings that practical ingenuity to the journeys of Nash, Stanley and Vorel, while hoping that ancient technology might one day change what has already been lost.[^introduction]

## Development by arc

### Arc One — A mechanic among the ruins

Hope begins as a resourceful builder, shaping old machinery into something her companions can depend on. She puts that ingenuity into Fate 4, the group's modified vehicle and mobile home, as well as into the practical problems of their journeys. Her ambition reaches beyond survival: she looks to the world's remnants for a way to undo a personal tragedy. That hope gives her a reason to keep moving, but it also makes powerful technology and bargains difficult to refuse. As she travels with Nash, Stanley and Vorel, she becomes part of a company whose members answer hardship in very different ways. The Soulbroker's compact binds some of them to a dangerous promise; Hope accepts it alongside Nash and Stanley, while Vorel declines. Her early ingenuity and willingness to risk herself establish both her strength and the danger in her belief that a sufficiently powerful device can repair the past.[^arc-one]

### Arc Two — A queen remakes Tietkalla

The cost of Hope's ambitions becomes physical when a rocket rebounds and leaves her dependent on mechanical limbs. Rather than retreat from the work that defines her, she adapts, builds and continues to act. In Tietkalla she turns that resourcefulness toward a wider struggle: she challenges an entrenched order and, after the old power structure collapses, accepts the crown offered by Nobieri and the assembled tieflings. Her rule brings reforms to sanitation and infrastructure and attempts to loosen the city's harsh social hierarchy. It is not a spotless reign. Hope authorizes executions and the removal of Softhead's tongue, and violence, shortages and rival claims persist. Her choices show a ruler prepared to use power decisively, even when the means sit uneasily beside her opposition to exploitation. The recovery of the Gem of Tides and Angelashi's release offer reasons for hope, but neither proves that Tietkalla's water supply or its political wounds are permanently healed.[^arc-two]

### Arc Three — Responsibility and sacrifice

Hope's growing authority does not protect her from the consequences of her decisions. At Razak Val, a redirected rocket blast kills leaders and bystanders; her account of why she fired it and Omnom's accusation of deliberate intent remain distinct. The loss of Follow and Rorf, and Sira's abduction before she can reach the refuge Hope offered, deepen the cost of her attempts to protect others. Her engineering skill remains a source of agency: she uses a dangerous portal device to escape threats, but its destructive setting and spent rockets underline that every tool carries limits. At CHIDS, the original companions choose to share the crimson Drive beam rather than let it destroy Earth. Their choice saves the world from that immediate destruction at the cost of their lives. Hope is declared dead with them, then restored in Sigil through a mirror rite. She returns alive, but survival does not erase the sacrifice or settle what it means for her obligations to the Weir.[^arc-three]

### Arc Four — The hand and the departure

After her return, Hope seeks a way back to Earth and is drawn into the conflict at White Tower. There, the hooded Voice privately directs her to abandon her right hand and take the living Hand of the Voice. Hope chooses to cut away her own hand and bind the new one to herself, gaining formidable power and a changed body at a serious cost. The Hand is not the separate Fist gauntlet, and the patron removes that relic from her before she leaves. Hope later says she wants to become a god, but neither that wish nor the Voice's claims settle what she will become. The patron sends her away alone. She refuses to bring Nash, Stanley or Vorel with her and walks through the portal, leaving the original company divided. The others witness her departure and changed form, but not the private commands and promises that shaped her choice.[^arc-four]

### Arc Five — A divided path, then a return

Hope's new companions and bargains carry her back to Tietkalla, where she helps set the empty railway station alight and later flees the attack by General Carthax. Omnom blames her for inviting the demons; Hope says the Hand drew them and that she hoped to lead them away. The fire and flight are real, but neither account establishes the whole cause of the invasion. She signs Tobacho's compact and agrees to work with the Principle, yet the Eyes and larger goals she seeks remain out of reach. Her journeys through the planes eventually bring her to Nash and then back to Elhandar, where she reunites with her former companions and founds the Harbor with Tietkalla's refugees. Stanley's distrust does not vanish at once: after Hope acknowledges her choices, their relationship begins to mend through a private apology and conversation. When the Voice later orders her to kill Diontalus, Hope refuses in a private exchange, showing that the Hand's power has not made her simply obedient.

Hope remains a queen to people who recognize her, but she does not treat that title as a claim to decide every refugee's future. She supports the Harborers' choice to stay, then, after Elhandar's fall, directs the surviving community toward the ships for Mars. When an attempt to feed her ring by desecrating bodies fails, she buries and mourns the refugee she disturbed. Hope and her original companions remain on Earth. She shares with them the separate quests and offers she had kept to herself; Stanley's private conversation with the Soulbroker becomes common knowledge only after he tells the group. Her loyalty to her companions has survived the separation, even as her relationship with the Voice and the consequences of her rule remain unsettled.[^arc-five][^family-retrospective]

## December 2021 retrospective — Road Warrior design, possible reckoning

In the [December 2021 bonus ~00:27–00:31](../../episodes/BONUS-2021-12.md), Kristen explains that **Hope was inspired by *The Road Warrior***: a damaged, serious and tactically capable wanderer. The experience of playing **Squirts** led Kristen to wonder whether a more comedic main character would have helped her enjoy the campaign differently. She speculates that Hope may face a future **reckoning over what she stands for**, even perhaps a tragic ending, but emphasizes that she **does not know the outcome**. Jon then describes **Hope and Stanley's imperfect parallels**, especially judging each other's bargains for power. These are **player perspectives**, not played new admissions, evidence Hope dies, or a forecast to be backdated into current canon.
