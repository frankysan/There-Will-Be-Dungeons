# Tylander Sullivan — the disputed master of the Black Mine

## Introduction

Tylander Sullivan moved between Slavetown's politics and the Black Mine's darkest passages, claiming injuries inflicted upon him by the Principal's regime while turning suffering people into instruments of revenge. His accounts of property, lost family and persecution were central to his dealings with the companions, but much of his earlier life survived only as testimony, a journal and contested documents.[^slavetown]

## Slavetown and the lost deed

In Slavetown, Tylander confronted the companions through a house filled with mutilated undead and a dangerous ritual basement. He demanded Orp's ruby necklace and displayed a folded parchment that he claimed established rights over the mine. The deed vanished as smoke before anyone could authenticate it. Tylander escaped the basement and manipulated the ensuing confrontation with Orp; after he struck Vorel with a lingering necrotic curse, Hope and Vorel attacked him, and the GM declared him dead. Others claimed he opposed the city's enslavement and the Principal's interests, but such political claims did not erase the violence he had committed.[^slavetown]

The following investigation complicated that apparent ending. A moving severed arm was found amid dead soldiers near his ruined house, and an interested magistrate reported that Tylander had returned and killed more troops. The companions did not themselves witness a resurrection there.[^return]

## The buried ritual

Deep in the Black Mine, the companions recovered a diary identified as Tylander's. Its entries spoke of Mariosa, other lost family members, a voice from below and the revenge it offered. These writings revealed what Tylander wished recorded, not an independently demonstrated account of his past.[^mine]

In an underground chapel, an apparent Tylander described himself as a former mayor of Pristine, betrayed by mine owners and officials, deprived of his family and reborn through a sinister patron. He admitted deliberately making enslaved captives into undead. The figure assumed the appearance of Catherine Speckles, while an injured woman separately identified herself by that name. The companions learned that the seated figure was not straightforwardly solid; deception and impersonation were part of the confrontation.[^mine]

Further within the ritual site, the party fought against magical defenses and a great beholder concealed beneath the platform, while Quizquaz remained a separate figure. The beholder was defeated and Quizquaz summoned a more terrible visitor, but this did not establish a fresh, independently witnessed final death for Tylander.[^ritual]

## Current status

Tylander had been pronounced dead in Slavetown, was subsequently reported active, and appeared in deceptive forms during the Black Mine confrontation. His later personal fate and legal claim to the mine are unresolved. His admissions concerning the undead are part of his legacy; neither the vanished deed nor his self-justifying history establishes rightful ownership or exoneration.[^ritual]
