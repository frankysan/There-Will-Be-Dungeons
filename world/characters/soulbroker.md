## Encounter and compact

After the Black Mine's deep ritual, the adventurers visit the Soulbroker's uncanny château in **E023**. **Hope, Nash and Stanley each sign** a compact involving action against **the Principle**, with a **one-thousand-day/night** interval described; **Vorel refuses**. These are four separate individual choices, not one unanimous contract. Hospitality, waivers and pact consequences must be distinguished from what is actually witnessed. [E022](../../episodes/E022.md) · [E023](../../episodes/E023.md) · [E024](../../episodes/E024.md).

The Weir is described as a place of great supernatural power and as a claimed destination for souls, but the actual contractual enforcement mechanism and its place in the wider cosmology are not independently established. Nobiery/Nobieri's later story of old military bargains is **his testimony**; an actual army returns in E054, but the precise ancient sacrificial terms are not witnessed. [E048](../../episodes/E048.md) · [E054](../../episodes/E054.md).

In E081 **Quizquaz offers interpretations** of the pact and proposes a catastrophic solution involving Earth. The companions **reject letting Earth die**. The consequences of their sacrifice, revival and later adventures should not be simplified into proof the Weir obligation has been discharged. [E081](../../episodes/E081.md) · [Chronology](../CHRONOLOGY.md).

## E128 — private warning and observable planetary breach

At **~01:10–01:28** in the [Arc Five / Season One finale](../../episodes/E128.md), the goat-man Soulbroker (name variously rendered **Kitobol Prith, Katoble Praeth, Catoblepraithe (?)**) appears **to Stanley alone** after Stanley tests Henatar's acquired guitar. He alleges that **Hope obtained divine annulment of her bargain**, **Vorel never signed**, and **Nash's manufactured dragon heart meant the Soulbroker never really possessed his soul**; **Stanley remains liable**. This is **the Soulbroker's statement of legal/metaphysical status**, consistent with some earlier evidence but not an independent soul-record inspection.

He also **claims** the Principle was an Earth human who originally made a deal to resist dragon devastation and protect Earth, then dishonored a confidential price, and now pursues godhood. He says the **Weir** are a hungry people living in a realm between creation and the Far Realm, intent on entering and **using soul/life energy** to sustain their claimed new and just order. His report of killing **Andreeste and other Fey courts**, and the proper name **Weir/Wyr/Weer**, need written/audio corroboration. **A visible event follows, not just rhetoric**: he lifts his hands, the sky **actually tears open in purple**, and a **vast six-limbed gear-scaled burrowing being arrives on Earth** and tunnels underground, triggering a huge sand wave. He calls it the beginning of planetary **conversion**, then disappears. Nash carries the four PCs on black-dragon back **above the storm** and Villa Ornath remains intact. **Creature's origins and effects are not fully known; its arrival is played.** Stanley later **tells the companions** this private story, so they learn it only afterward. His promises of peace/justice are interested claims and do not establish a beneficial transformation. [E128 ASR report](../../reports/E128_ASR_COMPARISON_2026-10-24.md). **Season One ends here; Soulbroker/Principle conflict unresolved**.
