# The Soulbroker

<a id="the-soulbroker--catoblepraithe-"></a>

Beyond the familiar world, the goat-headed lord who called himself the Soulbroker presided over the strange court of Château Weir. He offered sanctuary, knowledge and power, but made every gift part of a bargain. He spoke of souls as possessions and portrayed the Principal as the enemy standing between the Weir and Earth. His titles and offers were plainly heard; the distant history and moral necessity he claimed for them were far less certain.[^arrival]

<a id="encounter-and-compact"></a>

## The first bargain

The Soulbroker first appeared when his winged intermediary Quizquaz opened a rift beneath the Black Mine. He welcomed the exhausted travelers into a city suspended amid darkness, where a great whirlpool seemed to hold struggling souls, and invited them to his court. His courtesy did not make him disinterested: he wanted the adventurers to kill the Principal within a thousand days and nights, with their own souls at risk if they failed. **Hope, Nash and Stanley each accepted. Vorel refused**, choosing his own strength over the promised boon. The three signatories received the agreed advantage; the whole party did not enter one collective pact.[^compact]

The Soulbroker subsequently supplied leads about weapons, passageways and distant places, while probing Nash's extraordinary nature. Nash's dragon transformation was witnessed, but the Soulbroker's accounts of other people's histories and the ultimate fate of the promised souls remained claims from a powerful interested host.[^compact]

## His claim upon the living

The Weir appeared to reach beyond those three signatures. An old T'ietkallan elder said he had sacrificed newborns to shelter an army there; the travelers saw him enter Château Weir, and the soldiers later returned, but the ancient price was not independently witnessed. Quizquaz, meanwhile, offered a brutal interpretation of the compact: destroy Earth to destroy the Principal, and escape with the heroes' souls. The companions rejected sacrificing their world. Neither offer established a general law of souls or proved that the compact could be enforced exactly as its messengers threatened.[^army][^choice]

## The bargain behind a threatened world

An imprisoned black dragon described an older bargain between human colonists and the Soulbroker, but that story was testimony about a past the adventurers had not seen. Later, the travelers found that the pact's thousand-day objective remained on their quest list, while only Stanley bore a comparable visible mark. The precise time left was uncertain; the absence of a mark did not itself prove every obligation ended.[^dragon][^clock]

After Elhandar's fall, the Soulbroker spoke privately to Stanley. He alleged that Hope's obligation had been annulled, Nash's engineered heart had kept his soul beyond the claim, and Stanley alone remained liable. He called the Weir a people hungry for life and told a self-serving history of the Principal's past bargain, betrayal and ambition. Those were **his assertions**, not an independently inspected contract or proof of his supposed benevolence. But the danger did not remain a story: a purple rupture opened in Earth's sky and an immense scaled, gear-like burrower descended into the ground. Stanley only afterward shared what he had been told with his companions.[^breach]

## A claim broken, a claim repeated

Monsoon Cabbage later offered to remove the curse binding Stanley to the Weir. The companions prepared the rite; a hostile entity emerged from Stanley's wounded back, and Hope killed it. **The GM confirmed that Stanley's last soul obligation to the Weir was removed, while the benefits of his original bargain remained.** This was an accomplished exorcism, not merely Monsoon's promise, and it did not kill the Soulbroker.[^exorcism]

Yet when the heroes entered Château Weir again, Quizquaz accused the three original signatories of breaking their bargain and still threatened them. His demand was a character's claim, **not a demonstrated reversal of Stanley's completed exorcism**. Vorel's refusal was explicitly acknowledged. The degree to which Hope and Nash remained individually bound after their own earlier changes was never settled by this confrontation.[^meeting][^exorcism]

## The storm at Château Weir

The four companions returned with the Principal, their former enemy, under a temporary agreement against the Weir. The Soulbroker appeared before all five in his robed goatlike form. He demanded the Principal's death and said he had planted a Worldbreaker seed within Earth to turn the planet into a gateway for his people. Provoked by Hope and Stanley, he **claimed** he could withdraw it but refused; no such removal was witnessed. When negotiation collapsed, he grew to an impossible height and tore the château into a lightning-filled cyclone.[^meeting]

Within the storm the Soulbroker attacked the five. Lightning interacting with Nash's extraordinary body multiplied him into thousands of short-lived copies. The GM allowed **3,280 Nashes to unleash eighth-level fireballs**, adjudicating **58,950 fire damage**; the Soulbroker's body was destroyed, and the GM explicitly celebrated the slaying of a deity. The Weir's connection to Earth was then declared severed. The planted Worldbreaker was **not** thereby removed, and Quizquaz's death was not witnessed in this battle.[^defeat]

## Current status

**The Soulbroker was slain in the witnessed Château Weir battle**, and no later played appearance restores him. His stated ambitions for Earth failed, although the Worldbreaker he had introduced remained dangerous after his death. Nash finally banished that creature from Earth, and the Triangulosphere later restored the planet. The broken Weir connection, the unresolved nature of many of the Soulbroker's stories, and Quizquaz's separate fate should not be confused with a demonstrated extinction of every being who had served the Weir.[^aftermath][^identities]
