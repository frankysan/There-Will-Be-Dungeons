## Fall of Elhandar — E125–E128

The inhabited forest station of [Elhandar](places/elhandar.md) became a battlefield in [E125](../episodes/E125.md). An attacking castle and a large fleet bombarded the orbital sanctuary while armed mercenaries appeared inside its central audience chambers. High Elf Amanthalodar and the defenders fought back, but the station began an uncontrolled descent toward Earth. The companions, Lyrielle, Angelashi, her four children, Bok Bok and Chunks fled through failing transport corridors and across the damaged biodome to the concealed upper rooms of **[Villa Or'Nath](places/villa-ornath.md)**. They fastened themselves into magically emerging restraints. **E125 ends at the sound of an impact and a fade to black:** neither their survival nor the full station casualty count is yet shown.

[E126](../episodes/E126.md) establishes that the principal household **survived the first impact**. The villa remains a protected pocket among broken station machinery and displaced Harbor tieflings; Vorel discovers Earth desert and station wreckage beyond the cottage. The attackers' falling castle subsequently strikes the site again. **Several tieflings die in this second collision**, while the four children and Chunks are protected and survivors receive limited healing. The number of dead, the fate of every elf elsewhere in the station and the condition of the entire forest are not independently enumerated. The station is lost as a functioning orbital refuge, but this destruction must not be described as the extinction of the Elhandari.

In [E128](../episodes/E128.md), **Amanthalodar and Admiral Chrysorius are alive** and Elhandari ships arrive to search the wreckage. The elves **actually recover the natural metal heart** of the black dragon, previously concealed in the mountain; it is **not Nash's separately implanted engineered heart**. Hope directs surviving Harbor tieflings to join the evacuation; Angelashi, all four children, Lyrielle and Bok Bok also **board**. The ships **depart toward the Mars terraforming settlement with these refugees and the recovered heart**, while Stanley, Nash, Hope and Vorel stay by Villa Or'Nath on Earth. A manifest of all original station residents, exact death toll and immediate on-screen arrival on Mars are not provided at E128. A spoken Squirts-versus-Chunks roster inconsistency must not be silently resolved by changing the passengers' identities.

The later episodes distinguish **departure from confirmed continuation**. [S2E01](../episodes/S2E01.md) identifies Nash's family and allies as living under Elhandari protection on Mars; in [S2E02](../episodes/S2E02.md) the companions **physically arrive** at the local green terraforming zone, meet Amanthalodar and Chrysorius, and reunite with Nash's family. In [S2E03](../episodes/S2E03.md) Stanley's Prime election marks a new Elhandari leadership arrangement. The [S3E18 finale](../episodes/S3E18.md) still shows active Elhandari forces assisting Earth's restoration and then divergent choices: Stanley crosses into the Feywild with many Elhandari, while others remain on Mars or return to Earth. **Their community survives in several places; the destroyed station is not shown rebuilt.** See the [Elves and Elhandari people history](races/elves.md) for their separate social continuity.

**Evidence boundary:** These are conclusions from cited played-episode dossiers and processed ASR, **not independently listened original audio**. The High Elf's ancient ecological teachings and the attacker's accusations are attributed claims, not corroborated causes of the station's fall; later survival does not erase the uncertainty or casualties at the E125–E126 cutoffs.

## Worldbreaker's arrival and Earth-only banishment — E128 to S3E17

The invasion began as a **directly witnessed breach**, not a proven retelling of the Weir's ancient history. In [E128](../episodes/E128.md), the Soulbroker appeared privately to Stanley, gave his own account of Weir ambitions and the Principal's former defenses, then **opened a vast purple tear in the sky**. A colossal, six-limbed, gear-scaled burrowing creature dropped into Earth's desert and disappeared beneath the ground. Its arrival caused a massive sandstorm, from which Nash carried the other companions to safety in dragon form. Stanley later told the party what the Soulbroker had said. The creature was **neither captured nor destroyed**, and none of the Soulbroker's larger cosmological explanations became independently verified just because the sky-rift and burrower were visible. The later sessions identify this creature as **Worldbreaker / this campaign's Tarrasque**; it was not conclusively identified by a physical autopsy at the E128 cutoff.

Killing the Soulbroker's manifest body in [S3E12](../episodes/S3E12.md) **does not remove Worldbreaker**. The GM rules the Weir–Earth connection severed, while the already placed threat remains on Earth. In [S3E14](../episodes/S3E14.md), the GM describes the original planted “egg” as an enormous energy/seed rather than a previously observed shell, and the Principal supplies a Tarrasque identification and a partly attributed account of its extraplanar origin. The body only begins to emerge from lava before that episode ends. The GM's later adjudication treats this *particular* Tarrasque as **not native to the Prime Material Plane**, relevant to the eventual banishment; ordinary monster-lore assumptions are not a substitute for the campaign ruling.

In [S3E15](../episodes/S3E15.md), the Principal **agitates the lava and the mountain-sized creature fully emerges**. The heroes fight and lure it toward Vorel's prepared canyon. Two Nash *Fireball* castings are adjudicated as dealing **31 and 32 fire damage**. In [S3E16](../episodes/S3E16.md), Vorel **actually detonates** the canyon charges, and falling rock briefly slows the monster; the GM rules the explosives' fire ineffective. The contradictory fire rulings remain a **source/mechanics review question**, not something to quietly harmonize. The allied lizardfolk inflict a brief entanglement but suffer severe casualties, including five directly killed and further fighters destroyed by the redirected force burst. King Bokbok escapes. The Principal attempts to dominate the creature, then **attacks Nash** when Nash moves to banish it, revealing his desire to keep it available for his own plan.

Stanley **actually transforms Worldbreaker into a snail** with *Polymorph*. Nash's first attempted *Banishment* of the original creature, then one attempted on the snail, **fail**; his next, fifth-level attempt on the snail **succeeds** near the end of S3E16. The creature leaves Earth's plane, but **the one-minute concentration is not yet complete**, so it is not permanently gone at that cutoff. The Principal's moon-based chrome copies subsequently arrive while the spell remains vulnerable.

In [S3E17](../episodes/S3E17.md), the party protects Nash and his ongoing ***Banishment*** through the remaining rounds. Stanley later changes his own concentration from *Polymorph* to *Greater Invisibility*; the creature may therefore resume its original form **elsewhere**, but there is **no witnessed return to Earth**. Hope evacuates invisible Nash upward on a working windboard, and Nash does not break his spell. The GM explicitly declares **the full ten rounds complete** and adjudicates Worldbreaker **permanently banished from Earth**. The twelve additional chrome Principal bodies collapse immediately afterward. The inconsistent S3E16 “eight rounds left” versus S3E17 “six rounds left” bookkeeping remains a documented source question; the **completed GM ruling itself** is unambiguous in the processed episode account.

**Outcome boundary:** “Permanently banished” here means **permanent removal from Earth under the GM's ruling**, not demonstrated destruction of the creature, every Weir influence in existence or an independently charted destination plane. Earth is **still barren and the Rubistic Triangulosphere unactivated at S3E17's end**. The subsequent [S3E18](../episodes/S3E18.md) restoration—actual rain, plants and waterfalls, with loss of ordinary Earthbound mana-wave spellcasting—depends on the heroes' **separate authorization of the Ruby**, not on *Banishment* itself. Later warnings and the unidentified second Nash are **not proof the same creature returned**. All cited claims are bounded to the maintained played-episode/processed-ASR dossiers; **no original audio or GM rules handout was independently verified**. See [the antagonist index](ANTAGONISTS.md) and [consistency ledger](CONSISTENCY_LEDGER.md) for the surviving identity, fire and concentration uncertainties.

## Earth's restoration and spellcasting boundary — S3E18

Worldbreaker's absence after [S3E17's completed *Banishment*](#worldbreakers-arrival-and-earth-only-banishment--e128-to-s3e17) makes the final restoration possible; **the banishment itself does not heal Earth**. At the opening of the [S3E18 finale](../episodes/S3E18.md), the Elhandari under Amanthalodar still guard the **completed, unactivated [Rubistic Triangulosphere (“Ruby”)](technology/rubistic-triangulosphere.md)** at the Solar Mines core. The heroes discuss how using it will end Earth's existing mana-wave magic. Stanley does **not** decide unilaterally: Nash and Vorel assent, and Hope is included because the world's future also affects her people. **Stanley gives the order to activate the device; Amanthalodar acknowledges it**. The close-up physical insertion into the core is **not separately depicted**, but the immediately following GM narration establishes that the machine's world-scale effect **actually happens**, rather than remaining an unfulfilled plan.

Colored spectral dragons fly over Earth and its devastated ground **visibly greens**: rain falls, grasses and forests flourish, flowers bloom and waterfalls run again. The GM describes the planet as restored and its **mana-wave arcane field suppressed**, not as a mere future prediction. Monsoon Cabbage chooses to remove his protective helmet and **dies in the sunlight** during this transition; Hope retrieves the helmet. His death is a separately witnessed act, not proof that the restoration killed all innately magical creatures.

The effects are **place-specific and demonstrated in later scenes**. Still on Earth after the restoration, Nash attempts ***Fire Bolt* and *Shield*** without producing spells; on the **Moon**, his attempted ***Light* and *Acid Splash*** also fail. Nevertheless, his **innate floating** persists, so the GM's broad “magic gone” language must **not** be universalized into the elimination of all supernatural traits or every item. After the group travels beyond the local null field to **Planet Nash**, Stanley **feels access to spellcasting return and successfully casts *Wish*** to perform Nash's *True Resurrection*. The Moon examples support a **near-Earth extension** of the effect, not a proven universal radius or a complete map of affected planes and power sources.

**Outcome and limits:** Earth's landscape is genuinely renewed, but no scene guarantees that every settlement, individual, hidden infernal passage or future threat is healed or permanently harmless. The last [apparent second-Nash warning](../episodes/S3E18.md) remains unidentified, and is not evidence of Worldbreaker's return. The first restored-Earth council proposes reconstruction, resettlement and the Clench mission; those are **future work, not completed postwar conditions**. These observations come from the maintained episode dossier and processed ASR, **not newly auditioned original audio or a laboratory test of the magical field**.
