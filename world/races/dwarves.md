# Dwarves — ruined halls, mines and wandering communities

Dwarves appear throughout the Wastes in places separated by great distances and different histories. The companions encounter the remains of an underground settlement, the guards of a brutal mine, and the residents of a fading mining town far from Earth. They also meet dwarf craftsmen and ship crews. Together these encounters suggest a people with many lives and loyalties, not one kingdom or a single set of customs.[^scope]

## The ancient underground settlement

Near [Dusthill](../places/dusthill.md), a passage beyond a damaged water system leads the travelers into an old dwarven complex. Its skeletal inhabitants continue familiar routines among rooms and corridors, seemingly indifferent to visitors. A medical automaton, **C4N-D13**, still operates there, preserving a link with a community that has otherwise been lost. The party eventually finds **Queen Amelda** in a throne chamber, connected to a black, oil-like being that calls itself **Quizquaz**.[^ruins]

Quizquaz tells a story about the settlement's downfall and his bargain with its queen, but he has every reason to shape that account for his listeners. When the connection is broken and he is defeated, the unnatural life of the ruin is disrupted. The visitors do not witness how the settlement once governed itself in ordinary times. **Moag Ferhain** is a recorded regional name, but its exact identification with these underground halls remains uncertain.[^queen]

## Black Mine and the Oilbeards

The **[Black Mine](../places/black-mine.md)** is controlled at its upper entrance by armed dwarves of the **Oilbeard** name. They maintain machinery and guard the route to deeper workings. Their spokesman, **Scrolly Oilbeard**, claims an uprising has trapped people below and that the guards have blocked the great lift. Their account of the revolt, its numbers and its causes is the interested word of those who control the entrance.[^oilbeards]

Their treatment of a captive human called **Tickles**, kept in degrading confinement, is not merely a disputed story. It is cruelty the companions encounter. The later struggle with guards and rescue of prisoners exposes the mine's violence without making its rulers the representatives of all dwarven society. Other dwarves elsewhere are not shown to recognize the Oilbeards' authority or approve of their methods.[^captives]

## Burp, the bank and the trade routes

On the asteroid called **Blast Belly Rock**, the travelers reach **Burp**, a mining settlement whose fortunes have declined. Its old mithril wealth is remembered at the **Wet Whistle**, where the dwarf innkeeper **Ailboon Hardbottom** receives the company with suspicion before growing friendlier. An older story names a founder, **Lord Romanth Blast Belly**, but that history comes from the innkeeper's recollection rather than a witnessed founding.[^burp]

The deserted **Bank of Burp** still contains offices, contracts, a vault and security devices. Among its surviving papers is a signed record that the elven engineer **Tungsten Wheel** once worked there. The record corroborates a particular employment history, not a continued living town economy or proof of his current whereabouts. Marauders later capture surviving residents, including the innkeeper; the companions free them. When Fate Six returns much later, the dwarves have departed, with no definite destination shown.[^bank]

Dwarven lives are not confined to these mines. A weaponsmith provides magical equipment during the companions' travels, while a dwarven trade frigate at Sigil's docks refuses passage to Hope's separate company. Its crew defends itself when the visitors attack. The violence does not make that ship an enemy of every dwarf, and the vessel is not captured. Craft and trade are encountered activities, not qualities every dwarf must possess.[^trade]

## Current status

The ancient settlement's earlier life remains obscure; the Oilbeards' control of the Black Mine is not established as an enduring dwarven order after the companions leave; and Burp's residents have departed by the last recorded visit. No united dwarf realm, common leadership or final settlement for the wider people is shown. Their different communities remain connected chiefly by the travelers' encounters, not by a proven shared political history.[^status]
