# Lizardfolk — clans, oaths and the Great Fang

The lizardfolk of the Wastes appear in more than one community. Vorel travels far from his own people, but encounters with priestesses, desert hunters and the clans of Razak Val reveal societies whose customs and loyalties do not always agree. Their histories are not interchangeable.[^early]

## Hunters and the Golden Land

While separated from his companions, Vorel follows a lizardfolk summons and meets a priestess who knows his name and speaks of Fuxi, a Father of Fire. Her account concerns an ancient tribe and a divine captivity that she believes to be real; the encounter does not establish those claims as the history of every lizardfolk community.

Later, hunters from the Golden Land approach the travelers with raptor-like mounts. Their champion Diptak challenges Vorel over an alleged promise involving the priestess. After a fight, Diptak yields and pledges his clan's allegiance. The pledge is spoken and accepted, but no marriage is performed, and allegiance from that hunting party cannot stand for consent from all the tribes.[^golden]

## Razak Val and the Great Fang

The mountain-ringed settlement of [Razak Val](../places/razak-val.md) is home to powerful clans, advisers, hunters and dragon-priestly traditions. The **Great Fang** is a public office won or defended by **scaling**, a ritualized combat challenge. The Fengar household sustains political influence even while the reigning Great Fang, the cyclops Orp, holds power. In local tradition, the Council of Twelve and the shrine known as the Tailway connect leadership with dragon figures. The truth of those divine accounts is not settled by the ceremonies themselves.[^razak]

Orp's rule depends on captives brought to him as prey. He kills Diptak after the latter confronts him; later, Vorel challenges and kills Orp in a fight that devastates the ruling court when Hope's rocket strikes the gathered people. Omnom and the crowd recognize Vorel as Great Fang. A further challenge from Zercil ends in her death, with outside intervention complicating the honor of the succession. Vorel rejects the practice of feeding people to the ruler, calls for captives to be freed and initiates screening for a parasitic threat. Those orders do not prove that every captive was released or that all the clans accepted a lasting reform.[^succession]

There are competing accounts even of Razak Val's beginnings. Priestly tradition names **Razak the Bronze** as its first Great Fang. Much later, a being calling itself **Groxl** tells Vorel that it founded and united the clans. Neither claim settles the other; Groxl's account of artificial origins and divine wars remains testimony, not demonstrated shared ancestry.[^origins]

## Current status

After the struggle for Earth's future, Vorel stands among a surviving contingent of roughly fifty to sixty lizardfolk. He proposes returning to Razak Val to rebuild and welcomes surviving Kuromokishi into the resettlement effort. The group begins its overland departure, but its arrival and the reconstruction of Razak Val are not witnessed. Other lizardfolk settlements and the wider people's fate remain outside that final scene.[^final]
