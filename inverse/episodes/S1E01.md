The narrated **human world**, its **Anathema** and the **barrier** are elements of *Inverse's own setting*. ## Opening world premise — narrated setting, not a Wastes origin story

After out-of-character introductions, **GM Jon Jagger** opens with an ancient account. Around **100,000 years before the adventure**, a human mage whose name the ASR renders **Cathos Knull** attempted to purge magic from the human world. He created a plague named **the Anathema**, which grew uncontrollably and attacked things touched by magic: plants, animals, machines and entire peoples. The **Eventide Assembly**, composed of powerful mages and thinkers, **summoned or established the world called Inverse** as a sanctuary **out of phase with the human world**, protected by a **barrier**. Human societies on the other side subsequently declined without magic and its supporting institutions.

The Assembly also built **waystations** to maintain the barrier, then disappeared. The stations sometimes require repairs or renewed energy. The Anathema has **not previously crossed into Inverse**, according to the GM's introduction, but smaller barrier irregularities called **flux** sometimes appear. On a continent sounding like **Oferivia** or **Ferivia**—spelling to verify—the **southwestern Badlands** contain a waystation that is failing unusually quickly. A previous engineer sent to investigate has vanished.

This opening is **GM framing for Inverse**, stronger than a later NPC's theory about the waystation. It still does not supply a creator-verified orthography, a full world map or a demonstration that every historical detail was recorded verbatim by the ASR.

## Played story — what actually happens

### The assignment and the ambush

A new company is moving toward the frontier settlement **Common's Rest** aboard a **Foundation Tech train**. A dwarf businessman/engineer **Grizz de Wells** hired **Baffo, Garpoon and Lealta** to escort **Hemshaw**. The waystation reportedly failed about **five months earlier**, was repaired by **Grizz**, then malfunctioned again much sooner than normal. A later engineer (name sounding like **Dio/De Dio**) has **gone missing**; no body is found or confirmed in this episode.

Hemshaw tries to assemble a strategy meeting. Baffo would rather sleep and find pastries; Lealta earnestly misunderstands what a tabaxi is; Garpoon insults the detective while fussing over his pet rat. **Baffo is recognizable from fictionalized mystery books**, to Lealta's delight. The group has a mission but does not yet trust or understand one another.

Three **masked people in dark clothes** break into the rear train carriage and announce a robbery. Their apparent leader focuses particularly on **Hemshaw**, leading Baffo to suspect an attack on the engineer rather than an ordinary theft. The party fights. **Garpoon kills two attackers with axes**, Lealta uses divine light and later healing, and Baffo fights at close range with cat claws and his convertible musical weapon. A third attacker, identified as **Gordo** in the ASR, **jumps off the moving train and lies motionless** as it recedes. **His death is not witnessed or certified**. The two recovered bodies are **humans with official-looking wrist brands/glyphs**, so the incident does not establish that the unbranded colony attacked the train. The party did **not capture a living bandit to question**; Hemshaw's theory that these same people abducted the missing engineer remains conjecture.

### Common's Rest and an unauthorized human guide

The train reaches a small, rough, resource-poor Badlands town. **Elder Corala** says the previous waystation repair was only **five months ago** and another engineer subsequently disappeared; she cannot explain why it has already failed again. The elder mentions a local druid called **Spores** who knows the area. A strange cat that has watched the travelers transforms in her office into **Kyle's unbranded human druid**. He lives near a **human colony** and offers to guide them. Lealta and the others learn that **unbranded humans face legal punishment and exclusion**, but Common's Rest tolerates some people trying to survive on the frontier.

While Hemshaw prepares equipment in town, the party visits a sparse inn run by **Madeleine Shinetop/Shinetop** (name provisional), serving mainly **cabbage soup**, milk and beer. Baffo seeks finer food and secretly uses **Silver Sleep** in the outhouse, dropping liquid-like medication at the corners of his eyes and experiencing muffled sound and distance. Spores drinks milk in cat form, and the group learns more about one another. The inn is color and character development, **not proof of a connection to Dusthill, Slavetown or any Wastes tavern**.

Lealta receives a discreet invitation from a sharply dressed elf woman to meet outside the nearby human settlement. As the group travels southwest out of Common's Rest they pass a **part-built, unusually large building** beside a patch of green grove. They have **not yet reached the waystation or repaired it**.

### Land, suspicion and persecution

The elf, **Zia Rosewood**, insists that the **human community** has deliberately disrupted the station. Her argument is that the **Anathema would kill nonhumans but spare humans**, so humans have a natural incentive to destroy the barrier. Baffo presses for evidence and gets none: she does not know **which of roughly twelve or thirteen** residents supposedly interfered, nor how. She instead reveals plans to **buy inexpensive land for privacy** during the region's expansion and expresses open contempt for **unbranded humans already living there**. When asked why those settlers should not be compensated, she says **they have no claim**, because many live there illegally.

Baffo's custom **Inspector's Insight** suggests she genuinely believes her own accusations but cares much more about evicting the humans than finding corroboration. The party does **not establish that she sabotaged the station herself either**. Her proposed culprit, her material incentives and the real mechanical failure are **three different questions**.

As Zia leaves, **violet electrical cracks** appear in midair and release sudden **jets of water**. Small passages open and close, soaking the investigators. These are **directly observed flux anomalies**, independent of Zia's accusations. Another authority figure, **a genasi woman with armed orc escorts**, arrives to seize **Spores**. She alleges he attacked her during a previous dispute and is breaking the law by living unbranded. The party resists the arrest. Negotiation and intimidation fail to stop a **second fight**; Spores produces fog and spores, Lealta casts **Bless** and Sacred Flame, Garpoon uses axes, and Baffo fires from cover. The exact legal powers of the genasi woman remain uncertain.

### The flux catastrophe and the cliffhanger

As the fight continues, the air tears open again—this time on a huge scale. **Salt-smelling water with living fish floods the grove**, and a **large metal ship** forces partly through the rift. The breach **snaps shut across the ship**, cutting it; the enormous front section falls, **crushing several attacking orcs**. A new shallow pool or lake forms where dry earth had been. Survivors **fall or leap from the ship**, some injured, some perhaps dead.

The survivors are **humans wearing unfamiliar clothing**, including denim trousers, and some hold small rectangular devices that flash as they record what they see. They recoil in terror from Baffo and the other unusual people trying to help. **Baffo immediately abandons the fight to rescue crash victims**, calling on Lealta for healing; **Spores disperses Fog Cloud** and drives away a surviving attacker with fleas. **Garpoon catches another fleeing orc, Grit, and holds his head underwater until he stops moving**. The genasi woman watches the wreckage in shock; **her arrest effort is not resolved by any formal decision**.

The GM **identifies the wreckage and people as crossing the barrier from the human world associated with the Anathema**, the world Inverse's inhabitants believed devastated and sealed away. The survivors seem unfamiliar with Inverse's sapient nonhumans. **This is an actual cross-barrier arrival inside Inverse's story**, *not* evidence of a portal from Beau's *Wastes* or a shared campaign chronology. The flood/ship mechanism and condition of the displaced human world beyond this group remain to be investigated.

The episode **ends with the rescue efforts under way**, **the waystation still unrepaired**, its cause still unknown, the missing engineer not found, the human colonists not cleared or convicted, and the authority's pursuit of Spores unresolved. The host announces a **second part the following week**, but **Inverse S1E02 has not been examined in this dossier**.
