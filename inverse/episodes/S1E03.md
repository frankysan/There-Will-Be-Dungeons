**Continuity rule:** *Inverse* is an independent fictional reality from *The Wastes*. The GM describes a barrier between **Inverse's world, Erivia** (spoken/ASR spelling provisional), and its **human world, Earth**. This is **Inverse's own Earth** in the narrated cosmology and does **not establish a link to the Earth, CHIDS, Scorching, Soulbroker or other history in The Wastes**. Cast overlap and references to the other campaign are not diegetic proof of shared continuity.

## Episode scope and the two-month jump

The host explains that **Kyle and Kristen are absent this recording because of the birth of their child**, so this session follows only the players **Beau (Inspector Baffo Saltz)** and **Scott (Garpoon)** with **Jon Jagger as GM**. **Spores Meier and Lyalta Godfrey remain active characters in the mission but their separate assignment is not acted out in this episode**. The host calls the setting an “alternate universe story,” but does not declare a crossover.

Jon sets the scene **two months after the Common's Rest waystation rescue**. The protagonists' subsequent investigation, coordinated with **Grizz de Wells**, has led to the **Bound**, a cult of followers of **Alter/Altar**. They **revere the barrier** yet seek to **destroy it to free what they regard as the true rulers of their world**—a paradox reflecting **the cult's belief**, not proof their theology is correct. This resolves the earlier episode's uncertainty over the **name of Cindra Rosewood's movement**, without validating its claimed revelation about the Anathema.

The party suspects the Bound will target two objects associated with long-dead **warlock Mondoro Sackt** (surname rendered variously `Sackt`, `Sacked`, `Sack` by ASR):

- **`The Creator's Bound`**, a book attributed to Mondoro. **Lyalta and Spores go to an auction** hoping to prevent its sale or theft. Their success or failure **is not narrated here**.
- **A cipher/code** capable of translating Mondoro's writings. **Baffo and Garpoon travel several days to the Mines Monastery**, an enormous ancient library believed to hold relevant **personal journals**. Their objective is to **obtain or copy the cipher**, not simply take any old book with Mondoro's name.

The two artifacts, the warlock's name, the cult's desired use of them, and the stakes of the barrier require further evidence. **No cipher is actually retrieved in this installment.**

## Played story

### 1. A library protected by stone and bureaucracy

Baffo and Garpoon arrive after several days on horseback, having seen **distant campfires each night**. They do not know whether those people are pilgrims, scholars or enemies following them. The monastery entrance is carved into the sandstone-like face of a mountain: **two monumental twelve-foot stone slabs**, with a smaller **metal door in one side**. It is remote but **not abandoned**, and no town, train or waystation directly serves it.

A small doorman called **Murgit** speaks through a slot in the metal door, insisting they return **in an hour** when the library opens. **Garpoon threatens violence**. They remember Grizz's instruction that the monastery expects **an exchange of knowledge in return for access**. Baffo has brought a **book by Chando Walden/Walding**, fictionalizing one of his detective cases, to serve as payment, but Murgit will not open early for that reason. Baffo indulges suspicions that the doorman could be an impostor; his eventual **Insight indicates Murgit is frightened and truthful**, not a gnoll bandit who seized the library.

With Baffo's permission **Garpoon rams the metal door**, bending it and dislodging the sliding hatch. **Murgit opens rather than endure more damage**, then hides terrified in a little alcove with **a hammock, meal, cup of ale and cards**. The visitors have gained entry **through coercion, not a mutually agreed early opening**. This behavior has consequences throughout the recording.

### 2. Previs, Fulna and the dispute over proper research

Inside, receptionist **Previs Nocturo** tells them to register, **avoid open flames**, **remain with a scholar guide**, and provide **new knowledge in exchange**. Baffo offers a Chando book—about a mystery called **“The Dog That Wasn't Real”** in the provisional ASR. Previs regards it as possibly fictional, but takes it and summons a guide. Garpoon's threats intensify the hostility.

After roughly **fifteen minutes**, the guide **Fulna**, a younger white tabaxi, arrives and supplies Garpoon with a **small magical light** because the stacks are deliberately dim to preserve old books. Baffo insists that someone senior should personally find the cipher for him. Fulna says journals can be catalogued in multiple sections depending on content: **history, arcana/mysticism, spells/sciences**, and a protected **Dark Archive** for dangerous writings and objects. She does not know whether the cipher is in any one of them. GM **Insight establishes her sincere willingness to help**, despite Baffo's disdain.

The GM clarifies the research problem: **Mondoro Sackt wrote many books in an unknown language, and a journal is believed to contain their decoding key**. This is a **search for a reference or cipher within personal records**, whereas Lyalta and Spores are supposed to intercept **the Creator's Bound** at the auction. Baffo repeatedly demands a higher-ranked scholar and proposes using Garpoon's strength to force cooperation. **Garpoon eventually takes Fulna's side**, saying she is doing her job and that violence against her is inappropriate. After the argument **Fulna flees**, leaving the two without the permitted guide.

### 3. Murgit is dragged where he is forbidden

Unable to find the senior scholar, Baffo and Garpoon return to **Murgit** and falsely suggest **a promotion or authorized escort into the stacks** to convince the gnoll to lead them to **Jadar**. Murgit protests that **he is only a doorman, has always been forbidden in the stacks, and wants to return to his post**. Baffo offers **ten gold, initially handing over five**, and argues that Garpoon is his official bodyguard. The gnoll reluctantly follows them past reception into a vast, silent, dark, cool chamber.

The acoustics suppress the echo of even Garpoon's loud shouts for Jadar. Murgit knows of a very large protector named **Terek**, but does not know where Jadar is. Baffo and Garpoon alternate **flattery, threatened violence and attempted bribery**, and Murgit tries to return the gold to escape their demands. When the pair briefly turn away to confer about how to make him shout, **Murgit slips away**. The episode does not show Murgit's later punishment or lasting harm; it does show his acute fear and that **the protagonists breached library rules rather than exposing a confirmed hostile takeover**.

### 4. The forbidden flame draws the wrong kind of attention

Frustrated that the library's own people have not quickly located Jadar, **Baffo tells Garpoon to set a random book on fire**. Garpoon uses a tinderbox and holds it up like a flaming torch, shouting for assistance and threatening to ignite more volumes. Scholars and visitors panic and stream toward the exits. **Garpoon throws the burning book down**, where embers scatter onto other papers and books; for a moment, actual fire damage threatens the collection.

A huge, tattooed **Goliath called Terek** approaches, **puts out the flames** and refuses to indulge their demands. He says even a single burned book is lost knowledge and that the visitors **do not deserve aid**. No great inferno is established after his intervention. **Jeadar** (spelled **J-E-A-D-A-R** in Previs's on-screen scribble at ~01:07:17) then comes with **Fulna, Previs, a human crossbowman and a gnome guard**. He explicitly **bans the two visitors from the monastery** because they have broken its rules. Baffo again demands the cipher in the name of saving the world; the scholars defend their right not to serve threatening, book-burning outsiders.

The standoff becomes an **actual fight initiated after the heroes refuse to withdraw**, not proof that the monastery belongs to the Bound. Baffo instructs Garpoon to **fight nonlethally**; Jadar attempts a **Command** spell, Previs and a human shoot crossbows, Fulna attacks with claws, and the gnome strikes with daggers. **Baffo uses Insightful Fighting, claws and stealth**, while Garpoon deals substantial damage with **two hand axes and a Battle Master sweeping attack**. The rules conversation corrects Garpoon's AC: **he cannot simultaneously use a shield and two equipped hand axes**, leaving **AC 16 in this fight**, not his shield-inclusive value.

**Jeadar's Inflict Wounds deals 21 necrotic damage and knocks Garpoon unconscious**. A frightened and injured **Baffo flees deeper into the unlit stacks**, explicitly choosing to continue looking for the mission's book rather than remaining by Garpoon. This is the played action; it should not be generalized into a permanent abandonment or a confirmed end of their friendship. Garpoon rolls **a natural 20 among subsequent death saves**, waking at **one hit point**, where Jadar and the others have him covered with crossbows. They are **still alive, able and willing to talk**.

### 5. One detective, a catalog assistant and four records

Baffo navigates silently through a large **medicine/anatomy section**, eventually tracing toward the eastern wall and finding another multi-story reading chamber. There a tall mechanical helper, **Seeker**, pleasantly offers to search its historical catalog. It finds **four mentions of Mondoro**; this is an actual retrieval, substantially more than the uncompleted search the heroes had previously demanded.

The four records are:

1. **A historical account of Mondoro**: he was said to have made a **demonic pact** granting understanding of languages, but feared the demon might one day take his sight or powers; he documented secrets in obscure form as insurance. **Reported historical tradition, not verified narration of a witnessed pact.**
2. **A monastery census/family listing** naming **Mondoro as the uncle of Befren Trolley**, a follower of **Zelganith**.
3. **A monastery record of Befren's death**, reportedly murder by **highwaymen near Serifax**.
4. **A memo removing an old box marked “Mondoro Sackt” from the dormitories**; a note recommends that **potentially arcane or dangerous contents** be stored with other hazardous personal effects.

That final entry points toward the **Dark Archive**, where Seeker says such personal effects may have been placed. **The note alone does not prove the box still exists, belongs to the same Mondoro, contains the cipher, or that a cipher can be read without the missing auctioned book.** Seeker **cannot leave the history section**, cannot produce a drawing of the route, but identifies the Dark Archive as being toward **the south of the library**. Baffo proceeds there under continued fear of arrest.

### 6. Garpoon accepts aid; the episode stops before reunion

Back with the defenders, **Jadar offers to stop the fight**. He argues that the visitors entered a protected place and ignored its procedures and asks where Baffo is going. **Garpoon says Baffo sought the Dark Archive**, and the scholars, rather than finishing him off, **give Garpoon a potion of healing**. He reports **17 hit points restored**. Jadar leads him into the southern stacks toward a **locked stone structure forming the Dark Archive entrance**, where the group waits for Baffo to find them. The librarian sends his companions back to normal duties and asks that those evacuated by the fire be readmitted.

**No reconciliation between Baffo and Jadar is played yet.** On the other side of the library, Baffo has only just learned of the dormitory box's likely destination and is heading south. **The locked Dark Archive is not entered and the Mondoro cipher is not acquired before Jon ends the episode**, explicitly deferring the next events to another installment.

The ending is a **research and reunion cliffhanger**, not the close of the Bound conspiracy or a successful retrieval.
