**Continuity firewall:** ***Inverse* remains a separate fictional reality from *The Wastes*.** The finale contains a group of travelers with suggestive names, species and voices, while out-of-character hosts advertise the return of the Wastes campaign. This is **not sufficient to equate those in-story travelers with Wastes characters or join both timelines**. Keep them as an **Inverse encounter / possible performer-created parallel homage**, without importing Wastes adventures, CHIDS, Earth, the Principle or the Soulbroker.

## What this installment resolves

**By the end of S1E02, the waystation is actually repaired and the opening two-part plot concludes.** The engineer **Hemshaw is rescued alive**, a human named **Niko** is released from **Zia Rosewood's coercion**, **Zia is captured alive** and is to be escorted to face judgment in the city, and **her sister Cindra Rosewood is confirmed dead after Garpoon throws her off the tower**. A massive number of displaced **unbranded humans from a severed cruise ship** survive the flux event and gain **local refuge** in the Gnarls or Common's Rest. The town and a formerly hostile genasi agree to conceal their origin from outside authorities. **Neither national legal recognition nor long-term safety is established.**

**Cindra's goals differ from Zia's:** Zia has a self-interested land-and-expulsion plan and helps engineer the frame-up; Cindra is motivated by a more radical **anti-barrier religious conviction**. She claims centuries of history about the Anathema have been lies and that the inhabitants of Inverse belong in the “real world.” **The GM does not confirm that belief**. It is precisely the risk the protagonists refuse to impose on innocent people.

### The GM's explicit opening correction about history

Before recap, a listener asks why a **magical plague** would devour magic and whether Inverse lacks magic. **Jon answers directly at approximately 00:01:45–00:02:25**: the account of a human wizard creating the **Anathema** comes from **roughly 100,000-year-old accepted history** and **may not be fully accurate**. The plague **exists beyond the barrier**, but its specific **magical origin, explanation for consuming magic and even parts of the origin story are not fully verified** within this episode. This qualifies the confident dramatic **GM prologue at S1E01 ~00:05:45–00:07:50** without replacing it with a new “true” cosmology. The **barrier, flux anomalies and mechanically failing station** are more directly demonstrated.

## Played sequence — rescues, investigation, conflict

### 1. Emergency relief and a missing engineer

The massive flux rupture from S1E01 has cut the **front third of a modern-looking cruise ship** into the Gnarls. It lies embedded at an angle, with **hundreds of displaced humans**, many injured and some dead; their **mobile phones malfunction, spark and go dark**. Town residents, including **Elder Corala, tabaxi guard Tekuri, innkeeper Madeleine**, and even the genasi who fought the party, help pull passengers from the water. The GM says there are roughly **15–20 apparent bodies** amid far more injured or dazed people, including **eight dead orcs** from fighting and ship impact combined; **these are approximate immediate-scene counts**, not a documented final passenger casualty total.

**Baffo argues that stopping the barrier collapse matters to everyone still trapped in its path**. **Garpoon and Lyalta do not want to abandon the living**; **Spores scatters fungus on corpses** because, according to his understanding of balance and undead, the dead might otherwise rise in anger. This is Spores's **belief and character practice**, not an independently narrated mass undead outbreak.

The four realize **Hemshaw is nowhere among the rescuers**. Elder Corala believed **Tekuri was guarding him**, but Tekuri went straight to rescue the refugees after the catastrophe. She last saw the engineer near the town gate. **No one knowingly left him under continuing observation**. The genasi, despite her antagonism toward Spores, promises to keep assisting crash survivors and lets him help with the bodies.

### 2. Spores interviews two horses

After deciding to retrace the journey, **Spores uses ritual *Speak with Animals*** at a stable just outside Common's Rest. **Garpoon chooses between two horses**, although the choice is comically arbitrary. They identify themselves as **Roy and Tim**; the friendlier Roy indicates two people passed on toward the waystation after the noise of the crash. The suspicious Tim is reluctant to share information and repeatedly warns not to tell them anything. **Lyalta runs to obtain sugar cubes** (and beer), rather than feeding the horses Garpoon's suggested meat. Roy's directional indication points uphill toward the **waystation**, still prominent above the town.

During the search **Baffo asks Lyalta to become his new investigative assistant**: he admits his brusque manner antagonizes witnesses and wants her to keep people talking while he studies their claims. **She enthusiastically accepts**. This relationship is **played**, not merely a prospective joke. Garpoon continues to insist he will protect her.

### 3. The waystation and Zia's actual role

The waystation is described physically as a roughly **three-story tower with a dish and antenna**, standing on a hill with a cliff along one side. **Baffo observes deep drag marks and scattered tools** on the approach, including an **adjustable wrench recognizable from Hemshaw's belt**. Spores scouts in **rat form** through a door Baffo cracks open. Inside is a large square room with a **locked/protected central grating resembling a caged elevator**, and stairs rising through the building.

The rat sees **Hemshaw on his knees, a human man pressing a sword to him, and Zia Rosewood speaking to the human**. Crucially, Spores hears Zia instruct the man to **take Hemshaw upstairs to her sister, make him repair the station, then execute him and wait for townspeople to arrive**. This is direct observed involvement in an attempted cover-up, not just an interested landowner's accusation. Spores returns and warns the others.

The group's stealthy assault goes awkwardly: **Baffo's grapple against Zia fails**, though he gets inside; **Garpoon wounds her with axes**, **Spores poisons her with fungal spores** and **Lyalta supports with Bless and healing**. Baffo fires a **deliberately blunted arrow** into the human holding Hemshaw, knocking him down. Eventually **Zia tries to heal herself**, but another Spores attack interrupts her, and she collapses **unconscious but breathing**. **Zia is not killed in this battle.** The human on the stairs is also **knocked unconscious** rather than immediately executed.

Baffo carries **the unconscious engineer and human outside**, while Garpoon and the others secure **Zia** with rope and carry her. The human is awakened and names himself **Niko**, a resident of the Gnarls. He says **Zia repeatedly altered his mind**, compelling him to help; he has no recollection of a voluntary plan to kill the engineer. **Baffo's special Inspector's Insight detects no lie** in his explanation. That makes his status as a **coerced participant** much stronger than Zia's earlier blanket accusation against unbranded humans, although the exact spell/rules and full time course are not independently proven. **The heroes release Niko** and Spores directs him to shelter at his cave; his later reunion with villagers is narrated.

**Hemshaw wakes alive**, confirms Zia grabbed him during the rescue rush, and agrees to complete the repair. New rifts and violent shaking show the problem is worsening.

### 4. Cindra Rosewood and the barrier-worship doctrine

The party moves up through the station with Hemshaw; Spores changes into a scouting **possum**. On the upper level a second **silver-haired elf woman in dark robes**, **Cindra Rosewood**, waits near the rooftop and asks about a fleeing human (Niko). Baffo confronts her with Zia's land scheme. **Cindra confirms, effectively, that this was Zia's smaller plan** and says she intends something much grander.

**Cindra believes the sheltered residents have been lied to** about the Anathema, and that they should return to the “real world.” She says a power sounding like **Altar / Alter / Altar?** speaks to her. Through a **Religion check**, Jon explains there are groups that venerate **the barrier itself** as if it were divine, in an **illegal and sometimes violent cult-like movement**, though not every member is violent. **The cult name and the barrier's actual capacity to speak remain unverified.** Baffo objects to conducting a potentially world-ending experiment on unwitting inhabitants even if Cindra believes herself enlightened. The sister is **not the same person as Sashal Meadows**, the genasi law officer who helped at the wreck.

Baffo gives a signal and Garpoon charges. On the roof, Cindra uses commands that briefly **force Baffo to kneel**, a protective magical effect and **Inflict Wounds**; Garpoon wounds her with axes, **Spores attacks with fungal and insect magic**, and Lyalta protects and heals. **Increasingly frequent flux rifts blast seawater across the roof**, knocking combatants off their feet. Baffo successfully **grapples Cindra** and drags her to the ledge; Garpoon then **takes hold and deliberately throws her over the cliff**, intending to kill her. **Her body is actually recovered below and the GM explicitly confirms she is dead**, distinguishing it from a mere disappearance.

### 5. Real repair and end-of-one-shot resolution

While the confrontation unfolds **Hemshaw enters the roof repair room**. After Cindra falls, **Spores assists the engineer with wires and components**, relying partly on his earlier experience of helping with a repair though he does not understand all the technology. **Within about five to ten minutes**, the crackling air steadies, the waystation hums smoothly and **Hemshaw announces it repaired**. This is a **played restoration**, not just a plan, and the GM reiterates the station is functioning when the adventurers depart.

**Zia remains alive**, carried as a captive. **Niko** returns to his people, and **Spores tends Cindra's corpse with fungi** out of concern about the dead returning. This does not establish her undead resurrection or a change to the Anathema. Returning to **Common's Rest**, the heroes are thanked by the elder, guard **Tekuri**, locals and refugees. The elder explicitly tells Spores that **he has a place in town despite being an unbranded human**. Genasi officer **Sashal Meadows** acknowledges she misjudged the party and agrees to **keep the new arrivals' origin secret**.

The elder warns that passengers from **beyond the barrier would be legally classified as flux** and, if reported to the capital, could trigger persecution or death. She allows them to live among **the Gnarls' unbranded humans** or in **Common's Rest** and asks that the rescue be kept quiet. This is **a local refuge and secrecy agreement**, **not a change to Inverse-wide branding laws**. The party agrees Zia should **face judgment** for tampering with the waystation; she is **to be escorted toward the distant city called Serifax/Sarafax**, not executed. Baffo, Lyalta and Garpoon plan to return to **Grizz for their pay** and perhaps pursue future cases together. **Spores intends to stay nearby at his cave**, which Baffo promises to visit.

A supply caravan arrives, carrying mead and food and providing a much-needed celebration in a town accustomed to irregular deliveries. Yet the arrival of **survivors torn from families aboard the wreck** leaves an undercurrent of grief.

### 6. The closing caravan and recognizable-but-separate travelers

Near the end, a peculiar group of **four other adventurers** arrives with an ornate, top-hatted **goblin** and a wagon bearing supplies. In the ASR a leader introduces himself as **Dion Tollings / Deon Tollings** and identifies his companions as **Faith** (a tiefling), a **hooded red-eyed figure** with a name sounding like **No Haggard of Lunar Mountain**, and a **lizardfolk whose name is truncated or unclear**. The goblin is addressed by a name sounding like **Kabuck / Kabuk**. They praise the Inverse party's heroism but there is tension when Garpoon mistrusts the hooded figure. The newcomers are narrated as **their own traveling caravan-escort party**. **Do not backfill their exact identities from familiar Wastes character names** or silently decide that they are the very same people transported across realities.

The visitors carry **a letter from Grizz for Hemshaw**: the employer awaits the original escort group for payment, but **Hemshaw is to accompany these travelers north to address another engineering problem**. The caravan departs; Baffo, Lyalta, Garpoon and Spores reflect on their temporary partnership. **Jon explicitly closes the two-part adventure**: the waystation is restored, **Cindra dead**, **Zia going to justice**, the Gnarls temporarily sheltering unbranded human arrivals, Common's Rest celebrating, and the main companions alive.

**Outside the game**, the host then teases the show's **return to the *Wastes* campaign**. This is a broadcast transition, **not an on-screen cross-campaign portal**. The Inverse characters are not automatically part of the Wastes future.
