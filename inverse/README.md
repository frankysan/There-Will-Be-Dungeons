# Inverse — independent campaign continuity

**Inverse is a distinct fictional reality and campaign from *The Wastes*.** It is run by **Jon Jagger**, with familiar podcast performers portraying **different characters**. It is **not** a Wastes flashback, an alternate Nash history, a sequel to the Traversal Drive story, or an event in the Wastes chronology. The **human world** in Inverse's prologue belongs to **Inverse's own setting**; no link to *The Wastes* is demonstrated.

**Research status:** **S1E01–S1E03** now have provisional, source-indexed dossiers. S1E01–S1E02 form the completed initial two-part adventure; **S1E03 starts a separate Inverse investigation two months later** and closes before the monastery cipher is retrieved. **None of these episodes has been independently audio-verified. S1E04 remains unreviewed** despite its processed source.

## World premise and S1E01 starting state

**GM-narrated ancient history, explicitly qualified in S1E02:** A human mage (ASR **Cathos Knull**) **is believed** to have created **the Anathema** nearly **100,000 years ago**, causing a catastrophe in the human world. **Jon warns at S1E02 ~00:01:45 that this ancient accepted story may be inaccurate**, including the claimed magical origin. The **Eventide Assembly** establishes or summons **Inverse**, a refuge out of phase with that world, and powers a protective **barrier** through **waystations**. Smaller barrier anomalies are called **flux**. One frontier station is losing energy prematurely; a previous engineer has vanished.

**Played locations:** A **Foundation Tech train**, **Common's Rest** (small resource-poor frontier settlement), the southwestern **Badlands**, an **unbranded human grove**, a **part-built property** and an unrepaired **waystation** nearby. At the episode's climax, **a flux rift spills seawater, fish, passengers and a partial metal ship** into the grove. **Sarafax/Serifax**, a wealthier distant city, and an uncertain continent name **Oferivia?** are mentioned rather than explored.

**Social conflict at the first-episode cutoff:** Some humans have legal **wrist brands**; others, including druid **Spores**, are **unbranded** and face discrimination and arrest. The elf **Zia Rosewood** accuses their colony of sabotaging the station, while seeking to buy nearby land. **S1E02 later shows her personally complicit in coercion and a cover-up**, but this was not established in S1E01. Genasi officer **Sashal Meadows** tries to arrest Spores, later relents and agrees to protect refugees.

The GM identifies the human passengers' origin as the **other human world separated from Inverse by the barrier**, not as *The Wastes*. **S1E03 calls the world on the far side “Earth” and the world on this side “Erivia” (phonetic ASR); these are *Inverse-internal* terms**, never evidence that either world is part of Wastes continuity. The episode does not establish the ultimate condition of that human world.

## Ending of the initial two-part adventure — S1E02

**Directly played and GM-confirmed:** The front third of a cruise ship enters via flux, with hundreds of humans affected. **Niko is released from Zia's mental coercion**, **Hemshaw is rescued and repairs the waystation**, **Zia is captured alive and will face judgment**, and **Cindra dies after Garpoon throws her from the station roof**. **Sashal Meadows** and Common's Rest leaders agree to protect displaced humans who are legally considered flux, maintaining their **local refuge** and secrecy. **Spores is welcomed openly** by the elder, while **Baffo recruits Lyalta as his assistant** and the group plans to collect payment from Grizz. **The earlier missing engineer and the long-term durability of the repair remain unresolved.**

**Story-world boundary:** A caravan of **four adventurers with names reminiscent of The Wastes** appears briefly and takes Hemshaw toward another northern assignment. It is an **Inverse in-world encounter**, not proof that Wastes characters entered this reality or that the cosmologies are unified. The out-of-game host's announcement of a subsequent Wastes broadcast is likewise not a crossover. The full caveats and timestamps are in [S1E02](episodes/S1E02.md).

## Second Inverse mission — two months later (S1E03)

Working with **Grizz de Wells** after the Common's Rest rescue, the company investigates **the Bound**, a cult revering **Alter** and paradoxically seeking to **destroy the barrier**. They believe a pair of old warlock **Mondoro Sackt's** artifacts may be targets: **Lyalta and Spores depart for an auction** to protect a book titled *The Creator's Bound*, while **Baffo and Garpoon ride to the isolated Mines Monastery** to find a cipher thought to be in his journals.

**What actually plays:** Baffo and Garpoon intimidate doorman **Murgit**, antagonize scholar **Fulna** and receptionist **Previs**, then burn a book to force the attention of elder scholar **Jeadar** (whose first name is actually letter-spelled **J-E-A-D-A-R** in one note). A fight erupts; **Garpoon is knocked unconscious but survives a natural-20 death save**, accepts a healing potion and goes with the scholars to wait by the locked **Dark Archive**. **Baffo discovers Seeker**, an automated catalog assistant, who finds four historical references pointing toward Mondoro's lost belongings. The cipher, the actual box and the other team's auction results **are not recovered or shown by this episode's cutoff**. See the [S1E03 dossier](episodes/S1E03.md) for source timestamps and contested name spellings.

## Boundaries for ongoing work

**File identities:** Use the dedicated Inverse directory with per-episode files such as **S1E01.md and S1E02.md**. Never reuse Wastes **E001–E081** IDs for Inverse, or insert Inverse locations and history into the [Wastes chronology](../world/CHRONOLOGY.md), [narrative](../narrative/README.md) or [world guide](../world/WORLD.md). A later source could explicitly discuss a connection; **no such link should be presumed**.

**Evidence hierarchy:** Maintain **GM-narrated setup**, **directly played occurrences**, **NPC claims**, **cast retrospective explanations**, and **ASR-provisional spellings** as separate categories. No audio was listened to for this pass; no original SRT changed.

**Earlier S1E01 cutoff:** The waystation was still failing, Hemshaw was missing amid the crash, and the humans' legal fate was undetermined. These are **historically correct first-episode statements, superseded by S1E02**.
