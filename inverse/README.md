# Inverse — independent campaign continuity

**Inverse is a distinct fictional reality and campaign from *The Wastes*.** It is run by **Jon Jagger**, with familiar podcast performers portraying **different characters**. It is **not** a Wastes flashback, an alternate Nash history, a sequel to the Traversal Drive story, or an event in the Wastes chronology. The **human world** in Inverse's prologue belongs to **Inverse's own setting**; no link to *The Wastes* is demonstrated.

## Premiere setting

**GM-narrated ancient history:** A human mage (ASR **Cathos Knull**) reportedly creates **the Anathema** nearly **100,000 years ago**, causing a catastrophe in the human world. The **Eventide Assembly** establishes or summons **Inverse**, a refuge out of phase with that world, and powers a protective **barrier** through **waystations**. Smaller barrier anomalies are called **flux**. One frontier station is losing energy prematurely; a previous engineer has vanished.

**Played locations:** A **Foundation Tech train**, **Common's Rest** (small resource-poor frontier settlement), the southwestern **Badlands**, an **unbranded human grove**, a **part-built property** and an unrepaired **waystation** nearby. At the episode's climax, **a flux rift spills seawater, fish, passengers and a partial metal ship** into the grove. **Sarafax/Serifax**, a wealthier distant city, and an uncertain continent name **Oferivia?** are mentioned rather than explored.

**Social conflict:** Some humans have legal **wrist brands**; others, including druid **Spores**, are **unbranded** and face discrimination and arrest. The elf **Zia Rosewood** accuses their colony of sabotaging the station, but reveals an interest in **buying nearby land** and gives no specific proof. An unnamed **genasi** attempts to arrest Spores with orc support; the interruption by major flux leaves this legal confrontation unfinished.

The GM identifies the human passengers' origin as the **other human world separated from Inverse by the barrier**, not as *The Wastes*. The episode does not establish why the waystation fails or the ultimate condition of that human world.
