# Koh Spiros — rogue

## Overview

Koh is a quietly observant satyr, already inside the [guild](../places/delvers-guild.md) when his assigned companions arrive. Dark hair, bright blue eyes, a large purple scarf and flowing white clothing with crimson leather and brass details distinguish him. A sword and dagger hang at his waist. His quiet hooves suit his preference for remaining unnoticed, although he needs light to see underground.[^identity]

## Development and custody

### Caution and initiative

On the first expedition, Koh keeps to the edge of [Evelyn's](evelyn-grinkeeper.md) torchlight and takes opportunities to strike while the creatures are occupied. He also handles the less dramatic business of adventuring: buying tools, consulting his guide, gathering proof of kills and submitting it at the guild. Cassius hints at earlier dealings with him, but Koh offers no account that establishes a criminal past.

His caution allows decisive risks. With [Krandal](krandal-splenderbelt.md) holding the rope and lending divine guidance, he descends beneath the [farm cave](../places/farm-cave.md) to retrieve a dead delver's badge and glass orb. He alone sees a serpentine creature approaching during his climb back and urges the others to seal the passage. He later describes it to Cassius, who calls it a grick. His companions agree that the recovered [drift globe](../items/drift-globe.md) should be his. He declines a guild room because he must return home, though that home remains unseen.[^cave]

### Resourcefulness and reliance

The next morning, Koh admits that his earlier mysterious voice was an affectation. His reserve gives way to a more visible mixture of ingenuity and fallibility. Exhausted by the sanctuary stairs, he still runs the ten laps required by its first trial while his companions defend him. In the weight room, a strike meant for a Shadow instead fells Krandal, leaving Evelyn to restore their companion.

Koh's recovered globe becomes crucial when he calls forth daylight against the Shadows. He later finishes Studwell and, despite his diminished strength, rings the final bell with help from Krandal's guidance. The trials expose how much his individual successes depend on cooperation. He retrieves Sultan's enchanted sash but offers it to the group, treating a new reward as a shared resource rather than insisting that finding it makes it his alone.[^crypt]

### Family behind the guild badge

At home, the reserve that marks Koh as a rogue is born of an exhausted hope. His wife lies bedridden, and he has spent the household's former fortune seeking someone capable of restoring her. He tells the family he visits the guild to commission healers and magical cures, while secretly undertaking perilous paid adventures himself. He comes home in cleaner clothes, hides the signs of combat and devotes attention to his six-year-old daughter **Penelope**, who waits with her nanny. He needs the earnings, but he needs the trust and safety of those at home just as much. His deception protects them from the knowledge of how far their circumstances have fallen.[^home]

Koh's caution becomes even more consequential when the party follows a grick trail into a ruined sinkhole. Sultan's climbing sash provides an escape route, and Koh reaches the top while the others are still under attack. From above and then from concealment among the bushes, his arrows save the group, including Krandal when a grick knocks the cleric unconscious and begins to drag him away. Koh is willing to risk himself, though he has grown less willing to mistake boldness for wisdom. He argues for resting and reporting the dangerous dungeon instead of risking his companions on an unmeasured descent. Eventually he agrees to return after a respite, and a clever but precarious shield-and-rope attempt leaves him badly injured by hidden needles. Evelyn cures the poison, returning the care he has given the others.[^gricks]

His reserve has a more personal purpose than the group knew. With enforced time away from the guild approaching, Koh plans to spend it with his **family and daughter**, maintaining an appearance of aristocratic standing. Whether the image is a harmless social fiction or a deeper obligation remains unshown, but the revelation gives his ordinary insistence on going home new weight. Behind the quiet scout is a parent with a life apart from the Delvers.[^family]

### A blade that answers thought

Koh's discretion now serves an unfamiliar ability. The hilt taken from the woodland dungeon answers his concentration by producing a cyan blade without an ordinary metal edge. Its discoverer Evelyn brought it out, but the guild returns it to Koh once its lack of sentience has been established. Koh learns that it can hurt without leaving a cut. He becomes the subject of unwanted attention from a masked adventurer who calls him a scion and warns of others seeking the same awakening. Rather than embrace the stranger's certainty, Koh asks questions and searches for someone able to explain this new power.[^soul]

The intervening weeks show how carefully he must balance his two lives. He pays for care for his wife, preserves the family's outward comfort and quietly economizes. Among adventurers, he becomes the party's keeper of supplies and a discerning observer of strangers. He recognizes the false behavior of a purported child selling enchanted tarts, takes point evacuating civilians, and proposes a name for the three companions. He remains cautious but increasingly confident in the company they have made.[^service]

### The limits of careful planning

Koh's new position among the bronze ranks gives his caution a new purpose. He takes charge of the guild's shared library key and studies the flameskull before traveling, but the keep tests his ingenuity. He tries to silence a dangerous skull with Sultan's enchanted sash and instead tumbles from the tower window. He survives to explore the servants' rooms and discovers how the dead workers continue their tasks. When his companions fail to open a mysterious stone door, he studies the hidden instructions and realizes its last command must be pressed inward, rather than drawn in a circle.[^tower]

In the mirror-bound lord Enzo, Koh meets someone whose curiosity about death may match the desperation that drives him. He asks about a strange illness that no ordinary healer can relieve, but says only that it afflicts someone he knows. To the others, the intimacy of the question remains concealed. The old man's answer offers little comfort, and his private worry breaks through his customary reserve. Koh still manages to record the ancient signs, listen to warnings about a darkness below and finish the fight with his Soul Knife, whose force can strike the mind even when arrows glance off bone.[^tower]

### Lessons from Wildfire

Koh's search for answers takes him to a stern dragonborn who calls himself Wildfire. The silver-ranked warrior has a personal reason to seek him: his dead brother once carried the globe Koh recovered underground. In private, Wildfire discovers that Koh can pass thoughts directly into another mind. Their shared speech appears to them as silent words, and the unusual ease of it astonishes Koh. The lessons that follow demand patience and secrecy, qualities he already possesses, and gradually teach him that the powers he thought belonged to an ancient weapon can also belong to himself.[^wildfire]

In the southwestern quarter, Koh's training receives a sudden test. When a rusting creature threatens him in a blacksmith's shop, he springs past it with the blue blade in his hand. The old grip crumbles as he strikes, but the psychic edge continues to obey him without it. He then responds to a child's warning, finds a stirge afflicting a family's old dog and helps bring the animal safely back to its feet. His instinct is still to observe before rushing into danger, though new powers have made him bolder in the moments that demand action.[^well]

### The courage to retreat

Koh learns to lead without confusing courage with an obligation to fight everything. Beneath the city he helps a second guild party against a cube, makes use of the silent speech taught by Wildfire and proves himself a patient archer when the cave's great hook-bearing predator charges. He can take pride in the killing stroke, yet the sight of another such creature bending over its fallen companion reminds him that exploration always leaves questions behind. He offers the guild tangible evidence rather than claiming that his company has emptied the caverns of danger.[^s9]

### A different kind of hope

On the way home Koh returns to the well for the climbing sash he left hanging there. At home, Penelope has drawn him with daggers, as though the part of his life he keeps secret has crept into her imagination. He turns his actual adventures into bedtime stories, then sits beside his unresponsive wife as he has done so often. The new power to speak silently into other minds presents a possibility that feels more important than any blade: perhaps one day he may reach her despite her stillness. The thought comforts him, but he has not yet tried it.[^s9]

### A voice through the darkness

Koh's greatest gamble is made in the stillness of his own home. He wakes beside his bedridden wife and reaches into her mind, no longer content to let the doctors' ministrations speak for her. Instead of ordinary silent words he enters a dark place where she sits beside a flower, conscious and startled by his arrival. The embrace is brief. She begs him to leave before unseen others notice, saying that the town is safe while she remains. As the same dreadful droning grows louder and dunes rise about them, Koh is thrust back into his chair. The black sand falling from his wife's clenched hands is proof that something of the experience reached the waking world. He preserves a little of it for later study. Hope has finally become possible, but the danger is more intimate than he imagined.[^s10]

### A gift mistaken for divinity

On the guild's peaceful mission east, Koh treats his mental speech as a bridge to fish-people whose language the party barely knows. To his discomfort, the villagers hail him as a being from above and fall at his feet. He insists he is not a god and seeks out their more worldly trader. Under the lake his curiosity about a sunken chest nearly brings him into a mimic's jaws, yet it is also he who recognizes an ancient ring by speaking to it through thought. He gives the protective band to Krandal rather than keep it. When a ritualist turns the water against them, Koh finds a clever escape with Sultan's enchanted sash and helps subdue the spellcaster. He argues for mercy after victory, preferring to let the captive's people decide what to do with him.[^s10]

### Secrets beneath the lake

Beneath the village's still water, Koh's curiosity draws him toward the carvings that seemed to follow his family even into sleep. He learns the meaning of a strange wand by concentrating upon its ancient material; it trembles toward a cavity in the black-slimed stone, guiding Evelyn to a broken machine part whose missing gears invite more questions. Koh guides his companions through an abandoned market and helps kill the floating, beaked hunter hiding among the ruined stalls. Yet when their remaining breath begins to fail, he is the first to admit that finding another mystery does not give them time to solve it.[^s11]

### A friend in an enemy's face

On the lakeshore his wariness saves the company. He slips behind a robed stranger ransacking their saddlebags and silences her with one psychic strike, sparing her life. The giant armored guard she serves proves much harder to overcome, and Koh depends upon Krandal's healing while Evelyn draws the brute's sword. Once the fight ends, the strange magic upon the guard's face fades and a familiar face emerges: Broadside, last seen around the guild. Koh insists upon keeping the captives alive; they can explain more than corpses. His newly found wand, the old gear-crank and the gold accepted as compensation join the burdens he must carry back toward the guild.[^s11]

### A hidden route to the hollow

Koh is the one who notices the suspicious prison carriage on the road and works out where its attendants have gone. The wounded Boonefellow's news sends him back into danger even when the captured enemies could have made a neat end to the work. At the guild he asks the question that matters: where the missing elf has been taken, and how little time remains to stop whatever the researcher intends. His quiet determination contrasts with Evelyn's open challenge. He slips beside the river under cover of her distraction, keeps his companions connected through their thoughts and strikes the ritualist from behind before a sacrificed life can make the rite complete. When the ground falls away and the great roper emerges, his arrow pierces its enormous eye. He is quick to notice Faelan still drowning amid the floodwater and calls for rescue rather than celebrating victory.[^s12]

His wife's stillness remains with him beyond the guild's praise. The Delvers have discovered another path into the old darkness, but not what would bring her home. Koh's courage has earned him recognition; it has not made his two lives any easier to reconcile.[^s12]

### A missing fortnight and an old debt

Koh returns from Snake Island with weapons transformed by the island's serpentine culture, but little memory of the days spent there. His reluctance to celebrate comes from more than disorientation. In the two weeks he was gone, the care of his sleeping wife nearly failed for lack of money. A satyr named Chuck Abnaz—once his colleague among the Cloven Brotherhood's forgers—has quietly paid the doctors and now seeks repayment and a return to criminal business. Koh has no intention of going back. Yet the familiar voice at his doorstep and the strangers in the shadows remind him that protecting his family has never been a matter of simply earning more guild wages.

For the first time he tells his companions that he has been wearing an adventurer's voice over his ordinary manner, trying to sound as he imagined great heroes would. His true name remains on the guild rolls, a problem when the same city holds his family, his old associates and his growing reputation. Though he lets his friends know they should be wary of other satyrs, he withholds the full weight of Chuck's threat.[^s2e01]

## Current status

Koh is **alive and back in Atomer after two weeks on Snake Island**, most events of which he cannot remember. He possesses two **replacement serpent daggers**, **Dagger of Venom +1**, **four venom vials** and **fossilized-snake arrows**, together with his Soul Knife and earlier belongings except the two replaced daggers. He **buys and pays 2,000 gp for a Ring of Feather Falling**, which has been demonstrated but not yet used in an actual fall. The group **receives 1,000 gp each** for Snake Island; prior Lake Ruins mechanism pieces remain unsolved. At home **Penelope is well, his wife still unconscious**, and **Chuck Abnaz of the Cloven Brotherhood**, with three satyr associates, demands a fee for having paid her doctors and tries to recruit Koh back into forgery. Koh refuses, while only partly informing his companions and briefly uses **his normal unperformed voice** around them. He **helps destroy the skeletal minotaur's body** at Krandal's farmland and visits **Potato Grove** with his friends. **White Wedding** is registered for the following evening, not played. The missing Snake Island memories, wife/captor, mechanism and Brotherhood demand remain unresolved.[^s2e01][^april21]
