# Krandal Splenderbelt — cleric

## Overview

Tall and imposing, Krandal is a half-orc, half-human cleric whose seriousness rests on a simple obligation: work promised ought to be finished. He prefers Splenderbelt or Sir Splenderbelt until a friendship warrants his first name. A large holy necklace usually stays beneath his clothes. His even temper coexists with a fondness for potatoes, difficulty remembering names and a growing distrust of ceilings.[^identity][^background]

## Development and relationships

### The first guild job

Punctuality and preparation distinguish Krandal from his more hurried companion [Evelyn](evelyn-grinkeeper.md). He waits with descriptions of his assigned partners, declines unpaid work and shoulders the heavy supplies needed to seal the [farm cave](../places/farm-cave.md). A patron's warning about ceilings unsettles him, although he does not share it with the others until they are underground.

His resolve proves compatible with dependence on others. Blood-draining creatures nearly overcome him, and Evelyn's healing keeps him on his feet; he in turn heals and protects her. Engulfed by a darkmantle, he follows her voice out of its darkness and brings it down with sacred radiance. He later steadies [Koh's](koh-spiros.md) descent with rope and divine guidance, completes the concrete seal and supports Koh's claim to the recovered globe. Responsibility, for Krandal, includes both finishing the job and acknowledging another person's risk.[^cave]

### Faith, fallibility and mutual care

At Kord's mountain sanctuary, Krandal's faith becomes a practical defense against creatures that drain the living. He recognizes their vulnerability to sacred radiance, but knowledge does not make him invulnerable. Koh's mistaken strike fells him, and Evelyn must restore him; when a Shadow later incapacitates her, he returns that care. His endurance also carries him through a kettlebell blow that would otherwise have left him helpless.

His contribution reaches beyond surviving combat. He helps cleanse the fountains, tends the barely breathing Sultan and lends Koh the guidance needed to complete the final trial. He is attracted by better-paid work, yet accepts the guild's requirement that a chosen job be undertaken promptly. His developing partnership depends on the same mixture of stubbornness, healing and cooperation that sustained their first assignment.[^crypt]

### Courage at the edge of the ruin

The afternoon after the crypt adventure reveals a gentler habit beneath Krandal's imposing manner. He spends his free time watching and sketching birds, with a patience not always evident in his dealings with fellow adventurers. Before the next job, he befriends Jimmy, the guild's ratlike night acquisitions clerk, while still looking for a spyglass. At an abandoned trapper's cart in the woods, he discovers one thrush still alive among its dead companions, revives it with careful drops of water and names it **Yuri**. He takes the recovering bird along rather than leave it to die. His affection for small living things complicates his rough dismissal of the predatory gricks.[^yuri]

An expedition into the woods forces Krandal to rely less on the certainty of his faith and more on the instincts of his companions. He finds his sacred fire poorly suited to the gricks' quick evasions. One of the beasts tears open his bag and becomes absorbed by his spilled religious vestments and provisions, allowing the cleric to escape a close assault. Above the sinkhole he patches the bag, but another grick strikes him down and begins carrying him away. Koh's arrow saves him; Evelyn revives him. He remains more concerned than ever that hasty exploration could leave the company dead in a place no one understands.[^gricks]

Nevertheless he agrees to the others' return, and his mace finishes the last grick they can find. In the underground passages he offers his shield for Koh's improvised crossing and watches the plan fail among the poisonous needles. The ordeal leaves him with less equipment but a sharper sense of how much the three need one another. On returning to the guild he proposes to spend his enforced time away from fieldwork serving at the local temple rather than simply enjoying the reward.[^return]

### Service, humility and an ambition of his own

Krandal's choice to spend his enforced leave at Pelor's temple reveals a deep wish to be useful beyond the guild's assignments. He treats the sick, accepts a hard cot and places himself under an elder's strict direction. His forceful personality sometimes fails to fit the temple's solemnity: he begins a dragonborn warrior's treatment with dramatic showmanship and only then remembers the blessing needed to remove poison. Brother Maynard makes him pray on his knees. His failure is one of manners and attention, not indifference to the person he is healing.[^temple]

Yuri, the bird he had rescued, tests that care more painfully. Krandal realizes that he forgot the weakened creature in his pocket. After trying to revive it with water, he accepts Jimmy's help and lets the ratlike clerk take the bird into safe hands. During subsequent weeks Krandal continues to serve both the temple and city, making rain against a hellhound's fire and helping his companions through humbler tasks. He is proud of his faith but less interested in the trappings of wealth; much of his earnings are saved toward a potato farm. The dream is earnest, and it tells his companions something about the quiet life he hopes eventually to build.[^service]

### A keeper of the light

Krandal's delight at receiving his bronze badge sits comfortably with his earnest faith. He brings extra incense to the keep because it belongs in his devotions, even when the guild's supplier points out that it cannot defeat a flameskull. He is still a man whose equipment can get in the way of his ideals. In the expensive entry hall, one of his face maces knocks a chair apart; a skeletal servant comes to repair it and is destroyed after the party speaks to him. Krandal's attempts at quiet movement later fare no better, but he insists that he cannot lie to the patron about the furniture they damage.[^tower]

His most useful gifts prove simpler. He heals Koh after the satyr's dreadful fall, and his called-down water turns a rolling sphere of magical fire into thick steam, giving the company a path into the tower. In the final battle he shelters his friends beneath a brief shower and summons radiant and spiritual weapons against the flying skull. Before its mirror-bound counterpart he speaks without disguise about his own ambitions: serving Pelor, finding more adventures, earning a living and, somewhere beyond it all, gaining enough to pursue his love of potatoes.[^tower]

### An uncertain celebrant, a caring neighbor

Krandal wakes at Pelor's temple after a cockatrice's enchantment has turned his body to stone for a day. Before he has shaken off the stiffness, Brother Maynard asks him to perform a wedding. Krandal has little knowledge of the proper vows and only a smudged note written on his own arm to guide him, but he gives the ceremony his earnest attention. The newlyweds receive a blessing of light even where his speech stumbles. His repeated requests for personal payment meet the cleric's disapproval; serving Pelor and saving toward his potato farm are not always easy ambitions to reconcile.[^wedding]

When guild duty later takes him to a neighborhood well, Krandal notices a trail of rust that others have missed. He protects Koh with healing and helps a wounded tradesman, though his concern for the residents leads him to speculate too freely about a blacksmith hiding monstrous secrets. Once the truth proves more ordinary, he corrects the rumor himself. He is capable of recognizing when a frightening conclusion has run ahead of the facts, even when he remains convinced that the guild should act with courage.[^well]

### Responsibility beneath a holy place

When the hidden chambers lead into Pelor's own temple, Krandal refuses to see the disturbance merely as an affront to ceremony. He is equally mindful of the creatures still below, the frightened townsfolk above and the companions depending on him. In the underground retreat he falls behind, and Evelyn shields him before he takes her hand and helps her escape. Later the hidden droning shaft beneath the sanctuary's altar leaves him troubled, but not satisfied by the simple claim that something sacred ought never to be touched.[^s9]

Brother Maynard's anger gradually gives way to trust. He turns to Krandal with a quieter fear: two elves lying motionless, beyond the ordinary help that the temple has tried. Krandal sees a deliberate rhythm in one still finger and records it rather than pretending to understand it. It is the kind of unfinished duty he cannot easily ignore. Maynard recognizes the seriousness with which he has listened and entrusts him with an old devotional amulet. Krandal wears the gift as a sign of renewed fellowship, while his questions about the sleepers remain unanswered.[^s9]

### Searching for a lost meaning

Krandal's concern for the sleeping brothers does not end with the temple's failed cures. Before the guild's next assignment he borrows military and Elven books, finding a possible match between the tapping finger and an old lantern code. He records an unfamiliar word without claiming to understand it. Evelyn's ability to read Elvish helps him recognize it as a word for a lament sung at funerals, though what the sleeper meant to tell him is still unclear. Koh's guarded acknowledgment that he knows a similar illness gives Krandal a new reason to hope that the strange silence might be broken.[^s10]

### The gentleness of restraint

The journey toward the lake reveals both his ordinary humor and his seriousness about duty. He rides confidently, oblivious to the stirge Koh removes from his horse, and brings the party toward a village where their gifts and words are meant to make peace. In the submerged ruin he allows himself to accept an old protective ring, offered by Koh when he has left his heavier armor on shore. When the fish wizard attacks, Krandal orders him to yield rather than striking him down. Even after the party wins, Krandal refuses to accept the necessity of killing an unarmed captive. He takes the bound creature onto his own back, making its safe return to the village his immediate responsibility.[^s10]

### A rescuer beneath the stones

Krandal carries the living fish wizard through a maze of forgotten rooms, never losing sight of the responsibility that accompanies taking a prisoner. The ancient city yields disturbing signs of its former inhabitants, a monstrous beak and a curious bottle stopped with a chain. He opens the bottle upon his companions' advice and watches strange fog engulf their path before shutting it again. Soon there is only enough magic to reach the lake's air; his strong arms and steady progress help bring everyone safely to shore.[^s11]

### Mercy for an armored stranger

When a second danger comes from above, Krandal answers more than one call for help. He summons a glowing mace shaped like his own, which speaks a single word as it strikes, and bolsters his friends against a warrior whose shield seems too strong for ordinary weapons. The blow that topples the armored man comes from his spectral weapon, but Krandal refuses to leave the fallen enemy to bleed to death. He staunches the wounds and later gives magical healing, even knowing Koh must render the prisoner unconscious again for safe travel. The three captives make an awkward, uneasy procession back toward the city. Krandal is no less committed to learning why their enemy bore a familiar face.[^s11]

### The mercy of a full day's work

The pursuit from the fish village tests Krandal's patience with more than the foe's strength. He shares the travel burdens that Evelyn cannot safely manage on horseback and urges his companions onward toward the missing guild members. When the imprisoned Broadside offers redemption, Krandal is prepared to hear him out. He leads the company north with his usual faith in his friends and in Pelor; at the Noble Hollow he creates space for Evelyn to reach the ritualist and protects her against harm.

The enormous creature uncovered beneath the collapsing earth is far stronger than the cultists who brought the adventurers there. Krandal finds himself caught in its tendrils and jaws, yet twice compels it to drop those it has seized. His familiar spectral face mace hovers beside him, but the deed that best reveals his character is healing a former enemy. He spends Pelor's grace on Solburn after the warlock is mauled, choosing to save a companion of the guilty rather than decide his fate on the floor of the cavern. His care allows the warlock to join the battle against the real threat.[^s12]

### The land given and the faith questioned

Snake Island returns Krandal to Atomer with a serpent staff, new mail and the recollection of a princess who calls him beloved. He takes delight in the gifts even as he wonders how so much adventure could have vanished from memory. More substantial than the snakes is the scroll Helenar places in his hands: the old watchtower promised after Noble Hollow is finally his. He gathers Koh and Evelyn to view the riverbank plots and sees farmland where they see mostly ruin.

Before that journey, Krandal visits the temple and learns how deeply the underground opening has frightened its people. Rianu Keeves still sleeps, and a spell intended to revive the dead, strangely cast on someone alive, draws a single whispered word without waking him. Krandal keeps it close for study. At the old tower a sea hag forces him to see a self stripped of Pelor's light, embodying his most troubling private doubt: whether the life he chose has ever been the right one. His friends bring him out of that despair and the creature is defeated. He leaves the property with an improbable potential helper, a talking minotaur skull buried beneath his future potato fields.[^s2e01]

### The wedding he nearly missed

At the Hooper wedding Krandal is told to stay among the trees, too tall and too armoured for polite halfling festivities. His vigilance uncovers danger when a pair of red eyes vanishes into a lake duck. The bird seizes a knife and dives toward an elderly guest; Krandal strikes it from the sky with Pelor's flame and keeps the body for examination. Later, under a shower of wedding coins and jewels, he remembers how badly his new potato farm needs money. His earnest search brings him a small fortune and, inadvertently, into the Hooper family graveyard.

When Grandfather Hooper finally walks abroad as a dead man hanging from a moving oak, Krandal breaks the branch bearing him with a mighty blow of his face-carved mace. Without the grandfather, the walking tree falls still. After the battle Krandal stands with Evelyn in insisting that the dead man be buried, and he makes the visiting wizard surrender his claim to the contract's gold if he wishes to keep the dead grandfather's enchanted cards.[^s2e02]

### A defender who becomes a farmer

A bulette's assault upon the financial quarter leaves Krandal battered beneath an enormous foot. He trusts his companions and Pelor's protection through the struggle: he blesses the party, then eventually drives the carved snake-head of his staff into the beast's exposed eye. The staff stays wood, not a living python. Later, with the city celebrating, Krandal turns away from the guild's crowded hall and spends his days in the quiet fields north of Atomer.

The property promised to him has no roof worth repairing quickly and a clogged ditch barely worthy of irrigation. Krandal begins with what the land most needs. He clears mud, gathers fallen timber and plants cut potato pieces in the little plots. He hears the minotaur skull calling from beneath the soil, digs it up and mounts it on a homemade crossbar dressed in a ragged coat. The head agrees to watch the growing potatoes and asks for a small stone to spit at intruders; Krandal gives it one, pleased to see the improbable partnership take shape. He calls the farm **Spud Buds**, though he has not settled on a final name. When Cassius later proposes a secret expedition, Krandal agrees to carry his staff as a thief named **Cobra Commander**, even if he cannot bear to abandon his personalized maces altogether.[^s2e03]

### The priest with masked maces

Cassius asks Krandal to dress as a thief, though the cleric cannot bear to abandon the maces sculpted in his own image. Evelyn's solution is to give the faces masks of their own. Thus dressed as Cobra Commander, Krandal accompanies his friends and the visiting burglar beneath Withers Estate. He is the only one who cannot join Koh's private psychic conversations. He compensates by reading two scrolls of magical silence while Shrugs breaks through guarded doors with dynamite. In the old wine cells he sees strange capital letters, solving the way into the guardian chambers, and makes a private discovery inside a weathered doll: an aquamarine worth far more than any potato field has yet given him.

In the furnace room Krandal calls a torrent of water upon a living flame, leaving it tiny but alive. Later the water guardian swallows him and Shrugs; Krandal finally commands his carved Python staff to become a living constrictor, breaking free. When a third guardian blasts the party against the walls, Krandal nearly falls, but he keeps himself and Shrugs alive with a pulse of Pelor's mercy. The thief's mission ends in a successful escape with borrowed treasure bags, while the name of Withers's wrongdoing remains unproved.[^s2e04]

### Established by S2E06

After the Withers settlement, Krandal takes **7,800 gp cash and eight gemstone types** worth the remainder of his nominal 15,000-gp share, plus **5,000 gp** of Evelyn's unwanted remainder. He holds **Charlatan's Dice**, **Candle of the Deep**, and **Cloak of Protection**, which Cassius asks him **not to wear visibly inside Atomer**, as it is reported stolen; the dice and candle are **not handed in**. In S2E06 he **hides gems in a hollow tower-wall brick** and **cash under a boulder east of the farm**, where his minotaur-skull scarecrow watches; neither farmhouse nor tower is rebuilt. The skull says an unknown fishman watches from the waterline; identity unproved. At **Gemholm** Krandal **transforms Staff of the Python to block the fleeing red creature**, calls through Koh's telepathy for capture/questioning rather than a kill, but **does not intervene physically** when Evelyn kills it. No actual healing of possible slaad infections has occurred. [S2E05](../../episodes/S2E05.md) · [S2E06](../../episodes/S2E06.md).

## Earlier status (at S2E04 cutoff)

Krandal **actually participates in and escapes the S2E04 Withers heist** under alias **Cobra Commander**, carrying **two face maces masked by Evelyn's outfits**, not his giant public holy emblem. **Not included in Koh's Psychic Whispers**, he relies on spoken relays. He **casts both of the two Scrolls of Silence given to him**, one for the initial basement door and one for the final vault (not hoarded/unspent), and **privately pockets an aquamarine from an old doll worth ~500 gp, without openly declaring it**. **Create or Destroy Water (20 gallons)** shrinks, but does not slay, the fire guardian Fern. **Staff of the Python is ACTUALLY TRANSFORMED into a giant constrictor snake** to escape water engulfment; previous S2E03 use was only the carved staff. He **uses Preserve Life to revive unconscious Shrugs (14 HP) and heal himself (11 HP)**, later heals Evelyn (19) and himself (6), leaving all living; his own HP goes as low as 1 during air battle. **Gem-loot division or guild/Cassius payment is not shown**, mission equipment remains borrowed. The **Spud Buds farm and living minotaur skull** receive no new scenes here, and previous temple sleepers remain unresolved.[^s2e04][^s2e03]
