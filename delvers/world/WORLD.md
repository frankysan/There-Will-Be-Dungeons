# Delvers — guild society and the world below

## Adventuring as organized work

Adventuring guilds protect settlements and turn dangerous expeditions into organized employment. Delvers occupy the lowest league, carrying numbered wooden badges and guides while working toward greater standing. Cassius speaks of promotion and platinum heroes; another patron points out a silver-ranked adventurer. The full reach and hierarchy of this profession remain unclear.[^society]

The [guild hall](places/delvers-guild.md) brings hospitality, equipment and quest administration together. Its welcome can be generous in tone and economical in practice: badges are reused, and newcomers choose among the jobs left by earlier arrivals. Particular talents affect assignments. Cassius considers two divine casters unusual and sends the party to a crypt where their gifts may be useful.[^work]

## Caverns and the Underdark

The land beneath the settlements is dangerous even at its edges. A [farm cave](places/farm-cave.md) shelters stirges and creatures disguised as stone, while a fissure leads to a darker tunnel below. The guild guide classifies the piercer and darkmantle as hazards warranting caution. Koh's encounter beneath the fissure suggests a further danger, which Cassius identifies from his description as a grick.[^below]

Sealing that opening secures one route. It does not reveal the extent of the Underdark. Cassius says another delver sought a door with a face in a different part of it; the party's recovered badge links its work to that earlier disappearance, but it has seen no such door.[^door]

## Devotion and the strength of the living

Kord's mountain sanctuary combines a graveyard with athletic devotion. Running, chains and a rope leading to a bell form trials within its crypts, connected by fountains that have become fouled. Its devotees face enemies that drain the strength of the living. House blames the disaster on Studwell's fatal attempt to squat in sandals, an explanation the rescues and cleansing do not independently prove.

The party's divine gifts and Koh's recovered light help it survive the haunting. Sacred radiance harms the Shadows, while daylight weakens them. Restoring the basins brings clear water back through the sanctuary, and the two missing devotees leave alive.[^sanctuary]

## The forest ruins and the limits of rank

Another route below the earth opens in forest ruins guarded by dangerous gricks. The company reaches a worked-stone dungeon behind an old sinkhole and finds creatures already impaled by its spear and needle traps. The ancient passages contain symbols Evelyn can only partly interpret as **'sky people'**, their larger meaning still unknown. A woodland recluse, Veggie Sticks, fears that the guild's response will disturb the hippogriffs he protects, but cannot pretend to understand the dangers himself.[^grickwoods]

This third scouting job brings a new lesson in guild rules. The party is rewarded for finding and reporting gricks; killing extra creatures brings no bounty. The dungeon's discovery gives them grounds to request first opportunity to explore it, subject to more experienced adventurers intervening if necessary. Their three weekly wood-rank assignments are exhausted, and Cassius is seeking approval for bronze promotion. Whatever glory they have earned, the guild still regards them as novices who must rest before taking more work.[^ranks]

## Service and a wider city

The guild's demand for visible public service takes the Delvers into inns, alleys, market squares, sewers and workshops. Their enemies are as likely to be escaped pests or a charmed garment as the creatures of the Underdark. More experienced guild members remain responsible for the worst threats, while newcomers learn how to guide people out of danger and restore ordinary life after a monstrous intrusion.[^service]

The recovered rune-marked hilt becomes known as a **Soul Knife** when the guild's artificer examines it. Koh's ability to manifest its blue psychic blade attracts the notice of a mysterious high-ranking adventurer who calls him a scion. That stranger's account of awakened powers and hunted relics remains incomplete. Meanwhile, the guild's first refusal to promote the party gives way to approval after weeks of diligent service.[^revelation]

## A shadow beneath the lake

The northward mountain country shelters a keep whose inhabitants have worked long past their deaths. Its estate is sustained by ancient magic, while in an upper chamber a mirror speaks with the face of a lord whose skull circles the battlements in a shroud of green flame. The creature's defeat leaves the servants still and the reflection dimmed, as though the failure of his search for immortality might at last give way to rest.[^keep]

The old lord called himself Enzo Vanderbuck Withers. He remembered signs of people who escaped the darkness of the world below, and named a far deeper abyss the **Dawnless Dunes**. In his account, something at its heart was awakening and forcing underground predators into lands nearer the light. These words echo the Delvers' earlier encounters with gricks and strange carvings, but remain the testimony of a solitary necromancer, not an observed account of what now lies in the depths.[^dunes]

## A city listening to its wells

The guild's fears become more immediate when creatures accustomed to the dark appear beneath the streets and in workshops. Carrie Underworld sketches an order of hunters and prey, from small pests to gricks, and wonders whether the deepest predators have yet to arrive. The same strange black ichor reported from haunted places may be another sign of some wider disturbance. None of these explanations has been proved, but the guild takes the threat seriously enough to call its bronze companies together and distribute an urgent task: inspecting the public wells.[^city]

In the southwestern quarter, an unusual rust trail leads to a forge and a creature able to spoil ordinary metal. A second monster hides as a barrel, while another feeds unseen near a family's home. The Darkest Delvers free the square from immediate danger, yet the water itself remains unexamined. The well stands as a reminder that the city's apparent safety depends on what lies beneath its familiar streets.[^city]

## The buried city and its forgotten sounds

When the Darkest Delvers finally descend into the southwest well, they discover that the water shaft opens onto natural caverns and old worked stone. Similar traps and signs have been found elsewhere, but their makers remain unknown. Six adventurers unite to activate an underground mechanism, and the floor of Pelor's temple opens over a buried stair. The guild can no longer regard the strange architecture as a remote woodland curiosity: it lies beneath one of the city's oldest places of worship. Teams fill passages leading toward the public well with stone and concrete, yet the water itself remains untested.[^under]

The caverns hold piercers, gricks and a gelatinous predator, along with enormous hook-limbed hunters. The Delvers kill several monsters but discover further threats beyond the sections they have sealed. The guild calls in reserve adventurers, assigns guards to the temple and pays the teams who inspected the wells. Its leaders can offer competing hypotheses about ancient works and unseen threats, not a settled explanation. In the main sanctuary the Delvers uncover a second, narrow shaft whose deep drone seems to coincide with an elf's collapse. The old altar is covered again, leaving the sound and the condition of two unconscious elves mysteries that the city's clerics and guild have yet to resolve.[^under]

## A living shore and a submerged faith

The bronze-ranked guild parties go forth on a different order: not to clear monstrous nests, but to approach intelligent peoples with gifts and request permission to inspect ancient ruins. The Darkest Delvers carry fish and pearls east through farm country into freshwater swamps, where a small catfish-like community lives by fishing, trade and household work. Their simple homes and wary kindness are not the marks of a conquering army. Yet Koh's ability to place words within their minds frightens the villagers into worshipping him as a being from above. He denies being divine, but the misunderstanding endures.[^lake]

The submerged Elven-looking structure beyond their village repeats the passages and traps found beneath the city and the forest. Its ancient ring protects Krandal, and its lower tunnels lead to a kneeling devotee beside dark seepage from a familiar carved stone. The fish priest calls upon the Dunes and speaks of a god sleeping there. Such claims echo Enzo's talk of the deep dark and Koh's alarming experience in his wife's mind, but no one has proved these to be the same realm, force or god. The guild's peaceful mandate now confronts a choice: take the captive home without turning his frightened people into enemies.[^lake]

## The collectors from the marsh

The Delvers' return from the deep ruins reveals an unexpected danger above. Their guide Glepo Ma passes the fish wizard's shell-tipped staff to a black-cloaked rider wearing the skin of a darkmantle as a high cowl. He threatens the quiet settlement and leaves two companions to take whatever the adventurers have found. The Delvers prevail, choosing to keep both strangers alive alongside the captured fish wizard. The larger of the two has hidden a familiar face: when his false features fade, he looks like Broadside, a man encountered around the guild. No one yet knows how the stranger came to wear that face, or to whom he answers.[^collectors]

Glepo Ma tries to make amends by giving the party a gear-crank and a bag of money. He also swims off to warn the guild adventurers farther west, where the rider claims to be heading next. The deeper ruins have yielded a broken circular mechanism and a wand capable of finding hidden things, but the company leaves a great tooth-framed portal unopened. All three living prisoners depart on the Delvers' borrowed horses toward the guild. Their explanations are still to come.[^collectors]
