# Delvers — Season Two: The Forgotten Fortnight

Season Two begins not on a boat or before the door of a dungeon, but with three adventurers returning home. Koh Spiros, Krandal Splenderbelt and Evelyn Grinkeeper walk through Atomer under the midday sun, dressed as though a serpent king had given them a hero's wardrobe. Krandal's metal armor has become a coat of living-looking snake scales. Koh's arrows resemble fossilized snakes, and Evelyn wears heavy golden jewelry patterned with coiling bodies. Helenar Clearstone meets them in astonishment. The Darkest Delvers have been gone for two weeks; the guild feared them dead. They remember setting out for Snake Island and a gigantic serpent rising from the lake. The days between are missing.[^return]

## A kingdom forgotten

Their gifts offer clues but no clear story. Krandal's staff becomes a great python when he casts it to the floor. He remembers waking in a silk-draped bed, setting the python against a viper, and a princess named Hissia calling to him with evident affection. Evelyn remembers being dressed in a flowered cape atop a stepped pyramid, proclaimed queen to a cheering throng of serpentfolk. Koh recalls replacing a precious golden snake idol with a clay copy, only to be confronted by an undead man with a snake living inside his mouth. Each new memory raises questions: who held the power there, what the adventurers promised, and why no one can recall their return.[^memories]

The guild treats the commission as complete and counts out a thousand gold pieces for each of them. Their strange prizes are taken for inspection. Cassius loosens the jewelry that clings to Evelyn, and the necklace and ring prove to have their own protective enchantments. Koh and Krandal also find scraps of letters, one addressed to a best friend and one marked by a green kiss. Krandal remembers a village where a princess was promised to him in return for a stolen idol. Koh pictures the three companions riding enormous snakes toward distant mountains, his own mount saddled specially to keep him aboard. But the bright flashes end as quickly as they come. Even the great fang they have carried home tells them little.[^inspection]

## The lives they left behind

Koh hurries toward his wife and daughter. Before he reaches them, a satyr leaps down from a roof: Chuck Abnaz, known as Nasty, comes from the Cloven Brotherhood, the forgers Koh once worked among. The two weeks of Koh's absence have nearly emptied the money meant to keep his wife under medical care. Chuck has paid the doctors and now demands his reward, along with Koh's return to the Brotherhood. Behind him three other satyrs step from concealment. Koh refuses to resume his former work, but at home he learns that Chuck did indeed provide the money. His daughter greets him gladly and his wife still sleeps; neither comfort has removed the threat.[^koh]

Krandal comes home to a temple that has spent the fortnight sealing the dungeon entrance and guarding the dark stair. Brother Maynard is short of helpers, for fear has driven several priests away. Rianu Keeves lies unawakened in the infirmary, his finger no longer tapping its old code. Krandal heals his bruised body without waking him. Trying a higher magic upon one who has not died, he hears a faint word—*Kalizar*, or something very like it—from the sleeper's lips. He keeps the clue to himself and retires, finding that his mouse friend's old hole has been plastered over. He breaks it open and rejoices when the creature emerges unharmed.[^elf]

Evelyn's absent weeks have placed Margaret Dupayton in a difficult position. Her painted sleeping double has lain ill on the school bed with one invented affliction after another, until reports reach Evelyn's family. A letter from Lord and Lady Blackwood announces the arrival of their physician. Evelyn changes her traveling clothes for something suitable and returns to the dormitory. An old family doctor examines her and finds no sickness, but his curious spectacles reveal a mark bestowed by a goddess of love and passion. When the doctor departs, Evelyn begins to tell Margaret why the discovery troubles her. Their words remain private, for the scene turns away before the tale is told.[^evelyn]

## The old land on the river

The next morning the Delvers receive their inspected possessions, together with the guild's reports and Krandal's long-promised deed to a watchtower near the Noble Hollow. The job board has grown into a great map filled with fresh tasks. The three select a wedding at which a dead grandfather may be determined to appear; they are to keep watch for the Hooper family without ruining the ceremony. Koh buys a ring to protect himself from the falls he seems to suffer so often, and after their private errands the three walk north with Krandal to see the land that may become his farm.[^guild]

Little of the place deserves a farmer's pride yet. Weeds choke two plots and an old irrigation ditch; a cottage has lost its roof, and the tower's walls have broken open toward the river. Still, Krandal sees the soil beneath the weeds and speaks of growing potatoes. As Evelyn approaches the ruined tower, she senses undeath in the scarecrow standing in the field. From within the stone appears a sea hag so frightful that even Evelyn turns away. A skeletal minotaur rises from the earth and steals the scarecrow's skull for its head, denying that any visitor owns the farm.[^tower]

The hag finds a fear Krandal has rarely spoken aloud. She makes him see himself without the light of Pelor, as though his devotion and his life had been a terrible mistake. The vision drives him unconscious. Evelyn takes a crushing blow from the skeleton but reaches Krandal and restores his strength. Koh's psychic blade and new serpent dagger tear the minotaur's head from its body. The hag sends lightning across the field, yet the three stand again and drive her toward the river; Evelyn's hammer and Krandal's shining face mace together end the hag's life. Eels and fish tumble from the empty shell of her body.[^fight]

The fields fall silent, but the minotaur's skull still speaks. It protests the naming of the holding, then suggests that it might at least frighten birds from the crops. Krandal names the land **Potato Grove** and buries the skull while he decides what to do with it. The buildings are still ruins and no potato has yet been planted. Nearby sits the hag's cauldron, and the sight of it brings another Snake Island image: a bonfire, armed little snakes and three bound adventurers facing what might have been their last meal. That memory too breaks apart before it can offer an answer.[^ending]

## The wight wedding

The next afternoon, the wealthy Hoopers prepare to marry their son Limwick to Fresna Goodwine. The Delvers come not as honoured guests but as guards expected to keep a troublesome grandfather's ghost away from the family celebrations. The house is built to halfling measure, its halls crowded with servers and invitations, its gardens filled with dogs, dancers and fine wine. A guest illusionist, the Great Daufante, comes separately at the guild's request with his undead assistant Connor and a spider familiar named Hamlet. In bright robes and a jewel-studded skull mask, he makes a stage of every conversation; he is interested less in modest guild service than in mysterious magic he might acquire.[^wedding]

Koh serves the wedding guests and catches unsettling signs behind the merriment, including dark footprints that appear without a body to make them. Krandal, watching from the tree line, hears of a burial place deep in the woods. The expected white doves fail to appear during the ceremony: Evelyn slips invisibly behind the stage and finds them all dead in their cage. With the quick invention of a frightened hostess, she conjures the immense white goat she has carried as an ivory figurine for weeks, and the crowd cheers its miraculous appearance instead. The wedding goes on, but the woods continue to watch it.[^doves]

Krandal sees red eyes drifting among the leaves and later entering a duck on the ornamental lake. The bird snatches a knife and dives toward old Mr. Goodwine until Krandal strikes it down with sacred flame. After the traditional cornucopia scatters jewels among the trees, Krandal joins the scramble; the riches lead him to an old family graveyard, where no memorial to Grandfather Hooper can be found. Hamlet has overheard servants whispering that Goodwine killed the old man in the long years of trade wars, and that Hooper carried a precious deck of cards into death. Daufante coaxes Goodwine into the woods with false friendship, makes him receptive to enchantment, and elicits a dreadful confession: the elder and his companions beat the grandfather and hung him from a tree, leaving him there without burial.[^confession]

The grandfather now approaches as a withered wight swinging from a noose attached to a walking oak. Krandal shatters the bough and stills the tree; Koh protects the living Goodwine; Evelyn tries to restrain the dead man. Daufante's robe sends a storm of blue stars into the wight and at last knocks it down. The wizard extracts a Deck of Many Things from the body without daring to draw a card. Evelyn demands a burial, Krandal agrees, and even Daufante finds it expedient to assent. The old murderer and Connor lay the corpse in the family earth. Near the roots of the fallen tree Koh sees black sludge, a fresh unanswered hint of the dangers that have followed the Delvers through other ruins.[^rest]

The marriage festivities survive their dangerous secret. Daufante leaves the next day with the deck in exchange for surrendering his portion of the reward to the three adventurers. He gives Evelyn an invitation to visit the theatre and departs before anyone accepts it. The families feast in apparent peace, though the murder that haunted their reconciliation remains a truth known only to a few.[^cutoff]

## A shark beneath the pavement

The city barely settles after the wedding before the guild diviners warn of another visitor. A great bulette is swimming through the soil beneath Atomer, threatening to surface anywhere among its people. The bronze guild companies fan out across the streets; the Darkest Delvers wait in the grand financial quarter near Withers Estate, amid the fountain and obelisk of a little public park. Koh makes a simple warning bell from a dagger and a scrap of string. When its chime rises alongside tremors in the water, Evelyn runs toward the creature and leads it away from the more crowded buildings. The earth bursts open behind her. Koh's twin psychic blades flash in the hard back of the land shark, Krandal's blessing sustains the battle, and Evelyn's enchanted javelin lights the creature with lightning.[^bulette]

The monster leaps above them and crashes onto Krandal, leaving him battered and helpless under its weight. Evelyn seizes its massive cheek and twists its head away from the fallen cleric. From the ground Krandal thrusts his carved serpent staff into an unarmoured eye. The beast breaks free and dives once more, with Evelyn clinging to its fin as it tunnels through the square. It rises again only to die, and the guild's other companies come running at the sound of victory. From the opened jaws Koh gathers teeth to commemorate their work. Edgar Boonefellow claims the fin for his celebratory soup.[^combat]

But the creature has left something more troubling than a broken street. Beside the Withers mansion, a narrow gap leads downward into a clean stone room. Koh climbs into it under Evelyn's watch and finds a great stone slab furnished with leather restraints. Fresh scraps of food and leather suggest another visitor has used the place recently. A heavy door leads toward the mansion but will not open. Withers himself walks out in house clothes and claims his old residence contains many rooms unknown to him. Koh finds this hard to believe, but he does not force the door. The Delvers bring the discovery to Cassius, who accepts their report with more concern than he cares to explain.[^estate]

## A farmer, a daughter and a secret

Two days pass after the guild's victory feast. Krandal returns to his neglected fields and at last begins the work he has so often imagined. He pulls old timbers from the collapsed cottage, clears the earth from the irrigation channels and plants pieces cut from potatoes. A voice rises beneath the soil. Krandal unearths the minotaur's talking skull and builds it a scarecrow's body out of wood and cloth. Placed above the new crop, the skull gratefully accepts a stone to fling at crows. The name **Spud Buds** pleases its farmer for now, though he has not settled on what the property shall be called.[^farm]

Evelyn returns to the quiet duties of finishing school. The Bronze Ball is drawing near, and Lady Vestal expects the students to show the adventurers what civilized hospitality can accomplish. Evelyn signs up for household and servant management, courtship and marriage, and horseback riding, glad of a course that offers practical training rather than ornament. Margaret speaks with her of family pressure, her future marriage and the classes she dreads. Neither girl can escape the duties their families have imagined for them, however different their ambitions may be.[^school]

Koh spends the same days with his wife and daughter. Penelope receives one of the land shark's teeth, supposedly found by her father at market, while her sleeping mother hears a far more honest account of the work Koh has done. He does not risk speaking mind to mind with her again. When Chuck Abnaz appears at his door, the former forger's past becomes impossible to hold outside the house. Chuck has an opportunity: the new hole beside Withers Estate offers a route to costly jade and powerful treasures. He wants Koh to coordinate the thieves by psychic thought and promises to forgive his claimed debt. Koh is tempted, agrees to help, and learns that the financier behind the theft goes by the peculiar name **Suis Sac**.[^koh]

## The job no guild will record

Koh writes a forged guild job card and slowly sees the financier's alias for what it is: **Cassius spelled backward**. At the guild, Cassius admits it freely and brings all three Delvers aside. He suspects that Withers, the financier of many guild endeavours, is gathering forbidden artifacts and that his interest in Skull Keep was not entirely philanthropic. The guildmaster asks the Delvers to work with the Cloven Brotherhood and seek proof from inside the house, hidden behind assumed identities. He will pay for evidence from his own purse, but no official guild mission will record the venture.[^secret]

Koh learns that his old underworld name is **Flatfoot Skippy**. Krandal settles on **Cobra Commander**, a suitable name for a man with a snake staff, and Evelyn names herself **Long Jump Jenny**. Between these preparations Evelyn admits a longer-hidden truth: the family that sent her to finishing school is **Blackwood**, and she is its only daughter. Her friends now understand at least part of the danger she risks by adventuring openly. Cassius lends them magical boots to soften their footfalls and sets the meeting at the exposed passage for half past nine that evening. Carrie takes Koh to find a way to fit such shoes to satyr hooves, leaving Evelyn and Krandal behind in the guild. The door opens and an unfamiliar elf enters before they can leave. For now, the job remains a promise rather than a journey inside Withers's house.[^end]
