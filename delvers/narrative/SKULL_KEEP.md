# Skull Keep and the Dawnless Dunes

## New badges, old stones

The three adventurers returned to the guild expecting an ordinary morning and found themselves admitted to another kind of service. Cassius presented books on the responsibilities of the bronze ranks, urged them to learn what they could before a journey and entrusted Koh with a key to the guild library. They gave back the numbered wooden tokens of their apprenticeship and received the bronze chimera shields by which full adventurers could be known. Their first undertaking beyond the wood ranks would take them north to the ancestral home of the Withers family.[^badge]

Koh made the most of the library, where Carrie Underworld guided him toward what was known of a flying skull wreathed in fire. Helena Clearstone prepared two jars of holy water for the company, insisting that a defeated flameskull would rise again if they did not properly consecrate its remains. Krandal added incense to his supplies, less as a weapon than as a comfort to his faith. Thus armed with both useful knowledge and incomplete answers, they boarded the fine coach driven by Aegis.[^preparation]

Their fellow traveler was Alexandre Vanderbuck Withers, a wealthy and loquacious claimant who was determined to regain his forebears' estate. His house had once taken vacations in the keep by the lake, before some ancient misfortune left it abandoned. He promised them a handsome reward if they took care not to damage its antiques, and warned that the mirrors inside were unsafe. At the end of the day's ride they saw the old home standing on a rocky island, joined to the shore by a bridge. Far above the water, orange lights shone from a green flame that circled its tower without rest.[^arrival]

## The servants who never left

The gate opened upon hedges clipped with extraordinary care and stone buildings untroubled by decay. Evelyn felt the nearness of an undead creature among the bushes, though neither it nor the skull attacked. Koh counseled that they behave like respectful visitors until given reason not to. They entered the main house in that spirit, but Krandal's equipment clipped a carved chair and broke part of its frame. A skeletal tradesman emerged from a corridor carrying repair tools, and set to work as if the house had never ceased to be occupied. When Koh tried to apologize, the workman turned its head, shrieked and attacked. Evelyn shattered it with her hammer.[^servants]

More movement came from the neighboring rooms. Koh barred one door with a dagger and the company climbed toward the tower. A carved stone entrance stood behind a strange control whose movements brought bells and clicks, and Evelyn's curiosity summoned the flameskull to the window. Fire spread through the chamber. Koh tried to bind the skull with Sultan's climbing sash, but his leap carried him past the creature and against the outside wall. He fell into the courtyard below, battered rather than broken, while his companions sheltered from the flames.[^first-attack]

The house went on with its routine around them. A gardener clipped bushes near Koh's landing; a carpenter came to replace singed carpet, oblivious to the danger. The company wandered through unused bedrooms and a vast dining hall before discovering three preserved sheets of paper concealed in an old writing desk. Some of their signs matched those Koh had copied from the grick-infested ruins. Another showed a doorway; the third concealed a sequence that opened the stone entrance above. After a second skirmish with the skull, Krandal called down water to extinguish a moving ball of flame, and steam covered their escape into the tower staircase.[^riddle]

## The voice beyond the mirror

At the top of the stairs they found an alchemist's chamber. In the chair before a tall mirror sat a headless body in old finery, yet its reflection wore the face of a bearded lord. He named himself Enzo Vanderbuck Withers, and spoke with the exhausted fascination of someone who had lived much too long without company. He said that his search for immortality had failed, splitting his mind between the mirror and a flying skull and giving a grotesque semblance of life to the servants below.[^enzo]

Enzo did not immediately answer the company's questions. He wished to know what made them risk death. Krandal gave him honest reasons: the work, the honor of Pelor, the money and the promise of another adventure. Evelyn admitted that she was searching for someone, but would say no more. Koh spoke of a healing that remained beyond ordinary magic. He described a person sometimes conscious, sometimes withdrawn into a stillness from which no healer had restored them. Enzo could only guess at causes, perhaps hidden in blood or the health of the world itself. Koh listened, keeping to himself whom he meant.[^motives]

The copied signs interested the dead lord more than he had expected. He spoke of the sky people, those who had fled the depths, and of the **Dawnless Dunes** beneath the world. In his telling, a heart within that darkness had begun to wake, driving other creatures toward the surface. He hoped the change would be witnessed soon, though he was trapped here unable to make the journey himself. The three had heard too much to dismiss his warning, but nothing to prove it true.[^prophecy]

## An end to the flame

Enzo had little desire to continue his half-life. He invited the Delvers to climb above him and defeat the skull that was also himself. Evelyn led the way into the cold air at the tower's summit. She consecrated her hammer with light and sought to hold the creature's attention. Krandal called rain and radiant force, while Koh's arrows glanced against the hard bone. When the fight drew close, Koh changed weapons. His blue Soul Knife passed into the skull as if it had no solid substance to resist, striking instead at whatever mind held it aloft. The flames went out. He caught the fallen remains, and Evelyn poured holy water over them until ash washed away, leaving only two fiery-colored garnets.[^battle]

The keep was quiet on their descent. Enzo's image lingered only faintly behind the glass, and the servants who had guarded and repaired his home lay still. The three returned to Alexandre and Aegis after nightfall, their task complete though the estate had suffered damage. They had not learned all that lay beneath the lake, nor whom Evelyn was seeking, nor whether Enzo's strange intuition could lead Koh to a cure. Those questions would travel home with them. For now, **the Darkest Delvers had completed their first bronze assignment**.[^return]
