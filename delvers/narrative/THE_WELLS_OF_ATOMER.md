# The Wells of Atomer

## Lives beyond the guild

The Darkest Delvers' new bronze rank brought more than prestigious work. Ordinary assignments continued to carry them through the city, and each adventurer returned to a life that few of the others fully understood. Koh gained an unexpected mentor in Buck Broscale, called Wildfire. In the quiet training yard behind the guild, the silver-ranked warrior taught Koh to speak through thought alone. He spoke of his brother, who had died carrying the globe Koh had recovered, and of strange psychic relics that could bring danger to those capable of using them. Koh learned to shape his powers more deliberately, though his teacher's warnings left him with unanswered questions.[^koh]

Krandal's duties were no less surprising. A cockatrice had turned him to stone during one of the week's quests, and he awoke at Pelor's temple as the spell wore off. Before he had fully recovered, Brother Maynard summoned him to lead a wedding at the governor's mansion. Krandal tried to keep the words of the ceremony in order and struggled with the lines he had written on his forearm. The prayer nevertheless found its blessing, leaving the newly married pair with radiant rings. At school, Evelyn found a different kindness: Margaret, her trusted roommate, gave her a small enchanted bag able to conceal her great hammer. Together they prepared for the demands of a forthcoming bronze ball, where guild and finishing school would meet.[^companions]

## Rumors from underground

Cassius called the bronze adventurers to a meeting. Carrie Underworld had marked reports of strange creatures across the city, noting that one kind seemed to follow another toward the surface. The patterns troubled her: stirges, darkmantles, piercers and gricks were appearing in places where they had not been expected. She wondered what could be following them. The strange dark ichor found on other assignments was another cause for unease. The guild divided the city's wells among several parties. The Darkest Delvers chose the southwestern quarter, where recent monsters had already given them reason to be wary.[^meeting]

The well stood in a sunny square beside homes, shops and a blacksmith's forge. Koh befriended a boy and asked him to shout if he saw peril. Evelyn kept watch while Krandal discovered a trail of rust leading away from the water bucket. Following it into the forge, Koh found a bizarre insect whose feelers ruined metal. Nearby, a barrel in a warehouse revealed itself to be a living monster. In the fight that followed Koh's old stone-metal sword hilt crumbled, but the blue blade he conjured endured through the strength of his own mind. Evelyn overcame the strange barrel creature, and the three rescued an injured workman and Bruno, the family's exhausted dog.[^square]

## Beneath the wells

They returned to Cassius with evidence that monsters had reached the neighborhood. Yet their report left something important undone. A stone thrown into the darkness had proved little about what lay below or the safety of the water above. Cassius sent the Brigand Patrol to guard the square, and the Darkest Delvers hurried back carrying a sack of concrete. Passing Pelor's temple, they saw another guild party treating the public well to a spectacular burst of fire. It did little to reassure them.[^cutoff]

Koh tied his enchanted climbing sash to the well frame and descended into the cold shaft, his glowing globe following behind. A deep wound in the masonry opened upon a natural cave. Evelyn struggled down after him with the heavy sack, and Krandal brought up the rear. The passage twisted through wet stone and narrowed until even Krandal had to crawl. Evelyn smashed a nest of young rust monsters, and the company fought off stirges spilling from a hanging sack. Deeper still, they met a little stone creature who spoke in broken Primordial. Krandal shared his potato with the rock eater, winning an improbable friend and an anxious warning whose meaning remained unclear.[^descent]

Beyond the creature's home the rough cave joined worked stone. Its old symbols and cruel traps were eerily familiar: another remnant like the forest ruin where the Delvers had encountered the gricks. Here they met the First Sons Brotherhood, who had already slain a darkmantle and felt entitled to explore first. Edgar Boonefellow urged civility while his companions disputed the claim. Evelyn and Krandal pressed on, and the two parties agreed, uneasily, to let the Darkest Delvers lead. Koh leapt over opening floor traps and pinned a needle-trap switch with an arrow; the others followed with considerable care, leaving a few burdens behind until the passage could be crossed.[^rivals]

## The door beneath the sanctuary

In an old armory the Delvers found long rune-marked shafts that answered Koh's mental power with shining spearheads. A stone horn waited in a chest. Evelyn blew it, and the blast struck her and Krandal with terrible force, robbing them briefly of their hearing. Koh's quiet mind-to-mind speech guided them through the pain. After resting they ventured up another stairway, only to find six stone platforms, each fitted with a socket for the ancient spears. The mechanism required more weight and more people than they could provide alone. Koh called the First Sons back, and their combined company took its places upon the six platforms.[^armory]

The stone above them split. Not into an imagined portal but through the very floor of Pelor's temple, where the sanctuary's furnishings shifted and startled monks scrambled aside. Brother Maynard stared down at Krandal and his companions with utter disbelief. The adventurers had uncovered a hidden connection beneath the city, and damaged the temple in the process. First they had a more immediate obligation: together the two guild parties carried stone and concrete back into the narrow passages and sealed the connections leading toward the southwestern well. The friendly rock eater lent his strength. The city's drinking water had still not been tested, and the tunnels beyond the buried ruin remained a mystery.[^temple]

Instead of abandoning that mystery, the six adventurers searched further. They entered a spacious wet cavern where shapes on the ceiling proved to be waiting piercers. An odd patch of air beyond the pool seemed to lift scattered pebbles from the floor. Evelyn extended a javelin, felt resistance and discovered what the light had hidden: a gelatinous cube filled the passage ahead. The creature had not yet struck. The deeper threat was only beginning to show itself.[^cube]

## Shapes that stir below

The cube crawled toward the combined guild party, and the cavern erupted into an awkward fight beneath its watchful ceiling. Piercers tore loose from the stone, while a grick rose behind the adventurers and began dragging away one of the fallen monsters. Within moments the sorcerer Faelan was swallowed by the cube. Koh sent blades and arrows through its quivering body, and Krandal struggled with a piercer in the shallow water. Evelyn's lightning javelin finally split the gelatinous prison, freeing Faelan alive. The grick escaped into another passage, clutching its prey. The creatures they had defeated were not all that lived below the city.[^fighting]

A strange beaked head appeared from the shadows. Koh recognized the old name *hook horror* in his guild guide, but little was known about it. The Delvers chose retreat over an unpaid hunt. As soon as they turned, the creature lunged. Krandal stumbled on the wet ground, and Evelyn ran back to shield him. Its enormous hook caught her before Krandal could pull her clear; his healing light lessened the wound. Evelyn raised the horn they had found among the ancient weapons and blew it a second time. The blast staggered the horror and robbed it of its uncanny ability to find them. Koh's arrow struck deep into its open beak, and the giant fell. He returned for a hooked limb as proof of the encounter, only to glimpse another of its kind approaching the fallen body. It bent to the corpse while he slipped away.[^horror]

## The forgotten voice

They reached the temple through the underground stairway once more. Brother Maynard grieved over the damage to his ancient sanctuary, but Carrie Underworld was already inspecting it on the guild's behalf. The Delvers offered her their newly gained trophies: the immense hook, a bottle of cube gelatin, one strange spear and Evelyn's blasting horn. At the guild, Cassius heard reports from the well patrols and called up reserve adventurers to guard the discovered entrance. He promised that the Delvers would not bear the cost of the temple's damaged floor and paid them for their work. Yet the underground system defied the guild's efforts to understand it. It had remained hidden even from their searches. Cassius entrusted the party with a new mission for the following day: an old ruin occupied by the strange fish-people known as kuo-toa.[^guild]

Before resting, the three returned to help Carrie search Pelor's temple. In the main sanctuary Evelyn found a familiar pattern under plaster and wall hangings. Behind the altar, concealed in old stone and hidden by a wooden lectern, Krandal uncovered a narrow, dark shaft. From far below came the same uneasy drone the Delvers remembered from the earliest ruins. One elf among the worshippers collapsed. A small enchanted stone vanished into the darkness without revealing any bottom, and the party carefully restored the wooden cover until the guild could return with a proper plan. The temple had concealed more than an accidental door to the caves.[^altar]

That evening, each Delver carried the day into a different life. Koh reclaimed his climbing sash from the well and bought studded armor, while the three prepared water-breathing potions for the approaching lake journey. Evelyn acquired an ivory figurine said to summon a giant goat, though she did not yet call it forth. Back at school, Margaret tried to help her solve a problem neither monster nor blade could settle: a guild expedition clashed with tomorrow's formal ball planning. No workable disguise had been arranged.[^evening]

Koh went home to Penelope, who proudly showed him a drawing of her father as a dagger-bearing adventurer. He made his discoveries sound like fanciful stories, still concealing the truth of his work. Then he sat with his unresponsive wife, quietly holding her hand. For the first time he wondered whether the silent speech Wildfire had taught him might reach her, but his mental strength for that day was spent. He could only promise himself that he would try.[^kohhome]

Krandal returned to the temple, where Brother Maynard sought forgiveness for his anger and revealed another distress. Two elves lay motionless despite the priests' ministrations. One, the man who should have conducted the wedding Krandal had taken over, tapped a faint recurring rhythm with his finger. Krandal could not read it but kept a careful record. The other elf gave no sign of answering. Seeing the cleric's earnest concern, Maynard entrusted him with an ancient amulet of Pelor's devotion, which Krandal accepted and placed around his neck. The unopened tunnel, the silent elves and the words that might yet be spoken to the unconscious remained unanswered as night approached.[^elves]

## A voice beyond waking

At the first light of the following morning, Koh made his choice. Sitting beside his wife's still body, he tried the silent speech Wildfire had taught him. Instead of the fleeting thoughts of a companion, he found himself rushing through darkness toward her. She sat beside a single flower, and when she saw him she knew his face. They embraced. Her voice carried fear as well as relief: she insisted that whoever held her must not learn of Koh's visit, and that everyone in the town would be safe only while she remained. A low, terrible drone swelled about them as drifting sand became towering dunes. Koh was driven from the vision and awoke beside her bed. In her clenched hands he found a few grains of **black sand**, real enough to gather in a vial. He left for the guild with renewed hope and an unanswered dread.[^vision]

Krandal, too, had been awake with a puzzle. The rhythmic taps of the sleeping elf had led him through old military books toward a word in the Elven lantern code. He could make out its sounds but not its sense. On the ride east, Evelyn listened and recognized an old word for a funeral song, a dirge. Koh quietly admitted that he had heard of another person who could not be awakened by even powerful ministrations. No one yet understood whether the two mysteries were one, or whether they only echoed each other.[^code]

## The strangers at the lake

The guild had sent its bronze adventurers not to slaughter their strange neighbors but to seek ancient ruins without making enemies. The Darkest Delvers carried fish and pearls to a village of little catfish-like people living along the freshwater lake. Koh's gift for silent speech made their first contact less simple than he hoped. The villagers took his voice within their minds for the voice of a god from above. Though he protested, they knelt before him. An eccentric trader called Glepo Ma spoke the common tongue and offered another bargain: information about the ruins in exchange for a shovel, and a request that they retrieve a staff from beneath the water. He promised to watch their horses while they explored.[^shore]

Koh narrowly escaped a treasure chest that was actually a slow-swimming mimic. Leaving heavy armor and equipment onshore, the three drank potions that let them breathe beneath the lake. Under its weeds and ruined stone they found the same turns, hidden mechanisms and deadly corridors they had encountered before. In an old chest Koh discovered a ring that answered his mental touch and protected its wearer from harm. He offered it to Krandal, whose great hands made the little band seem frail until it grew to fit his finger. The old spear shafts remained undisturbed as the group swam deeper.[^dive]

A flooded pit led them below the familiar rooms into another cavern, where a catfish-like ritualist knelt before an ancient carving and a dark stain spreading through the stone. He met their appearance with a whirling torrent that nearly dragged Koh into the depths. Krandal ordered the stranger to the ground, while Koh climbed the enchanted sash to escape the current; Evelyn turned the whirlpool's own force into a flying charge. They restrained rather than killed the shaken spellcaster, who begged them to listen. He spoke of the **Dunes**, where a god slept, and of his desire to reach that god again. The Delvers could not know whether this was the truth or a frightened devotee's belief. They bound him carefully to Krandal's back, choosing to return him to his people for judgment. Yet the way to the sunlit shore was still far above, and the water-breathing magic would not last forever.[^wizard]

## The turning stone and the hidden city

Before leaving the cavern, the Delvers looked again upon the shrine where their prisoner had prayed. The familiar three hills and spiral of the Dawnless Dunes had been cut through solid stone, and black slime still trickled from the darkness behind it. Koh recognized the wizard's strange stone-metal wand as one that could point to secrets deliberately concealed. When he directed it toward the carving, it trembled in his hand. Evelyn worked her fingers through the sticky opening and drew out a thick quarter-circle of ancient metal and stone. Little gears ran around its curved edge, and a place for another gear lay empty. The wizard said he had brought this piece from still deeper chambers.[^disc]

The potion's dwindling magic made the return a matter of minutes, yet curiosity led them down a flooded passage toward the drone that seemed to rise through the water itself. They emerged into an old market of weathered arches, collapsed woven goods and stone furniture. Something crunched in one of the rooms. Koh crept closer and saw a floating brainlike beast drawing stirges into the beak protruding from its front. The creature was quick to attack, curling barbed tentacles around Evelyn and Koh. Evelyn fought its jaws open with her bare hands, Krandal fired his new crossbow and Koh's blades finally brought it down. The great beak became a gruesome pair of trophies lashed to Krandal's shoulders.[^beast]

A fountain adorned with the head of a hook horror offered another curiosity: an ancient stoppered bottle pressed against its drain. Krandal opened it and released a flood of smoke that filled the chamber until he sealed it again. At the far end of the old tunnels Koh and Evelyn caught sight of a still larger mystery, a sealed stone door beneath a snarling face and steps carved as rows of teeth. But there was no time to force an entrance. The three returned to the water, swam toward daylight with their prisoner and broke the surface just as their breathing magic began to fail.[^fountain]

## The bargain on the shore

Glepo Ma was busy entertaining the fish people upon the Delvers' horses when the travelers came back. Their gifts of fish and pearls had helped make a festival of the morning. The little trader, however, grew pale at the sight of the captive wizard. He rushed the adventurers into his hut, and they found among his wares a stone gear-crank that seemed to belong with the piece just recovered below. Before they could decide what to ask in payment, another visitor arrived.[^return]

Through the straw walls they saw a black horse and a robed rider crowned with the dried body of a darkmantle, its eyes set with red glass. He demanded the wizard's conch-shell staff. Glepo Ma, trembling, passed it to him; the rider threatened the entire village and rode away toward the bullywug settlement, ordering two armed companions to seize anything the adventurers brought from the ruins. The Delvers stayed hidden long enough to understand the danger. Koh slipped silently out behind the stranger's robed accomplice and brought her down without killing her. A broad, heavily armored warrior with a saw-toothed blade and a shield carved in the same tooth motif as the underground door then turned to fight them.[^rider]

The battle dragged across the beach. Krandal threw divine flames and called forth a shining version of his face mace, which flew after the warrior as if guided by his hand. Koh dodged behind huts, striking where he could, while Evelyn met the relentless shield and blade with her warhammer and javelins. Krandal's spectral mace finally knocked the figure down. He saved the enemy's life; as the others stripped off weapons and armor, the warrior's face lost a layer of magic and settled into the familiar features of **Broadside**, the fighter once seen around the guild. The discovery gave them reason to question much more than the two strangers' orders.[^battle]

They resolved to take all three captives alive to the guild. Glepo Ma, ashamed of the bargain that had endangered his neighbors, let the Delvers select a heavy bag of gold and the stone gear-crank from his shelves. They sent him swimming west to warn the guild adventurers bound for the bullywug camp. With the captive fish wizard, the robed caster and the apparent Broadside secured on their borrowed horses, the Delvers rode from the lakeshore toward home. The messenger's warning had not yet arrived, nor had the guild heard the strange news from beneath the lake.[^departure]
