# The Darkest Delvers — The First Arc

Three strangers enter the Adventurers' Guild in Atomer with little in common beyond a wish to work. The satyr Koh Spiros needs money for a family whose comfortable life is slipping away. Krandal Splenderbelt serves Pelor with boundless enthusiasm and imagines a modest potato farm. Evelyn Grinkeeper has the strength and convictions of a knight, though her schooling demands that she appear to be a very different young woman. Their first small jobs bring them into a world of stone ruins, strange creatures and old powers beneath the earth. Their rise through the guild does not solve the mystery. It merely allows them to understand how large it may be.[^boundary]

## 1. The guild's smallest promises

Their first meeting at the guild begins with Evelyn frightening a horse and Krandal trying to keep the morning's schedule intact. Cassius gives them wooden badges and an assignment to protect a sheep farmer from stirges. They descend through a fissure where living shadows and disguised stones attack from above. Krandal's faith keeps his companions fighting, Koh's eye for danger leads them away from a serpentine threat below, and Evelyn's force closes the cave. They return with trophies, the badge of a dead adventurer and an enchanted globe that follows Koh, earning their first wages. In the guild's ordinary bustle they discover how easily heroism becomes work: items must be accounted for, reports submitted, and the next appointment made. They are not yet famous. Their reward is the chance to keep going.[^first]

## 2. The mountains, the crypt and a third job

Their next journey takes them to Kord's mountain sanctuary, where the boundary between devotion and stubborn strength is harder to distinguish than any temple priest might prefer. The training halls and fountains have been disturbed, and a deadly contest has left bodies among the equipment. The Delvers restore what they can, make new allies and emerge with the remarkable climbing sash whose magic often proves more useful than any ordinary rope. They also learn the price of keeping one another alive when a blow meant for one companion finds another. Their third assignment carries them among gricks, ancient traps and a forest dweller who knows the surrounding wilds better than any guide in town. Here a broken-looking weapon hilt seems to call to Koh with a power he has never understood. An adventure that began with pest removal has revealed workmanship and dangers that do not belong to any simple natural cave.[^early]

## 3. Lives outside the badges

For a time they return to their other duties. Krandal serves the sick at Pelor's temple, where kindness and impropriety coexist beneath the elder's watchful eye. He nurses an injured bird and gains the trust of a dragonborn veteran. Evelyn slips between the formality of a young lady's school and the freedom of traveling with weapons on her back; her friend Margaret helps conceal a second life that might be forbidden if exposed. Koh returns to a house where his daughter is growing up and his wife does not wake. Medical attention drains his resources, and the guild wages mean more to him than gold for its own sake.

The old hilt changes his prospects and makes them more dangerous. Carrie Underworld calls it a Soul Knife, and in Koh's hand it manifests a blade of light that strikes the mind. A masked visitor recognizes him as a scion and warns that others seek the same hidden power. The Delvers still understand little of what they have found, but their private reasons for taking risks have become inseparable from the mystery of old ruins.[^lives]

## 4. The road to bronze

The guild will not promote them merely because they survived the forest. They must show that they can protect the city and work among ordinary neighbors. Across a busy stretch of local assignments the trio fights strange creatures in markets, streets, drains and workshops. A supposed monster turns out to be a foolish rumour; a real danger is concealed in a plate of tempting tarts; a burning building demands rescue rather than battle. Their efforts build relationships with artificers, priests, guild staff and senior adventurers who come to recognize their strengths.

Krandal still counts what he needs for his farm. Evelyn finds enchantments and unusual gear that help her manage her two lives. Koh balances new abilities with the pressure of his family's secret troubles. When their service is recognized, the three settle upon the name **the Darkest Delvers** and finally earn the bronze rank. Their first substantial commission as a stronger company lies at an old keep beside the lake.[^bronze]

## 5. The lord who could not leave

Skull Keep remains unnaturally tidy, watched over by dead servants who maintain its old routines. Its human occupants have long disappeared, but a flameskull still circles the tower. Hidden writing and dangerous rooms lead the party to the mirror-bound remnant of Enzo Vanderbuck Withers, a lord trapped by his own search for immortality. He has little wish to continue his divided existence. He speaks instead of peoples who fled a deeper world and of the **Dawnless Dunes**, where a sleeping heart may be drawing underground predators toward the surface. His words disturb the party, but there is no proof that they describe the world truly.

In the tower the three fight the flaming skull, and Koh's mental blade brings its final defeat. Holy water prevents an immediate return. The keep's animated servants fall still and Enzo's reflection dims. Their contract ends with an uneasy victory: a dead lord may finally rest, while his account of what lives beneath the lake seems to connect the guild's earlier mysteries.[^keep]

## 6. Under the city's feet

Around Atomer monsters begin appearing where ordinary people work and sleep. The guild sends bronze companies to inspect public wells, and the Delvers confront threats in a busy square before descending the southwestern shaft. Beneath the water they find natural caves joined to the same patterned architecture they have already seen in the forest. With the assistance of another bronze squad, they activate a mechanism that opens the floor of Pelor's temple above them.

The cavern passage is sealed against the public well, though the safety of the water itself is not proved. More monsters remain in unexplored places. A separate shaft under the temple's main altar carries a deep drone, and two elves collapse into a silence even the healers cannot break. Krandal finds a rhythm in one sleeper's fingers; Evelyn tentatively connects its coded letters to a funeral song. At home, Koh finally reaches his unconscious wife through silent speech. He finds her conscious in a realm of darkness and dunes, where she begs him to leave before others discover him. When he returns, black sand falls from her fists. He keeps a few grains, and the private mystery begins to resemble the city's wider affliction.[^wells]

## 7. The lake and the broken machine

The next guild order calls for diplomacy before violence. The Darkest Delvers bring fish and pearls to a settlement of freshwater kuo-toa, where Koh's telepathy is mistaken for divine speech. They make a bargain with a trader named Glepo Ma and descend beneath the lake to inspect ruins that echo the other dungeons. Krandal receives a protective ring; a fish ritualist attacks beside a carving of the Dawnless Dunes. The party subdues him alive, and he claims a god sleeps in those depths. A wand taken from him reveals a hidden fragment of an old geared disc behind the black seepage. The Delvers find an abandoned market, a floating beaked hunter and a fog-making bottle. A vast door framed with teeth remains closed when their underwater breathing begins to fail.

They reach the surface with their captive, only to discover that the trader has passed the ritualist's staff to a mounted stranger wearing the hide of a darkmantle. His two companions try to rob them. The Delvers prevail without killing either attacker, and one man's altered face resolves into Broadside, a silver-rank fighter known from the guild. They leave the village with prisoners, a matching-looking gear crank, and the suspicion that the guild itself has become entangled in the darkness below.[^lake]

## 8. The Noble Hollow

The road home brings a fresh cry for help. Boonefellow, a wounded ally from the First Sons Brotherhood, has escaped an ambush at the bullywug village. Faelan has been seized and Norman has vanished. The Delvers send their prisoners safely to the guild in a carriage, follow the tracks as far as they can, and return for the truth of the matter. In the guild's magic-bound interrogation room Broadside and the robed Cheet reveal that the darkmantle-cowled rider is Marek, a researcher who has gone too far into old summoning lore. A high elf is to be sacrificed at midnight beside the petrified tree called the Noble Hollow. Broadside offers to help save the colleague who fell under the same influence as himself, and Boonefellow joins the rush north.

At the green-lit hollow they find Faelan inside a ritual circle and Norman bound nearby. Broadside restrains Solburn while the Delvers break the rite. Evelyn brings Marek down without killing him, but when the ancient disc shatters the earth collapses into a cavern below. A huge roper emerges, producing the piercers seen throughout their earlier adventures. Krandal calls on Pelor to force it to release his friends and even heals Solburn, once an enemy. Koh wounds its great eye; Evelyn strikes the place where its young emerge, killing the enormous creature. Faelan and Norman are saved, though a fallen cultist is swallowed by the monster and never seen again.[^hollow]

## After the first arc

Several days later the guild has heard their whole story. The senior officer Selenar Everbloom acknowledges their courage and clears their names. Broadside and Solburn are treated as men who acted under a darker influence; Marek faces a future trial and Cheet is to receive rehabilitation. Yet the guild has no extra money to pay, consumed as it is by a northern war against vampires. Krandal is instead promised the deed to an old tower in the farmlands, a place that may one day serve his long-held dream. The bronze ball approaches, the ancient door beneath the lake has never opened, Koh's wife still sleeps, and the carved symbols and dark sand remain unexplained.

For now the three Delvers are neither untested recruits nor heroes whose work is finished. The guild's hall returns to its noisy routine, until a short bearded visitor arrives with buckets full of coins and snakes and demands that someone take the **Snake Island** job. The offer hangs in the air as this first chapter of their shared lives closes.[^end]
