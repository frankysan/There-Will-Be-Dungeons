# Season Three — After the Broken Ball

The Bronze Ball has become the city's worst nightmare. By the hour when the guests once expected to be home, the governor's ballroom lies breached and half-forgotten creatures are still emerging from the earth. The Delvers spend the last dark hours helping hold the damage at bay, but their guild is already turning inward: Cassius is under investigation, the heroes have been grounded, and a new inquiry threatens everything they have done in secret.

For Evelyn, the catastrophe brings an unexpected parting. Having escorted young women from the ruined ballroom, she hears Lady Vestal name two schoolgirls still missing. The mysterious Gentleman Cavalier Traveler stays beside her just long enough to explain the curse he believes is consuming him. Somewhere beneath Minotaur Mesa, he says, the evil heart that ruined his companions may still wait. Evelyn promises to find it. She refuses his tentative kiss, but not the quest; then his crimson portal closes and he is gone.

Koh takes his recovered wife back into the family home he nearly lost. His relief lasts only until the first dawn and the guild's prohibition. Told they cannot draw official work or pay, the Delvers nonetheless investigate the damaged bank. There Koh quietly empties wealthy people's deposit boxes, including a gem-rich hoard belonging to Alexandre Withers. Krandal watches in disbelief, drawing a firm line between obstructing an enemy and claiming another person's property. Upstairs, their attempt to make the bank safe turns into a battle with a hook horror and the discovery of its still-living egg. Krandal insists this creature may yet be given a different future. He calls it Samuel and carries it away.

When the guild finds the party clearing more of the wrecked ballroom, the grounding becomes enforceable. Lady Vestal takes Evelyn back toward the school, but her reproach opens into a revelation. The institution founded to train heroic young women has gradually shed its martial purpose. Beneath its fountain survives a secret passage, and at its end a stone door bears the mark of its founder, Loran Ilfelkyr. The heart-shaped hilt that Vestal placed in Evelyn's hands at the ball comes to life when Evelyn remembers the good she has done. Its blade lengthens with her self-belief, then falters at the door. She has found an inheritance, but not yet the confidence or power to claim everything behind it. Before dawn she leaves messages for her companions and sets out to face the family who still do not know her true life.

The farm also attracts visitors. An imposing serpent-bodied royal guard says Princess Hissia was taken below Atomer and swears to serve Krandal until she is rescued; in the meantime he puts his strength to work digging the long-delayed irrigation ditch. Then old Mr Goodwine and his son arrive with more money than Krandal ever expected to see, offering an enormous expansion to his potato enterprise. He will take their agricultural book and a plow, but he refuses to promise them ownership, profit or control before he knows what they truly want.

Koh has his own old associates to face. He takes the bank gems to the Cloven Brotherhood, asking them to convert the loot into money and keep away from his family. The conversation lays bare how little the old criminal world has released its claim on him. Yet back at the guild, behind a locked door, the greater confession is to his daughter. The merchant she thought she knew is a Silver League hero called One Shot, and the dragon scale he gave her came from a beast he helped defeat.

That ability to reach minds becomes the guild's reason to let him help once more. At Pelor's temple he joins Krandal to enter seven tormented elves' dream worlds, one after another, and free each with a curse-removing prayer. It is a small, genuine victory against a darkness still holding many more. The Delvers end their first episode of the new season separated: Evelyn traveling toward an uncertain reckoning, Krandal still tending the strange promises gathering around his farm, and Koh finally beginning the work of recovering the people left behind.

The separation lasts longer than anyone had hoped. While Evelyn's carriage rolls westward through five days of increasingly crowded roads toward smoky Vermicity, Koh and Krandal settle into a daily rotation of rescuing Khalazar's broken victims. Each day their spells relieve another seven; each following day more bewildered elves arrive. Koh clings to the new freedom of evenings with his wife and daughter. Krandal returns to the farm and tries to convert its rutted fields into a proper livelihood. He buys a reddish donkey called Sean, accepts Gleepo Ma's unlikely frog acquaintance Muck Muck as a trial worker, and sets his Snake Island guard to plowing. He has not sold the farm to the eager Goodwines, but the plow they supplied has become indispensable.

In Vermicity, the Blackwoods' elegant home feels smaller than the road. Evelyn's parents have preserved every comfort for their supposedly fragile daughter, but none of it answers what she has become. Their plan is to marry her to Julian Thomas, a prosperous family's heir with more interest in combining fortunes than knowing the woman before him. She refuses. She tries to explain the blessing she carries, the companions who need her and the person she has sworn to find; her parents reach instead for the familiar explanation of illness and impose ever stricter supervision. Her letters pass beneath her mother's searching eye as she quietly asks Margaret to reach the Delvers.

The city around Koh is changing too. A stone barrier rises through the markets and along the shattered financial district, taking his old house into the threatened quarter. In Carrie's laboratory a more personal mystery grows. Using the red shard recovered from Khalazar's dream and blood Anathala willingly gives for research, Carrie constructs an amulet that shines in the presence of certain afflicted elves. Her explanation for why Khalazar chose Anathala rests on a theory about her ancient family; Koh receives the object without mistaking theory for proof. One evening outside his endangered home the jewel blazes. He finds a half-conscious captive of the darkness digging into the soil, knocks the victim down with a deliberately nonlethal psychic blow, and carries the unconscious elf to safety. The next day the victim wakes lucid. Their work at the temple has not been wasted, but Koh begins to understand that the darkness has different depths, and that not every case must be reached by the same means.

The farm yields its own, darker lesson. Poor early growth convinces Krandal to spend real money on a proper barn where potatoes can be readied before planting. His serpent guard throws himself into the construction and leaves decorative snake heads on the roof. The new shoots at last look promising—until Muck Muck warns of a stranger at the edge of Noble Hollow. The figure is gaunt, naked and strangely desiccated, yet it throws magical bolts and suffocating black tendrils into the crops. Krandal fights it alone, demands an explanation and hears the answer directly in his head: someone has promised a reward for his death. The bat insignia of Khalazar marks the creature. When the cleric's face maces finally smash its head, tiny spiders scatter from within. Carrie can identify the body's mummified elven remains and deliberate surgeries, but even she cannot identify the one who ordered the attack.

Julian's confidence in Evelyn proves no match for her determination. She refuses his renewed proposal as readily as the first, and her family shuts her into her room. The crisis that finally persuades her parents does not arrive in a gentleman's calling card. A vampire forces the window open, intending to charm and feed on the young noblewoman. Evelyn takes up her warhammer, resists his glamour and fights him in her nightclothes, wounding him with radiant blows. Her parents witness her drive the creature into the street beneath a thrown javelin. A higher-ranked vampire slayer later finishes it, but the Blackwoods have already seen what their daughter is. They watch her heal her wounds with light and finally concede that her heroic life is real and hers to choose. She summons a celestial elk, promises to visit again and rides back toward Atomer.

Before she arrives, Koh makes one further journey into familiar woodland. He persuades the reclusive gnome Veggie Sticks to help Carrie improve the glowing amulet, and the two speed back through living trees. The completed charm can now indicate a direction as well as a presence. The feral elves have not vanished, but the special hunting duty pays Koh a modest stipend, and he moves his family to a smaller house near the guild. Krandal's barn and fields are flourishing. Meanwhile anonymous stories of three valiant heroes begin appearing around town, praising Cassius, though the master remains under investigation and the Delvers remain grounded.

Three months after the ruined ball, the separated companions converge on an Atomer transformed. A wall encloses the danger beneath the financial district, while arriving Silver League adventurers fill inns, buildings and tents. A herald proclaims that the city is now a gateway to the underground ruins, offers sponsored dungeon raids and names the new rules for licences, expeditions and restricted districts. Evelyn rides in on her elk, Koh moves through the crowd watching for enchanted stragglers, and Krandal has come in for supplies. The darkness has not been banished; it has become the reason thousands are gathering at their door.

The crowd has drawn an entirely different kind of adventurer to Atomer. Beyond the new wall, the presses of *The Daily Quest* thunder all day, converting expeditions, losses and advertisements into salable news. Stories about the three grounded Delvers interest editor Chroma even more than the latest Silver fatalities beneath the city: the guild has forbidden adventures to heroes who, according to an unattributed scrap of prophecy the office purchased from a ratlike informant, might be uniquely important to Atomer's fate. Chroma sends for a veteran correspondent, an elderly halfling with a wide top hat, a duffel of journalistic equipment, a never-ending appetite and a sharp instinct for a story. Dumbledwark of Raisinbunch has returned to the trade.

He first finds Koh in the newly purchased family home. Anathala invites him to tea, but Koh has spent too long hiding from his former companions in crime to answer every question a stranger brings through the door. He speaks kindly of his absent guildmaster and acknowledges the grounding, yet refuses the journalist's suggestion that a truth spell might ease the interview. Dumbledwark senses the caution, takes a photograph and proceeds onward. At Loran Ilfelkyr's Finishing School, Lady Vestal seems no happier to receive a correspondent until he offers to entertain the assembled students with a class photograph. Their eyes fixed on his floating crystal camera, they miss the movement of the fountain behind them. Lady Vestal descends the concealed stairs and brings up Evelyn, dressed again in the fine clothes of a Blackwood daughter. Evelyn is happy to defend Cassius, even letting slip the notion that he may have feelings for the regional director Selenar Everbloom, but she knows little of what the guild has charged him with. She likes the possibility of preserving the Delvers' next adventures in photographicals, and Dumbledwark leaves with the beginnings of a professional invitation, but not yet agreement from her companions.

On the way to the potato farm he discovers that Krandal's enterprise is doing more than growing: Glepo Ma is selling produce from a market cart. Dumbledwark purchases one of the potatoes, has a blacksmith cover it in gilding and presents the extraordinary trophy to a delighted cleric. Krandal has always considered nourishing people his real calling, and answers the reporter's philosophical challenges in those terms. Yet his extraordinary sincerity also leaves a door open. He voluntarily signs consent to a Zone of Truth and attempts to explain why the Delvers remain grounded. Questions about questionable acts performed while helping the city lead him back to the ruined bank, where Koh left with other people's money. Krandal will not condemn the family man who has saved him and his friends, but a reporter has now learned something Koh chose not to say. Krandal draws the line at bringing that reporter to his friend's home for another magically assisted interrogation. The interview ends in a more earthy fashion when the visiting halfling contributes an entirely literal deposit to Krandal's farm compost.

The correspondent returns to the Chimera Guild still wanting the man everyone else refuses to produce. Cassius is absent, his office occupied by Zoltar the Wizened of the Guild Academy. In the course of a guarded interview Zoltar first blames Cassius and the Delvers for the catastrophe of the Bronze Ball. As the journalist presses, the newer truth of Atomer's business emerges in the acting guildmaster's own account: the Academy profits handsomely so long as Khalazar's buried empire remains an endless supply of Silver adventures, while Cassius wanted the danger ended quickly. The Darkest Delvers might actually do it. Dumbledwark proposes giving the public a heroic victory without allowing the lucrative expeditions to stop. Zoltar is persuaded, and hands him a licence to take the Delvers into the dangerous Dungeon C4, promising to free them from their grounding the next day. While his new confidant fetches the marmalade that Dumbledwark has been demanding all afternoon, the reporter takes a second, very different prize from the crates in Cassius's former office: a magical warlock's tome, carefully slipped beneath the equipment in his camera bag.

By evening Dumbledwark rides away with both items. Cassius is still inaccessible, the Delvers remain unaware of the arrangement, and C4 has not been entered by their party. Zoltar has promised a chance to return to adventuring, yet the offered dungeon already carries reports of deaths. The adventure ahead may bring the glory the newspaper wants—or be the means by which the Academy keeps the most troublesome heroes from finishing the job they began.

The promised assignment finally carries them back beneath the governor's ruined ballroom. Mirrors channel sunlight down the old roper breach, and Gruber's lift takes the three Delvers and their new correspondent to the licensed gates of C4. A satyr hawker tries selling an ancient Elvish cipher, but the reporter can read such inscriptions magically and no bargain is struck. Beyond a warning and a locked inner door they find a ponderous stone button, an ingenious flooded channel and a massive wheel. Evelyn strains against the mechanism while Koh remembers a peculiar stone-geared crank acquired months before. Dumbledwark floats his camera through the dark, taking photographicals of the workers. Krandal conjures a rush of water down the old funnel; the wheel turns, and the way opens.

The dungeon holds evidence of what became of its previous visitors. Koh draws a pair of green boots from a deep drain without touching them, only for severed feet to emerge when Krandal tries them on. The name Zigzag is sewn inside, but a spell reveals them to be magical Boots of Speed and Krandal claims them. Nearby Evelyn frees an ornate golden mace jammed in the water wheel, though a severed hand tumbles down with it. White cords hanging from the ceiling are sticky and alive with movement beyond the light. The reporter offers a recovered foot to one and watches it rise into the darkness. Beyond the wheel lies a wetter, older cavern, a corpse afloat in a pool and a body in crushed plate armour. Koh quietly takes the numbered shepherd's shield underneath the latter; Dumbledwark, for his part, has more questions than scruples. He animates a patch of moss to ask about enormous claw marks, then has the crushed corpse describe a thing that killed it: a climbing, lightning-spitting behir.

Evelyn finds the wreck of a stone golem in a side cavern. It has already been broken apart in another battle, but a large blue crystal remains in its chest. She slips the crystal into her bag. Nearby, a great mound of dung conceals something stranger still. Detecting magic within it, the reporter reaches in and pulls at a ringed finger, discovering one of the supposed C4 casualties. Iron Monkey is alive. The monk has survived by hiding within the beast's own droppings, and insists the monsters' eyes and noses will not find anyone who joins him. Dumbledwark sees the logic and covers himself in dung; Evelyn tolerates a little of it on her armour but refuses the hiding place, while Krandal and Koh keep their distance. The monk's survival already calls the Guild's list of dead into doubt.

It is Evelyn who calls the beast to them, banging her growing heart-blade against the walls. The behir winds through the dark with a body like a long serpent and lightning playing around its jaws. Its breath strikes Krandal brutally and scorches Evelyn even as she partly dodges, but the cleric heals himself and wards his companion. Koh darts in and out of the shadows, cutting with psychic blades; Evelyn's sword flares with smiting light. A crushing coil nearly swallows her, but she heals herself and draws Sanctuary around her. The monster turns upon Iron Monkey instead, biting off one of the survivor's arms before he falls back into the dung. Dumbledwark, torn between heroics and his obligation to report, sends inspiration to the others through flashes from his hovering camera and records the danger from his vile hiding place. The fight ends when Krandal's summoned mace finds the behir's throat. As the monster crashes down toward Evelyn, Koh makes a clumsy leap and catches her hand, carrying them both clear in a burst of magic.

The dying behir leaves acid and scattered sparks behind. No one among the four visitors has died; Iron Monkey has survived, though dreadfully wounded, and nothing shows that he receives treatment before the story closes. Over a procession of the day's captured photographicals, Kyle narrates one final act: the recovered blue crystal fits the entry chamber's promise and opens their way out of C4. Whatever story the paper prints about that victory—and whatever happened to Cassius—remains beyond this episode's ending.

After C4 the Guild offers the Delvers a different sort of return to service. Adventurers swarming the new underground passages cannot safely remain there through the night: their mirrored shafts cease carrying sunlight when the sun sets, and the creatures of the deeper stone grow bolder. The Academy wants a refuge, but the likely hosts are flumphs, floating inhabitants of the dark with traditions unfamiliar to the Guild. It is for that reason that the three heroes ride Gruber's lift alongside an appointed intermediary. Destroying Angel is an imposing centaur in worn leathers and long white braids, a Silver adventurer whose task is to keep the human and nonhuman sides talking.

They find the flumphs' establishment at the end of a carpet of violet moss. Its carved doors are marked *Deep Winds Spa*, and the lobby staff hurry to accommodate four figures who seem much too large and nervous for a house of rest. A flumph receptionist named Poof leads them to the mistress, whose oversized bow and hookah are as theatrical as the rituals demanded of visitors. Angel kneels properly and offers a little jar of surface air, a rare breath carried down from the sunlit world. The headmistress accepts the gift and invites them to enjoy the facilities before she will consider making a home for adventurers in her domain.

The spa is unlike any dungeon the Delvers have fought through. Skeletal minotaurs bathe beside a roper, trolls clink glasses near submerged treasure chests with teeth, a beholder glides from basin to basin wrapped in a towel, and flumphs bustle through the halls with little water orbs and steaming dishes. Angel assures the party these strangers have come to a neutral refuge, not a battlefield. That does not make Krandal comfortable about removing his armour—or his beloved belt, from which his maces are eventually banished by a polite attendant. Koh settles into a hot pool as though he had spent his whole life waiting for it. Evelyn is less willing to undress among monsters. Instead she questions a pair of skeletal minotaurs, learning that their people say a cursed Minotaur Mesa lies far to the north near Noble Hollow, then seeks a private room.

The private door calls Evelyn's name without a voice in the air. The flumph attendants reluctantly give permission for her to enter. Inside, a great fishlike creature lounges in a pool, three red eyes set one above another and long tendrils curling over the tiles. It turns the pages of her mind as readily as she turns a book, finding the memories of her family, school, and the handsome Traveler she promised to help. According to this visitor, her Traveler remains bound to the Astral Plane until Khalazar is killed. Evelyn is delighted at what seems an easier solution than a journey beneath Minotaur Mesa, but the knowledge is only as trustworthy as this stranger. He dismisses her and calls for Krandal next. Reading the cleric, he finds childhood potato fields and Snake Island's buried recollections. The distant drumming that echoes in Krandal's mind stops his reading. He professes to know where Princess Hissia is kept, yet never tells Krandal; even a momentary suggestion that she is dead dissolves into a correction of his own expression. The conversation ends with the visitor asking for Koh. None of these private revelations gives the party proof of their ancient mysteries.

The day's hospitality is not altogether uneventful. A beholder puts an anxious Krandal to sleep with a glance, while Koh sips wine beside the monster in untroubled companionship. Evelyn receives a massage and unfamiliar cheese in the privacy of a room. Angel lends a sympathetic ear to a weeping ghost, until the headmistress comes to her with an urgent request. Water elementals visiting the spa have lately brought black sand from deeper below. Something has accumulated in the filtration equipment, frightening the staff away. Clear it, the flumph says, and the Guild's adventurers may be welcome. Angel calls the party out of its unlikely holiday, and the Delvers dress and arm themselves again.

The filtration works are dark, dusty and neglected. Evelyn fills them with magical daylight. At once a black-grained ooze writhes out of the slats, recoiling from the light, and the four try to hold it fast. Within the mass is the shape of an impossibly thin man with long red claws, recalling to Krandal the hollow assassin that once attacked his farm. The thing envelops him before he can understand it and flees as a great rolling ball, carrying the cleric in crushing darkness toward the baths. Koh runs after his friend, hacking at the shape with his psychic blades. Destroying Angel's arrows and Evelyn's lightning strike pierce the mass as it moves, but none can reach Krandal to pull him free. His own radiant prayer hurts it without breaking its hold. As the black ball bursts back among the startled spa guests, Koh retrieves his Drift Globe and thrusts it into the ooze. Daylight flares from the globe; the dark prison breaks open and Krandal tumbles out alive. The long-clawed creature scrambles toward the public pool, only for Koh to finish it with one final psychic strike.

The flumphs find the filters clean when they look again, and their attendants hurry to scrape dark residue from the survivors. Angel rescues Vapo, stuck to a wall where the ooze had struck, while Krandal—who has had rather more than enough of the day—is ushered toward a private massage. The night refuge the Guild wants has not yet been built, and no final alliance is sealed before the session ends. As Koh watches his battered friend disappear down the hall, another private door speaks his name and asks him to come closer. He answers with a question: who is calling? The voice has no time to reply.

The voice beyond the private door does not leave Koh waiting for long. He enters the bath where an aboleth lounges, three eyes set above one another, its great fishlike form almost lost in the warm waters. It toys with his story, reading of Anthela and their daughter, then offers a contest more interesting than a simple conversation: Koh may try to look into its mind in turn. Twice Koh wins. First he finds himself beneath the lake near Withers Keep, looking at the creature's submerged throne and its translucent-skinned servants. The aboleth calls itself a former beast of the lake, and tells him that Khalazar seeks the blood of an ancient people, the Dolbluth, in order to regenerate. The claim hangs in the air without proof. Koh's second plunge finds an impossible labyrinth of thoughts. He cannot uncover what Dolbluth blood means, but he sees a skull-framed black door, a shining triangle and three hollows set into a stone altar. Deeper still he sees the aboleth exalted by thousands of worshippers, with tiny flumphs offered to it like delicacies. The creature calls such dreams its plans, not its present life. Koh leaves with a courteous threat that the surface will not submit so easily.

The Delvers spend an actual night in comfortable rooms at Deep Winds. Destroying Angel has gone ahead by morning; the flumph headmistress is willing to host other adventurers for a price. The Guild has no constructed camp, but its envoys have at least proven the house can shelter guests. The three follow the winding route toward the lift, until Koh spots stairs they passed without seeing on the way down. Down they go, past a carved stone figure with a human ribcage, batlike face and long wing-fingers spread across the walls. Three Silver League adventurers have died on these stairs, one in a moth-wing flight suit, another leather-clad and armed with small throwing blades, and a tattooed Goliath bearing an enormous maul. Evelyn collects their rank badges, together with the moth helmet. Koh removes a ring from the leather-clad corpse and a necklace from the Goliath, careful enough to appear solemn despite his instinct for salvage. No one can yet tell why these adventurers died.

The stairs end at a cavern faced with hundreds of rune-covered stone circles. Some have holes opening behind them, others fold together like interlocking spirals; they look strangely akin to the tablet fragments the Delvers found near Noble Hollow and surrendered to the Guild. An altar is carved with worshipping figures, gricks and hook horrors gathered beneath a spiderlike form. Koh sees black sand resting in its three hollows. Beyond rises the door from the aboleth's mind: skull above, glowing triangle across dark metal. If three things must be put into the altar to open it, those things are not here. The party test the mechanisms, and Evelyn even strikes a space where one plate is missing. It is only rock. Then a disc begins to open by itself.

The first body inside is long dry and pale, an elf hollowed by surgery and time, so much like the dreadful corpse Krandal once met at his farm that the resemblance is impossible for him to ignore. Evelyn pulls it into the open. The next is not content to lie still. Soon mummified elves slide out of their wall recesses, ancient eyes lighting and spectral hands reaching for the intruders. Koh's blades cut from the shadows, Krandal raises Pelor's symbol and at last uses his power to turn undead, and Evelyn pursues a creature that tries to escape up the stairs. Some of the mummies flee, others attack with strange cold and unseen bonds. One seizes Krandal by a paralysing force and lifts him rigidly toward an empty niche; Koh's next psychic strike breaks the spell and sends his friend back to the floor. Another hides itself from sight, but Evelyn catches its dusty tracks and the light of her smiting blade reveals it long enough to cut it down. They could remain for more fights with a cavern full of tombs, but decide the sensible thing is to live long enough to report what they have seen. They leave the door sealed and climb the seemingly endless stairs to daylight.

Atomer is crowded with adventurers wearing nearly identical treasures from the new dungeons, while the cost of nearly everything climbs. Krandal's potato stall is a rare source of cheap food, although he learns his workers have not yet been paid a wage; Gleepo Ma also says something dark has been moving in the barn. Back at the Guild, Carrie identifies the dead adventurers by their badges as Hypnomoth, Shadowblade and Black Bear. Koh hands her the ring and necklace: they are a Ring of Evasion and an Amulet of Health, while the moth helmet is ordinary. She can guess that the newly discovered stairs are an Umbrel Flight noted on an old Withers map, and that three keys might open the triangle door, but she cannot produce any of them. Neither has the Guild restored the Delvers to free service. Koh begins to look for a lift pass that he might copy; no pass is obtained before the friends part.

Evelyn returns to the hidden school beneath the fountain and discovers that her luminous sword can lengthen into a great lance. It opens the magical door for her, and the attendants of Loran's sanctuary welcome her to an elegant meal. The enchanted white-and-blue armour still hangs just out of reach. Krandal, meanwhile, heads to his farm to investigate the reported disturbance. A potato rolls from the barn; a slaughtered rat and a torn leathery case hide beneath the pallets. Then something small and unlikely rubs affectionately against his ankle: a chickenlike youngster sheathed in beetle armour, with tiny hooked hands. The forgotten hook-horror egg has hatched, and its baby has made Krandal its own. He can only stare at his new companion as the episode ends.

The next morning, the three Delvers put discretion above orders. Koh has studied enough lift passes to forge one of his own, and the papers prove better than their acting when the guard notices they are not on the Guild's approved list. Krandal saves the deception with a bluff so commanding that the guard fears making an enemy of the Silver League heroes. He even gives the man a potato. The forged document gets them past the lift's inspection, and they descend again into the deep dungeons with no assignment and no permission. This time Koh takes the covering from the amulet Carrie made with his wife's blood, and it glows as he turns it through the darkness. Following its light, they pass the staircase to the sealed triangle door and crawl still farther through narrow wet stone. Another great skull-arched door waits there, already open. Behind it lie broken animated statues, glyphs pressed into paving stones, old Elvish writing and levers that someone has already manipulated.

That someone is a weary cat-faced woman buried under a huge backpack and an even greater store of indignation. Goggles Granny has a scar across one eye, tiny spectacles and a tail that announces her nature from behind. She accepts Krandal's peace-offering potato without ceremony, and grumbles that her map has brought her to a room even she cannot solve. Beyond them black ooze fills a chamber wider than any ordinary hall. Its far ledges are supported by three enormous stone faces: a weeping countenance, one hiding its eyes as it screams, and a toothed mouth full of burning figures. Three little keys lie on an altar, made with the heads of a grick, a hook horror and a batlike Necromantis. The old woman discovers she knows the language on the wall, and helps Koh translate a grim puzzle of horrors consuming hope, darkness and dawn, and gricks devouring everything.

The three platforms are not readily reached. Evelyn ropes in a little boat, illuminates its prow with Daylight and begins polling through the black slime. Her pole catches on something below, and she falls into the filth. For a moment the weight of her mail threatens to pull her under, but she kicks herself and the boat all the way to the far ledge. Koh races across broken handrails with his magical sash holding him in reserve. Krandal, determined not to be outdone, tries to float a key across on the head of his spectral mace, only to find that its force cannot support solid metal. While they consider other plans, he opens his bag and discovers the newborn hook horror has curled up among the supplies, pecking at every ration it can reach. Its affection has not made it trainable.

Granny has a more extraordinary answer. She gives Krandal a potion without adequately explaining which one; he swallows it expecting to grow, and instead shrinks to a stout, tiny version of himself. She drinks her own enlargement draught, grows to a towering height and pitches the rope-tethered cleric across the slime to the distant platform. By the time everyone can reach a lock, the puzzle is far from over. Wrong keys and wrong lever positions unleash lightning, and Krandal suffers more of it than anyone. Evelyn, who has escaped drowning only to be shocked by the stonework, discovers the key to the light: one lever extinguishes its glow, another burns brightly, and the third must be held halfway between. The screams and the tears and the flames finally fall into their proper places. The last lock consumes its metal key. All around them the surface of the black lake begins to fall, draining into great basins at the corners of the hall.

Where the pool stood is a ruined lower floor and a small altar. A narrow ceramic vessel stands there, bound with gold and crowned by a bat's skull. As Evelyn approaches it, a heartbeat quickens in her ears. She unscrews the gilded cap, and the beating immediately stops. Within rests only a dried, shrivelled heart. Krandal recognizes its ventricles, but neither his faith nor Koh's stories can tell them to whom it belonged. Koh suspects a weighted trap under the vessel. With his fingers he lifts it while holding its place on the plate. Granny takes out her merchant's balance: gold, ceramic and heart together weigh twenty-five pounds. The party entertains increasingly inventive replacements—torches, candles and, from Granny's wondrous alchemical jug, mayonnaise inside a shirt—but none of that is needed. Evelyn finds a stone weighing twenty-three pounds. Two pounds of Granny's measuring weights complete the amount. In a final, careful exchange, Koh and Granny put the stone and weights on the pedestal, and no trap responds. Evelyn packs away the heart.

At the other side of the chamber the old woman considers going home, but is persuaded to follow the friends at least as far as the next descent. The ancient staircase turns out to be longer than her patience, and she stops to catch her breath. They thank her for her help, and she returns safely toward the town whose troubles she says she is trying to end. Only the three Delvers continue to the catacomb of the ancient elves. There they place the recovered vessel upon the altar before the great triangle door. It fits perfectly into one of the hollows. Two hollows remain empty, and the two staircases still wait beside them. They have found one piece of the puzzle, but not the way beyond the gate.

The one heart rests in its hollow at the base of the triangle door, but there are two vacancies beside it. The Delvers take the left-hand stair and climb for what feels like forever. Out in the winding darkness they see eyes vanish around corners and pale crawling things scatter out of sight. They become lost. They rest, and soon each seems to have made the exhausting journey back to Atomer alone. The days of Silver City seem to have caught up with them at last, but the experiences awaiting them are far too neatly tailored to their fears to be trusted.

At Krandal's potato farm the workers stand with their tools discarded before them. Gleepo Ma and Nopa Ropa accuse him of withholding wages and failing the companions he promised to help; even poor Chad Brad's post has been moved forward to join their ranks. The Goodwines arrive on a cart and announce that they have legally purchased the soil beneath him and are taking the labourers away. The snake man threatens revenge for Princess Hissia. Krandal tries to face them down with a command amplified by magic, and for an instant their human and animal outlines melt into writhing swarms of rats. Only the armoured driver of the Goodwine cart remains himself. He raises a hand to his bucket helm and motions for silence. Krandal withdraws with his imagined treasure and the determination to make his farm a juster place. The revolt, though, has never happened; his sense of guilt is real, and his knowledge that his people had been working without wages makes it sting.

Koh meets a different ruin. In the streets posters promise ten thousand gold for One Shot, the criminal who supposedly exposed the elves to the Hereditary Drone. At the Guild he finds Anthela weeping beside Zoltar and hears accusations that he has destroyed his own family. Koh cannot reach his wife's mind. He suspects the vision, calls up his psychic daggers and strikes at the Zoltar who tries to arrest him. That false man falls as if killed, and Koh runs past angry familiar faces into streets that disappear into darkness. No public bounty has been posted, no actual dagger has killed Zoltar, and Anthela is not here to denounce him. The rats' invisible labour has built a counterfeit city out of his dread.

Evelyn's refuge at Loran's school is the next lie to fall. Her ghostly attendants fade; then her hair is gone, and a doctor and her mother seem to stand beyond the door of an asylum. They say she was never a paladin, that Loran and the Traveler belong to a disorder of her imagination. She remembers her companions, lifts her special mirror and glimpses the back of her head with a rat attached to her neck. She refuses the command to submit. The straitjacket becomes insubstantial around her. This dream cannot imprison her, and she wakes where their bodies truly lie.

Krandal's silent armoured cart-driver comes to him in the dying false landscape and leads him through its dark. There he and Koh find each other, and Koh recognizes the man's sun-and-lance markings. He calls himself Radiant Lance, hero of Pelor, and says his mind has remained inside their jailer's for about a century and a half, while his body decayed in what he names the Black Basilica. He cannot carry them out, but knows Evelyn has broken loose. While Koh strains to pull the nightmare apart, the knight persuades a despondent Krandal to play cards. Koh sees what a city enslaved by black-clad corpse-soldiers beneath a floating pyramid might look like, but these, too, are visions inside the prison. Pelor's apparent displeasure, voiced when Krandal prays badly, belongs to the same false world. Only the kindness the knight offers in his last game begins to feel like something independent of the rat-swarm's malice.

In the real Basilica Evelyn stands in a great columned hall. Koh and Krandal lie on its stone floor with little rats biting the backs of their necks, and the rats stretch away in long chains, tail to teeth, toward an airborne congregation. Their exposed brains flash bright and strange colours as a single voice speaks through them. An armoured skeleton lies nearby, also bound to this network. Evelyn cuts the rats at her friends' necks, and the two men wake. Radiant Lance thanks Krandal for their game and disappears from the mind-world. The floating rat mass calls itself the All Rat and offers the heroes an urn with a hook-horror's golden head. It asks to keep what rests inside: an organ it says came from Khalazar and gives the congregation its strength. Evelyn will not accept a vessel robbed of its contents.

The battle is a storm of rats, lightning and suddenly changing memories. Rat-tails twist into lashes, psychic blasts hurt in ways armour cannot stop, and the floor sometimes seems to turn into the summit of Skull Keep or a wedding feast from long ago. Evelyn fights with her sword and javelin, Krandal prays and heals his companions through the worst of the attack, and Koh hurls his psychic knives. At last Koh recalls a more extraordinary skill. He leaps straight into the wriggling mass and tears the whole urn free. Then he throws a blade into empty air and vanishes after it, reappearing well away from the creatures with his prize. At once the chorus ends. The coloured light dies within the little heads, the chains come apart, and the rats run off in all directions, ordinary and frightened.

They turn to the old armour that had lain on the floor. Time has gnawed the chain mail and sword away, and a human skeleton sits in the bucket helm, but the tabard and long lance have somehow endured. The cloth shows a horse, the sun and the lance of Pelor. Krandal dons it with reverence, taking up a relic the man he met in dreams might once have worn; they put the long lance away for later. The Delvers rest for an hour amid the fading ornament of the Black Basilica. Krandal's infant hook horror, provisionally christened Scroungy, eats whatever scraps of meat or rat it can find, and Evelyn ties a little length of her magical pink rope about it so she can find it again.

A call echoes from beyond the doorway. The little hook horror answers, and three huge adult hunters arrive after the released rats, moving with their blind faces and keen senses through the passage. Krandal tries to present Scroungy in peace, but the leading beast rears to strike him and the others circle to flank. Koh's psychic blade reaches the monster first, piercing its head and killing it with a single blow. The remaining pair cross their hooked arms and retreat into the dark; the baby struggles in Krandal's hands, then scrambles eagerly back into his pack. They leave the place under cover of stealth, with Koh's Drift Globe passed to Evelyn to light their way and the door's great crank recovered as they go.

The stairway down brings them again to the three hollows before the dark metal triangle. One urn is still there from their earlier labour, so they take it back with them rather than abandon it. Now Evelyn's bag holds two: the first crowned by a bat's skull and containing a dry heart, the second with a golden hook-horror head and an organ the rats had coveted. The third is still missing. The friends choose not to descend the last unexplored stair immediately. Even their courage has limits, and they know too well what the hours of darkness can bring. Instead they walk to the Deep Winds Spa, whose flumphs once thanked them for clearing their filters. The price of gratitude has risen to a thousand gold pieces for each traveller's room and meal. Koh protests, but they pay and are welcomed inside. No one has slept there yet when the story leaves them, two urns in hand and another path still waiting in the depths.

Morning comes again in the depths, where morning is measured by the flumphs' wishes rather than sunlight. The Delvers have slept through the expensive night they purchased at Deep Winds Spa and come away restored and strangely inspired. Krandal had meant to question the skeletal minotaurs and the aboleth about the missing Princess Hissia. The questions, their host promises, may be remembered in a later flashback; for now they have no answer to carry back to his workers. Rather than climb the left-hand path to the rat-haunted Black Basilica, the three turn right at the old triangle gate and follow Koh's blinking bracer toward the source of the darkness. A long crooked stair becomes a passage of petrified tree roots and worn rock. In a narrow tunnel their boots begin breaking bones, little rat skeletons and fragments of stirge and darkmantle bodies. Krandal imagines a fearsome hybrid beast that had possessed all of these different appendages at once. Koh knows better, but sees no harm in allowing him his “Uberdark.”

The crunching has already warned somebody. The ground shivers, whispers sound, and a tremendous voice demands to know who disturbs the guardian of the cave. Evelyn identifies them as the Darkest Delvers, and the disembodied menace becomes an invitation. Around the corner comes a cheerful blue-green tortle, five feet and some inches tall, plainly dressed and worn thin by long solitude. Barclay has survived for what he estimates to be three or four months, abandoned by a research expedition and unable to find his way back to daylight. His first protection is simply an extraordinary quantity of bones arranged to sound an alarm when strangers approach; his second is a camp with little rows of cultivated mushrooms, a cooking spit, an adapted leather harness for bells, and a luminous lantern. He introduces its occupant, Chance, who cannot speak aloud but manifests a spectral hand that shakes the visitors' hands and scratches a crude greeting in the dirt. The Delvers do not know Chance's story, and Barclay is glad enough for the companionship to accept the invitation to come along. He says he is a cleric of Ptah, god of craft and knowledge. Krandal offers him a potato for the fire, and, watching Barclay thank his god at every turn, begins to wonder whether he himself has been sufficiently grateful to Pelor.

Two enormous grick-faced and bat-winged doors stand side by side behind the camp. Barclay can tell that the four plates on the floor are safe pressure switches. The four stand upon them, and the doors open, revealing paired corridors. Koh and Evelyn take one lane, Krandal and Barclay the other. They keep in touch through Koh's psychic whispers, for every device that opens one way seems to matter in the other. A vast chasm separates each pair from another plate, and pressing the right switch on one side raises a bridge on the other. Koh crosses by throwing a psychic blade and teleporting to its landing place, then discovers that crossing back requires Evelyn's strength and his animated sash. Barclay and Krandal carefully walk their own fleeting bridge. Later, both pairs encounter floors marked with subtle glyphs. The inscriptions along one wall belong to the switches across the divide. One mistake brings a warning hiss of gas, but they find the correct correspondence before the mechanism punishes them. At the next opening they step together on four corner plates and the ceiling gives way to two stone golems.

What ought to be a straightforward battle becomes a baffling duet. Koh strikes his construct with a magnificent psychic critical blow and is already imagining it dead when the dungeon master reminds him that a mindless creation of stone cannot be wounded by psychic daggers. It does not fall. The monsters prove even stranger when Krandal's spectral weapon strikes one and the golem on the other side suffers the blow. Their enemies and the poison jets are somehow linked across the rooms. Barclay, knowing such constructs cannot be charmed, instead lends magic to Krandal's ordinary mace, strengthening it with radiant power. Evelyn dispels a cloud and finds that the other pair can suddenly breathe. Her lightning javelin tears stone apart on the far side while Koh turns to ordinary steel, which at least cuts where psychic force could not. The golems hit terribly hard, forcing both clerics to heal and Evelyn to shield herself while she fights. At last a blow from Krandal's spectral mace brings both damaged constructions down in unison. Two fresh bridges extend. The pairs climb and return to one another's sight, alive and battered.

They emerge above a hall so long that one companion's light barely reaches the others. In its centre a great spiral column is punctured by air vents; the room seems to breathe, sucking everything inward and then blowing it away again. Rocks litter the floor, but Koh and Barclay recognize shapes that are not rocks at all. Gricks lie coiled and waiting, including a vast bloated alpha. Koh disappears into the shadows to prepare a strike. Evelyn, meanwhile, lights the hall with the globe Koh had given her and makes for the tower. She discovers that the safest way up is a sort of dance, waiting when the jets breathe outward and slipping past them as they suck the air in. Her clumsy first steps become graceful as she follows the rhythm of the wind. At the summit is another little ceramic vessel, crowned not with a bat or hook horror but the golden face of a grick. She removes it. The vast breath stops immediately.

The silence wakes the sleeping predators. Koh's dagger flashes from concealment and fells one grick; another staggers under his thrown blade. When the great alpha rises, Koh vaults into its reach, drives another critical psychic strike into its head and kills it with the swagger of a rogue who has found his true quarry. Krandal puts Barclay's blessing to use against another grick, while the guest throws a bolt of holy light after a retreating enemy. Some of the smaller beasts die, and one slithers away into safety. Koh celebrates the idea that he, not the bloated monster, must now be the alpha of the Underdark. Evelyn has already secured their real prize in her bag beside the first two urns. All three are recovered at last, and still not one is set on the three empty recesses before the sealed triangle door.

Rather than press their luck with whatever awaits beyond that door, they find the doors and bridges now open and retrace the expedition's route toward the old forest entrance. Here they find wooden paths and bridges over the ancient pits, apparently built during the intervening research. They pass out among the trees, avoiding Gruber's lift and the prospect of running directly into Guild authorities while their grounding remains in effect. Veggie Sticks is no more pleased than ever to have adventurers tramping through his woods. Barclay's apologies for his team's felling of trees do not impress him, though his preoccupation with the local hippogriffs is too strong to sustain a quarrel for long. The party asks the gnome druid to send them to Atomer through the trees as he has done before.

Before they leave, Barclay offers to hide the three Silver knights from divining eyes. He casts Nondetection on them, and all three badges promptly fall from their coats. The effect is real, but its reason is not yet known. Evelyn removes hers, and Krandal throws his into the forest, terrifying Veggie Sticks with the idea that its owners might come searching through his sanctuary. The gnome scrambles after the badge, then insists they name proper trees, not Evelyn's shrubs. A cedar near a town well proves a more discreet choice than the Guild oak. They join hands and travel through trunks and branches, emerging in a swirl of leaves at a busy Atomer well as evening falls. To distract the witnesses, Krandal conjures a frightening roar farther down the street. They have escaped the dungeon with all three urns, but they have not opened the door that requires them. It will have to wait for another journey.

They come into Atomer in the evening with leaves still caught in their clothes, and discover there is no longer any refuge in pretending that the Guild has merely made a mistake. Evelyn demands the Silver badges from her companions. If the Guild can follow them by some hidden enchantment, she will carry the badges herself. She has a more important thing to protect. The three urns pass from her keeping to Koh's, far from whatever eyes may be upon their medals. Then she tells them to meet her at the Guild in two hours. No one is to start a fight; she intends to make Zoltar explain himself to the town. Koh vanishes into the alleys, Krandal stumbles off toward the farm, and Evelyn hides in a bush to call an enormous elk out of the air.

Koh knows Atomer's narrow streets too well to be caught easily. In a passage near his house he spots a broadside with a familiar bowler-hatted face: Dumbledwark of Raisinbunch, the wandering reporter, is promising a scandalous book called A Prison City. Among the secrets it advertises are the reasons Cassius remains under guard, the greed of the Guild and the disaster of the Bronze Ball. Koh pockets the advertisement, unsure how much of it to believe. He finds the door of his house locked and blocked from inside. Slipping through an upper window, he discovers Anthela peering out through a hole in the door, a chair braced beneath its handle. For three months he has helped her find quiet sleep with the familiar touch of his mind each night, and now he has spent a night underground without warning her. She is exhausted and terrified. He reaches her with his whisper and then his arms, makes her tea and hears her ask whether he is at last going to end the terror below their city. Their daughter has seen a desert in troubled dreams; Koh has no way to tell whether such visions mean anything. On the table is a sealed letter from Dumbledwark, sent through the Daily Quest, offering him an exclusive interview. Koh tears it open and finds a single useful message: Cassius is being held at the Wildfire estate.

Krandal's journey begins less smoothly. An old Guild acquaintance, Edgar Boonfellow of the First Sons, hears him blunder into cans and steps into his path. Boonfellow has been ordered to find the Delvers, but the dwarf looks weary of the situation and agrees not to have seen his friend. If Krandal really goes below again, he asks him to look for Phelan. A little farther on, the cleric passes singers performing advertisements for Dumbledwark's coming book and wonders how much of the town's grief has already become a marketplace. At his farm, he finds the Goodwines with a cart and a bag of gold. The sight is unsettling after the All Rat's nightmare of foreclosure, but the truth is different. The Goodwines have been buying splendid potatoes for the liquor they make. Gleepo Ma has handled the sale honestly and the farm remains Krandal's. Krandal takes the four hundred gold pieces and divides most of it among the three employees who have worked without pay, handing ninety-five pieces to each. Gleepo Ma is overjoyed, Muck Muck stuffs his share into his clothes, and Nopa Ropa throws the coins away. The serpent warrior did not come to this field for gold; he came because Krandal had promised to find his princess.

The promise gives Krandal another memory, a conversation he had at the Deep Winds Spa on the night before he recovered the third urn. He had returned to the aboleth, the old and dreadful mind that delighted in trading secrets, and asked after Hissia. It had told him that Princess Hissia laboured in the deepest Dawnless Dunes upon Khalazar's great machine. Whether the creature spoke the truth he could not know, but at last he had a place to name. Nopa Ropa draws his sword, cuts his hand to bind himself to the quest and promises to fight beside Krandal in the coming descent. A little patience is demanded of him; there are matters in the city that must be settled before dawn. He agrees to spend the night forging new armour and preparing for the journey.

Evelyn, meanwhile, has ridden to the school of Loran Ilfelkyr. The elk has proved easier to summon than to ride, and she clings to its neck in a thoroughly unheroic fashion until she reaches the guarded grounds. She leaves the creature to watch for strangers and descends beneath the fountain to the secret chamber where Loran's armour has so often rejected her. The radiant plates hover beyond the shimmering ward. Again and again she reaches for them, and again and again she fails. Looking into the mirror gives her no answer. Then one of the school's warning bells rings. Girls scream above her; a hook horror has erupted through the wooden floor. For an instant the beautiful armour ceases to be the object of her private ambition. It is simply something she needs to defend the children. She reaches through the barrier and takes it. The breastplate unfolds across her shoulders and back, the skirt and blue flowers of Loran's dress spreading beneath it, and she runs upstairs in a suit of shining plate. Her blade strikes the beast; soon the schoolgirls see the intruder fall. Evelyn mounts the elk again, this time with greater resolve, and rides into town to summon the people to judgment.

At the Guild a crowd comes with her. Koh quietly draws more people toward the gathering; Krandal arrives with his maces in hand. Zoltar the Wizened emerges from the Guild doors flanked by two guards. Evelyn throws the Silver badges at his feet and accuses him of secretly following their every step. The Guild uses divination to track its adventurers, he admits without apparent shame. He insists that Silver City has lifted Atomer from obscurity, enriched the town and supplied a multitude of new Gold Leaguers. But Evelyn has a harsher measure of his success. She asks about the elves lost since the Bronze Ball, about the girls from her school and the people whose families have had no answers for three months. Koh speaks of his wife's long suffering and the lives the dungeons have taken from their neighbours. Krandal, remembering his farm and the duties he had nearly failed, pledges his strength to the town rather than to a distant bureaucracy.

Helenar Clearstone comes forward with a parchment bearing thirty names of elves who vanished at the Bronze Ball. The three friends read the names aloud, passing the list between them, while the people recognize lost neighbours and wonder why their plight was kept from them. Old members of the Guild, wood, bronze and silver, cross over to Evelyn's side. Zoltar's protest becomes feebler as the crowd turns. He retreats into the Guild, but Koh follows him. In the emptying corridors Koh puts a hand against the door of his office and names the place where Cassius is kept. Zoltar confirms it. Koh tells him that if he has any wish to make amends, he will bring their guildmaster home instead of hiding behind a locked door. Zoltar agrees to ride to the Wildfire estate, with Koh and a great procession following.

At the gate of the Wildfire home, Valentine appears with Cassius. The missing guildmaster has been confined for months, but he is alive and walks without a jailer's hand on him. The crowd cheers. Koh warns Zoltar to leave before the rejoicing grows dangerous, and the wizened Academy man spurs his horse away, alive and disgraced. Cassius speaks softly with his old friends and says his ambitions for Atomer did not suit the Academy. He will make them ready for the journey they have sought so long. The Guild fills with celebration, and at his old desk Cassius gives each of the three Delvers twenty thousand gold pieces for the coming campaign. Food and supplies will be prepared by morning. Fire, radiance and protection against deathly magic, he advises, may serve them better than anything else. He removes a blue-gem ring that Selenar once gave him and presses it into Koh's hands, asking only that the rogue bring it back still attached to him. It is a ring of free action, another hope laid upon a friend.

Before dawn each makes ready. Koh has his back marked with protective magic in the form of a Life Well tattoo, obtains a rapier of exceptional enchantment and fills his supplies with powerful healing potions. He gives Krandal the old ring that has saved him so many times from dangerous falls; Krandal solemnly pays him a potato. Evelyn chooses a saddle suited to her great steed, an amulet of devotion and potions of her own, then returns to the school to sleep with the pink rope clasped in her hand. Krandal's preparations demand more than weapons.

Another memory from the spa returns to Krandal, darker even than the first. The aboleth had told him that the skeletal minotaurs beneath his farm survive by the curse of Khalazar, and that the end of the curse would bring their final death. Chad Brad, the friendly minotaur whose head guards the potato fields, deserved to hear the truth. Krandal tells him what he has learned and offers to seek a better fate if one can be found. With Chad Brad's skull under his arm, he follows the remembered path through Noble Hollow and down the great hole left by the roper battle. Below his farm he finds a circular hall of graves, with the names of minotaur heroes inscribed upon the walls above their bones. Krandal calls out, and Chad Brad answers with a great echoing moo. The scattered bones of one grave rise into the towering figure of Corgan Greathorns. One by one his companions rise as well.

Krandal bows and asks what he can do for Chad Brad. The minotaurs do not ask him to preserve their unending undeath. They have been bones for longer than they should have lived; they desire the freedom of their final rest. But to help end the curse, they will fight. Nine of them agree to march under Krandal's banner, and he returns to the farm with their promise. He digs up Chad Brad's old remains and performs a difficult work of necromancy, placing costly gems in the skull's eyes to rebuild his loyal companion's skeletal body. Chad Brad rises once more, huge, whole in the sense that his bones can stand and move, but not returned to warm flesh. Together with the nine warriors of the crypt there are ten skeletal minotaurs to follow Krandal, and Nopa Ropa has sworn to join them. The cleric has sought to redeem his duties and unwittingly gathered an army.

When the new morning dawns, one further surprise waits in the potato field. A silver chain and a rod protrude from the earth. Krandal pulls, and a perforated flail crowned with the shape of a potato comes free amid golden incense. A divine voice blesses him for the good he has begun to do. It is a Devotee's Censer, a healing weapon of radiant power and a holy sign of Pelor. Now the company is armed, Cassius is back in his Guild, and allies are ready to answer the call. The three urns remain in Koh's keeping and the ancient triangle door remains shut. The descent is promised for the next day of their story, not yet begun.

The next morning the old Guild returns to the depths. Cassius has divided his forces: bronze and wood knights are to hold the approaches while the Darkest Delvers and a few trusted Silver parties go farther into the dark. Edgar Boonfellow and Norman Green come with the First Sons; Dominic Reeves and Hiroshi represent the Brigand Patrol; the Five Emissaries bring Vivian Plume, Omen Wright and a halfling evoker called Darmah Edstream, who has prepared a teleportation circle to carry the missing elves to safety. Krandal has brought a stranger army of his own. Nopa Ropa wears newly wrought golden protection and carries the snake-staff his employer once owned; ten skeletal minotaurs lumber down the steps, their leader Corgan Greathorns among them, while Chad Brad holds a comparatively small mace of disruption in his giant hands. Evelyn's newly armoured form walks with the great elk she has saddled. Koh bears the sword of fine steel he bought in town, another curved dagger at his other side, and a leather cover pierced with narrow compass-like slits for his red blood detector, so its light need no longer blaze through a bare gem.

At the black altar the three hollows wait exactly as they were left. Koh takes the bat, hook-horror and grick-headed urns from his keeping and offers one to each of his friends. Evelyn sets hers in place, Krandal follows and Koh completes the row. All three vessels turn of their own accord, their gilded heads facing the sealed metal triangle. A neighbouring mummy tomb opens, and a figure draws herself from the ancient niche. She is a woman scarcely more than leathery skin, linen and bone, her hair hanging below her waist in a matted river alive with tiny spiders. She speaks to the three Delvers, but the other adventurers hear nothing and reach for their weapons. Her words are in their minds, translated from a tongue older than any of them. Shaolin Nym, she calls herself: architect of the Dolbluth, a people who abandoned their emperor rather than follow his betrayal of the Seldarine.

She tells the three that Khalazar was Emperor Orgoth Argus and that his own people sealed him here thousands of years ago. Others have tried to stop him by dismantling his machines, she says, but the creature returns again and again. If they wish to destroy him for good, they must use the organs in their urns to rebuild his true body, add a drop of the ancestral blood already borne upon Koh's wrist, and kill him in that fully restored form. The Delvers ask for more; she has little else to give but a warning not to turn back. At her gesture the urns rise from their sockets, ready to be taken, and the iron triangle parts. As it opens her fingers fall away, then her arms, until she crumbles into dust. The company steps through into the great desert beneath the world, and the gate shuts behind them.

There is no wind. The dunes swallow the feet of the great majority of the companions and even their voices seem lost in the stillness. Low walls, the outlines of ruined courtyards and homes, and a broken black obelisk rise through the sand. Evelyn sends her celestial elk ahead, and the companions begin to call the animal Albert, or Mr Handsome when they admire the creature's self-possessed gait. Koh slips into the darkness beside them, linked to Krandal, Evelyn and two of their allies by psychic whispers, and finds an unexpected gift in the ring Cassius gave him. Where others sink and stumble, he strides over the sand as though it were firm stone and leaves no tracks. They climb a great dune and find a field of blue light at its summit: hundreds of small shards glittering around the cracked remnant of a once enormous crystal.

Koh and Evelyn remember this light. In Khalazar's dream city it hung overhead before falling and shattering, and similar blue crystals have appeared within the constructs they fought on their journey. Here a section has been cut away deliberately, as if someone mined the fallen light to build something else. Koh picks up a jagged little shard to study later and drops it into his bag. They avoid a roper that lies beneath the sand farther on, and the long descent from the dune opens into a view of eight black obelisks, braziers of green flame and the sloping sides of a vast pyramid. Figures move at its foot. Some patrol with shining psychic spears; others push the sand back with shovels or carry burdens from the broken tombs. Through Darmah's spyglass Evelyn sees gaunt, exhausted elves labouring below; farther forward Koh recognizes one of the Bronze Ball's waitstaff among them.

Koh goes forward alone to silence the guards. His psychic blades flash behind the obelisks and a spear goes dark, then another. He draws the bodies away and catches their weapons before they can clang on stone. Dominic Reeves slips around the other side of the temple. The enslaved workers are not enemies to be killed. Koh attacks them with a more careful blow of his mind, leaving each unconscious and breathing, hiding them where his friends can gather them. He marks each success with a new rivet formed in his glamoured armour. At the edge of the dune Krandal and his skeletal companions lose their footing. The fall is comically noisy but dangerous: a command calls out from somewhere above, and the elves immediately turn toward the pyramid. Koh dashes between them, taking down as many as he can before they vanish into the doorway.

From afar the companions glimpse an upper balcony. A woman in a faded purple dress seems to be fanning someone on a bed, and Krandal fears she may be Princess Hissia, though he cannot yet recognize her for certain. The others edge forward while Darmah offers Koh a spell to climb the sheer wall. His hooves and hands become sticky, and he scales the black pyramid like a spider. At the same time Dominic enters the lower workshop and finds a dreadful work in progress. Under scaffolding a statue almost ninety feet high is being assembled with a face shaped like Khalazar's and a mouth open in a silent scream. A mechanical spider has been taken apart for parts, and black liquid feeds through tubes into the unfinished figure. Buck Wildfire is there, and Withers, both labouring alongside other exhausted townspeople. Neither has yet been brought out of the spell.

Koh creeps over the upper balcony and onto the ceiling of a bedchamber that he recognizes from the vision inside Anthela's mind. A yellow-green scaled woman with serpent eyes waves a fan; beside her, the muscular Keeves rubs oil across an unmoving body on purple sheets. It is leathery and withered, ornamented with gold and a great bat-like headpiece. It seems very like the emperor's resting form, but it does not stir. Koh passes above guards without their notice and moves toward other rooms beyond. His new ring protects him when a guardian tries to bind him by magic, and at last he puts his fine steel rapier to use against another foe. He has glimpsed enough to tell his friends that many prisoners remain inside, beyond those already lying unconscious outside.

Then a shadow crosses the dunes. A gigantic bat flies silently to the balcony, its vast bones outlined with something like dark skin, green jewels glinting at its throat and a harness clinging to its ribs. Hanging above the bedchamber it turns its head and makes small sharp clicks, searching the room. Koh begins to suspect that even his perfect stealth may mean little against its ears. He carries an unconscious devotee of Pelor away from the upstairs passage and descends toward the workshop, where Dominic is still taking captives out of their enforced labours. Beyond them the bat peers down over the courtyard, and the expedition realizes that they must act before every guard and slave turns upon them.

Darmah has chalk enough for two gates home. The unconscious cannot be piled on top of one another without terrible danger, and only a limited number will fit in each casting; freed people who can walk may hurry through in greater numbers. Krandal could use his power to end the curse one prisoner at a time, but his best prayers will also be needed if Khalazar wakes. They agree on a dangerous compromise. Evelyn will draw the great bat away on the elk, while the others bring the unconscious and the remaining workers into the lower chamber. She turns Loran's blade into a lance and gallops into the dunes. The enormous creature takes wing and follows her.
