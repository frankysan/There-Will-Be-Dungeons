# Season Three — After the Broken Ball

The Bronze Ball has become the city's worst nightmare. By the hour when the guests once expected to be home, the governor's ballroom lies breached and half-forgotten creatures are still emerging from the earth. The Delvers spend the last dark hours helping hold the damage at bay, but their guild is already turning inward: Cassius is under investigation, the heroes have been grounded, and a new inquiry threatens everything they have done in secret.

For Evelyn, the catastrophe brings an unexpected parting. Having escorted young women from the ruined ballroom, she hears Lady Vestal name two schoolgirls still missing. The mysterious Gentleman Cavalier Traveler stays beside her just long enough to explain the curse he believes is consuming him. Somewhere beneath Minotaur Mesa, he says, the evil heart that ruined his companions may still wait. Evelyn promises to find it. She refuses his tentative kiss, but not the quest; then his crimson portal closes and he is gone.

Koh takes his recovered wife back into the family home he nearly lost. His relief lasts only until the first dawn and the guild's prohibition. Told they cannot draw official work or pay, the Delvers nonetheless investigate the damaged bank. There Koh quietly empties wealthy people's deposit boxes, including a gem-rich hoard belonging to Alexandre Withers. Krandal watches in disbelief, drawing a firm line between obstructing an enemy and claiming another person's property. Upstairs, their attempt to make the bank safe turns into a battle with a hook horror and the discovery of its still-living egg. Krandal insists this creature may yet be given a different future. He calls it Samuel and carries it away.

When the guild finds the party clearing more of the wrecked ballroom, the grounding becomes enforceable. Lady Vestal takes Evelyn back toward the school, but her reproach opens into a revelation. The institution founded to train heroic young women has gradually shed its martial purpose. Beneath its fountain survives a secret passage, and at its end a stone door bears the mark of its founder, Loran Ilfelkyr. The heart-shaped hilt that Vestal placed in Evelyn's hands at the ball comes to life when Evelyn remembers the good she has done. Its blade lengthens with her self-belief, then falters at the door. She has found an inheritance, but not yet the confidence or power to claim everything behind it. Before dawn she leaves messages for her companions and sets out to face the family who still do not know her true life.

The farm also attracts visitors. An imposing serpent-bodied royal guard says Princess Hissia was taken below Atomer and swears to serve Krandal until she is rescued; in the meantime he puts his strength to work digging the long-delayed irrigation ditch. Then old Mr Goodwine and his son arrive with more money than Krandal ever expected to see, offering an enormous expansion to his potato enterprise. He will take their agricultural book and a plow, but he refuses to promise them ownership, profit or control before he knows what they truly want.

Koh has his own old associates to face. He takes the bank gems to the Cloven Brotherhood, asking them to convert the loot into money and keep away from his family. The conversation lays bare how little the old criminal world has released its claim on him. Yet back at the guild, behind a locked door, the greater confession is to his daughter. The merchant she thought she knew is a Silver League hero called One Shot, and the dragon scale he gave her came from a beast he helped defeat.

That ability to reach minds becomes the guild's reason to let him help once more. At Pelor's temple he joins Krandal to enter seven tormented elves' dream worlds, one after another, and free each with a curse-removing prayer. It is a small, genuine victory against a darkness still holding many more. The Delvers end their first episode of the new season separated: Evelyn traveling toward an uncertain reckoning, Krandal still tending the strange promises gathering around his farm, and Koh finally beginning the work of recovering the people left behind.

The separation lasts longer than anyone had hoped. While Evelyn's carriage rolls westward through five days of increasingly crowded roads toward smoky Vermicity, Koh and Krandal settle into a daily rotation of rescuing Khalazar's broken victims. Each day their spells relieve another seven; each following day more bewildered elves arrive. Koh clings to the new freedom of evenings with his wife and daughter. Krandal returns to the farm and tries to convert its rutted fields into a proper livelihood. He buys a reddish donkey called Sean, accepts Gleepo Ma's unlikely frog acquaintance Muck Muck as a trial worker, and sets his Snake Island guard to plowing. He has not sold the farm to the eager Goodwines, but the plow they supplied has become indispensable.

In Vermicity, the Blackwoods' elegant home feels smaller than the road. Evelyn's parents have preserved every comfort for their supposedly fragile daughter, but none of it answers what she has become. Their plan is to marry her to Julian Thomas, a prosperous family's heir with more interest in combining fortunes than knowing the woman before him. She refuses. She tries to explain the blessing she carries, the companions who need her and the person she has sworn to find; her parents reach instead for the familiar explanation of illness and impose ever stricter supervision. Her letters pass beneath her mother's searching eye as she quietly asks Margaret to reach the Delvers.

The city around Koh is changing too. A stone barrier rises through the markets and along the shattered financial district, taking his old house into the threatened quarter. In Carrie's laboratory a more personal mystery grows. Using the red shard recovered from Khalazar's dream and blood Anathala willingly gives for research, Carrie constructs an amulet that shines in the presence of certain afflicted elves. Her explanation for why Khalazar chose Anathala rests on a theory about her ancient family; Koh receives the object without mistaking theory for proof. One evening outside his endangered home the jewel blazes. He finds a half-conscious captive of the darkness digging into the soil, knocks the victim down with a deliberately nonlethal psychic blow, and carries the unconscious elf to safety. The next day the victim wakes lucid. Their work at the temple has not been wasted, but Koh begins to understand that the darkness has different depths, and that not every case must be reached by the same means.

The farm yields its own, darker lesson. Poor early growth convinces Krandal to spend real money on a proper barn where potatoes can be readied before planting. His serpent guard throws himself into the construction and leaves decorative snake heads on the roof. The new shoots at last look promising—until Muck Muck warns of a stranger at the edge of Noble Hollow. The figure is gaunt, naked and strangely desiccated, yet it throws magical bolts and suffocating black tendrils into the crops. Krandal fights it alone, demands an explanation and hears the answer directly in his head: someone has promised a reward for his death. The bat insignia of Khalazar marks the creature. When the cleric's face maces finally smash its head, tiny spiders scatter from within. Carrie can identify the body's mummified elven remains and deliberate surgeries, but even she cannot identify the one who ordered the attack.

Julian's confidence in Evelyn proves no match for her determination. She refuses his renewed proposal as readily as the first, and her family shuts her into her room. The crisis that finally persuades her parents does not arrive in a gentleman's calling card. A vampire forces the window open, intending to charm and feed on the young noblewoman. Evelyn takes up her warhammer, resists his glamour and fights him in her nightclothes, wounding him with radiant blows. Her parents witness her drive the creature into the street beneath a thrown javelin. A higher-ranked vampire slayer later finishes it, but the Blackwoods have already seen what their daughter is. They watch her heal her wounds with light and finally concede that her heroic life is real and hers to choose. She summons a celestial elk, promises to visit again and rides back toward Atomer.

Before she arrives, Koh makes one further journey into familiar woodland. He persuades the reclusive gnome Veggie Sticks to help Carrie improve the glowing amulet, and the two speed back through living trees. The completed charm can now indicate a direction as well as a presence. The feral elves have not vanished, but the special hunting duty pays Koh a modest stipend, and he moves his family to a smaller house near the guild. Krandal's barn and fields are flourishing. Meanwhile anonymous stories of three valiant heroes begin appearing around town, praising Cassius, though the master remains under investigation and the Delvers remain grounded.

Three months after the ruined ball, the separated companions converge on an Atomer transformed. A wall encloses the danger beneath the financial district, while arriving Silver League adventurers fill inns, buildings and tents. A herald proclaims that the city is now a gateway to the underground ruins, offers sponsored dungeon raids and names the new rules for licences, expeditions and restricted districts. Evelyn rides in on her elk, Koh moves through the crowd watching for enchanted stragglers, and Krandal has come in for supplies. The darkness has not been banished; it has become the reason thousands are gathering at their door.

The crowd has drawn an entirely different kind of adventurer to Atomer. Beyond the new wall, the presses of *The Daily Quest* thunder all day, converting expeditions, losses and advertisements into salable news. Stories about the three grounded Delvers interest editor Chroma even more than the latest Silver fatalities beneath the city: the guild has forbidden adventures to heroes who, according to an unattributed scrap of prophecy the office purchased from a ratlike informant, might be uniquely important to Atomer's fate. Chroma sends for a veteran correspondent, an elderly halfling with a wide top hat, a duffel of journalistic equipment, a never-ending appetite and a sharp instinct for a story. Dumbledwark of Raisinbunch has returned to the trade.

He first finds Koh in the newly purchased family home. Anathala invites him to tea, but Koh has spent too long hiding from his former companions in crime to answer every question a stranger brings through the door. He speaks kindly of his absent guildmaster and acknowledges the grounding, yet refuses the journalist's suggestion that a truth spell might ease the interview. Dumbledwark senses the caution, takes a photograph and proceeds onward. At Loran Ilfelkyr's Finishing School, Lady Vestal seems no happier to receive a correspondent until he offers to entertain the assembled students with a class photograph. Their eyes fixed on his floating crystal camera, they miss the movement of the fountain behind them. Lady Vestal descends the concealed stairs and brings up Evelyn, dressed again in the fine clothes of a Blackwood daughter. Evelyn is happy to defend Cassius, even letting slip the notion that he may have feelings for the regional director Selenar Everbloom, but she knows little of what the guild has charged him with. She likes the possibility of preserving the Delvers' next adventures in photographicals, and Dumbledwark leaves with the beginnings of a professional invitation, but not yet agreement from her companions.

On the way to the potato farm he discovers that Krandal's enterprise is doing more than growing: Glepo Ma is selling produce from a market cart. Dumbledwark purchases one of the potatoes, has a blacksmith cover it in gilding and presents the extraordinary trophy to a delighted cleric. Krandal has always considered nourishing people his real calling, and answers the reporter's philosophical challenges in those terms. Yet his extraordinary sincerity also leaves a door open. He voluntarily signs consent to a Zone of Truth and attempts to explain why the Delvers remain grounded. Questions about questionable acts performed while helping the city lead him back to the ruined bank, where Koh left with other people's money. Krandal will not condemn the family man who has saved him and his friends, but a reporter has now learned something Koh chose not to say. Krandal draws the line at bringing that reporter to his friend's home for another magically assisted interrogation. The interview ends in a more earthy fashion when the visiting halfling contributes an entirely literal deposit to Krandal's farm compost.

The correspondent returns to the Chimera Guild still wanting the man everyone else refuses to produce. Cassius is absent, his office occupied by Zoltar the Wizened of the Guild Academy. In the course of a guarded interview Zoltar first blames Cassius and the Delvers for the catastrophe of the Bronze Ball. As the journalist presses, the newer truth of Atomer's business emerges in the acting guildmaster's own account: the Academy profits handsomely so long as Khalazar's buried empire remains an endless supply of Silver adventures, while Cassius wanted the danger ended quickly. The Darkest Delvers might actually do it. Dumbledwark proposes giving the public a heroic victory without allowing the lucrative expeditions to stop. Zoltar is persuaded, and hands him a licence to take the Delvers into the dangerous Dungeon C4, promising to free them from their grounding the next day. While his new confidant fetches the marmalade that Dumbledwark has been demanding all afternoon, the reporter takes a second, very different prize from the crates in Cassius's former office: a magical warlock's tome, carefully slipped beneath the equipment in his camera bag.

By evening Dumbledwark rides away with both items. Cassius is still inaccessible, the Delvers remain unaware of the arrangement, and C4 has not been entered by their party. Zoltar has promised a chance to return to adventuring, yet the offered dungeon already carries reports of deaths. The adventure ahead may bring the glory the newspaper wants—or be the means by which the Academy keeps the most troublesome heroes from finishing the job they began.

The promised assignment finally carries them back beneath the governor's ruined ballroom. Mirrors channel sunlight down the old roper breach, and Gruber's lift takes the three Delvers and their new correspondent to the licensed gates of C4. A satyr hawker tries selling an ancient Elvish cipher, but the reporter can read such inscriptions magically and no bargain is struck. Beyond a warning and a locked inner door they find a ponderous stone button, an ingenious flooded channel and a massive wheel. Evelyn strains against the mechanism while Koh remembers a peculiar stone-geared crank acquired months before. Dumbledwark floats his camera through the dark, taking photographicals of the workers. Krandal conjures a rush of water down the old funnel; the wheel turns, and the way opens.

The dungeon holds evidence of what became of its previous visitors. Koh draws a pair of green boots from a deep drain without touching them, only for severed feet to emerge when Krandal tries them on. The name Zigzag is sewn inside, but a spell reveals them to be magical Boots of Speed and Krandal claims them. Nearby Evelyn frees an ornate golden mace jammed in the water wheel, though a severed hand tumbles down with it. White cords hanging from the ceiling are sticky and alive with movement beyond the light. The reporter offers a recovered foot to one and watches it rise into the darkness. Beyond the wheel lies a wetter, older cavern, a corpse afloat in a pool and a body in crushed plate armour. Koh quietly takes the numbered shepherd's shield underneath the latter; Dumbledwark, for his part, has more questions than scruples. He animates a patch of moss to ask about enormous claw marks, then has the crushed corpse describe a thing that killed it: a climbing, lightning-spitting behir.

Evelyn finds the wreck of a stone golem in a side cavern. It has already been broken apart in another battle, but a large blue crystal remains in its chest. She slips the crystal into her bag. Nearby, a great mound of dung conceals something stranger still. Detecting magic within it, the reporter reaches in and pulls at a ringed finger, discovering one of the supposed C4 casualties. Iron Monkey is alive. The monk has survived by hiding within the beast's own droppings, and insists the monsters' eyes and noses will not find anyone who joins him. Dumbledwark sees the logic and covers himself in dung; Evelyn tolerates a little of it on her armour but refuses the hiding place, while Krandal and Koh keep their distance. The monk's survival already calls the Guild's list of dead into doubt.

It is Evelyn who calls the beast to them, banging her growing heart-blade against the walls. The behir winds through the dark with a body like a long serpent and lightning playing around its jaws. Its breath strikes Krandal brutally and scorches Evelyn even as she partly dodges, but the cleric heals himself and wards his companion. Koh darts in and out of the shadows, cutting with psychic blades; Evelyn's sword flares with smiting light. A crushing coil nearly swallows her, but she heals herself and draws Sanctuary around her. The monster turns upon Iron Monkey instead, biting off one of the survivor's arms before he falls back into the dung. Dumbledwark, torn between heroics and his obligation to report, sends inspiration to the others through flashes from his hovering camera and records the danger from his vile hiding place. The fight ends when Krandal's summoned mace finds the behir's throat. As the monster crashes down toward Evelyn, Koh makes a clumsy leap and catches her hand, carrying them both clear in a burst of magic.

The dying behir leaves acid and scattered sparks behind. No one among the four visitors has died; Iron Monkey has survived, though dreadfully wounded, and nothing shows that he receives treatment before the story closes. Over a procession of the day's captured photographicals, Kyle narrates one final act: the recovered blue crystal fits the entry chamber's promise and opens their way out of C4. Whatever story the paper prints about that victory—and whatever happened to Cassius—remains beyond this episode's ending.

After C4 the Guild offers the Delvers a different sort of return to service. Adventurers swarming the new underground passages cannot safely remain there through the night: their mirrored shafts cease carrying sunlight when the sun sets, and the creatures of the deeper stone grow bolder. The Academy wants a refuge, but the likely hosts are flumphs, floating inhabitants of the dark with traditions unfamiliar to the Guild. It is for that reason that the three heroes ride Gruber's lift alongside an appointed intermediary. Destroying Angel is an imposing centaur in worn leathers and long white braids, a Silver adventurer whose task is to keep the human and nonhuman sides talking.

They find the flumphs' establishment at the end of a carpet of violet moss. Its carved doors are marked *Deep Winds Spa*, and the lobby staff hurry to accommodate four figures who seem much too large and nervous for a house of rest. A flumph receptionist named Poof leads them to the mistress, whose oversized bow and hookah are as theatrical as the rituals demanded of visitors. Angel kneels properly and offers a little jar of surface air, a rare breath carried down from the sunlit world. The headmistress accepts the gift and invites them to enjoy the facilities before she will consider making a home for adventurers in her domain.

The spa is unlike any dungeon the Delvers have fought through. Skeletal minotaurs bathe beside a roper, trolls clink glasses near submerged treasure chests with teeth, a beholder glides from basin to basin wrapped in a towel, and flumphs bustle through the halls with little water orbs and steaming dishes. Angel assures the party these strangers have come to a neutral refuge, not a battlefield. That does not make Krandal comfortable about removing his armour—or his beloved belt, from which his maces are eventually banished by a polite attendant. Koh settles into a hot pool as though he had spent his whole life waiting for it. Evelyn is less willing to undress among monsters. Instead she questions a pair of skeletal minotaurs, learning that their people say a cursed Minotaur Mesa lies far to the north near Noble Hollow, then seeks a private room.

The private door calls Evelyn's name without a voice in the air. The flumph attendants reluctantly give permission for her to enter. Inside, a great fishlike creature lounges in a pool, three red eyes set one above another and long tendrils curling over the tiles. It turns the pages of her mind as readily as she turns a book, finding the memories of her family, school, and the handsome Traveler she promised to help. According to this visitor, her Traveler remains bound to the Astral Plane until Khalazar is killed. Evelyn is delighted at what seems an easier solution than a journey beneath Minotaur Mesa, but the knowledge is only as trustworthy as this stranger. He dismisses her and calls for Krandal next. Reading the cleric, he finds childhood potato fields and Snake Island's buried recollections. The distant drumming that echoes in Krandal's mind stops his reading. He professes to know where Princess Hissia is kept, yet never tells Krandal; even a momentary suggestion that she is dead dissolves into a correction of his own expression. The conversation ends with the visitor asking for Koh. None of these private revelations gives the party proof of their ancient mysteries.

The day's hospitality is not altogether uneventful. A beholder puts an anxious Krandal to sleep with a glance, while Koh sips wine beside the monster in untroubled companionship. Evelyn receives a massage and unfamiliar cheese in the privacy of a room. Angel lends a sympathetic ear to a weeping ghost, until the headmistress comes to her with an urgent request. Water elementals visiting the spa have lately brought black sand from deeper below. Something has accumulated in the filtration equipment, frightening the staff away. Clear it, the flumph says, and the Guild's adventurers may be welcome. Angel calls the party out of its unlikely holiday, and the Delvers dress and arm themselves again.

The filtration works are dark, dusty and neglected. Evelyn fills them with magical daylight. At once a black-grained ooze writhes out of the slats, recoiling from the light, and the four try to hold it fast. Within the mass is the shape of an impossibly thin man with long red claws, recalling to Krandal the hollow assassin that once attacked his farm. The thing envelops him before he can understand it and flees as a great rolling ball, carrying the cleric in crushing darkness toward the baths. Koh runs after his friend, hacking at the shape with his psychic blades. Destroying Angel's arrows and Evelyn's lightning strike pierce the mass as it moves, but none can reach Krandal to pull him free. His own radiant prayer hurts it without breaking its hold. As the black ball bursts back among the startled spa guests, Koh retrieves his Drift Globe and thrusts it into the ooze. Daylight flares from the globe; the dark prison breaks open and Krandal tumbles out alive. The long-clawed creature scrambles toward the public pool, only for Koh to finish it with one final psychic strike.

The flumphs find the filters clean when they look again, and their attendants hurry to scrape dark residue from the survivors. Angel rescues Vapo, stuck to a wall where the ooze had struck, while Krandal—who has had rather more than enough of the day—is ushered toward a private massage. The night refuge the Guild wants has not yet been built, and no final alliance is sealed before the session ends. As Koh watches his battered friend disappear down the hall, another private door speaks his name and asks him to come closer. He answers with a question: who is calling? The voice has no time to reply.

The voice beyond the private door does not leave Koh waiting for long. He enters the bath where an aboleth lounges, three eyes set above one another, its great fishlike form almost lost in the warm waters. It toys with his story, reading of Anthela and their daughter, then offers a contest more interesting than a simple conversation: Koh may try to look into its mind in turn. Twice Koh wins. First he finds himself beneath the lake near Withers Keep, looking at the creature's submerged throne and its translucent-skinned servants. The aboleth calls itself a former beast of the lake, and tells him that Khalazar seeks the blood of an ancient people, the Dolbluth, in order to regenerate. The claim hangs in the air without proof. Koh's second plunge finds an impossible labyrinth of thoughts. He cannot uncover what Dolbluth blood means, but he sees a skull-framed black door, a shining triangle and three hollows set into a stone altar. Deeper still he sees the aboleth exalted by thousands of worshippers, with tiny flumphs offered to it like delicacies. The creature calls such dreams its plans, not its present life. Koh leaves with a courteous threat that the surface will not submit so easily.

The Delvers spend an actual night in comfortable rooms at Deep Winds. Destroying Angel has gone ahead by morning; the flumph headmistress is willing to host other adventurers for a price. The Guild has no constructed camp, but its envoys have at least proven the house can shelter guests. The three follow the winding route toward the lift, until Koh spots stairs they passed without seeing on the way down. Down they go, past a carved stone figure with a human ribcage, batlike face and long wing-fingers spread across the walls. Three Silver League adventurers have died on these stairs, one in a moth-wing flight suit, another leather-clad and armed with small throwing blades, and a tattooed Goliath bearing an enormous maul. Evelyn collects their rank badges, together with the moth helmet. Koh removes a ring from the leather-clad corpse and a necklace from the Goliath, careful enough to appear solemn despite his instinct for salvage. No one can yet tell why these adventurers died.

The stairs end at a cavern faced with hundreds of rune-covered stone circles. Some have holes opening behind them, others fold together like interlocking spirals; they look strangely akin to the tablet fragments the Delvers found near Noble Hollow and surrendered to the Guild. An altar is carved with worshipping figures, gricks and hook horrors gathered beneath a spiderlike form. Koh sees black sand resting in its three hollows. Beyond rises the door from the aboleth's mind: skull above, glowing triangle across dark metal. If three things must be put into the altar to open it, those things are not here. The party test the mechanisms, and Evelyn even strikes a space where one plate is missing. It is only rock. Then a disc begins to open by itself.

The first body inside is long dry and pale, an elf hollowed by surgery and time, so much like the dreadful corpse Krandal once met at his farm that the resemblance is impossible for him to ignore. Evelyn pulls it into the open. The next is not content to lie still. Soon mummified elves slide out of their wall recesses, ancient eyes lighting and spectral hands reaching for the intruders. Koh's blades cut from the shadows, Krandal raises Pelor's symbol and at last uses his power to turn undead, and Evelyn pursues a creature that tries to escape up the stairs. Some of the mummies flee, others attack with strange cold and unseen bonds. One seizes Krandal by a paralysing force and lifts him rigidly toward an empty niche; Koh's next psychic strike breaks the spell and sends his friend back to the floor. Another hides itself from sight, but Evelyn catches its dusty tracks and the light of her smiting blade reveals it long enough to cut it down. They could remain for more fights with a cavern full of tombs, but decide the sensible thing is to live long enough to report what they have seen. They leave the door sealed and climb the seemingly endless stairs to daylight.

Atomer is crowded with adventurers wearing nearly identical treasures from the new dungeons, while the cost of nearly everything climbs. Krandal's potato stall is a rare source of cheap food, although he learns his workers have not yet been paid a wage; Gleepo Ma also says something dark has been moving in the barn. Back at the Guild, Carrie identifies the dead adventurers by their badges as Hypnomoth, Shadowblade and Black Bear. Koh hands her the ring and necklace: they are a Ring of Evasion and an Amulet of Health, while the moth helmet is ordinary. She can guess that the newly discovered stairs are an Umbrel Flight noted on an old Withers map, and that three keys might open the triangle door, but she cannot produce any of them. Neither has the Guild restored the Delvers to free service. Koh begins to look for a lift pass that he might copy; no pass is obtained before the friends part.

Evelyn returns to the hidden school beneath the fountain and discovers that her luminous sword can lengthen into a great lance. It opens the magical door for her, and the attendants of Loran's sanctuary welcome her to an elegant meal. The enchanted white-and-blue armour still hangs just out of reach. Krandal, meanwhile, heads to his farm to investigate the reported disturbance. A potato rolls from the barn; a slaughtered rat and a torn leathery case hide beneath the pallets. Then something small and unlikely rubs affectionately against his ankle: a chickenlike youngster sheathed in beetle armour, with tiny hooked hands. The forgotten hook-horror egg has hatched, and its baby has made Krandal its own. He can only stare at his new companion as the episode ends.

The next morning, the three Delvers put discretion above orders. Koh has studied enough lift passes to forge one of his own, and the papers prove better than their acting when the guard notices they are not on the Guild's approved list. Krandal saves the deception with a bluff so commanding that the guard fears making an enemy of the Silver League heroes. He even gives the man a potato. The forged document gets them past the lift's inspection, and they descend again into the deep dungeons with no assignment and no permission. This time Koh takes the covering from the amulet Carrie made with his wife's blood, and it glows as he turns it through the darkness. Following its light, they pass the staircase to the sealed triangle door and crawl still farther through narrow wet stone. Another great skull-arched door waits there, already open. Behind it lie broken animated statues, glyphs pressed into paving stones, old Elvish writing and levers that someone has already manipulated.

That someone is a weary cat-faced woman buried under a huge backpack and an even greater store of indignation. Goggles Granny has a scar across one eye, tiny spectacles and a tail that announces her nature from behind. She accepts Krandal's peace-offering potato without ceremony, and grumbles that her map has brought her to a room even she cannot solve. Beyond them black ooze fills a chamber wider than any ordinary hall. Its far ledges are supported by three enormous stone faces: a weeping countenance, one hiding its eyes as it screams, and a toothed mouth full of burning figures. Three little keys lie on an altar, made with the heads of a grick, a hook horror and a batlike Necromantis. The old woman discovers she knows the language on the wall, and helps Koh translate a grim puzzle of horrors consuming hope, darkness and dawn, and gricks devouring everything.

The three platforms are not readily reached. Evelyn ropes in a little boat, illuminates its prow with Daylight and begins polling through the black slime. Her pole catches on something below, and she falls into the filth. For a moment the weight of her mail threatens to pull her under, but she kicks herself and the boat all the way to the far ledge. Koh races across broken handrails with his magical sash holding him in reserve. Krandal, determined not to be outdone, tries to float a key across on the head of his spectral mace, only to find that its force cannot support solid metal. While they consider other plans, he opens his bag and discovers the newborn hook horror has curled up among the supplies, pecking at every ration it can reach. Its affection has not made it trainable.

Granny has a more extraordinary answer. She gives Krandal a potion without adequately explaining which one; he swallows it expecting to grow, and instead shrinks to a stout, tiny version of himself. She drinks her own enlargement draught, grows to a towering height and pitches the rope-tethered cleric across the slime to the distant platform. By the time everyone can reach a lock, the puzzle is far from over. Wrong keys and wrong lever positions unleash lightning, and Krandal suffers more of it than anyone. Evelyn, who has escaped drowning only to be shocked by the stonework, discovers the key to the light: one lever extinguishes its glow, another burns brightly, and the third must be held halfway between. The screams and the tears and the flames finally fall into their proper places. The last lock consumes its metal key. All around them the surface of the black lake begins to fall, draining into great basins at the corners of the hall.

Where the pool stood is a ruined lower floor and a small altar. A narrow ceramic vessel stands there, bound with gold and crowned by a bat's skull. As Evelyn approaches it, a heartbeat quickens in her ears. She unscrews the gilded cap, and the beating immediately stops. Within rests only a dried, shrivelled heart. Krandal recognizes its ventricles, but neither his faith nor Koh's stories can tell them to whom it belonged. Koh suspects a weighted trap under the vessel. With his fingers he lifts it while holding its place on the plate. Granny takes out her merchant's balance: gold, ceramic and heart together weigh twenty-five pounds. The party entertains increasingly inventive replacements—torches, candles and, from Granny's wondrous alchemical jug, mayonnaise inside a shirt—but none of that is needed. Evelyn finds a stone weighing twenty-three pounds. Two pounds of Granny's measuring weights complete the amount. In a final, careful exchange, Koh and Granny put the stone and weights on the pedestal, and no trap responds. Evelyn packs away the heart.

At the other side of the chamber the old woman considers going home, but is persuaded to follow the friends at least as far as the next descent. The ancient staircase turns out to be longer than her patience, and she stops to catch her breath. They thank her for her help, and she returns safely toward the town whose troubles she says she is trying to end. Only the three Delvers continue to the catacomb of the ancient elves. There they place the recovered vessel upon the altar before the great triangle door. It fits perfectly into one of the hollows.
