# The Rise to Bronze

## A life beyond the guild

The Delvers' first three assignments had carried them into a sealed cave, a sanctuary filled with stolen strength and a ruin haunted by gricks. Success had left all three battered and richer, but even the guild required them to turn away from work for a time. Krandal went to the great temple of Pelor, a place built to welcome sunlight and shelter the sick. There he offered the strength of his hands and the power of his prayers to those who could not make the journey to a healer. His earnestness amused and exasperated the temple's grave elder, who welcomed the help but demanded obedience to his hours and rituals. When a red-scaled veteran arrived suffering from a fiend's venom, Krandal began with a showy healing and discovered that mending wounds did not purge poison. The second blessing did. His visitor departed cured, but Krandal was sent to pray quietly until his sense of propriety improved.[^temple]

He had nearly forgotten a smaller life. Yuri, the thrush he had rescued from a trapper's cart, had spent far too long without water inside his pocket. Krandal found the bird still alive but terribly weak. At the guild he gave it water and tried to nurse it back to health before Jimmy, the ratlike acquisitions clerk, took it gently into his own care. Krandal could only hope the little creature's strength would return.[^yuri]

Evelyn returned to an entirely different world. Behind the school's gardens, respectable dormitories and carefully arranged dresses, she was hiding a life of armed adventure. She climbed through her upstairs window to avoid notice, only to face the curiosity of a loyal bunkmate and the searching questions of Lady Vestal, who had reason to doubt the story that Evelyn had been with her family. Her friend had guarded the secret in another way, too. Threads taken from enchanted dresses had been woven into Evelyn's heavy chain mail. What once took a laborious time to put on could now be donned with a moment's movement, allowing the paladin to pass more easily between the life expected of her and the one she had chosen.[^school]

Koh returned home as carefully as ever. His family knew the trappings of a prosperous household, but he knew what had happened to its fortune. Each doctor's visit and attendant's wage cost him money he dared not openly admit was running out. Adventuring paid for more chances to treat his wife, who remained bedridden, and he came home to his daughter Penelope still concealing the source of that money. The division between his two lives had become a discipline in itself.[^koh]

## A blade with no edge

The ancient hilt from the grick-infested dungeon had been waiting at the guild for inspection. An enthusiastic tiefling artificer named Carrie Underworld presented it to Koh as something neither wholly understood nor easily reproduced: an old sword that answered the force of thought. She called it a Soul Knife, though the original Elvish suggested other names. Carrie herself drew only a tiny flicker of color from the hilt. In Koh's hand, a long blue blade sprang to life, as bright as an inner flame yet solid-looking as crystal. He discovered that it could pass through a wooden table without cutting, but its touch hurt living flesh without leaving blood or a scar.[^blade]

Another observer had been watching. A white-robed, masked mage with an immense hat and a golden tear regarded Koh as a *scion*, warning that others with awakened psychic gifts were searching for artifacts and gaining power from them. The stranger departed before offering anything Koh could easily act upon. Carrie was delighted by the discovery, less certain of its meaning, and suggested that a dragonborn veteran called Buck Broscale might understand more of these new powers. Koh left with the hilt, the blade and a new kind of uncertainty.[^scions]

## Service before advancement

The guild did not share the Delvers' first hopes of promotion. Selenar Everbloom had learned that the gricks they slew were already poisoned and weakened by the underground traps. Their victories were real, but not enough to earn the next rank. The guild wanted adventurers whom the city recognized as helpful, not merely dangerous. Cassius therefore sent them into a string of smaller assignments among the people whose streets and buildings lay within the guild's care.[^denial]

They cleared giant wasps from an inn and discovered that a frightening darkmantle rumor concealed only a rat in a cape. Outside the mountain sanctuary of Kord, they drove back twig blights and saw House and Crush again, now less strangers than acquaintances. A sewer assignment brought them face to face with a carrion crawler and left Krandal far less clean than his companions. In the marketplace, a disguised hag was selling enchanted tarts to those whose dreams she intended to devour. Koh used the memory of his own daughter to recognize that the apparent little girl behaved unlike a child. Evelyn held the public's attention with increasingly extravagant feats, while Krandal frightened onlookers away with the sound of a false earthquake. Their senior companion Molly defeated the hag while the Delvers kept the people out of danger.[^montage]

Further work led them to a fire started by a hellhound. Krandal brought down rain, Evelyn cleared exits with her hammer, and Koh used his climbing sash to reach people trapped above the flames. They later found a genuine darkmantle in a tannery, subdued clothing turned murderous by a curse, and freed a man whose tunneling had been compelled by a glowing-brained cranium rat. The accomplishments were less spectacular than ancient dungeons, perhaps, but the streets were safer for their passage. With every return to the guild they gathered payment, acquaintances and a growing reputation.[^service]

## What the future might hold

Their earnings gave each a different kind of freedom. Krandal watched his coins with an eye to a future potato farm. Koh calculated the cost of care, servants and respectability against the danger of his next job. Evelyn enjoyed the ability to purchase oddities and useful equipment without asking permission from those who oversaw her school. The three no longer resembled newcomers waiting nervously beside the guild's first board.[^wealth]

Cassius eventually brought better news. Their deeds in the city had persuaded Selenar to approve their move to the bronze ranks. The badges and orientation would come in the morning; for one final night, they were still wood-ranked members. They chose their first bronze assignment from the board: Skull Keep, an old castle where an insulting, flying flameskull had driven other hopeful adventurers away, perhaps for good.[^bronze]

They went shopping together. Evelyn chose magical paints and a lightning-bearing javelin, and left her hammer for an enchantment that would make its strongest blows more terrible. Krandal purchased protection against fire, arranged for stronger armor and ordered two new maces engraved with his own face. Koh replenished the daggers and arrows spent on their earlier work and arranged, provisionally, for his shortsword to be enchanted. A change of mind at the end left the sword's final form unsettled. The repairs, new weapons and enchanted armor would still need collecting on the morrow.[^shopping]

At last they gave a name to what the three had become. They had gone again and again toward the places beneath the world's surface, attracted by dangers other people fled. Koh proposed **the Darkest Delvers**; Krandal and Evelyn embraced it. Bronze badges had yet to be placed in their hands, and the flameskull still waited in its keep, but their company no longer lacked an identity.[^name]
