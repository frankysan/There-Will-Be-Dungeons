# Year Two — Stones, Souls and the Great Fang

**Narrative status:** Opening chapters covering corrected **E055–E065** after the played three-month downtime. **Full spoilers** for these episodes and the first arc. This is an editorial retelling drawn from **processed Parakeet SRTs, not an audio-verified transcript**. The [April 2019 bonus](../episodes/BONUS-2019-04.md) offers out-of-character interpretation, not a third scene or definitive unseen event. The full first arc ends in [Year One](YEAR_ONE.md).

## I. Three months and a throne

Tietkalla has changed in the months since Hope Akmenos became its queen. Its walls have been rebuilt, its surviving vehicles repaired, and the administration has orders to install toilets and end older structures of servitude. **Hope has abolished a serf-like status imposed on the houseless**, and declared that employment should follow ability instead of inherited family rank. The noble houses outwardly applaud her program and quietly obstruct it. She soon discovers that administering a city may involve more disputes over clothiers and armorers than battlefield glory. Even basic construction has nearly exhausted the city's stone, wood and steel, prompting the suggestion of trade with the enemy-controlled lands of the Principle.

Her friends try to make a new sort of life. **Vorel and Bok Bok** run **the Golden Sands**, an inn and adventurers' guild whose rules of honest talk, fair dealing and kinship are modeled on the **Pillars of Decency**. **Stanley** learns from the Tidebearers, helps build a hospital and studies medicine under the shadow of his still-present possessor **Diontalus**. **Nash**, haunted by recurring dreams of the Solar Mines, performs finger-puppet shows for children using the small extra arm he brought back from the froghemoth. **Angelashi** reveals they are going to have a child. Nash's first scene of being crushed by a truck, blown apart and reunited with P0D is a **nightmare**, not a gruesome death that requires another resurrection.

The past returns to court in person. The gnome **Softhead**, lost beneath Dusthill long before, steps forward as an envoy and self-described agent of the **Principle**. He offers peace talks, supplies and, most urgently, the promised release of surviving **Dusthill captives**. He insists the original raid was the work of Slavetown's magistrate rather than his ruler. None of that is yet verified, and Queen Hope's response is severe: she **orders his tongue removed and cauterized**, keeping him alive to carry the message that she is prepared to meet. A captured Twenty-Sixer is ordered executed before other rebels. Her newly founded kingdom is becoming more secure—and more frightening.

Elsewhere in the keep, an intruder attacks Hope in her bedroom, invokes an obscure demonic name and strikes with a poisoned blade. With a hidden revolver, a sharpened artificial horn and palace guards, she survives; the assailant does not. The party later gathers at the Golden Sands, where a visiting dwarf trader describes distant roads, the dwarven empire, Slavetown and Principle City. The conversation is soon overtaken by a racist tavern brawl. The heroes have scarcely resumed ordinary fellowship when **a burning object tears across the sky** and **stones begin to rain on Tietkalla**.

**Evidence:** [E055](../episodes/E055.md). A later [April cast roundtable](../episodes/BONUS-2019-04.md) explains Stanley's private motivations during downtime, including research into freeing himself from Diontalus, without implying the full party heard that confession.

## II. A storm of stone and angry dead

The next session begins in chaos. Stones crush buildings and kill people who have no part in the struggle over the crown. The heroes split their attention: Hope races toward the keep, while Stanley, Vorel and Nash try to lead residents into **underground sewer shelters**. They open manholes, summon neighborhood help and guide frightened families out of harm's way. They save people. They also witness deaths that no successful evacuation can undo, and Stanley carries the weight of a child's death as he follows the others into the keep.

Under the castle, their ancient ally **Nobiery** prepares a dangerous rite to ask the **ancestors** what has attacked the city. Hope's spirit leaves her body for a barren **ethereal landscape**, where four severe tiefling figures demand answers to moral questions before offering any judgment. On the other side of the ritual boundary, hostile ghosts reach for the living. **George Miller**, whom Diontalus murdered in the caves, returns as an angry spirit and attacks the group. He **prematurely ages Vorel** before Nash's spell finally disperses him. Another apparition named **Martha Miller** calls for him and retreats; whether this visitation tells the complete truth about their relationship or afterlife remains uncertain.

The ghosts are not the only visitors. In the ethereal realm, **Quizquaz** approaches Hope. He claims a new war is forming among **Andreeste, the Soulbroker and a destructive contractor whose name sounds like Dollo Poppio**. He talks of litigation before the **Grim Council of the Weir** and offers a terrible bargain involving **Melgor and fifty souls**. He further says that a **dragon crossed into the world and was killed**, and that its arrival is tied to the stones. Hope does not agree, and does not step out of the safety circle. It is a compelling story from a demon with every incentive to bargain, **not independently observed proof of a dragon's death or the true cause of the falling stones**.

At the same time, **P0D appears to Nash as a spirit**, recalling a friendship that her death could not erase. Nash speaks to her, but remains in the land of the living. Vorel's ghost-inflicted aging is not permanent either: **Stanley brings the Tidebearer priestess Emenil**, and with diamond dust produced through an improvised application of Nash's magic, she **restores Vorel's youth**. Stanley demonstrates a new ability to **create water** through his Fishbird practice.

That apparent moment of relief becomes another crisis. **Nobiery reveals the royal party's demonic dealings in front of Emenil**. For a devoted Tidebearer, the revelation threatens the legitimacy of Hope's rule and the truth the city has been told about the Weirlords. She demands that the people know. Stanley argues for patience and unity; Nobiery orders an **animated corpse to attack her instead**. The episode ends while the creature bites her. **Her survival or death is not decided at this cutoff.**

**Evidence:** [E056](../episodes/E056.md).

## Interim E056 checkpoint

The city has now endured an actual three months of government **and** a new disaster. The pregnancy is real in the story, the stone storm has killed civilians, Hope has a serious legitimacy problem, and the party's alliances are no longer secured merely by the coronation. George and P0D **appeared as spirits**, not as living resurrected companions. Vorel **was restored before episode end**. E057 confirms **Emenil survives and is healed**, and locates an extraordinary **impact crater**; whether Quizquaz accurately described the dragon or its killers remains open.

## III. A crater of green and a cargo of prisoners

The threat to Emenil is real: **47 Carnage bites her**, Stanley's flying sword cannot stop the first attack, and **Stanley heals her** after Hope orders Nobiery to halt his dead servants. The dispute is not over. Nobiery admits he **told his people the gods sheltered them when he had in fact contracted with the Soulbroker**. Emenil agrees to learn more rather than immediately expose him, still shaken by the deception.

Seeking the source of the rocks, the party takes a rebuilt **royal monster truck** into the desert, leaving **Fate 4** at home. Among shattered stones they find something the Wastes should scarcely contain: **a steaming jungle-like crater**, dotted with green life and glowing plants. A big body or object has been **dragged away by numerous bugbear-sized footprints** and a vehicle; whether it is the dragon from Quizquaz's account is not proven by the tracks alone.

They dig up an unlucky leprechaun with a lost pot of gold. But **Diontalus** abruptly takes Stanley's body and **kills him, devouring the magic**, leaving **a lucky coin for Hope**. The companions are furious. A **Wormfather** war party arrives with bugbears, a robed leader and a venomous winged pet; **Stanley destroys the flying creature**, and the bugbears continue fighting. When the remaining enemies fall, Vorel cuts open a skull and uncovers a **living black parasite** on the brain—real evidence that something has been riding inside the raiders.

The trip home is absurd and tense: **Nash flies off as a dragon**, the truck leaves **bananas** along its path, and he follows them until his transformation ends. Hope dispatches a search party that **retrieves him alive**. Soon the capital sees an altogether different visitor: the **Principle**, a gigantic **metallic figure wearing red rings**, arrives to discuss peace with Hope.

**Evidence:** [E057](../episodes/E057.md) · [E058](../episodes/E058.md).

## IV. A bargain in steel

The Principle speaks courteously and unapologetically. He offers **rail trade, rebuilding resources, an embassy and a local Steel God prayer hall** in return for cooperation. He contends that the Dusthill raid was not authorized by him personally and insists his coercive empire can save mortal souls from Weirlords. The heroes neither embrace that moral vision nor choose a suicidal attack in Hope's throne room. They demand **paid, voluntary labor** and defensive control of railway access, and cautiously proceed.

He makes a far more startling proposal: **Diontalus must be imprisoned**. Before the group can properly understand the danger, the Principle raises a crystal and **pulls the archfey's presence out of Stanley**. Diontalus screams as he is drawn into it; the metallic ruler puts the crystal away. **Stanley is apparently free**, at least for now. No one knows whether the ancient ruler is trustworthy enough to keep this sudden freedom from becoming another bargain's trap.

Then **actual Dusthill survivors arrive**, not just a letter promising their release. **Talia, Bruce Bane, Jimster, Shun'ti and others** are welcomed into Tietkalla. They describe forced life at **Salivating Fields** and their years of separation. Their reunion is real, even if the fate of every missing Dusthill citizen remains to be investigated.

Another obligation intrudes: **Diptak summons Vorel** to reclaim his lizardfolk birthright. The adventurers take **Fate 4**, Diptak and Vorel mount hunting lizards, and the group crosses the sands toward Razak Val. A vast cloud of bloodsuckers drives them into **Stanley's magical hut**, which Nash protects with a mound of earth. At journey's end the reigning Great Fang proves to be **Orp**, the cyclops they once helped steer away from other people. He has built a new home by making the whole settlement deliver **living humans for his meals**.

**Evidence:** [E058](../episodes/E058.md) · [E059](../episodes/E059.md).

## V. Honor has a price

At Razak Val, Vorel thinks he will find the simplicity of the old lizardfolk code. Instead he finds **clans, priesthood, alliances and court politics**, all coiled around the title **Great Fang**. The cyclops calls Vorel his friend and refuses to fight him. Diptak urges a formal contest, and **Orp casually kills Diptak with a single blow**. The loss makes Vorel's duty immediate and personal.

The diminutive, articulate **Mirion Fengar** explains a system in which a new ruler is selected through a **scaling duel**, yet powerful families shape who may rule and keep their own interests alive after a victory. A **Council of Twelve** is worshipped above the valley; **Cave Fengar** hosts a grand dinner before combat. The nobles discuss the **Demon Vold**, dragon traditions and the threatened encroachment of the Principle. The party must decide how much of the system they can tolerate if Vorel wins.

At the feast Orp **finally accepts the duel**, to be held **the next morning**. Guests contend with living snake appetizers and a terrible creature cooked as **“chicken lord.”** Nash slips out toward a latrine, where a cloaked stranger reveals **Nash's own face, unscarred**, and claims to be **Nash from the future**. Only Nash hears this claim in the played scene. The stranger's true origin—and the outcome of the approaching duel—remain out of reach at the end of E060.

**Evidence:** [E060](../episodes/E060.md).

## Interim checkpoint — after E060

**Emenil survives**, **Dusthill survivors have actually returned**, and **Diontalus is apparently contained in a crystal held by the Principle**. The **railroad does not yet exist**. **Orp has killed Diptak but remains Great Fang**, while Vorel's scheduled challenge has **not yet been fought**. The eye-bearing, unscarred man claiming to be future Nash is an **unverified private encounter**, not established time-travel history.

## VI. Two Nashes, one lost heirloom, and a dead Tyrant Slayer

The unscarred stranger Nash meets in the latrine is not alone. Another man with Nash's face emerges, differently dressed and wearing glasses. Both claim they are versions of Nash from different futures, and both demand that their younger self help steal **a preserved Fengar egg**. Their explanation of time is almost deliberately absurd, but their intimate knowledge of Nash and their apparent magic persuade him to proceed. Nash sends two guards to sleep, enters the heirloom room and distracts **Bramie the Tyrant Slayer**, who marvels at the chamber's construction while the other two take the egg and vanish.

Nash leaves Bramie **alive**. That distinction matters when the court learns that Bramie has been found **stabbed in the back with his own dragon-tooth weapon**. Zercil, Bramie's formidable sister, condemns the visitors and challenges both Orp and Vorel for the Great Fang title. Orp, still primarily interested in being fed and entertained, appoints Vorel an impromptu **sheriff**. There are **three identical human sets of footprints** in the chamber and a missing egg, but the signs of theft do not identify the killer. When Nash admits that two supposed future selves brought him there, a sudden chill suggests that his disobedience of their secrecy warning may not be harmless.

Meanwhile the political center shifts around Vorel. A northern **Zark** hunter says the **Demon Vold** is not merely a source of exotic food but a dangerous weakening between worlds, with invaders and a name reminiscent of **Dollo Poppio**. Vorel seeks family records about the **god-killer** and hears the beginning of an ancestral tale from priestess Typalla. Before either investigation can reach its goal, the murder stops the gathering.

**Evidence:** [E061](../episodes/E061.md). The visitors' supposed origin, the identity of the murderer and the ancient prophecy behind the egg remain unresolved.

## VII. A flaming shield and a shattered court

Vorel questions Zercil, who tells him that the settlement's ruler has made everyone an accomplice to **keeping human beings as food**. Her proposed solution is a bargain: kill Orp together, and Vorel will become **her husband and second-in-command** while she claims Great Fang. He will not accept it. Back at the feast he denounces the nobles and priests who have tolerated the captives and demands that Orp's reign end immediately, not at sunrise.

The effort spirals into violence. Orp scarcely understands the scale of what his position has done, while Mirion and the guards demand that Vorel's companions be seized. Vorel leaps onto Orp with the **flaming shield conjured through the Eye of Groxl**. Hope fires the rocket installed in her mechanical arm. Because of the Eye's strange influence over ranged attacks, the projectile strikes Vorel's vicinity; the resulting explosion devastates the crowded hall, **killing Mirion, priestess Typalla, the Elder of Families, General Toxar and numerous other residents, including people not taking part in the attack**. Omnom survives and orders the surviving guards to stand down. This is **not an unqualified rescue or a clean succession**.

Propelled far outside the hall, Vorel and Orp continue their desperate contest. The cyclops mauls and crushes him, but Vorel fights on, using the flaming shield to strike the enormous eye. When Orp finally collapses, **dead**, Vorel barely survives. Standing atop his fallen body with the shield raised, he watches the people of Razak Val answer with their weapons and a growing chorus of approval. **Omnom names him Great Fang.** Vorel's earlier self-declared kingship has become **publicly acknowledged office**, gained at terrible cost.

**Evidence:** [E062](../episodes/E062.md). **Mawstorm** is Vorel's separate sword; the fatal shield is an **Eye of Groxl** effect.

## VIII. The Fang's first test

Zercil will not accept her rival's glory. She glides to Orp's body with the family's **Tyrant's Bane**, ready to receive the crown Vorel had contemplated leaving behind. But Vorel speaks of protecting all lizardfolk, displays brilliant changes of scale colour, and earns the assembled people's applause instead of surrendering it to her. Now Zercil attacks him directly.

Vorel has almost no strength left. **Stanley privately heals him** in the struggle; **Nash becomes a dragon**, drawing bows from the astonished crowd. Vorel sees small dark shapes seeming to writhe behind Zercil's eyes and tries to end the fight **without killing her**. Hope, witnessing the peril and hearing his warnings, fires one of **Scully's Obsidian Nails**. Zercil collapses into dust. The public can see that an outsider interfered, yet Nash's performance as a dragon helps steady the crowd, and Vorel remains their **Great Fang**. Omnom agrees to advise him but angrily remembers the slaughter, especially priestess Typalla's death, and makes clear that Hope has earned a lasting enemy.

There is little opportunity to enjoy sovereignty. Vorel turns first toward the **human pit beneath the throne**, where some **two to three hundred weakened captives** wait under guard. **Stanley announces their freedom**, and Nash shapes rooms of earth so the captives can undergo examinations and receive help. They are **not all released during this episode**. One prisoner, a calm, legless entertainer calling himself **Follow**, seems different to Stanley; he is kept apart. Vorel and Hope find a **live black worm in Zercil's bedding** and seal it in a container. Nearby, the Fengar children discover that both parents are dead. Meanwhile Stanley alone catches the gaze of a young human girl among the crowd—and she vanishes.

These are beginnings rather than resolutions: the worm's purpose is not fully known, the murdered Tyrant Slayer has no proven killer, the egg remains missing, and a whole population of newly promised free people must still be safely cared for. **The reign has begun, but whether Vorel can reform Razak Val remains open.**

**Evidence:** [E063](../episodes/E063.md).

## Interim checkpoint — after E063

**Orp is dead E062, Zercil dead E063, and Vorel is publicly acknowledged as Great Fang alongside Hope's existing queenship in Tietkalla.** The social price of Hope's rocket, dishonorable interference in Zercil's fight and the deaths of Razak Val's leaders will not be erased by a new title. **The captives are being screened but have not all been released**. The captured black worm, the suspicious man **Follow**, the vanishing girl, **Bramie's murderer**, and the **future-Nash egg theft** remain open. The **Principle still possesses Diontalus's crystal as last shown**, and **Emenil was alive at the last Tietkalla cutoff**.

## IX. The feast that ate a city

In Razak Val, Vorel's new authority is put to its first impossible test. The **Sun's Witnesses** huddled beneath the throne are too ill to travel but too strange to trust. An unusually healthy captive named **Follow**, legless and carried on wheels, laughs at the doctors' efforts; he can **speak within their heads**. He points out one prisoner whose thoughts seem wrong. Stanley finds nothing clearly abnormal, even after an invasive search, while Omnom presses for harsher methods. Vorel calls **Dollo Poppio**. A body seizes, eyeballs lengthen on stalks, and **worms spray over the party**. Vorel's flames burn off the visible intruders and kill the transformed man.

Then the whole pit erupts. The blinded and starving humans change into **chicken lords**—grotesque creatures that engulf attackers with their mouthparts. **Nash closes one chamber and blasts the pit with fire**, but dozens are only part of a horde. Aboveground, lizardfolk have acquired **extra arms** and are battling their own people. It is no longer possible to grant freedom to the captives in any simple way. **Omnom remains to fight**, accepting Orp's recovered **Necklace of Fireballs** from the adventurers. Vorel, Great Fang of a city he has just inherited, must leave it for a time or perish with it. He directs his companions toward the Zarks at Kervalon, on the suspicion that they brought the tainted Sun's Witnesses.

Their **raptors** race through a canyon pursued by chicken lords. A narrow rope bridge gives way beneath Hope; the others escape and help hold the pursuers off until she climbs back to safety. Many monsters plunge into the ravine. After camping in the desert, the friends choose the dangerous purple sands over returning to search for **Fate 4**. Then the engine of **Fate 4 itself** is heard across the wasteland. Stanley's megaphone calls it back. A little girl appears in the door, briefly vanishes, and is followed by **Follow**, who asks whether the adventurers would like a ride in their own stolen vehicle. With him is a massive orc, now occupying its damaged driving compartment.

**Evidence:** [E064](../episodes/E064.md). The city's fate, Omnom's survival, and the Zarks' guilt are all unresolved.

## X. Old sins, new families and rain

On the road, Follow names the girl **Sira** and the enormous orc **Rorf**. He tells a story about a place his unusual people called **Home**, destroyed by gobbears; a lizardfolk hunt then massacred most of their traveling group as mutants. Follow claims to manipulate minds, Sira can disappear and Rorf can change shape. The little girl's ears show scars where their pointed upper parts have been cut off. Stanley speaks in **Elvish** and she understands.

Follow also has a darker secret. His companions tried to rescue him by arranging succession fights against Orp, removing those heirs who stood in challengers' way. **Rorf admits that he killed Bramie Fengar.** The murder blamed on intruders or perhaps Zercil has a new confessed perpetrator, while the strange theft of the Fengar egg by Nash's other faces remains a separate crime. Follow wants only safety for Sira; when Hope offers her realm of **Tietkalla** as a future home, he accepts, though no one has yet arrived there.

Ahead, the purple horizon fills with thunder. **Rain**, almost unbelievable to these wasteland travelers, sweeps across the sand. Rorf struggles with the driving and Hope takes over Fate 4, only to bog it in deep mud. **Nash raises the ground with magic** to free the wheels, then carves a wide water-diversion basin near **Stanley's Tiny Hut** as the storm intensifies. Sira settles into a borrowed bedroll; Stanley hums an old, half-remembered melody in a language she understands. Far away, lightning reveals an enormous creature's short arms and head.

Night brings more improbable visitors. Men wearing Nash's face arrive one after another, each insisting he comes from some future. They have **two copies of the Fengar egg**, conflicting stories about whether it should have been **a magical leg**, and a warning that a planet might explode. One egg remains behind as the others depart. But no one knows what to believe. The ground shivers under the hut, the puddles ripple, and a **Tyrannosaurus rex approaches Fate 4**. Vorel hides beneath the vehicle, ready to defend it. The giant sniffs the car. Their next fight—or escape—is still beyond the edge of this recording.

**Evidence:** [E065](../episodes/E065.md). The [July-archived Q&A](../episodes/BONUS-2019-07.md) separately explains creator intentions; it is not another night in the storm.

## Where the story stands after E065

**Vorel is still Great Fang**, but **Razak Val is engulfed in a creature-and-lizardfolk conflict**, Omnom is last seen fighting, and the surviving captives' fate is unknown. **Rorf has admitted Bramie's murder**. Follow, Sira and Rorf have **accepted an offer of eventual Tietkalla refuge** while remaining with the party in **Fate 4**. **One Fengar egg copy is with the party**, while the egg-versus-leg puzzle and the apparent future Nashes' true provenance remain unresolved. **A T. rex has reached the RV in a sudden rainstorm**, not been killed or escaped.
