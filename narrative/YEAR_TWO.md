# Year Two — Stones, Souls and the Great Fang

**Narrative status:** Opening chapters covering corrected **E055–E080** after the played three-month downtime. **Full spoilers** for these episodes and the first arc. This is an editorial retelling drawn from **processed Parakeet SRTs, not an audio-verified transcript**. The [April 2019 bonus](../episodes/BONUS-2019-04.md) offers out-of-character interpretation, not a third scene or definitive unseen event. The full first arc ends in [Year One](YEAR_ONE.md).

## I. Three months and a throne

Tietkalla has changed in the months since Hope Akmenos became its queen. Its walls have been rebuilt, its surviving vehicles repaired, and the administration has orders to install toilets and end older structures of servitude. **Hope has abolished a serf-like status imposed on the houseless**, and declared that employment should follow ability instead of inherited family rank. The noble houses outwardly applaud her program and quietly obstruct it. She soon discovers that administering a city may involve more disputes over clothiers and armorers than battlefield glory. Even basic construction has nearly exhausted the city's stone, wood and steel, prompting the suggestion of trade with the enemy-controlled lands of the Principle.

Her friends try to make a new sort of life. **Vorel and Bok Bok** run **the Golden Sands**, an inn and adventurers' guild whose rules of honest talk, fair dealing and kinship are modeled on the **Pillars of Decency**. **Stanley** learns from the Tidebearers, helps build a hospital and studies medicine under the shadow of his still-present possessor **Diontalus**. **Nash**, haunted by recurring dreams of the Solar Mines, performs finger-puppet shows for children using the small extra arm he brought back from the froghemoth. **Angelashi** reveals they are going to have a child. Nash's first scene of being crushed by a truck, blown apart and reunited with P0D is a **nightmare**, not a gruesome death that requires another resurrection.

The past returns to court in person. The gnome **Softhead**, lost beneath Dusthill long before, steps forward as an envoy and self-described agent of the **Principle**. He offers peace talks, supplies and, most urgently, the promised release of surviving **Dusthill captives**. He insists the original raid was the work of Slavetown's magistrate rather than his ruler. None of that is yet verified, and Queen Hope's response is severe: she **orders his tongue removed and cauterized**, keeping him alive to carry the message that she is prepared to meet. A captured Twenty-Sixer is ordered executed before other rebels. Her newly founded kingdom is becoming more secure—and more frightening.

Elsewhere in the keep, an intruder attacks Hope in her bedroom, invokes an obscure demonic name and strikes with a poisoned blade. With a hidden revolver, a sharpened artificial horn and palace guards, she survives; the assailant does not. The party later gathers at the Golden Sands, where a visiting dwarf trader describes distant roads, the dwarven empire, Slavetown and Principle City. The conversation is soon overtaken by a racist tavern brawl. The heroes have scarcely resumed ordinary fellowship when **a burning object tears across the sky** and **stones begin to rain on Tietkalla**.

**Evidence:** [E055](../episodes/E055.md). A later [April cast roundtable](../episodes/BONUS-2019-04.md) explains Stanley's private motivations during downtime, including research into freeing himself from Diontalus, without implying the full party heard that confession.

## II. A storm of stone and angry dead

The next session begins in chaos. Stones crush buildings and kill people who have no part in the struggle over the crown. The heroes split their attention: Hope races toward the keep, while Stanley, Vorel and Nash try to lead residents into **underground sewer shelters**. They open manholes, summon neighborhood help and guide frightened families out of harm's way. They save people. They also witness deaths that no successful evacuation can undo, and Stanley carries the weight of a child's death as he follows the others into the keep.

Under the castle, their ancient ally **Nobiery** prepares a dangerous rite to ask the **ancestors** what has attacked the city. Hope's spirit leaves her body for a barren **ethereal landscape**, where four severe tiefling figures demand answers to moral questions before offering any judgment. On the other side of the ritual boundary, hostile ghosts reach for the living. **George Miller**, whom Diontalus murdered in the caves, returns as an angry spirit and attacks the group. He **prematurely ages Vorel** before Nash's spell finally disperses him. Another apparition named **Martha Miller** calls for him and retreats; whether this visitation tells the complete truth about their relationship or afterlife remains uncertain.

The ghosts are not the only visitors. In the ethereal realm, **Quizquaz** approaches Hope. He claims a new war is forming among **Andreeste, the Soulbroker and a destructive contractor whose name sounds like Dollo Poppio**. He talks of litigation before the **Grim Council of the Weir** and offers a terrible bargain involving **Melgor and fifty souls**. He further says that a **dragon crossed into the world and was killed**, and that its arrival is tied to the stones. Hope does not agree, and does not step out of the safety circle. It is a compelling story from a demon with every incentive to bargain, **not independently observed proof of a dragon's death or the true cause of the falling stones**.

At the same time, **P0D appears to Nash as a spirit**, recalling a friendship that her death could not erase. Nash speaks to her, but remains in the land of the living. Vorel's ghost-inflicted aging is not permanent either: **Stanley brings the Tidebearer priestess Emenil**, and with diamond dust produced through an improvised application of Nash's magic, she **restores Vorel's youth**. Stanley demonstrates a new ability to **create water** through his Fishbird practice.

That apparent moment of relief becomes another crisis. **Nobiery reveals the royal party's demonic dealings in front of Emenil**. For a devoted Tidebearer, the revelation threatens the legitimacy of Hope's rule and the truth the city has been told about the Weirlords. She demands that the people know. Stanley argues for patience and unity; Nobiery orders an **animated corpse to attack her instead**. The episode ends while the creature bites her. **Her survival or death is not decided at this cutoff.**

**Evidence:** [E056](../episodes/E056.md).

## Interim E056 checkpoint

The city has now endured an actual three months of government **and** a new disaster. The pregnancy is real in the story, the stone storm has killed civilians, Hope has a serious legitimacy problem, and the party's alliances are no longer secured merely by the coronation. George and P0D **appeared as spirits**, not as living resurrected companions. Vorel **was restored before episode end**. E057 confirms **Emenil survives and is healed**, and locates an extraordinary **impact crater**; whether Quizquaz accurately described the dragon or its killers remains open.

## III. A crater of green and a cargo of prisoners

The threat to Emenil is real: **47 Carnage bites her**, Stanley's flying sword cannot stop the first attack, and **Stanley heals her** after Hope orders Nobiery to halt his dead servants. The dispute is not over. Nobiery admits he **told his people the gods sheltered them when he had in fact contracted with the Soulbroker**. Emenil agrees to learn more rather than immediately expose him, still shaken by the deception.

Seeking the source of the rocks, the party takes a rebuilt **royal monster truck** into the desert, leaving **Fate 4** at home. Among shattered stones they find something the Wastes should scarcely contain: **a steaming jungle-like crater**, dotted with green life and glowing plants. A big body or object has been **dragged away by numerous bugbear-sized footprints** and a vehicle; whether it is the dragon from Quizquaz's account is not proven by the tracks alone.

They dig up an unlucky leprechaun with a lost pot of gold. But **Diontalus** abruptly takes Stanley's body and **kills him, devouring the magic**, leaving **a lucky coin for Hope**. The companions are furious. A **Wormfather** war party arrives with bugbears, a robed leader and a venomous winged pet; **Stanley destroys the flying creature**, and the bugbears continue fighting. When the remaining enemies fall, Vorel cuts open a skull and uncovers a **living black parasite** on the brain—real evidence that something has been riding inside the raiders.

The trip home is absurd and tense: **Nash flies off as a dragon**, the truck leaves **bananas** along its path, and he follows them until his transformation ends. Hope dispatches a search party that **retrieves him alive**. Soon the capital sees an altogether different visitor: the **Principle**, a gigantic **metallic figure wearing red rings**, arrives to discuss peace with Hope.

**Evidence:** [E057](../episodes/E057.md) · [E058](../episodes/E058.md).

## IV. A bargain in steel

The Principle speaks courteously and unapologetically. He offers **rail trade, rebuilding resources, an embassy and a local Steel God prayer hall** in return for cooperation. He contends that the Dusthill raid was not authorized by him personally and insists his coercive empire can save mortal souls from Weirlords. The heroes neither embrace that moral vision nor choose a suicidal attack in Hope's throne room. They demand **paid, voluntary labor** and defensive control of railway access, and cautiously proceed.

He makes a far more startling proposal: **Diontalus must be imprisoned**. Before the group can properly understand the danger, the Principle raises a crystal and **pulls the archfey's presence out of Stanley**. Diontalus screams as he is drawn into it; the metallic ruler puts the crystal away. **Stanley is apparently free**, at least for now. No one knows whether the ancient ruler is trustworthy enough to keep this sudden freedom from becoming another bargain's trap.

Then **actual Dusthill survivors arrive**, not just a letter promising their release. **Talia, Bruce Bane, Jimster, Shun'ti and others** are welcomed into Tietkalla. They describe forced life at **Salivating Fields** and their years of separation. Their reunion is real, even if the fate of every missing Dusthill citizen remains to be investigated.

Another obligation intrudes: **Diptak summons Vorel** to reclaim his lizardfolk birthright. The adventurers take **Fate 4**, Diptak and Vorel mount hunting lizards, and the group crosses the sands toward Razak Val. A vast cloud of bloodsuckers drives them into **Stanley's magical hut**, which Nash protects with a mound of earth. At journey's end the reigning Great Fang proves to be **Orp**, the cyclops they once helped steer away from other people. He has built a new home by making the whole settlement deliver **living humans for his meals**.

**Evidence:** [E058](../episodes/E058.md) · [E059](../episodes/E059.md).

## V. Honor has a price

At Razak Val, Vorel thinks he will find the simplicity of the old lizardfolk code. Instead he finds **clans, priesthood, alliances and court politics**, all coiled around the title **Great Fang**. The cyclops calls Vorel his friend and refuses to fight him. Diptak urges a formal contest, and **Orp casually kills Diptak with a single blow**. The loss makes Vorel's duty immediate and personal.

The diminutive, articulate **Mirion Fengar** explains a system in which a new ruler is selected through a **scaling duel**, yet powerful families shape who may rule and keep their own interests alive after a victory. A **Council of Twelve** is worshipped above the valley; **Cave Fengar** hosts a grand dinner before combat. The nobles discuss the **Demon Vold**, dragon traditions and the threatened encroachment of the Principle. The party must decide how much of the system they can tolerate if Vorel wins.

At the feast Orp **finally accepts the duel**, to be held **the next morning**. Guests contend with living snake appetizers and a terrible creature cooked as **“chicken lord.”** Nash slips out toward a latrine, where a cloaked stranger reveals **Nash's own face, unscarred**, and claims to be **Nash from the future**. Only Nash hears this claim in the played scene. The stranger's true origin—and the outcome of the approaching duel—remain out of reach at the end of E060.

**Evidence:** [E060](../episodes/E060.md).

## Interim checkpoint — after E060

**Emenil survives**, **Dusthill survivors have actually returned**, and **Diontalus is apparently contained in a crystal held by the Principle**. The **railroad does not yet exist**. **Orp has killed Diptak but remains Great Fang**, while Vorel's scheduled challenge has **not yet been fought**. The eye-bearing, unscarred man claiming to be future Nash is an **unverified private encounter**, not established time-travel history.

## VI. Two Nashes, one lost heirloom, and a dead Tyrant Slayer

The unscarred stranger Nash meets in the latrine is not alone. Another man with Nash's face emerges, differently dressed and wearing glasses. Both claim they are versions of Nash from different futures, and both demand that their younger self help steal **a preserved Fengar egg**. Their explanation of time is almost deliberately absurd, but their intimate knowledge of Nash and their apparent magic persuade him to proceed. Nash sends two guards to sleep, enters the heirloom room and distracts **Bramie the Tyrant Slayer**, who marvels at the chamber's construction while the other two take the egg and vanish.

Nash leaves Bramie **alive**. That distinction matters when the court learns that Bramie has been found **stabbed in the back with his own dragon-tooth weapon**. Zercil, Bramie's formidable sister, condemns the visitors and challenges both Orp and Vorel for the Great Fang title. Orp, still primarily interested in being fed and entertained, appoints Vorel an impromptu **sheriff**. There are **three identical human sets of footprints** in the chamber and a missing egg, but the signs of theft do not identify the killer. When Nash admits that two supposed future selves brought him there, a sudden chill suggests that his disobedience of their secrecy warning may not be harmless.

Meanwhile the political center shifts around Vorel. A northern **Zark** hunter says the **Demon Vold** is not merely a source of exotic food but a dangerous weakening between worlds, with invaders and a name reminiscent of **Dollo Poppio**. Vorel seeks family records about the **god-killer** and hears the beginning of an ancestral tale from priestess Typalla. Before either investigation can reach its goal, the murder stops the gathering.

**Evidence:** [E061](../episodes/E061.md). The visitors' supposed origin, the identity of the murderer and the ancient prophecy behind the egg remain unresolved.

## VII. A flaming shield and a shattered court

Vorel questions Zercil, who tells him that the settlement's ruler has made everyone an accomplice to **keeping human beings as food**. Her proposed solution is a bargain: kill Orp together, and Vorel will become **her husband and second-in-command** while she claims Great Fang. He will not accept it. Back at the feast he denounces the nobles and priests who have tolerated the captives and demands that Orp's reign end immediately, not at sunrise.

The effort spirals into violence. Orp scarcely understands the scale of what his position has done, while Mirion and the guards demand that Vorel's companions be seized. Vorel leaps onto Orp with the **flaming shield conjured through the Eye of Groxl**. Hope fires the rocket installed in her mechanical arm. Because of the Eye's strange influence over ranged attacks, the projectile strikes Vorel's vicinity; the resulting explosion devastates the crowded hall, **killing Mirion, priestess Typalla, the Elder of Families, General Toxar and numerous other residents, including people not taking part in the attack**. Omnom survives and orders the surviving guards to stand down. This is **not an unqualified rescue or a clean succession**.

Propelled far outside the hall, Vorel and Orp continue their desperate contest. The cyclops mauls and crushes him, but Vorel fights on, using the flaming shield to strike the enormous eye. When Orp finally collapses, **dead**, Vorel barely survives. Standing atop his fallen body with the shield raised, he watches the people of Razak Val answer with their weapons and a growing chorus of approval. **Omnom names him Great Fang.** Vorel's earlier self-declared kingship has become **publicly acknowledged office**, gained at terrible cost.

**Evidence:** [E062](../episodes/E062.md). **Mawstorm** is Vorel's separate sword; the fatal shield is an **Eye of Groxl** effect.

## VIII. The Fang's first test

Zercil will not accept her rival's glory. She glides to Orp's body with the family's **Tyrant's Bane**, ready to receive the crown Vorel had contemplated leaving behind. But Vorel speaks of protecting all lizardfolk, displays brilliant changes of scale colour, and earns the assembled people's applause instead of surrendering it to her. Now Zercil attacks him directly.

Vorel has almost no strength left. **Stanley privately heals him** in the struggle; **Nash becomes a dragon**, drawing bows from the astonished crowd. Vorel sees small dark shapes seeming to writhe behind Zercil's eyes and tries to end the fight **without killing her**. Hope, witnessing the peril and hearing his warnings, fires one of **Scully's Obsidian Nails**. Zercil collapses into dust. The public can see that an outsider interfered, yet Nash's performance as a dragon helps steady the crowd, and Vorel remains their **Great Fang**. Omnom agrees to advise him but angrily remembers the slaughter, especially priestess Typalla's death, and makes clear that Hope has earned a lasting enemy.

There is little opportunity to enjoy sovereignty. Vorel turns first toward the **human pit beneath the throne**, where some **two to three hundred weakened captives** wait under guard. **Stanley announces their freedom**, and Nash shapes rooms of earth so the captives can undergo examinations and receive help. They are **not all released during this episode**. One prisoner, a calm, legless entertainer calling himself **Follow**, seems different to Stanley; he is kept apart. Vorel and Hope find a **live black worm in Zercil's bedding** and seal it in a container. Nearby, the Fengar children discover that both parents are dead. Meanwhile Stanley alone catches the gaze of a young human girl among the crowd—and she vanishes.

These are beginnings rather than resolutions: the worm's purpose is not fully known, the murdered Tyrant Slayer has no proven killer, the egg remains missing, and a whole population of newly promised free people must still be safely cared for. **The reign has begun, but whether Vorel can reform Razak Val remains open.**

**Evidence:** [E063](../episodes/E063.md).

## Interim checkpoint — after E063

**Orp is dead E062, Zercil dead E063, and Vorel is publicly acknowledged as Great Fang alongside Hope's existing queenship in Tietkalla.** The social price of Hope's rocket, dishonorable interference in Zercil's fight and the deaths of Razak Val's leaders will not be erased by a new title. **The captives are being screened but have not all been released**. The captured black worm, the suspicious man **Follow**, the vanishing girl, **Bramie's murderer**, and the **future-Nash egg theft** remain open. The **Principle still possesses Diontalus's crystal as last shown**, and **Emenil was alive at the last Tietkalla cutoff**.

## IX. The feast that ate a city

In Razak Val, Vorel's new authority is put to its first impossible test. The **Sun's Witnesses** huddled beneath the throne are too ill to travel but too strange to trust. An unusually healthy captive named **Follow**, legless and carried on wheels, laughs at the doctors' efforts; he can **speak within their heads**. He points out one prisoner whose thoughts seem wrong. Stanley finds nothing clearly abnormal, even after an invasive search, while Omnom presses for harsher methods. Vorel calls **Dollo Poppio**. A body seizes, eyeballs lengthen on stalks, and **worms spray over the party**. Vorel's flames burn off the visible intruders and kill the transformed man.

Then the whole pit erupts. The blinded and starving humans change into **chicken lords**—grotesque creatures that engulf attackers with their mouthparts. **Nash closes one chamber and blasts the pit with fire**, but dozens are only part of a horde. Aboveground, lizardfolk have acquired **extra arms** and are battling their own people. It is no longer possible to grant freedom to the captives in any simple way. **Omnom remains to fight**, accepting Orp's recovered **Necklace of Fireballs** from the adventurers. Vorel, Great Fang of a city he has just inherited, must leave it for a time or perish with it. He directs his companions toward the Zarks at Kervalon, on the suspicion that they brought the tainted Sun's Witnesses.

Their **raptors** race through a canyon pursued by chicken lords. A narrow rope bridge gives way beneath Hope; the others escape and help hold the pursuers off until she climbs back to safety. Many monsters plunge into the ravine. After camping in the desert, the friends choose the dangerous purple sands over returning to search for **Fate 4**. Then the engine of **Fate 4 itself** is heard across the wasteland. Stanley's megaphone calls it back. A little girl appears in the door, briefly vanishes, and is followed by **Follow**, who asks whether the adventurers would like a ride in their own stolen vehicle. With him is a massive orc, now occupying its damaged driving compartment.

**Evidence:** [E064](../episodes/E064.md). The city's fate, Omnom's survival, and the Zarks' guilt are all unresolved.

## X. Old sins, new families and rain

On the road, Follow names the girl **Sira** and the enormous orc **Rorf**. He tells a story about a place his unusual people called **Home**, destroyed by gobbears; a lizardfolk hunt then massacred most of their traveling group as mutants. Follow claims to manipulate minds, Sira can disappear and Rorf can change shape. The little girl's ears show scars where their pointed upper parts have been cut off. Stanley speaks in **Elvish** and she understands; **later, despite her usual silence, Sira speaks in Elvish to ask for a song**.

Follow also has a darker secret. His companions tried to rescue him by arranging succession fights against Orp, removing those heirs who stood in challengers' way. **Rorf admits that he killed Bramie Fengar.** The murder blamed on intruders or perhaps Zercil has a new confessed perpetrator, while the strange theft of the Fengar egg by Nash's other faces remains a separate crime. Follow wants only safety for Sira; when Hope offers her realm of **Tietkalla** as a future home, he accepts, though no one has yet arrived there.

Ahead, the purple horizon fills with thunder. **Rain**, almost unbelievable to these wasteland travelers, sweeps across the sand. Rorf struggles with the driving and Hope takes over Fate 4, only to bog it in deep mud. **Nash raises the ground with magic** to free the wheels, then carves a wide water-diversion basin near **Stanley's Tiny Hut** as the storm intensifies. Sira settles into a borrowed bedroll; Stanley hums an old, half-remembered melody in a language she understands. Far away, lightning reveals an enormous creature's short arms and head.

Night brings more improbable visitors. Men wearing Nash's face arrive one after another, each insisting he comes from some future. They have **two copies of the Fengar egg**, conflicting stories about whether it should have been **a magical leg**, and a warning that a planet might explode. One egg remains behind as the others depart. But no one knows what to believe. The ground shivers under the hut, the puddles ripple, and a **Tyrannosaurus rex approaches Fate 4**. Vorel hides beneath the vehicle, ready to defend it. The giant sniffs the car. Their next fight—or escape—is still beyond the edge of this recording.

**Evidence:** [E065](../episodes/E065.md). The [July-archived Q&A](../episodes/BONUS-2019-07.md) separately explains creator intentions; it is not another night in the storm.

## Interim checkpoint — after E065

**Vorel is still Great Fang**, but **Razak Val is engulfed in a creature-and-lizardfolk conflict**, Omnom is last seen fighting, and the surviving captives' fate is unknown. **Rorf has admitted Bramie's murder**. Follow, Sira and Rorf have **accepted an offer of eventual Tietkalla refuge** while remaining with the party in **Fate 4**. **One Fengar egg copy is with the party**, while the egg-versus-leg puzzle and the apparent future Nashes' true provenance remain unresolved. **A T. rex has reached the RV in a sudden rainstorm**, not been killed or escaped.

## XI. What is an engine without a vehicle?

The rain does not relent. Beneath **Fate 4**, Vorel watches a giant Tyrannosaurus rex sniffing at the machine that has carried the friends so far. He rushes to draw the creature away, raising his **Eye of Groxl's flaming shield** so that it will follow him toward Nash's drainage pit. His plan is valiant but too late. With almost no regard for its human-made obstacle the dinosaur **tramples and destroys the RV**, then plummets into the deep hole. **Nash scorches it with Fireball**, and Vorel kills it with repeated blows from the returning shield. The wreck subsequently erupts in flames.

Hope says little, devastated. Trying to atone, Vorel cuts a **dagger from a dinosaur tooth** and assembles bones, surviving wheels and other wreckage into a wheeled **Fang Fate Five**. It cannot replace the original Fate 4, and even this makeshift cart will be unusable in the trees soon to come.

Those trees begin with a voice inside everyone's mind. Strange lizardfolk gather in the dark rain, and above them appears a floating, tentacled **mind flayer**. He names himself in words that resemble **Oubvayx** and claims to speak for **Dollo Poppio**, a greater master. He tells Hope a story in which **Andreeste has a claim upon the souls of Tietkalla**, while the Soulbroker's bargain blocks her: help him defeat the **Principle**, he promises, and the party might be free of their bargain at the price of the kingdom. Hope refuses another deal with demons.

The creature holds **Sira** helpless in the air. The others attack, but its projected or magically shielded presence seems beyond their reach. **Follow rises from his chair in a desperate effort to rescue his daughter**, and is slammed to the ground. **Rorf turns into a huge bird** and flies toward the enemy, only to be crushed into a tiny fragment. A whole **forest of dark, leafless trees bearing living eyes** springs up as the mind flayer vanishes with Sira, telling the friends to find him at **Kervalon**.

Stanley finds Follow dying. The man has barely enough life to demand that his daughter be rescued and to hear Stanley promise he will do everything he can. Then Follow dies. **Stanley and Hope bury him**, while Nash and Vorel lash out at the strange trees. In the following hours Stanley even turns Nash into a mouse to rebuke him, though the spell wears off. The sky stays dark, and the rain continues long after another night's sleep.

Eventually, walking through the watching woods, they reach a small settlement whose residents look like **uniformed skeletons**. These strangers insist they are travelers from elsewhere among the stars, stranded with a crashed ship. One offers a mysterious **powering coil** and an extraordinary **green, spectral horse and carriage**. Unlike Vorel's salvage cart, the carriage bends the crowded tree trunks aside as it carries the four adventurers toward Kervalon.

**Evidence:** [E066](../episodes/E066.md). The mind flayer's bargain is **hostile testimony**, not an accepted contract; **Rorf and Follow are dead**, and **Sira is abducted**, not safely resettled.

## XII. The spirit in the sky and the beast in the basement

As the spectral carriage races through the forest, Hope finds herself briefly in **Dusthill**, with her old flesh hand, meeting **Jimster** and seeing the derelict **Fate 4** before the very repairs that would make it hers. A song plays as she touches the wheel. This remembered past vanishes when the present carriage bucks and leaves the forest for a star-filled expanse.

A translucent **dragon spirit** appears before the companions. It smells a great dragon's mark within **Nash**, calling a name like **Kazdejira**. It has even grimmer news. It claims that humans, before the Scorching, constructed a **device to move the Earth itself** away from attacking dragons; now **Oubvayx**, acting for **Dollo Poppio**, has found it beneath Kervalon. The terrible rain and eye-bearing trees are the first incursions of another realm. The enemy will bring more of that world here, and the kidnapped girl **Sira** is to become the vessel for something far worse.

Vorel asks for the dragon's blessing and is told that its strength is spent. The spirit says it has already died and names the **Eye of Groxl itself as “the god killer” in Vorel's possession**. The ASR form `Grojil` is merely a pronunciation approximation of **Groxl**, per owner clarification; the spirit's historical claims remain unverified. Its final warning is both urgent and strange: against such a **Lord of Madness**, the companions should rely on reason rather than accepting every vision as real. They cannot permanently kill every demon lord; they must prevent the passage. **These are the spirit's explanations and instructions**; none of the four has yet opened the ancient machine to see how it works.

Then the carriage descends and deposits them outside the **ruined keep of Kervalon**, where a column of light strikes the interior. Armed lizardfolk stand motionless on the battlements. The heroes imagine dazzling entrances: a transformed T. rex, invisibility, a dragon, or magical teleportation. But they settle upon a quieter plan. **Nash digs a tunnel beneath the outer walls**, pushing the earth aside with magic while Hope checks the darkness ahead. They reach an old basement, and from within comes the tread of something very large.

The group reunites behind the sealed entrance and breaks through together. A towering heap of **flesh and misplaced bones** lashes out at Vorel. Each new wound seems to let more **animated skeletons** spill from its body. Vorel fights with the Eye's burning shield, Hope blasts the skeletons, Nash experiments with a shrieking illusion and icy magic before unleashing fire, and **Stanley's flying sword Diplomacy delivers the final blow against the flesh creature**. More skeletons fall around them, but the keep is not yet theirs.

As they explore the empty stone side rooms, they hear other footsteps. **Hope looks ahead and sees a lizardfolk holding a rifle, waiting in ambush**. Far above, the column of light still shines into the fortress. Neither the captives nor the machine are found. The next room may be more dangerous than the last.

**Evidence:** [E067](../episodes/E067.md). Hope's Dusthill interlude **does not restore Fate 4**; the dragon's ancient-machine account is **attributed testimony**, and the proposed dinosaur breach **is never cast**.

## Interim checkpoint — after E067

**Fate 4 is destroyed**, and Vorel's **Fang Fate Five** is an unusable salvage cart left behind. **Rorf and Follow die E066**, and **Sira is kidnapped**, her whereabouts inside/near Kervalon unverified. **Hope has rejected a new demonic bargain**. A **spectral dragon claims** that an ancient planet-moving machine threatens Earth, but **the party has not confirmed the device or its function**. The four have **entered the ruined keep through Nash's underground tunnel**, defeated one flesh-and-skeleton monster, and found signs of an armed lizardfolk ambush ahead; **the rescue and world-saving mission are still in progress**. Omnom and Razak Val's survivors remain outside the story's present view.

## XIII. A clever trap, a king of teeth and a shell of ink

The four friends are still beneath **Kervalon**, where Hope has spotted a rifleman waiting in a dark room. The voices of their earlier battle have already betrayed their presence, but Nash cannot resist devising a way to turn the guards' pursuit against them. **He builds walls where there were passages and imagines a false door**, so that pursuers chasing Vorel down a hall will be led into a trap. Stanley and Hope help refine the plan; the many minutes spent explaining exactly how to time the illusion, the dirt and the retreat become a kind of contest of their own.

**Vorel steps forward and invokes his authority as the Great Fang**, denouncing lizardfolk who have fallen under outside influence. The answer is a burst of bright **energy-rifle fire** from every direction. Vorel retreats through Nash's prepared route, drawing **a group of melee lizardfolk into the hall**, while riflemen hold farther back. **Nash seals the pursuers behind a wall**—one part of the trap works. The rest of the companions choose their moment.

As Vorel leaps out again, **Stanley finally performs the dinosaur transformation they once only debated**. The lizardfolk becomes a towering **Tyrannosaurus rex**, crushing one guard on the way down, scattering others with his tail and swallowing another in his jaws. When someone notes that **rex means king**, Vorel cheerfully accepts the new, joking title. But a floating **mind flayer** exerts mental control over the great beast. **Hope shoots it twice**, breaking its spell concentration before it can use the dinosaur against his own friends. Then a psychic wave **stuns both Hope and Vorel**.

The lizardfolk riflemen fire again and again. Their beams batter the enormous animal until **the temporary dinosaur body fails** and Vorel staggers back into his ordinary form, badly wounded but alive. Hope and Vorel can do little while stunned, leaving **Nash and Stanley** to fight the growing danger. **Nash's fire and ice** fill the underground chamber; **Stanley's Diplomacy flies into the mind flayer's head**, after which the creature **collapses into a strange empty skin slick with black oil**. No one can tell whether this was the end of the kidnapper's true being. Eventually the remaining exposed guards fall. The companions can still hear those trapped behind Nash's walls, until **their frantic hammering suddenly ceases**. Rather than open the cell, they reinforce the masonry.

They destroy small black worms found creeping around the corpses, rest inside **Stanley's Tiny Hut**, and receive his healing words and **Song of Rest**. Ahead of them, a brilliant light descends through a circular staircase and apparently into the floor. **Nash has never sensed stronger magic**. When Hope casts a rock into the beam, it disappears. Vorel throws in a dead lizardfolk: its body twists into impossible shapes, divides and vanishes as well. **Even Hope's leg temporarily curls into a spiral when she nears the energy**, but becomes normal once she draws away.

The group climbs the surrounding stairs to the storm-lashed spire, finding neither the source nor a route beneath it. Nash cannot draw the earth from below the beam: it fights his magic. Back in the underground chamber **Hope follows a trail of tiny black worms**, spots them emerging from a crack, and directs Nash to remove the surrounding masonry. Beyond it lie **two heavy steel doors, a strange panel of buttons and a glass lens**, guarded only by the crawling things.

Hope reaches out with her one **flesh hand** to press a button. **The panel leaps at her as a mouth full of teeth**. She pulls back just in time; the fingers are still hers. The doors do not open. Somewhere below, the mystery continues.

**Evidence:** [E068](../episodes/E068.md). The [August public-release bonus](../episodes/BONUS-2019-08.md), recorded as **July 27** according to Scott, later clarifies that Beau intended the **light and worms to point to a secret lower chamber**. This is **GM puzzle-design testimony**, not proof the ancient machine is already found.

## Interim checkpoint — after E068

**Vorel, Hope, Nash and Stanley are alive beneath Kervalon after the fight.** The mind flayer's **visible body was destroyed**, but its enduring identity and Sira's fate remain unknown; **the trapped lizardfolk's sudden silence does not establish their deaths**. **Vorel's T. rex transformation actually occurred**, but ended under rifle fire. The magical light can **disintegrate matter and distort nearby bodies**; no one has entered it. The party has discovered **concealed steel doors**, yet **the biting control panel prevented their first attempt at using it**, and no machine has been directly inspected. **Omnom/Razak Val remain unobserved**, as in the earlier cutoffs.

## XIV. Doors do not forget

Kervalon's deep **steel doors** wait beneath the strange shaft, guarded by the hungry control panel that nearly cost Hope her only flesh fingers. But it does more than bite: it **speaks**, forbidding their passage on behalf of the **Door Authority**, for all the doors that the travelers have wronged. Nash finds the one threat it cannot bear—**acid**—and persuades it to open only after giving it a small dose, so it may claim coercion before its superiors.

Beyond the doorway, **grape-scented purple fog and black worms** choke an old transport shaft. A small, broken **funicular** offers a path down. The friends break open its doors and coax the old controls into action. They descend past eerie pink flowers, unaware that the spores will **put Nash and Hope to sleep**. Suddenly the car races downhill out of control. Vorel and Stanley tumble against its walls; **Stanley conjures Feather Fall**, and though the machine wrecks itself at the bottom, the four survive.

They stagger into a vast, forgotten place called the **Center for Human-Demon Interdisciplinary Studies**, or **CHIDS** according to an old sign. There are dead receptionists, ancient machines, and an unusually cheerful projected receptionist, **Julia**, still reciting the virtues of human and demon engineering. Her map depicts **laboratories, containment sectors, executive suites, employee living quarters and trains**. Crossing the security barrier requires cards belonging to people who have been dead for generations.

So the friends search the **living pods**. They find a demon scientist's skeleton still floating because of a wondrous pair of **self-cooling, flying underwear**, which Nash eagerly dons. Vorel and Stanley visit a ruined **McDonald's**, emerging with an old staff card and a bottle labeled **Coca-Cola**. At a derelict **oxygen bar**, Hope finds another card and whiskey. Nash tries the mask she activates, only to **inhale poisonous brown fumes** and stagger away coughing.

At the checkpoint some of the stolen identities work. **Hope's do not**. Ceiling-mounted weapons unfold, **Diplomacy** stabs at the machinery, and **Vorel bends the security door aside** so she can escape. The group boards a subway through the enormous complex. Most stations are buried under rock; others still lead to shadowy corridors. They choose the **executive offices** as a possible route to those controlling the machine. There, in the quiet, Hope feels tentacles reaching for her head.

**Evidence:** [E069](../episodes/E069.md). The facility's preserved presentations reveal its self-description, not a proven account of every ancient event.

## XV. A game in the mind

The supposed monster behind Hope is a **tentacled plush doll**, which squeaks when squeezed. Hope keeps it. Another relic, a **green-lettered computer**, asks whether the companions would like to play a game. It directs them to a conference room with eight thick wired glasses.

While they search, **Vorel sings**, calling himself the **Lizard King** once more. Small floating creatures drift through the walls, gather around him and **nibble pleasantly at his mind**. Nash's songs invite them too. But the little things recoil from **Hope and Stanley**, and the doll's squeak scatters them altogether. Whether they are good omens, strange psychic animals or something less benign is difficult to know.

At the conference table **Nash dons the glasses**. The dead offices brighten, people return to their desks, and his own limbs become blue and tentacled. The image is not merely a movie. He finds himself in a bustling illithid workplace where thoughts replace voices. A casually dressed illithid named **Charles Dickens** approaches, furious that after **fifty years** his talents have been wasted in a minor job while his rival **Ovaxiad** seizes the great prize.

Charles claims a machine called the **Traversal Drive** can transport whole worlds, and that his superior intends to use it to bring their realm into Earth's place. But the reporting systems can be sabotaged. With **Hope** joining Nash inside the glasses, Charles gives them a data stick and asks them to **upload false numbers**. Perhaps, he says, it could delay the dreadful project for months. Charles makes no secret that he cares chiefly about humiliating his rival.

Hope slips through an office as a **cockroach**, then watches from a desk disguised as a **coffee mug**. Nash uses a sleeping worker's empty workstation, tentacles pressed against a scanner, and persuades the old machine to accept the strange file. The moment seems successful—until a real employee sees that Nash does not belong there. **Hope changes into a car and crashes through desks** as security alarms begin to wail.

The worst part is that the alarms **also sound in the abandoned physical office**, where Stanley and Vorel are waiting. The two investigators tear away their headsets just as the glasses **turn to grasping membranes**. Then **flaming hounds leap into the room**, followed by giant **orange ape-like security guards**. Stanley turns one guard into **a tiny snail**. Nash makes Vorel huge enough to **catch two hellhounds and swing them into others**. Bullets, magic, claws and flame fill the room, and the friends realize the fight will not end quickly.

**Evidence:** [E070](../episodes/E070.md). Charles's cosmology and the data upload's eventual effect remain **speaker claims and unmeasured consequences**.

## XVI. Bureaucrats above and below

The battle continues with extraordinary violence. **Vorel smashes the fiery dogs together**, **Stanley sends Diplomacy and booming thunder through others**, and **Hope's radiant ammunition** burns away fiends. The second ape tries to strike Vorel, but his huge fists and a hellhound club finish it. At last, as the final burning hound falls, only the alarms remain. The **first ape is still alive**, helpless in its snail form.

In the dark hall another strange figure emerges: **Alvert**, a tiny leathery imp with a baby's body and an old man's face. He begs not to be hit and announces himself an investigator from the **Underworld Bureau of Investigation**. Alvert has been watching the party, says he knows much of this place, and has come to discover why **the Weir's demons are stealing souls** that Hell believes belong to other afterlives. He is not polite about mortals, or elves, or the workings of damnation. Nor is there any reason to trust him automatically.

But he offers a shocking story about the **Principle**. Long ago, he says, a human scientist in these halls altered himself to survive the dragons' attack, becoming a nearly immortal servant of the **Steel God**. To continue living and resisting demons, Alvert alleges, the Principle uses human gene farms and terrible biological processing. **It is Alvert's account, with parts explicitly framed as rumor**, not a scene the heroes witness. The very thought unsettles the group.

The imp suspects **engineering** contains the **Traversal Drive**, yet cannot open its unpowered door. The party returns to the executive offices once more. Nash and Hope contact the old screen, where a message **claiming to be Charles Dickens** says their data upload worked, then shuts the connection to prevent detection. The machine spits out a smoking piece of hardware; Nash cools and pockets it. Vorel and Stanley retrieve the **still-transformed snail**. They put it aboard an empty train and send the train off toward apparent enemy patrols, hoping a collision will restore a furious security ape in their path. **They never witness the collision or the snail's return to its former shape**.

The travelers walk down the rail tracks, through **bitter cold and growing ice**. At an **Archives** station even its door has read the **Door Street Journal**, telling them of their reputation. Persuasion and a stolen card get the friends into the bright reception hall, but its inner doors have no power. Rather than rest, they continue to the **data center**, where a great steel portal is buried beneath **massive blue-white ice**.

**Stanley heats the hidden metal**, and chunks break loose, but the melting water freezes again almost at once. **Vorel throws the Eye of Groxl** into the barrier, gouging its surface. Hope sees a better path: a **ventilation shaft high above the ice**. Nash burns away some frost; Stanley and Vorel work together to heat the metal until the duct opens.

To lift each person through, the party shares Nash's miraculous **floating underwear**. For a moment Stanley wears it, then Hope, then Vorel, who hoists the wounded Nash as though carrying a bride. Soon **all four crawl along the frozen duct**, shivering as new ice creeps over its walls. **Alvert refuses to follow**, too frightened of the cold. Far below them, the rails are quiet.

**Evidence:** [E071](../episodes/E071.md). The Principle biography, afterlife politics and machine location remain **Alvert's testimony**; the team has **not yet entered the data center proper**.

## Interim checkpoint — after E071

**All four protagonists are alive and inside the frozen ventilation duct near CHIDS's data center**, with **Alvert remaining outside**. They have **not reached or inspected the Traversal Drive**, restored engineering power or rescued Sira. **The demonic hounds and one ape are killed; a second ape was sent away still transformed into a snail**, with its fate unresolved. ## XVII. The lady beneath the fans

Ice clings to every surface of the high **data-center ventilation shaft**. Stanley crawls ahead, choosing among branches and listening for the roar of machinery below. When the passage ends at a wall of ice, he heats the metal so it can melt away. It works—but the whole vent **sags beneath the weight and tears open**. Hope finds herself swinging over darkness, Nash slides after her, and Vorel holds fast to his friend. **Stanley calls down Feather Fall**, and the four float safely into a chamber vast enough to swallow a football field.

The floor hides blinking lights beneath the ice. Towering racks of ancient computers stand beside fans as high as buildings, blowing unbearable cold across the room. Vorel melts a hole down to a strange computer panel, but Nash's efforts at its keyboard only reveal more preserved corpses buried in the ice. Then, in the darkness, somebody speaks to **Toby**, as though he were sitting in the next room.

She is a **tiny, impossibly old elf**, buried in a dozen layers of clothing, with a wandering eye and a voice that never stops. She calls herself **Cerinthala**, or something near it; her companions are **Toby, Carl, Gordon and Benson**, four human figures sealed in blocks of ice around a makeshift camp. She talks to them, argues with them, asks them to make tea, and treats their frozen silence as company. The adventurers are less sure what to think.

Cerinthala says she has lived here since the world before the Scorching. Without access to **Elhandar**, the refuge of elves, she cannot truly rest in death: she must **wither** on an Earth that no longer welcomes her kind. She asks Stanley for a promise to get her home. He refuses to guarantee success, but pledges that **he will do everything he can**. The elf produces a little **wooden-totem necklace**. When Stanley puts it on, strength washes through him; the amulet grants him greater protection.

Now she has a bargain of her own to keep. She says the mind flayer told her to **seal the engineering passage**, but she can unlock it with the ancient controls. She produces a tablet, touches it, and declares the route **open**. She tells of **ships at Gordon Shuttle Bay**, of **crystals that explode when they hear noise**, and of terrifying experiments in which human scientists created **lizardfolk soldiers to fight dragons**. Some of her words sound like long-forgotten history; others sound like the prejudices of an age that treated Vorel as a tool instead of a person. The party listens, without knowing which are true.

After the strange negotiations, Stanley casts **Tiny Hut**, providing somewhere warm enough to rest. Hope checks her **sending stone** and gets a worried, imperfectly heard message from home, then sends word that they are still trying to save the world. During her watch she accepts an invitation to share Cerinthala's **Maltaweed soup**. The elf's stew is much more pleasant than the metal scraps dropped into its cauldron suggested, and she talks with Hope about strange old beliefs, the Eye of Groxl, and the ache of loneliness.

When morning arrives, the group has recovered its strength. **Stanley steps out of the magical hut**, and suddenly great hands of ice seize him. **Four towering creatures**, with frozen men suspended inside their bodies, rise from the floor and hammer at his friends. One starts **carrying Stanley away**, while Vorel meets the others with the **Eye of Groxl's flame**, and Nash makes Vorel huge with magic. Hope rushes to save the prisoner. In the giant's hand, **Stanley's new necklace begins to draw tight around his throat**.

**Evidence:** [E072](../episodes/E072.md). Cerinthala's origins and experiments remain **her account**; the amulet's danger is **directly played**.

## XVIII. Promises in the ice

The amulet is no blessing alone. **Stanley tries to force it off**, but it clings too close to his neck; when he brings a dagger against it, **lightning shocks through his body**. Trapped in the giant's fist, he sends **Diplomacy** into its ice wrist, heating the blade until torrents of meltwater spill from its wounds.

**Nash remembers the Solar Mines**, where workers had to hide from living sources of terrible heat they called **Slow Peters**. Against the frozen monsters before him, he now has a different sort of answer: **Fireball**. He spends the sorcerous power to protect Vorel as the blast engulfs the ice creatures. Vorel batters them apart with the Eye's burning shield. But pieces of the shattered bodies contain more than ice. The preserved human figures inside **start to rise on their own**, blue-eyed and hungry, the party calling them **“ice boyfriends.”**

Hope sends explosive ammunition into the great arm holding Stanley. **It breaks apart**, and he crashes down to the floor—alive and able to stagger toward her. The other icy horrors break, burn or sink out of sight. Their escaped corpses lunge at Vorel and Stanley until the group defeats them too. And then, as though she has merely heard loud neighbors, **Cerinthala appears**, protesting the destruction of her companions. **Stanley's necklace loosens**.

The ice itself speaks. **Toby** forms a head and mouth to say that the frozen men feared Cerinthala would leave them. She scolds him and apologizes to the guests, and under pressure admits something much worse: **she had first considered eating or using the travelers**, until Stanley's promise made them more useful to her alive. The necklace was not a customary elven gift at all. **She crafted it as a curse**, binding Stanley to her and reacting when his thoughts turn against her. He has never promised more than his best efforts, but the amulet does not seem interested in that distinction.

Stanley does not forgive the deceit. Vorel suggests that an elf who keeps returning from death could be killed every morning until she is delivered home. Cerinthala briefly changes into a more beautiful, youthful form and begs Stanley to protect her. He refuses to let her hurry them into space: **the girl Sira and the threat to Earth come first**. At last the old elf points them toward **maintenance corridors beneath the frozen room**, where she hopes to help them escape.

It takes hours. Vorel melts and digs **roughly twenty-five feet of ice**, while his companions drag away frozen chunks before they can reform. At last they reach a service passage lit by old red lamps, where Cerinthala takes the lead. Her comments about lizardfolk as laboratory creatures still offend the party, but the tunnels lead them into **rooms lined with enormous biological cylinders** and patches of **pink, living membrane**.

Nash, unable to resist touching the unknown, **pokes one patch with his staff**. A tendril wraps around it, wrenches it free and **snaps the staff into two pieces**. One shortened half, with its nail, still works as a magical focus. Cerinthala claims these rooms once shaped Vorel's ancestors, and offers to make him a child by combining an egg with accelerated **infernal-wave** technology. **Vorel refuses**. He needs neither a laboratory-born son nor the old woman's permission to remain the Fang.

At a bank of four elevators, one summoned lift plunges away with a crash. The next arrives, but **stalls between floors**, darkness filling its interior. Vorel pries the doors apart and finds only concrete. He tears open a roof hatch instead, and Hope climbs up to inspect the fraying cables. Something wet spatters from above. She looks higher, sees **glinting eyes multiplying in the shaft**, and then the legs of a **giant spider-shaped creature** descending straight toward them.

**Evidence:** [E073](../episodes/E073.md). The lab's **physical vessels and dangerous membrane are witnessed**; the claim that ancient scientists created all lizardfolk and vampires remains **Cerinthala's testimony**, pending further evidence.

## Interim checkpoint — after E073

**All four protagonists and Cerinthala are alive near the roof of a stalled CHIDS elevator**, with a **gigantic spider-like creature descending**. **Stanley's +1 AC totem necklace remains on him, and Cerinthala has admitted it is cursed**, tightening and shocking him when he turns against her. **Nash's staff is broken**, with its nail-bearing portion still usable as a focus. **Vorel refused an offer to engineer a child**. Cerinthala **actually remotely unlocked the engineering access**, but **the group has not physically inspected or activated the Traversal Drive**, nor rescued **Sira**. **Alvert's whereabouts beyond the E071 data-center exterior are not newly observed**. The [September public-release bonus](../episodes/BONUS-2019-09.md) adds important cast explanations and speculations, not new played events.

## XIX. The Big Red One

Something crawls down the elevator shaft toward the companions, eight glittering eyes above a dark, furry body. Worse still, **hundreds of smaller spiders rain from the darkness**, each marked by a strange, exposed piece of **red, brainlike tissue**. The creatures bite and poison; Hope is bitten again and again as she helps haul the ancient elf out of the stalled elevator. Vorel raises the flaming **Eye of Groxl** like an umbrella and burns the falling pests out of the air.

The final elevator cable gives. **Stanley casts Feather Fall** and the group abandons the swaying lift. Above them the huge spider scrabbles down the shaft wall. Stanley changes the beast into **a sheep**; it plummets out of sight, strikes the floor and bursts back into its true arachnid form. The creature survives the fall and tries to dominate Vorel's mind, compelling him to gather prey for its feast. **Nash's Fireball disrupts that hold**, and Vorel returns to the fight. At the bottom of the shaft **Hope fires two devastating shotgun shots**, killing the spider matriarch.

There is enough of the fallen creature left for Vorel to turn its armor into gifts: a **shield for the shrunken Cerinthala** and a tiny matching guard for **Hope's spare mechanical hand**. Their uneasy guide keeps eating handfuls of dead spiders while the companions pry open a door into the lowermost laboratory level.

The passage beyond seems to be breathing. **Pink flesh coats the walls and closes across the corridor** when certain people approach. It allows Hope, Stanley and Vorel through, yet bars Nash and the old elf. Nash remembers that he attacked a similar growth with his staff—and that it broke his staff in return. Perhaps he has made it angry. With astonishing seriousness he sings **songs from the ancient world**—the kind of jingles he learned in the Solar Mines—and the opening loosens enough for him to pass. Cerinthala's attempted tune goes less well. **The passage closes completely around her, and her body falls out lifeless.** The others carry the small corpse on the shield Vorel just made. A black, oily residue comes away on their fingers; Nash decides to taste it, and his tongue soon begins to itch and swell.

A little farther down, **two lizardfolk bearing the same sort of energy rifles as Kervalon's defenders** fire on them. Vorel's flaming shield kills one, and Stanley's flying sword **Diplomacy** finishes the other. They find a control room beyond. Through a window an **illithid at a computer** sees them, hurriedly takes **a disk from the console**, hides it in his robe and attempts to flee.

Hope is ready. Vorel makes a launching step with his hands, sending her high across the room on the power of her artificial leg. **She grabs the illithid from behind and holds him in her mechanical arm**. But the creature has already called something else. In a storm of dark mist **an enormous red mass emerges**, filled with moving faces and thrashing tendrils. It batters Vorel, tearing at more than his body: **something reaches into his memories and steals pieces away**. Nash becomes his **black dragon** self and finds the same force trying to consume his mind. Stanley heals Vorel, only for the captured illithid's **Mind Blast** to strike him down again.

The thing begins to make new fighters from its own flesh. **A Vorel steps out, tethered by red strands to the monster**. Then a huge **dragon-Nash** emerges, equally connected, wearing the face and form of a friend. The living companions cannot know which memories have been lost; only that **their thoughts no longer quite belong to them**.

**Evidence:** [E074](../episodes/E074.md). The spider queen's death, Cerinthala's lifeless body and the red mass's two replicas are played events; the ultimate meaning of the memory-drain penalty remains under review.

## XX. A passage out of the world

The copy of Nash blasts **acid breath** at its original. But the real Nash, a black dragon, is **immune to acid**, and the mimic's strike fails. The vast red thing turns on Stanley, knocking him unconscious and **devouring part of his memories too**. Vorel staggers back into battle, the **Eye of Groxl blazing**, and **destroys the central red horror**. Its two fleshy imitations dissolve at once.

Hope has never released the illithid. Her metal arm tightens as the mind flayer struggles, and Nash's dragon teeth finally bring the fight to an end, **ripping the body away while Hope holds the head**. Stanley lies motionless, but his body fights for life; he **stabilizes after three successful death saves**. The group searches for a way to heal him, eventually letting him rest in the old room until consciousness returns.

Their rummaging yields peculiar clues. Beneath Cerinthala's many coats lie **a transparent blue jewel** and **an old employee ID showing her name and a beautiful red-haired face**. A cryptic **password note** does not open the computer. The illithid's concealed disk is nowhere to be found. But the dead mind flayer has a darker secret: **Hope opens his skull and finds a black worm**. Another emerges from each of the slain lizardfolk guards. **All three worms are crushed**, a warning that infestation has found its way into this buried world.

Through a further security door is a little sealed room inside another room, lined with unfamiliar metal and so quiet the adventurers hear their own bodies. **Hope coordinates the controls** while Vorel, Nash and Stanley turn valves and press buttons. At last, a small cradle opens, presenting **an unusual white sleeve-mounted weapon with two triggers**, one producing a **red** shot and the other **green**. Vorel eagerly collects it. It seems to do no damage when pointed at an enemy.

Just then **Cerinthala wakes**. Her death has not been permanent. For a brief moment she appears confused, searching for Toby and the coats that have been stripped away. Then the false alliance falls apart. She **calls for Toby, paralyzes Hope with magic, and announces that her illithid employer wants the party taken alive**. The **blue jewel** the party recovered becomes the seed or vessel of **another immense icy Toby**, and the old elf runs.

The adventurers destroy the ice form with flame, exposing the animated human inside, then kill **Toby's corpse** as well. Stanley **dispels Cerinthala's wall of ice** and gives chase, but the old woman vanishes near the living, flesh-lined corridor. Only a trail of oily residue and patches of ice hint at how she passed. The team now knows she **can return from the dead**; whether that makes her safe, trustworthy or immortal in every circumstance is quite another matter.

The white weapon seems nearly useless until Vorel fires **red and green charges into two walls**. Two apertures open, each leading straight into the other. **Nash recognizes a paired-portal trick from an ancient entertainment device**, and the group realizes it may have found an escape route far better than another collapsed elevator. **Vorel hands the single portal device to Hope**.

The bugbears arrive first. Metal crashes in the halls, flames sweep the fleshy corridor, and **armed goblinoid troops pour toward the laboratory**. Nash sends a **Fireball** down the passage, wiping out their front ranks. As the others take position in the back room, **an air-blowing vent suggests another possible passage**, but no one explores it. Vorel steps forward to lure the survivors toward the others, who have planned a route through the portals. Ahead are **dozens of bugbears—and an illithid in their ranks**. Vorel bellows a challenge in Goblin. The enemy charges.

**Evidence:** [E075](../episodes/E075.md). A linked red/green portal pair is actually demonstrated, but **the proposed escape through it has not yet happened**. Cerinthala's resurrection and betrayal are observed; her ultimate fate remains unknown.

## XXI. Through the portal and into containment

The bugbears answer Vorel's taunt with a charge. This time the **portal escape works**. A paired aperture carries the party behind much of the pursuit, isolating a **second mind flayer**. Vorel grapples the enemy, Stanley's blade strikes and Nash burns it, but **Hope's shotgun ends the fight**. There is little time to celebrate. The illithid has already ordered the troops to return, and the adventurers flee rather than battle the entire force. The optional back-room ventilation route they noticed at the end of E075 stays unexplored.

They hurry through CHIDS, seeking a way beyond the living, fleshy obstruction. It continues to **refuse Nash**, even after admitting his companions. In the end **Dimension Door**, not another song, brings him through safely. A damaged catwalk over a cavernous containment shaft blocks their progress. Hope passes **the one portal device to Nash**, who floats across in his impossible underwear, then fights a great blue-black winged predator that dives from the darkness. Hope kills the creature with her shotgun; more cries echo below. The others finally cross by reconfigured portals, leaving that dangerous pit behind.

Deeper within the installation, translucent blue figures work at phantom laboratory routines. They talk as if continuing experiments from a lost age, and their typing leaves the real keyboards unmoved, yet they can react to the living party's voices and even a touch. The surviving terminals display **lizardfolk shapes and equipment modifications**. These are clues to an old program, but not yet a complete history of the species.

The next room is an armory. **Hope discovers two rockets that fit her existing launcher**, plus chilled red and blue medicinal-looking supplies. She takes **five bottles each of Monster Mender and Blue Bull**. Nash ventures a taste of blue liquid and briefly feels unusually awake; nobody mistakes that moment for a full medical trial. Vorel, meanwhile, finds clothing designed for bodies like his: a silvery suit, shoulder grappling harpoons, mighty jump boots and a protective helmet. The outfit **speaks instructions directly into his mind**, equips him to confront dragons and even shields him from mind-control effects. It also interferes with the unarmored defense he once enjoyed. Beneath the fabric, **the Eye of Groxl seethes at something still deeper in containment**.

They press onward between **pulsing flesh walls**. Nash sings again to stop the passage from closing around him. This time the corridor holds—at least until the episode ends.

**Evidence:** [E076](../episodes/E076.md). Portal evacuation is **actually performed** here, unlike E075's proposal. Blue apparitions, equipment readouts and the Eye's feelings are observed; the identity of the living flesh and the exact nature of the experiments are still unknown to the heroes.

## XXII. The man in the cylinder and the Nash in the wall

The constricting passage releases the heroes into another sterile region of **CHIDS Containment**. A secured checkpoint yields to Hope's clever use of the portal device; another pair of apertures clears a pit full of fans and strange beams. Beyond it lies a great chamber crowded with vertical vats of **green liquid and dead lizardfolk bodies**. Their screens are not merely medical monitors. They advertise education and training, but also **surgical control of behavior, fear and memory**, a disturbing glimpse of the institution's treatment of living subjects.

Two enormous vessels stand apart from the rest. One lies shattered and marked with the characters **G-R-0-X-1**. The other holds a motionless winged figure, with an identifying name that sounds like **Lethanphius**. The Eye in Vorel's chest bristles with loathing toward this stranger and humiliation at the broken tank. As the party debates what to do, the flesh wall itself takes action, making instruments from tendrils and opening the vessel.

The winged man wakes tormented and confused. He mourns a **father whose voice he cannot hear**, declares himself defiled and summons a dark weapon. The fight is brutal. Vorel fires his new shoulder harpoons and tries to restrain the opponent, only to have **two digits torn away** and later a whole **hand severed**. Nash enlarges Vorel; Stanley struggles to sustain the others; Hope finally discovers a dial on the portal apparatus. **It does more than open gateways**: in *neutralization mode*, pulling both triggers sends out a long **necrotic beam**. Her first extraordinary shot pierces the winged adversary, but also **strikes Vorel**, felling him. Though the beam shears the attacker almost in half, its **black-metal skeleton and upper torso continue to fight**. Stanley restores Vorel to consciousness, Vorel drinks a Monster Mender, and finally **Nash's Fire Bolt destroys the still-living upper half**. Nash freezes and preserves his friend's severed parts, hoping there is a way to restore them.

Now the walls answer. A **humanoid rises from layers of living tissue**, speaking for a vast **council of stored minds**. It claims the flesh was once a repository for the wisdom of CHIDS staff and has become a cooperative guardian of the building. The council says it kept many dangers contained, shielded itself from illithid minds and had allowed the party to reach this room in hopes of recruiting it. The apparent crystalline metal angel, it says, was an **abducted heavenly subject**; the shattered **GR0X1** tank contained an escaped **anti-dragon lizardfolk prototype** with traces of the same power. This may be a crucial missing piece in the Eye of Groxl's history, but it comes from a **self-interested witness**.

Then another figure rises from the wall, wearing an image of **Nash Maggard**. It claims to be a Nash from another possible future, trapped for eight centuries, and recounts a terrible history in which the adventurers lose the world to a **Soulbroker bargain around the Traversal Drive**. It says the party will be tempted with a way to free themselves and save Sira only by sacrificing others, and that a single person's self-sacrifice could instead stop the machine. The living Nash is not convinced. Even this alleged elder recalls things that do not match the injuries happening in front of him. **Their exchange is a warning or allegation, not a future already written.**

The council claims an **illithid force has reached the engineering center** where the Traversal Drive is kept. It offers an opportunity to rest, access to locked supplies and a plan to deliver the party near the invaders. The GM closes the session by **awarding level nine and proposing a full rest and hand reattachment with Monster Mender**; the actual procedure and overnight rest are **played in E078**, rather than fully carried out in this episode. The proposed strike remains ahead; Sira is still missing.

**Evidence:** [E077](../episodes/E077.md). The living wall and second Nash-shaped figure **manifest in the scene**. The speaker's account of time travel, future betrayals and planetary destruction is **unverified testimony**; it does not supersede the played timeline.

## Where the story stands after E077

**All four protagonists survived the containment battle** and the GM granted **level-nine advancement and a full-rest transition** for the next session. Vorel wears an anti-dragon suit; **the GM announces a plan for repairing his severed digits and hand**, and that repair is **explicitly carried out during E078**. **Hope controls the one portal device**, whose portal and **necrotic-beam** functions have both been used; friendly fire was dangerous. The **hive council proposes an assault landing near engineering**, but it has not yet delivered the heroes there. **Sira remains captive, the party has not activated the Traversal Drive**, and the alleged future Wall Nash's dire account remains contested. The [October Q&A](../episodes/BONUS-2019-10.md) offers out-of-character context, not another played chapter.

## XXIII. The council's gifts and the gnomes in the vat

The party does not immediately disappear into an overnight rest. Vorel first challenges the **flesh council**, demanding it stand and fight. The collective says it cannot create independent soldiers or detach any of its organism without dissolving the cohesion of its many minds. It offers something else: **weapons-cache codes**, **two Rings of Spell Storing for Stanley and Nash**, and transportation that may carry the four heroes past the illithids' outer defenders. Most startlingly, its account of the lizardfolk program distinguishes **the species that already existed in another world** from **CHIDS's locally bred soldiers**. It is a crucial claim from someone claiming to have operated the laboratories, though still a witness rather than an infallible archive.

Before the great push, Stanley speaks quietly to Vorel. If the other Nash's story is true and stopping the Drive demands someone take Sira's place, **Stanley would volunteer himself** rather than sacrifice the girl or the world. He insists Vorel get the others out. This promise is private, conditional, and deeply unwelcome to the warrior. No sacrifice has happened yet.

They return to the preparation bay and carry out the rough medicine promised at the end of the last episode. Nash extracts Vorel's frozen hand and digits from his magically cooled clothing, the others align them with the injured flesh, and **a Monster Mender potion knits them back in place**. Vorel wiggles functioning fingers again. The party turns to the locked boxes. Hope pulls out a **crimson 'Vorpal Laser Sword'**, a metal hilt that creates an energy blade. Stanley and Nash claim blades too. Vorel finds wrist-mounted versions that bolt directly onto his anti-dragon suit. These are **strength-based weapons**, inconvenient for Hope's dexterous attacks but ideal for the large lizardfolk. The group fills **Stanley's Bag of Holding** with **seventeen spare swords, two additional wrist blades and approximately eleven armor sets**, dreaming of arming a future royal guard.

At last they shelter in **Tiny Hut and complete a real eight-hour rest**. Stanley stores two magical heals in his new ring and Nash charges his with three Chromatic Orbs; Hope exchanges a reassuring message with home. Nash wakes up aching and inexplicably altered, as though parts of his magical knowledge have slipped away. The source does not resolve exactly what happened to him. The council, now prepared, warns the heroes **not to blow up the Drive**: uncontrolled power could destroy the planet. It says the safer aim is to **disable the illithid operation**, rather than smash the apparatus. Stanley's intuition is that the council is sincerely telling them what it believes, though sincerity need not mean technical certainty.

The walls open four soft beds. The heroes lie down, and **living conduits carry each of them** toward engineering, cushioning every turn. They emerge unexpectedly beside **an illithid at a terminal**. Hope rushes forward with her unfamiliar red laser sword, Vorel ignites both wrist weapons, and Nash takes a cherished diamond from his wedding ring to fuel a new spell. The party dispatches the alien and nearby guards. Then shouting draws them to another room, and an impossible pair of familiar faces.

**Chance and Eddie Manchester III**, the gnomes last seen vanishing into a sand pit long ago, hang bound and gagged over a tank of small alien creatures. Two additional illithids, a robed human mage, and a security ape threaten them. The rescue is a brutal melee of mind blasts and spell failures. Vorel becomes **stunned and caught within an illithid's tentacled mouth**, seconds from losing his brain. Nash empowers comrades with **Twinned Haste**, Stanley transforms into a monstrous **giant ape**, and **Eddie** fights free and lands the critical knife strike that **kills the illithid holding Vorel**. Nash dispatches the surviving illithid with his new **Chromatic Orb**, and Stanley's ape tears apart the security creature. The gnomes survive and the allies gather in exhausted disbelief.

**Evidence:** [E078](../episodes/E078.md). The ancestors-of-lizardfolk explanation and apocalyptic Drive hazard are **the council's claims**, while the hand repair, long rest, material acquisitions, flesh-tunnel transport and gnome rescue are **played outcomes**.

## XXIV. Two rockets into the heart of engineering

Chance and Eddie tell a strange tale. After the collapse that separated them from Nash and Stanley, they wandered tunnels and old tracks, were taken by bugbears, and found themselves living as well-fed prisoners in an illithid recreation area. Their captors eventually bound them above that awful vat. With their old companions restored, the adventurers try to regroup. There is **no second overnight rest**, only healing supplies and a later short pause. A newly cautious Stanley and a still-resourceful Vorel do not want to announce their arrival to every guard.

They hatch a grotesque disguise. Vorel empties a **dead mind flayer's head into a mask** for Nash, who dresses in dark robes and **floats using his magical underwear**. Stanley imitates the red-robed dead human, the gnomes pose as captives, and the others adapt their postures. Nash projects a voice to make a claim about their captives being summoned by an illithid superior. **Two security apes accept the ruse**, and the party walks through a guarded junction without firing a shot.

Deeper passages show that **CHIDS has been overtaken by new purple-blue crystal**. They reach a strange door whose markings suggest a **four-fingered hand**. Chance cannot open it, and Nash's grim excursion to fetch a dead illithid's severed hand proves equally unsuccessful. The barrier may require life or a magical signature, but no one fully solves it. Instead **Stanley takes Hope with him through Dimension Door** to its far side, where she completes a **paired portal** already begun on the near wall, admitting the rest.

The enormous space beyond is beyond anything the group has seen in the labs: a place where old machinery sprouts from crystal like fossils, a vertical **beam of brilliant energy** intersects a rainbow-lit pool, and **a dying-looking dragon blends into the tail of a vast worm**. **Three chanting illithids**, **Cerinthala** and a much larger alien leader occupy the site, with **lizardfolk on high metalwork**. They have finally come to **the physical engineering chamber of the Traversal Drive**, though neither the machine's exact function nor the girl's predicament is fully exposed. The heroes wait behind cover, trying to preserve equipment they have been warned not to destroy.

Hope has **two rockets** from the preparation bay. Their plan is simple and carefully aimed: rather than bombard the mysterious lower apparatus, she fires at the enemy-filled scaffolds high above. A round of **Blue Bull** first grants unnatural speed and focus; several companions pay for it by vomiting violently, but press ahead. Hope fires her **first rocket**, and bodies, metal and debris rip across one side of the room. The explosion knocks an illithid into the central beam, and its body comes apart in ribbons. She reloads and **fires the second**, sweeping the other side. **Cerinthala and several more illithids are thrown into the light and visibly disintegrate**. Given the elf's earlier return from death, no one can safely declare her **permanently extinguished**.

The great illithid leader, however, is **not gone**. He takes damage, recoils and **floats upward**. The warped dragon-worm and enormous beam remain. In an instant, the party has upset the battlefield and cleared many enemies, but the struggle for the planet and **Sira's life** is just beginning.

**Evidence:** [E079](../episodes/E079.md). The precise whereabouts of Sira in the machine room need audio/visual verification. The scene ends **after the two rocket blasts**, not after boss defeat, Drive shutdown or rescue.

## Where the story stands after E079

**Hope has spent both rockets.** Several lizardfolk guards, **three illithids and Cerinthala's currently observed body** have been destroyed in the first strike. **The much larger illithid leader survives and ascends; the dragon-worm and beam remain, with Sira not rescued.** The group now includes **Chance and Eddie**, both alive. Vorel's severed limbs were **actually repaired in E078**, and an **eight-hour rest in E078 plus a separate short rest in E079** are distinct events. The **portal gun has already bypassed the crystal lock**, but **the Drive is neither deactivated nor destroyed**. ## XXV. The illithid commander falls, and the room becomes stars

The explosive opening has left CHIDS engineering choked with smoke and wreckage, but the largest mind flayer still hangs near the central light. The GM's return to the scene places **Sira inside the illuminated region**, with the mangled body of a dragon merging into a monstrous many-mouthed worm. The girl is present—but the heroes have not freed her.

The party has scarcely begun to press the advantage when the commander turns upon them. **Stanley** scouts closer and casts out an improbable arsenal of spare laser swords, meaning to animate them. The weapons themselves resist that spell because they are magical, so **ten metal pipes from the damaged scaffolding** become his flying army instead. **Vorel** tries his grappling harpoons; **Hope** shoots and draws fire. **Nash becomes a young black dragon** and breathes through two seeming commanders, only to discover they were Mirror Image doubles. The real one fires a **Mind Blast** across the room, stunning **Vorel, Nash, Chance and Eddie**. Stanley's *Feather Fall* catches two of the falling allies.

The creature in the central pool begins disgorging **green-glowing, explosive baby worms**. At the illithid's direction, the **large red memory-eaters** from the lower laboratories phase into the room too. Hope shoots the small worms and learns to detonate them in lethal chains, while Stanley's animated pipes batter the commander. Then **black necrotic lightning** strikes. Both guest gnomes fall unconscious. A red creature attacks **Eddie** on the floor and steals more memory. Another small worm folds itself over **Chance** as if wrapping him in a cocoon. The party's need to rescue them is suddenly as urgent as its need to reach the Drive.

Somehow, Eddie returns to consciousness on **a natural twenty death save**. Stanley's moving pipes continue their relentless assault, and his **stored Healing Word** later brings Chance back from the brink. Meanwhile Nash, still a dragon, melts surviving lizardfolk with acid, catches a last guard in his claws, carries him aloft and drops him to his death. High above the ruins, Nash **kills the great illithid commander with a final acid breath**. The alien's body crumbles away. It is a victory, but not the one for which they came.

The **dragon-worm calls itself Dolo Popio** in its changing speech and declares it will finish the fight itself. It mentions an old **contract with a name the transcript renders uncertainly** and changes the world around the combatants. The lab vanishes into a **magical, black, star-strewn expanse**, with platforms of rock and crystal hanging in space. The same writhing monster, luminous pool and **Sira** remain at the center. Whether the machine moved the room, opened another realm, or imposed some stranger reality remains unanswered.

On the new battlefield, the dragon-worm's presence fills the party with terror. Hope trembles, later Nash is also frightened, but **Vorel's CHIDS helm protects him** from that condition. Freed from the earlier stun, he fires his **power-jump boots** and leaps across the void at the monster. He draws a power from the **Eye of Groxl** only hinted at in the previous bonus: a **flame shield that he reshapes into a huge flaming sword**. The warrior slams it repeatedly into the dragon-worm and holds on as the creature tries to throw him free. Stanley uses *Dissonant Whispers* and his animated/loyal **Diplomacy**, while **Nash's acid scorches the dragon's ruined scales** and he tears apart one of the red monsters.

Then Dolo Popio spreads its wings, gathers the glowing motes of the starry void, and **unleashes an enormous breath attack**. The episode ends **before the saving throws, damage, or aftermath of that attack are played**. The leader illithid has died and both gnomes have survived terrible injuries, but **the central enemy remains alive, Sira is still captive, and the Traversal Drive has not been shut down**.

**Evidence:** [E080](../episodes/E080.md). The commander kill, Chance's rescue and Vorel's flame-blade are performed; the contract's name, literal nature of the starry battlefield and incoming breath's consequences **remain open**.

## Where the story stands after E080

**Alive at episode cutoff:** Hope, Stanley, Vorel, dragon-form Nash, Chance and Eddie. **Commander Ovaxiad/Uvexiad is killed by Nash**; **Dolo Popio remains active**. **Stanley animates ordinary metal pipes, not the spare magical laser swords**, and uses one stored *Healing Word* to restore Chance. **Vorel demonstrates the Eye of Groxl's flame-shield-as-blade** and attaches himself to the dragon-worm in the space-like arena. The alien's **incoming breath is unresolved**, as is the Drive and Sira's fate. The previous E079 rocket use remains the only two-rocket salvo.
