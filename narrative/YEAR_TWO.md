# Year Two — Stones, Souls and the Great Fang

**The Wastes, second major researched story arc — corrected E055–E081**

**Full spoilers.** This is an editorial story synthesized from **processed-Parakeet episode dossiers, not independently audio-verified**. Dialogue is paraphrased. **Events the party witnessed** are distinct from private visions, uncertain ASR and explanations given by self-interested speakers. [Year One](YEAR_ONE.md) covers the first arc; the [original longer Year Two draft](../research/legacy/2026-10-22/YEAR_TWO_PRE_ARC_CONSOLIDATION.md) preserves the former episode-by-episode prose.

## Prologue — A crown does not make the world safe

Three months after **Hope Akmenos** becomes queen of **Tietkalla**, four people who once fought their way out of Dusthill are learning to live with their own victories. Hope abolishes inherited forms of servitude while nobility and material shortages frustrate reform. **Vorel** and **Bok Bok** run the **Golden Sands** tavern and adventurers' guild. **Stanley** works with the Tidebearers to establish a hospital and seeks relief from **Diontalus**, the archfey sharing his body. **Nash**, haunted by the Solar Mines and P0D's death, entertains children using the tiny extra hand he brought back from the froghemoth. **Angelashi** tells Nash she is pregnant.

But **Hope, Nash and Stanley have signed a Soulbroker pact to destroy the Principle**; **Vorel refused to sign**. The Principle's empire still holds people the adventurers left behind. Their kingdoms, promises and surviving families will collide with a threat much larger than any one throne.

## I. Stonefall and the metallic visitor

The first news of peace comes from **Softhead**, once thought lost beneath Dusthill and now speaking for the **Principle**. He promises the release of Dusthill captives and says an underling was responsible for their enslavement. Hope does not believe that absolves him. **She has his tongue cut out**, leaving him alive to take her reply. Elsewhere an assassin attacks her in her bedroom and dies. Hope's kingdom offers neither comfort nor simple justice.

Then **stones rain from the sky**, killing civilians and crushing Tietkalla's buildings. **Quizquaz** appears, claiming a dragon and an old bargain lie behind the catastrophe; Hope refuses another deal. **P0D briefly appears to Nash as a spirit**, not as a resurrected companion. **Vorel is unnaturally aged**, but the priestess **Emenil** restores him. When the tiefling elder **Nobiery** exposes the Soulbroker arrangement that underlies his people's religious authority, an animated corpse attacks Emenil amid the dispute; **Stanley heals her**, and she survives.

The heroes investigate a strange **green crater** surrounded by stones and discover tracks where something large was hauled away. This does not prove Quizquaz's explanation of a fallen dragon. **Diontalus takes Stanley's body and kills a leprechaun** the party has unearthed. A fight with **Wormfather bugbears** yields a more unsettling piece of evidence: **a living black parasite attached to a raider's brain**. Nash briefly flies off in dragon form and is retrieved after Hope organizes a search.

At last the **Principle** appears at court, a huge metallic figure wearing red rings. He offers resources, rail trade, a shrine and an embassy in return for cooperation, and the party insists that new labor must be **paid and voluntary**. The Principle does something even more consequential: **he extracts Diontalus from Stanley into a crystal**. The archfey's captivity is witnessed, though the Principle's future intentions are not. And **Dusthill captives really return**—including **Talia, Bruce Bane, Jimster and Shun'ti**—carrying memories of forced farm labor. Not every missing person is accounted for.

A new summons soon pulls the company toward **Razak Val**, where **Diptak** wants Vorel to reclaim a lizardfolk birthright.

[E055](../episodes/E055.md)–[E059](../episodes/E059.md). The stonefall, returned prisoners and Diontalus's removal are played; the demons' and Principle's histories remain attributed accounts.

## II. The Great Fang's ruined coronation

The reigning **Great Fang** of Razak Val is **Orp**, a cyclops the party once knew. Now the city feeds him **living human captives**. **Orp kills Diptak** and agrees only reluctantly to face Vorel in a formal succession duel. Behind the title stand lizardfolk clans, the **Fengars**, priestess **Typalla**, an Elder of Families and officers who benefit from the system. At a feast **Nash meets strangers bearing his own face**, who claim to be versions of him from different futures and enlist him to steal a preserved **Fengar egg**. Nash distracts **Bramie the Tyrant Slayer** while they take it; **Bramie is alive when Nash leaves**, but is later found stabbed. The alleged time travelers' origin is not established.

Bramie's sister **Zercil** offers Vorel an alliance against Orp if he will accept her authority as husband and subordinate. He refuses, denounces the city's reliance on human food and challenges Orp **before the scheduled duel**. **Hope's rocket is redirected through the Eye of Groxl's unusual influence**, striking near Vorel and devastating the crowded hall. **Mirion Fengar, Typalla, the Elder and others die**, including people not fighting the party. The bloodshed is part of the victory, not something a celebratory retelling can omit. Vorel fights Orp outside, kills him and is **recognized by Omnom as Great Fang**.

Zercil will not yield. Vorel tries to stop her without killing her, but **Hope shoots her with an Obsidian Nail**. Omnom is furious at the outsiders' intervention and the civilian dead, even while helping the new ruler. Under the throne **hundreds of human prisoners** await screening and freedom. **Stanley begins medical work**, yet not all are released. The heroes capture **a living black worm** from Zercil's bedding. A legless prisoner named **Follow** appears unusually clear-headed, and a little girl seen by Stanley vanishes mysteriously.

What Vorel inherits proves impossible to fix in a day. **Sun's Witnesses** in the pit transform into **chicken lords**, and lizardfolk above sprout extra arms and attack each other. **Follow speaks telepathically**, sensing something wrong in the crowd, but cannot avert the transformation. Nash contains part of the disaster with fire; **Omnom remains behind to fight** as the company flees through a canyon. The fate of Razak Val's surviving people is not shown.

[E060](../episodes/E060.md)–[E064](../episodes/E064.md). **Orp and Zercil are dead, Vorel publicly holds the title Great Fang**, and the feast's fatal collateral harm is established. The supposed future Nashes' legitimacy and exact outcome of the outbreak are unresolved.

## III. The family who never reaches sanctuary

The fleeing adventurers hear a familiar engine: **Fate 4**, somehow being driven by a massive orc named **Rorf**. With him are **Follow** and his young daughter **Sira**, who can disappear and whose damaged elf-like ears bear marks of past persecution. Follow describes the loss of their refuge **Home**, lizardfolk hunters and his own capture. Rorf admits that he **murdered Bramie Fengar** to help provoke succession fights and rescue Follow. That confession resolves the murder question at the group's current level of testimony, but is **not the same as Nash's future-faced visitors stealing the egg**. Hope invites this unlikely family to seek refuge in Tietkalla. They accept; **they do not reach it**.

Rain, an astonishing event in the Wastes, sweeps the purple desert. Nash uses earth magic to free the stranded RV and divert floods around **Stanley's Tiny Hut**. Sira responds to Stanley's Elvish songs. The alleged future Nashes return with incompatible accounts of the stolen egg and a supposed future disaster. Then an actual **Tyrannosaurus rex** emerges from the storm. Vorel tries to lure it into Nash's pit, but it **tramples and destroys Fate 4**. Nash and Vorel kill it; Vorel's pitiful wheeled **Fang Fate Five** cannot replace Hope's traveling home.

Now **an illithid appears**, naming a power resembling **Dolo Popio** and urging an infernal bargain that might spare the heroes while abandoning Tietkalla. Hope refuses. The creature seizes **Sira**. **Rorf transforms into a great bird** and is crushed while trying to save her; **Follow dies protecting her**, extracting Stanley's promise to retrieve his daughter. Black, eye-bearing trees grow around the party. The illithid directs them toward **Kervalon**, and the girl disappears with it.

The travelers then encounter skeletal-looking people who claim to have come from the stars. Their **green spectral carriage** carries the friends through the impossible forest and out into a star-filled expanse. A **spectral dragon** tells of ancient humans allegedly building a machine to **move Earth away from dragons**, now commandeered by the illithids to bring another realm and **Dolo Popio** into the world through Sira. It calls the **Eye of Groxl** a god-killer. These are the spirit's claims, not a witnessed technical demonstration.

[E065](../episodes/E065.md)–[E067](../episodes/E067.md). **Fate 4, Rorf and Follow are lost**; **Sira is abducted alive**, and Stanley's promise becomes the immediate cause of the next expedition.

## IV. Beneath Kervalon, behind the doors

The carriage brings the company to ruined **Kervalon**, beneath a vertical beam of powerful light. **Nash tunnels under the defended keep**, avoiding the grander suggested entrances. In the basement **Stanley's animated sword Diplomacy** destroys a flesh-and-bone monster spawning skeletons. Lizardfolk with **energy rifles** protect the way down. Nash builds a trap of sealed earth walls, confining several pursuers; **the party never checks whether they die inside**. **Stanley actually Polymorphs Vorel into a T. rex**, whose temporary form is damaged under rifle fire. Another mind flayer's visible body collapses into a strange **oily skin**, not convincing evidence of a permanent end.

The group encounters the great beam: objects, even a corpse, **twist and vanish inside it**, and Hope's leg briefly distorts nearby. They search for a concealed lower passage guarded by an animated, **biting door terminal**. Its **Door Authority** holds the heroes responsible for mistreating doors elsewhere; **Nash negotiates with acid**. An old funicular takes them past purple mist and dangerous spores before crashing. Stanley's **Feather Fall** keeps them alive.

They reach **CHIDS**, the **Center for Human-Demon Interdisciplinary Studies**, a buried city of laboratories, dead offices, rail stations and automated defenses. The projected receptionist **Julia** cheerfully praises an age of cooperation between humans and demons. That is **how the institution advertises itself**, not proof every explanation is true. The companions scavenge access cards, investigate the remains of a **McDonald's**, and find fantastical **cooling, flying underwear** that Nash adopts.

In the executive offices a computer offers a game. Using wired glasses, **Nash and Hope experience a living illithid workplace** and meet an apparent employee named **Charles Dickens**, who detests a rival called **Ovaxiad**. Charles alleges a **Traversal Drive** could transport worlds and begs the visitors to upload forged numbers to delay the rival's plan. They attempt the deception through shifting VR identities—Hope even changes into a car—but alarms sound both within the simulation and in the physical office. **Fiery hounds and ape-like security** attack. The companions win, although an ape Stanley turned into **a snail** is sent away on a train without a witnessed later outcome. A message claims Charles's sabotage worked; **its practical effect is not measured**.

An imp called **Alvert** claims he investigates the Weir's theft of souls from Hell and speculates that **the Principle was once a CHIDS scientist** who transformed himself, maintaining his unnatural existence through brutal human processing. These are accusations, partly explicitly rumor. Pursuing the Drive, the company heads through icy rails toward a data center. Its main door is sealed in fast-growing frost, so they use a **ventilation duct** instead, sharing Nash's levitating underwear to reach it. Alvert will not follow them into the cold.

[E067](../episodes/E067.md)–[E071](../episodes/E071.md). The existence of CHIDS and lethal security are directly observed; **Charles's virtual sabotage, the spectral dragon's machine history and Alvert's Principle biography** still require independent corroboration.

## V. Cerinthala and the living underworld

The frozen duct fails, and **Stanley's Feather Fall** sends the party safely into a computer hall of enormous fans. There they meet **Cerinthala**, an ancient, talkative elf, living beside human figures frozen within ice. She calls them friends, especially **Toby**, and begs Stanley to help her return to **Elhandar**, the elven refuge. Stanley promises to try, refusing to put her ahead of saving Sira. Cerinthala gives him a protective-looking **wooden amulet** that soon **tightens and shocks him** when he resists her will. Her icebound companions attack as hulking creatures; once their shells break, the animated corpses fight on. Cerinthala admits she made the amulet as **a curse** and had considered using the adventurers for her own survival.

Even an unreliable guide has knowledge. Cerinthala operates old CHIDS controls, claims scientists **engineered lizardfolk for war against dragons**, and offers to build Vorel a son from altered eggs; he refuses. In the lower laboratories **living pink tissue** grabs and breaks **Nash's staff**. A stalled elevator is besieged by spiders with exposed brain tissue. Stanley turns the great matriarch briefly into a sheep, and **Hope eventually kills it**. Vorel fashions small shields from its shell.

The pink walls admit Hope, Stanley and Vorel but obstruct Nash and Cerinthala. **Nash sings his way through**; the membrane crushes **Cerinthala** into apparent death. Farther on, an illithid retreats with **a disk taken from a console** and calls forth a giant **red memory-eater**. It strips memories from **Vorel, Nash and Stanley**, creating tethered doubles. **Vorel destroys it**, the copies dissolve, and **Hope and Nash kill the illithid**. The disk remains missing. The party destroys **three black headworms** found in surrounding corpses.

In a secured, sound-dampened chamber the group obtains **one strange white device with red and green triggers**. Firing onto suitable walls creates **paired portals**. Cerinthala then **revives**, calls an **icy Toby** through a recovered blue jewel, paralyzes Hope and betrays the group to her illithid employer. They destroy the ice creature and its undead human core, but **the elf escapes**. As armed bugbears rush the lower lab, Nash kills the front ranks with **Fireball** and Vorel calls out a challenge. At the E075 cutoff, **escape through the portals is still only planned**.

[E072](../episodes/E072.md)–[E075](../episodes/E075.md). Cerinthala's death and return are played; her claims about **all** lizardfolk are not equivalent to the subsequently observed CHIDS research. Stanley's amulet and the memory-drain effects have not been demonstrated permanently cured.

## VI. The Eye, the hive, and an impossible future

In E076 **the portal escape actually works**: the group gets past most of the charging bugbears, isolates another illithid and **Hope kills it**. They do not wipe out the entire host. They pass hostile living flesh with **Nash's Dimension Door**, cross a broken shaft using portals and his flying underwear, and defeat a winged predator in the dark. A laboratory holds translucent **blue scientist echoes**, not clearly alive and not actually moving their physical keyboards.

An armory yields **two rockets for Hope**, **Monster Mender** and **Blue Bull**, and a lizardfolk **anti-dragon outfit** with a mentally instructing nanoweave suit, harpoons, powerful boots and telepathy-dampening helmet. It protects Vorel but interferes with his **unarmored defense**. The **Eye of Groxl** grows agitated as the adventurers approach vats of **dead lizardfolk** and computer programs openly describing **behavioral and neurological reconditioning**. Those are direct clues to a ruthless weapons program, not proof CHIDS created every lizardfolk anywhere.

Two tanks stand apart: a broken one labeled **G-R-0-X-1** and one containing a black-winged, metal-boned **Lethanphius**. The Eye feels anger toward Lethanphius and apparent shame at the other vessel. The living walls open the intact tank. In the struggle Lethanphius **tears off Vorel's digits and hand**. Hope discovers the portal device has a **long necrotic beam mode** and inadvertently **strikes Vorel while destroying much of Lethanphius**. Stanley revives Vorel; **Nash's Fire Bolt kills the surviving torso**, then Nash freezes his friend's lost parts.

A humanoid grows from the wall, speaking for a **hive council** that claims to preserve the combined minds of old CHIDS staff and to defend the site from illithids. It calls **GR0X1** a divinity-derived anti-dragon prototype and says CHIDS bred its own soldiers from **lizardfolk that already existed elsewhere**. This might bear on the **one Eye of Groxl**, but the account is a witness's explanation, not settled artifact history. Another wall-figure claims to be a **Nash from a future in which the friends betray one another and the world is destroyed** over the Drive. Present Nash doubts him. The apparent future is **testimony, not an event already played**.

At E077's end the GM grants **level nine** and foreshadows rest and limb repair. **E078 actually performs the reattachment with Monster Mender and completes the eight-hour rest**. Stanley privately offers Vorel his life if Sira must be replaced in the machine. The hive provides **two Rings of Spell Storing**, codes for an arsenal holding crimson laser swords, wrist blades and spare armor kits, and a warning that **destroying the Drive outright might destroy Earth**. Stanley finds the warning sincerely expressed, not necessarily infallible. Then the living walls **carry the four through organic conduits toward engineering**.

[E076](../episodes/E076.md)–[E078](../episodes/E078.md). The precise inventory and homebrew mechanics remain in the dossiers; the council and Wall Nash are **attributed witnesses**, not authoritative future history.

## VII. The machine beneath the world

The flesh tunnels deposit the friends near an unsuspecting **illithid operator**. They kill him and nearby guards, then find **Chance and Eddie Manchester III**, the missing gnomes from the old Leopold facility, bound above a tank of small tentacled creatures. Two additional illithids, a robed caster and a security ape threaten them. A mind flayer nearly **eats Vorel's brain**, until **Eddie kills it**; Nash kills the other illithid with **Chromatic Orb**, and **Stanley, polymorphed into a giant ape**, destroys the ape. The old companions survive.

To pass another checkpoint the party disguises Nash with **a dead illithid's hollowed head**, his floating underwear and a projected voice, while Stanley takes the caster's robes. **Two ape guards believe the ruse**. Beyond lies a crystalline door decorated with a **four-finger handprint**. Chance's folded hand and a dead illithid's severed hand fail to unlock it. **Stanley Dimension Doors with Hope to the far side**, where she completes a pair of portals so the others can follow.

In the **Traversal Drive engineering cavern**, the party finally sees huge machines overgrown with blue-purple crystal, a central beam and **a decaying dragon front joined to a monstrous worm body**. **Cerinthala**, chanting illithids, a larger commander and ranged lizardfolk guard the chamber. Hope uses **both rockets** from the armory, aiming at elevated positions rather than risking the lower machine. Guards fall, and **three illithids and Cerinthala's visible body disintegrate in the beam**. Her earlier return from death means that her irreversible end is not shown. The commander survives the opening salvo.

The ensuing battle is worse. **Nash becomes a young black dragon**, initially attacking the commander's **Mirror Image** doubles. **Mind Blast** stuns Nash, Vorel and both gnomes; Stanley catches falling allies with **Feather Fall**. The dragon-worm **Dolo Popio** produces explosive small green worms, and large red memory-eaters reappear. **Hope triggers chain explosions**, while **Stanley tries Animate Objects on the magical laser swords**, discovers they are ineligible, and instead raises **ten loose metal pipes** to batter the commander. Necrotic lightning fells the gnomes. **Eddie survives with a natural-twenty death save; Stanley uses a stored Healing Word to bring Chance back**. Nash eventually **kills the illithid commander with acid breath**.

Dolo Popio remains. Invoking an uncertain **contract**, it changes the battlefield into a **star-filled void of floating rocky platforms**. The literal mechanism of that scene is unknown. Vorel activates his suit's powerful boots, leaps to the abomination and reshapes the **Eye of Groxl's flame shield into a blazing sword**. Nash burns hostile flesh; Stanley supports the battered company. The monster inhales to loose a terrible breath, and the encounter rolls into the finale.

[E078](../episodes/E078.md)–[E080](../episodes/E080.md). **Both rockets have been spent, the commander is dead, the gnomes have survived to this point and the central machine remains active**.

## VIII. The last chance in space

Dolo Popio's breath is **a swarm of large worms**, not conventional fire. **Chance slips off a floating platform and vanishes into the void**, never to reappear in the played scene. **Stanley reaches Sira**, trapped within the bright center. In the fighting Hope kills a worm burrowing into her chest with **her detachable metal horn**, which she leaves behind. **Vorel removes his new nanoweave suit and uses it to bind the demon's jaws**, holding them shut against further worm breath. **Eddie nearly dies again**, and Stanley expends another stored Healing Word to revive him.

Among the drifting debris is a **fire truck**. Hope leaps onto its ladder, loads **Scully** with radiant darts and **shoots Dolo Popio through the head**, destroying its manifested body. The space-like arena collapses into **CHIDS engineering**. Chance does not return. **Stanley, Eddie and Sira remain behind an invisible barrier within the central beam**, while Hope experiments with dangerous frequency and color controls.

The lights dim. **Quizquaz**, now an enormous oil-black winged visitor speaking for **Kitobol Prith**, urges the company to **let the beam destroy Earth**, thereby, he claims, destroying the **Principle** and satisfying their Soulbroker contract while he sends them to other worlds. Stanley refuses. The demon alleges the Drive is merely conducting energy from the **Void**, and **a powerful willing subject could absorb it**. These are interested claims, although the settings he describes later visibly operate.

**Stanley volunteers to remain alone** in the beam. Quizquaz permits **Eddie to leave** and **Sira to slip out invisibly**; she later appears alive beside Eddie. **Stanley's Augury to Moha** returns a favorable omen and a warning that it is his final result. Vorel challenges Quizquaz to volunteer instead, then proposes a different choice: **the four protagonists will share the energy together**. Quizquaz, unwilling to take that burden himself, explains that a flattened wave allows movement and **a high-frequency crimson setting** concentrates the absorption. He leaves.

**Eddie agrees to operate the console** and promises to protect Sira. While learning the controls he secretly **steals Hope's Bowie knife**. Sira thanks Stanley and says goodbye. **Hope, Vorel, Nash and Stanley enter the beam together**, clasping hands and claws. Eddie engages it. Hope is knocked unconscious; Nash's dragon form collapses; Vorel at one damage instant still has ten hit points. Then **all four dissolve into white light** and disappear.

The GM **explicitly declares them dead at the E081 episode cutoff**. The scene does not establish whether their fate is irreversible, where they go, or what Earth and the Drive look like after the energy's passage. **Sira is free and alive beside Eddie. Chance remains unlocated in the void.** The friends chose not to trade the world away.

## Epilogue — What remains when the light goes out

The protagonists began the second arc as would-be rulers and reformers, bound in different ways to the people they had saved and the bargains they had accepted. Their route through Razak Val and CHIDS exposed the cost of their own violence, the dehumanizing ambitions of old laboratories, and the appeal of letting someone else pay for survival. Their final choice was **to enter together rather than let Stanley bear the cost alone or permit the planet's destruction**.

**Observed through E081:** Vorel was publicly named **Great Fang**, but **Razak Val and Omnom's later condition remain unobserved**. **Diontalus was extracted into the Principle's crystal**. **Follow and Rorf died**, and **Sira was later rescued**. **Chance disappeared into the space-like void; Eddie survived**. **Nash killed the large illithid commander** and **Hope killed Dolo Popio's manifested body**. **Cerinthala's currently visible form disintegrated earlier**, but her ability to return makes permanence doubtful. The **four protagonists disappeared and were GM-declared dead**; permanent afterlife, the machine's long-term condition, the **Principle's fate**, and fulfillment of the old soul pact are not depicted.

Do not mistake the claims of the **spectral dragon, Charles Dickens, Alvert, Cerinthala, the hive council, the alleged future Wall Nash or Quizquaz** for neutral narration.
