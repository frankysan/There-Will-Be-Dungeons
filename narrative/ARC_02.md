# Arc 02 — Chains and Bargains

## I. A freedom offered by captors

Across the Wastes came the convoy that had appeared on the horizon as the companions escaped the buried facility. **Nash** and **Stanley** rejoined **Hope** and **Vorel** beside Fate 4, carrying stories of a prison overseen by a voice and of two gnomes lost beneath the sand. They had barely found one another before vehicles were surrounding their moving home. At first they wondered whether these were the goblins who had stolen people from Dusthill. The weapons, discipline and numbers of the newcomers soon argued otherwise.

Their attempt to escape became a running battle. Armed riders closed on the vehicle; a formidable orc bearing a **katana** and firearms forced his way onto the roof. He demanded obedience in the name of **the Principal**, claiming lawful authority that meant little to the injured travelers he was trying to arrest. They fought until they could fight no longer. Their surrender was compelled by force. Manacles replaced their weapons, and Fate 4 disappeared from sight as they were confined to a windowless transport.[^arc02-1]

After days of hunger and thirst they were brought to **Slavetown**. Its crowded streets and impressive businesses stood beside an institution that bought and held people. Among those imprisoned were the very Dusthill residents the party had set out to find. The adventurers could tell them the town's water had been restored. They could not open the prisoners' chains.

A magistrate accused the newcomers of attacking property protected by the Principal's laws. Judgment would be made through an ordeal in the **Rusty Cage**, where the four companions and the elderly traveler they had carried from the desert faced five armed orc initiates. Weapons dropped into the arena; magic and steel decided what the proceedings called justice. The companions survived and defeated their opponents. Yet the triumph at the end of the fight was not the same thing as freedom.[^arc02-2]

Only afterward did the city's authorities perform a public ceremony declaring the four innocent and releasing them. **Lady Saltbubble** and **Sir Chickenfinger**, warriors of the **Koromukishi**, offered an invitation to join their order. Whatever the ritual promised, its judgment applied to the celebrated fighters alone. The Dusthill people remained enslaved.

The company recovered an imperfect but functioning **Fate 4**, accepted directions from a nervous street-map seller called **Slippery Brent**, and saw enough of Slavetown to understand that privilege and desperation lived on adjacent streets. Hope sought mechanical work, Stanley pursued repairs to his arms, and Vorel withdrew from the city's press of strangers. Nash briefly felt relief from an item Hope lent him, but none of them knew whether it had changed what troubled his body.

At the **Buxom Confederate**, a prosperous local patron named **Celibus Stump** congratulated them. He had profited by betting on their unexpected arena victory, and his offers of money and lodging came with a useful new acquaintance. Nearby, Stanley recognized **Talia**, the mother of young **Bruce Bane** from Dusthill. She remembered her son and feared what it would cost to leave. Stanley sought even a night of safety for her, but the negotiation did not free her from her owner or bring her home.[^arc02-3]

As the others followed their separate errands, Nash drank, gambled and confronted a man known as **the Jeweler**. The two moved toward a fight that neither fully controlled. In Slavetown, surviving the arena had proved easier than deciding which bargains could be trusted.

## II. The price of a missing deed

The next days made the distinction between shelter and freedom painfully clear. **Vorel and Bok Bok** found lodgings at a hotel, and Stanley arranged temporary refuge there for **Talia**. She could spend a night away from the establishment that controlled her, but the arrangement rested on negotiation and might be withdrawn. Talia's fear, and Stanley's promise to help, belonged to a private conversation that the rest of the company had not witnessed.

Nash's confrontation with the Jeweler turned physical. A fight that had seemed to be about fists involved magic, and Nash's own **Thunderwave** shook the room. He later emerged injured, drunk and without his staff, wraps, clothing and much of his money. His explanations of what had happened were less certain than the missing possessions. The companions gathered him up as the city's opportunities for work and trade continued around them. Hope took a place at **Owen's garage**, and the others visited shops and workshops, trying to find a way back to Dusthill's captives.[^arc02-4]

**Stump** offered help, but linked it to a **mine deed** he wanted recovered. The Jeweler proposed another use for the same document: a challenge to the powerful **Oilbeards** and the trading system that enriched them. Nobody had produced the deed. Even its description shifted between a copper mine and a gold mine. The Jeweler alleged that Dusthill had been betrayed by a Hillmaster and a gnome resembling **Softhead**; the accusation was grave, and it remained his accusation rather than discovered proof.

Slavetown had once been called **Pristine**, according to accounts the companions heard. The expanding influence of the Principal's soldiers, Oilbeard interests, the city's magistrate and local sheriff had not made it one simple chain of command. The **Koromukishi** followed their own martial role; Stump courted power through money and influence; the Jeweler wanted the mine deed for a competing bargain. The companions might be useful to each of them without being safe in the hands of any.[^arc02-5]

Violence interrupted the negotiations at the Jeweler's establishment, **the Sapphire**. Armed men connected to a group called the **Lactid Cartel** arrived with a robed spellcaster and accused the Jeweler—now addressed as **Bertrand**—of failing to hand over an item. That item was not shown to be the mine deed. Shots turned the building into a battlefield. **Trigger** and **Donk** fought beside Bertrand; Vorel charged through the attackers; Stanley's magic conjured the *appearance* of Quizquaz, not the return of the creature they had destroyed beneath Dusthill. The fighting spilled into the street, where the local sheriff intervened. Bertrand survived after receiving healing. **Donk died**.[^arc02-6]

Before the group returned to the hotel, several **gnomes offered timepieces** as a diversion while **Stanley's coin purse was stolen**. The theft prompted a chase and confrontation. These were Slavetown strangers, **not Chance and Eddie**, the missing guests from the desert facility.[^arc02-38]

An enormous one-eyed figure came to the travelers' hotel, shaking its walls and reaching for Vorel. The giant sought a stolen necklace he called his **shiny**. In the confrontation that followed, the sheriff also faced the creature, and clues pointed the party toward a strange man associated with purple clothing and a house on **Thorn Row**. It was too soon to know which names the man had used or who had taken the giant's treasure. **The GM later recounted** that **Sheriff Randy died** after shooting at the cyclops in this confrontation; the immediate scene alone had not established his fate.[^arc02-39]

Inside the Thorn Row house, Hope found mutilated bodies, a written warning addressed to **Timber**, and a trap that exploded when she investigated it. A signature resembling **Red Scarlet** did not reveal who had sent the message. The adventurers found a firearm, a Liberators pamphlet and other possessions whose history was uncertain. Hidden stairs led down to chambers smelling of decay and foul ritual work. There they discovered a necklace set with red stones, close to the one the giant described, but its provenance was still unknown.

Among the victims lay **Randall Owen**, the garage owner who had employed Hope. This time the death was directly identified in the scene; her outrage carried her farther into the house. The group encountered an apparent figure that dissolved into deception, then a ritualist who demanded the ruby necklace. He spoke of **Catoblepraithe**, a being he called **the Soulbroker**, as a patron he intended to summon. His threat was not the same as the patron's arrival. When the mutilated dead rose around the companions, their hunt for the deed had become a struggle against a power whose reach they did not understand.[^arc02-7]

## III. A death that would not stay settled

The dead rose from the mutilated remains beneath Thorn Row, barring the way between the companions and the ritualist who had demanded the ruby necklace. **Tylander Sullivan** spoke from beyond an iron gate, certain that fear and his strange preparations would drive his pursuers away. Spells, an immovable rod and brute strength helped the four withstand the corpses; then Vorel, enlarged by magic, forced an opening through the house itself. Tylander escaped into Slavetown before they could reach him.[^arc02-8]

The town seemed to be emptying. Beyond its streets the giant **Orp** still wanted his stolen necklace. Nash brought it to the cyclops while Stanley tried to keep him calm. Tylander appeared near Orp and turned the giant's uncertainty against the party. Orp knocked Nash from a dangerous height, but **Stanley slowed his fall**; moments later Tylander's magic left the giant breathing but unconscious. Whatever Orp had threatened to do, he was no longer defending himself.

**Hope and Vorel** pursued Tylander around the fallen giant. He struck Vorel with a wasting touch that left the lizardfolk carrying a phantom pain. When Tylander tried to surrender, Hope and Vorel kept striking him. His apparent death was unmistakable to those who had fought him, even if the full nature of his magic was not. Warriors of the **Koromukishi** arrived with soldiers of the Principal's Host, praised the killing and discussed whether Orp should also die. The companions argued for the giant's life. A parchment recovered from Tylander's body resembled the missing mine deed only until it **dissolved into smoke**, leaving them with no document to trade for the Dusthill captives.[^arc02-9]

Orp awoke and, after difficult persuasion and the promise of food, was drawn away from the city. **Nash and Vorel** accompanied him into the desert while **Hope and Stanley** returned to Slavetown. The party was divided again, not by a supernatural barrier this time, but by choices about whom they would protect and how far they were prepared to deceive someone dangerous.

Nash and Vorel sent the hungry cyclops toward traffic at **Esophageal Pass**, persuading him that an ordinary dagger was a magical device through which they could call him. It was a deliberate falsehood and a potentially lethal choice. They did not witness what Orp later did to the travelers they had placed in his path. Then Vorel heard a summons from lizardfolk to the east. Nash went back to Slavetown alone; he did not see the rites that followed.[^arc02-10]

A priestess among Vorel's people spoke of **Fuxi, Father of Fire**, and of an ancient promise or captivity. Vorel experienced a fiery sky, enormous creatures beneath the earth and the struggle of being swallowed and reborn through flame. He could describe that vision, but neither the priestess's cosmology nor the vision's physical location was proven to his absent companions. When he awoke, Bok Bok was near him, and the road back to the others remained open.

In Slavetown, Hope and Stanley found fresh horrors. At **Randall Owen's home**, another mutilated victim lay behind a once-ordinary domestic door. Tylander's house on Thorn Row had become rubble surrounded by dead soldiers. A severed human **arm** stirred in the wreckage and nearly strangled Stanley. They fought it and later burned it. Its movements were plain to see; whose arm it had once been was still an inference.

The enormous **black dragon** from the desert flew overhead. In the street beneath it, Nash's skin darkened into scales and flames burst from his body, harming Hope and Stanley. Nash heard a low voice calling for **“Mother.”** His companions saw the fire, not the private voice; he could not know what the speaker meant.[^arc02-11]

An appointed magistrate came to **Nash, Hope and Stanley** with a story more disturbing than the arm. He said **Tylander had returned**, slain troops and become difficult to kill through infernal bargains. The companions had not seen a resurrected Tylander themselves. The magistrate pointed them toward the **Black Mine**, claiming that a land record connected **Catherine Speckles** to its legendary 999th level. His offer of rewards—and a promise that the Dusthill captives could be freed—was a bargain, not their freedom already accomplished. The three agreed only conditionally, while **Vorel was still returning** from his separate ordeal. A new destination had taken shape, and the party had not yet learned whether the man they killed could be killed at all.[^arc02-12]

## IV. Pixie Blush and a city on fire

By the time they prepared to travel, **Slavetown** had grown eerily quiet. Some of its people and enslaved captives had been moved toward the **Salivating Fields**, according to reports; no one had promised their safety. The company still needed supplies, and some doors were locked against them. **Hope, Stanley and Nash** broke into a general store, then sought weapons and other necessities while the injured and unsettled companions tried to regain their footing.

At an apothecary, a small tiefling offered remedies for Nash's distress. He took a preparation later called **Pixie Blush**. His perceptions slipped almost at once: ordinary people seemed to vanish, objects shimmered and the streets no longer held their shape. Meanwhile, Hope received a card reading and a warning of danger from the apothecary. It was advice or prophecy from one speaker, not a future guaranteed to unfold.[^arc02-13]

Elsewhere **Vorel**, now back in town with **Bok Bok**, met a woman in a striking **red bandolier**. She invited him to join a plan to rob **Forbisher's Holdings**, offering the excitement of another dangerous bargain. Her identity and affiliations were not established by her clothing. Vorel could relay the offer to the others; it did not mean that he or the company had actually robbed a bank. By then Nash had gathered explosives, and his hallucinatory fear was making him difficult to protect.

The three others returned to a locked **foundry** in search of equipment. The owner confronted them with a gun and fired. **Stanley** was badly wounded; **Vorel killed the owner** during the struggle. Whatever the legal rights of a business owner or strangers taking supplies, those consequences were played, not rumors that could be brushed aside. The wounds followed the party into the next crisis.[^arc02-14]

Nash, meanwhile, saw what appeared to be a **green elf**, an encouraging stranger who offered him comfort and urged him toward the explosives shop. The encounter belonged to Nash's drug-altered perception; none of his companions had established the stranger's presence. But the **dynamite** was real. Nash set it alight with a spell, and the resulting blast tore through the district. Fire spread toward crowded businesses, the **Buxom Confederate** and the hotel. The apparent voice that had encouraged him might have been a hallucination; the destruction and the terror it caused were not.

The companions struggled to gather their wounded. They confronted **Celibus Stump**, crossed streets turning dangerous with flame and fought to get Stanley into a vehicle. Vorel attacked a motorist trying to leave the city. **Hope secured a working station wagon** from the garage area; she had **not repaired Fate 4**. They collected Nash and drove toward the Black Mine as Slavetown burned behind them. The full loss of life was unknown. They had not liberated the Dusthill prisoners, and no one could truthfully say the entire city—or everyone still within it—had been destroyed.[^arc02-15]

The desert did not offer an easy escape. The following journey began with Hope driving the exhausted, injured party through worsening wind. Lightning flashed, but the violence around them was **sand**, not rain. The storm buried the station wagon, forcing the travelers to dig themselves free and stay together against near-blind winds. **Vorel** helped pull them out; their next shelter was the hollow carcass of an immense dead desert creature.

Small cloaked figures approached with crude weapons, and one struck Vorel's scales. **Hope's threatening voice in Infernal** drove them away. No one had identified the strangers or shown that they were killed. The party remained beneath the dead creature, waiting for the storm to clear and unable to tell whether the winds would smother Slavetown's fires or spread the ruin farther.

The road that had begun in flames now ended in darkness and blowing sand. When the wind settled, they would have to face the Black Mine—and the demands made in return for promises of freedom.[^arc02-16]

## V. The Black Mine and the long fall

Wind scoured the hollow carcass in which the companions waited. Their **station wagon** was buried somewhere in the sand, and the fate of the burning town behind them was still unknown. The small cloaked attackers had retreated, leaving the four to wait for a break in the storm. In that uneasy shelter, **Nash** spoke about the **Solar Mines**.

He told his companions of captivity and beatings, of finding a **nail** and using it to fight his way out. He had named the nail **Freedom** and fixed it to his staff. Escape had not erased his memories, or his doubts about people hurt along the way. They were hearing **Nash's account**, not watching an independently witnessed past. The name could be confused with another possession: the **firearm called Freedom** found in Thorn Row, which was not this nail. Hope and the others also wondered whether the storm might quench Slavetown's fires. Nobody knew that it had.[^arc02-17]

When the weather eased, the companions abandoned their damaged vehicle and continued on foot. At the **Black Mine**, a vast quarry and guarded shaft opened into the mountainside. **Oilbeard dwarves** claimed enslaved miners had risen against their overseers. They had blocked the only lift, they said, to starve the workers below. Their account explained the guards' policy, not the true condition of everyone trapped beneath them. One of the guards kept a **human prisoner called Tickles** on all fours in a helmet, treating him like a pet. The party recoiled at the cruelty but argued over whether to fight the slavers at once or get down to the miners first.

Claiming authority from the Principal's magistrate, the companions agreed to descend. The guards insisted the lift was stuck and the mine was almost impossibly deep. Numbers concerning **nine hundred and ninety-nine levels** circulated as rumor or reported property records; none of the four had reached such a place. **Stanley** cast **Feather Fall**, and they leapt together into the shaft, landing on the stranded elevator far below the surface. A marker put them near an early numbered level. Their attempt to restart the lift brought something worse than uncooperative machinery: **undead workers surged from the dark galleries** as the platform began rising.[^arc02-18]

The fight filled the elevator with clawing figures, gunfire and spellwork. **Vorel's fiery rage** and Stanley's moving sword cut paths among the attackers, while Nash and Hope tried to keep the party from being overwhelmed. As they neared the upper guards, the dwarves saw the undead crowding their lift and **sent it downward again** rather than help. In the confusion **Tickles jumped onto the platform**. He was still a human captive, however his captors had forced him to behave. For a time he survived beside the four as the struggle resumed.

A heavy **Thunderwave** and the chaos of fighting damaged the lift. A support **chain snapped**. The platform tilted, then fell, rushing beyond the numbered galleries. The group passed at least one distant level marker before darkness and speed made any precise count meaningless. Shortly before impact, **Stanley slowed their fall with another Feather Fall**. Wreckage and undead bodies plunged into the chamber below, but the living travelers survived. **Tickles also survived that fall**. It would be another danger, not the elevator's descent, that finally took his life.[^arc02-19]

Far beneath the worked mine, the party followed a rocky passage to a chamber filled with crushed dwarf skeletons. A skull-ended lever promised a possible way onward. **Hope pulled it**, and the ceiling came down. She escaped the worst of the rockfall, but the others and Tickles were buried or injured under stone. The session ended with their rescue unresolved.

Hope and her companions dug out the survivors. **Stanley** was in severe pain; **Tickles** lay beneath a dented helmet and did not respond. Only now did they conclude that the captive had **died**. They rested among the broken rock and, testing the trap again, found a **second control**. By operating the levers together, they opened a hidden passage rather than causing another cave-in.[^arc02-20]

Beyond it lay a sealed study where fragile books crumbled to dust. A journal survived. **Tylander Sullivan** had written of lost family, the days of his suffering, and a **voice beyond a door at the bottom of the Black Pit** that, he claimed, offered vengeance. His handwriting was evidence of his beliefs and intentions, not proof the unseen speaker had described the past truthfully. A skeleton lay on the bed, but the diary's presence did not identify whose remains they were. Other pages shifted into unreadable symbols. The party took the journal and found a trapdoor leading deeper.

The new cavern was crowded with **mushrooms**, a chasm and **glyphs** whose effects they could not safely predict. A badly wounded, tentacled **displacer beast** attacked among the ledges. It seemed to slip from place to place; Vorel finally killed it after a dangerous struggle over the pit. When the party used part of the creature to test a glyph, a cloud of **magical darkness** spread across their route. They went into it holding on to one another and following the wall.

While they searched through that darkness, **Hope alone heard whispers**. One voice pleaded that she **find it**. The others could see nothing and had not heard what she heard. She did not yet know whether the plea came from a prisoner, an enemy, or something wholly different. Their path forward was still hidden.[^arc02-21]

## VI. Ritual, deception and the opening of the rift

The company emerged from darkness into choking mushroom beds beside another hazardous ledge. Some of the growth was only an obstacle. Other **large purple fungi** moved, reaching with tentacles and attacking the travelers. The party fought them off and picked a cautious way past further glyphs. When the crossing seemed too dangerous, **Vorel's enlarged body became a living bridge** for his companions. They were making their way by ingenuity through defenses no one had completely explained.

At the end of the passage waited an extraordinary door. Its metal face **spoke**, complained of being neglected, and called itself **Dorothy**. She offered riddles and warnings of danger, as if being a door had never prevented her from forming opinions. **Stanley** argued that she deserved more independence than simply obeying whoever approached. Whether Dorothy was a sentient creature, a speaking enchantment or some other trick remained uncertain. She nevertheless let them pass, and they crossed another gap by rope and magic to reach a ruined chapel.[^arc02-22]

Within stood **Tylander Sullivan**, seemingly returned after the companions had killed him near Orp. Eight thin, hooded followers labored among mutilated remains. Tylander offered explanations for his hatred: he said he had once been the mayor of **Pristine**, before agents of the **Principal** and the **Oilbeards** destroyed his position and murdered his family. Cast into the Black Mine, he claimed, he had received a new body and power from a patron. The journal had prepared the companions for parts of this confession; neither the journal nor his speech could prove all the accusations.

He also said he had turned enslaved miners into **undead**, blaming them for failing to fight their oppressors. Whatever he had suffered, the cruelty of the creatures already encountered gave the companions reason to take the confession seriously. Yet his account of motives and allegiances remained his own. To unsettle them further, Tylander assumed the appearance of **Catherine Speckles**, a demonstration of disguise or transformation—not evidence that every woman bearing that name was merely him.

An attempted shot passed through the figure on the raised chair. His voice came from elsewhere. **The visible Tylander was not a solid, defeated enemy**. He set his eight followers on the party; they changed into grotesque forms and attacked. The companions fought a bitter battle against the things Tylander called the **Fingers** of his patron. Victory over them did **not** mean Tylander had been killed again.[^arc02-23]

In a chamber piled with bodies, they found another horror: a gravely mutilated woman who identified herself as **Catherine Speckles**. She asked them to tell **Bertrand, the Jeweler**, that she loved him and was sorry. Then she ceased responding, and the party believed her dead. She was a prisoner who **claimed Catherine's identity**, not the same demonstrated impersonation they had just seen from Tylander. Whatever the real history of those two encounters, collapsing them into one person would erase her final words.

No mine deed emerged from the chapel. A locked chest offered another uncertainty, but they could not establish what lay inside. Ahead, a ritual chamber pulsed with strange light. **Tylander** appeared near a cauldron, beside an enormous **winged black figure** with luminous features. The figure spoke to them. Only as the confrontation continued did the companions recognize a familiar adversary: **Quizquaz**, the oily creature they had fought beneath Dusthill. He was **not** automatically the patron Tylander had promised to summon.[^arc02-24]

The way back closed when **Vorel's tail touched a glyph**, bringing stone down behind the party. Strange fields and vibrating projections guarded the central platform. Hope fired into its defenses; Vorel's size and strength helped test the barrier, while eye-like stalks attacked from the platform. The floor broke open to reveal that these were parts of a single monstrous body: an enormous **beholder**, its central eye and many beams turning the battle into a struggle against fear, sleep and stolen control.

**Vorel fell under a charm** and struck out at his companions. They fought to subdue him rather than pretend he had freely chosen to betray them. When the beholder was finally defeated, Vorel was incapacitated and the enchantment's consequences remained for his friends to confront. Quizquaz was still present, a separate being from the many-eyed monster and from Tylander himself.[^arc02-25]

From the beholder's remains **Quizquaz took a dark gem**. He used it to open a whirling tear in the chamber, a passage through smoke and impossible space. From beyond it began to emerge an immense **goat-like face and grasping fingers**—the figure Tylander had invoked as **Catoblepraithe, the Soulbroker**. The companions had won a terrible fight only to find that something more powerful was coming through.

The arrival was not yet a welcome, a bargain or even a completed crossing. What the newcomer wanted, and what it would demand, belonged to the next part of their story.[^arc02-26]

## VII. The price of a soul

The tear in the ritual chamber widened. **Quizquaz** had pulled a dark gem from the fallen beholder and opened a way through it, but the enormous figure now stepping into the Black Mine was not the oily demon the companions had fought beneath Dusthill. A **goat-like being** called himself **Catoblepraithe**, later giving his title as **the Soulbroker**. He spoke easily to the four battered travelers, as though an invitation could follow naturally from the ruin around them.

**Vorel** was still suffering from the beholder's magical influence. The newcomer calmed and healed him, removing the immediate danger of his turning on his friends. The relief was real; the reasons behind it remained the host's to explain. Catoblepraithe proposed food, rest and information, then sent Quizquaz ahead to arrange the welcome. After the company had survived the dungeon, the offer of a bed was difficult to refuse.[^arc02-27]

They crossed through a portal onto a bridge over impossible darkness. **Château Weir** rose around them amid distant lights, with a vast whirlpool beneath it filled with struggling forms. Their host described these as **souls**. The sight of suffering was plain enough, but its machinery, the identities of the trapped and the trustworthiness of its keeper were not proven by his explanation. In a carriage through the strange city, Vorel apologized for the blows he had struck while charmed. He had not chosen the enchantment, yet his companions still bore its consequences.

At their guest house the staff treated hospitality as an elaborate profession. **Baba** attended to the accommodations; food, baths, music and attentive servants awaited. The guests were asked for papers concerning consent and dietary needs, written in **Infernal**. They studied what they were being asked to accept. Those service forms were **not** the binding soul compact the host would later propose. They spent the night, slept and recovered, although a good meal did not turn the place into a safe or disinterested refuge.

**Hope dreamed** that she returned to the **Haven** and met her mother. In the dream her mother spoke of a cost paid for their reunion and claimed that Hope's **father had survived**, only to be taken by a woman with a **red bandolier** and the **Dead Men**. Hope awoke with a new possibility, not independent proof of anyone's survival or whereabouts. Elsewhere in the guest house, **Vorel heard a childlike voice** pleading through a door. He did **not** open it, and the voice's identity remained unknown. In the morning Hope recounted her dream, and the party connected its red-bandolier figure to the woman who had approached Vorel about a bank robbery in Slavetown. That resemblance made a lead, not a confirmation.[^arc02-28]

The Soulbroker received them in his throne room. He asked what each hoped to change: **Hope** wanted to heal the Wastes, **Nash** to confront the oppression of the Solar Mines, **Stanley** a fairer world, and **Vorel** strength without surrendering himself. Catoblepraithe presented **the Principal** as the great obstacle and offered knowledge and power in exchange for a task.

The task was immense. They were to **kill the Principal within one thousand days and one thousand nights** or, under the proposed compact, lose the **souls bound by it**. In return he promised an immediate boon and leads toward artifacts that could make the attempt possible. **Hope signed. Nash signed. Stanley signed. Vorel refused.** He had been healed, had shared the rest and had advanced in ability with his companions, but **he did not enter the soul bargain** or receive its signatories' contractual gift. The other three accepted a debt whose terms they had heard from the very being who meant to profit by it.[^arc02-29]

The Soulbroker began probing the secret within Nash, and pain drove him into a private ordeal. Nash seemed to return to the **Solar Mines**: guards hunting him, a terrified girl, vats holding suspended bodies, the brutal **Bulger** and a controlling **Warden**. He struggled with the nail he called Freedom. The images were given to him by the Soulbroker; whatever truth they held, the others were **not present inside the experience**.

Then Nash changed **before their eyes** into a **black dragon**. He flew and spoke while the others watched a power that could no longer be dismissed as an uncontrolled flash of fire. The game's contemporary account identified a **dragon-metal heart** within him, an extraordinary relic tied to a black dragon's lineage and dangerous experimentation. That explanation carried more weight than guesses made about Nash's earlier symptoms, but the names and precise workings still called for contemporary records. Even here the change came with **exhaustion**, rather than the unlimited freedom his new wings seemed to promise.[^arc02-30]

Catoblepraithe offered more leads. He spoke of paired firearms called **Fox and Scully**, an elusive red-bandolier keeper and a route to a far-off elven destination. **Because Vorel had not signed the compact, the Soulbroker dismissed him from the audience.** With only Hope, Nash and Stanley remaining, Catoblepraithe proposed using **Celestium** ignited by dragon breath to seal **Esophageal Pass**. These were **his claims and proposed strategy**, not artifacts recovered or defenses already destroyed. He gave Nash **keys to Château Weir**, with restrictions on where a portal could open. When Hope asked about her father, he admitted that he relied on agents and offered only a qualified report about possible transfer toward **Principal City**. As for the missing mine deed, he claimed it had already been consumed in the ritual that brought him forth.[^arc02-40]

The companions had no deed to bring back, no certainty about Hope's father and no liberated Dusthill prisoners. They had, instead, Nash's visible new form and a compact binding **three** of their souls to a mission none could yet see how to complete. **Vorel remained outside the agreement**. When the Soulbroker's audience ended, the four still had to escape the Black Mine where this bargain had begun.[^arc02-31]

## VIII. The Heroes of the Wastes

The return portal deposited the companions back in the depths of the **Black Mine**. Nash's dragon shape could carry him, but it drained him; the others began measuring the danger of another transformation. **Vorel fashioned a shield from the defeated beholder's teeth**, an improvised piece of protection rather than a newly proven magical relic. They cleared fallen rock from their path, passed the chapel and again found the chamber where the woman calling herself **Catherine Speckles** had died.

They looked for something to bring to **Bertrand**, who had cared about Catherine and had asked them to find the deed. They still had neither the woman nor the deed. The heavy chest they had failed to open earlier had not disappeared: **only now** did they force it open, finding money, gems and two potions that Stanley identified, **not** the missing title document. The party would have to decide what these discoveries meant after reaching the surface.[^arc02-32]

Beyond the chapel, the speaking door **Dorothy** stood between them and the route they thought led out. Distrusting her warnings, they **set the wooden door on fire**. Smoke filled the narrow tunnel, and as they struggled to see a safe way forward **Dorothy's voice continued to speak**. Setting the wood alight did not prove the being behind the voice dead or destroyed. A claim that weeks had passed in the mine came from what characters believed Dorothy had said, not from any calendar they had checked. They ended that stretch of their ordeal **still belowground**, with smoke and a hidden hazard blocking their way.

The hazard caught **Nash**. He fell into a **spiked pit** but survived when the others hauled him up using rope and the equipment they carried. The company retraced its way through the old passages toward the great elevator shaft. They had seen the lift smashed far below; nobody proposed riding it back to daylight. **Nash became a black dragon once more**, and with spells and makeshift supports the companions climbed aboard him for a perilous flight. When he reached the **upper guard hall**, the dwarven overseers were still waiting.[^arc02-33]

The **Oilbeards** met them with guns, including a wheeled weapon they called **Bessie**. Vorel leapt among the guards; Nash's dragon breath tore across their position; Hope and Stanley fought through the answering fire. The weight of weapons and wounds forced **Nash out of dragon shape**, leaving him hurt and exhausted while the others continued. At least one dwarf escaped during the fighting. The company had reached the top of the mine, not ended the Oilbeards' power everywhere in the Wastes.

Their victory made prisoners of the guards who remained. The overseer **Scrolly Oilbeard** was held alive and interrogated. The companions **injured him, healed him, then injured him again** while making threats. Finally they **threw him down the elevator shaft**. Whatever the dwarves had done to the enslaved miners, this was deliberate coercive cruelty by the protagonists, not a peaceful exchange or a mercy they could claim afterward. They searched the hall and found a locked prison room. From behind its door **Vorel heard Bok Bok calling**. The other captives were not yet identified, and the room was still closed when that moment ended.[^arc02-34]

The lock gave way only afterward. **Bertrand, Trigger and Bok Bok** emerged from the cell. Bertrand's explanation was bitter: he said he had come to return the **repaired Fate 4**, aided by Bok Bok, and had been captured by the guards. The party had not witnessed his journey or the repairs; what they could now see was that their familiar vehicle was available again. Bertrand learned that Catherine was apparently dead and the deed gone. He spoke of Tylander having been executed years before, and speculated that the lost deed might have concerned **dragon metal**. His grief and his theories were his own, no substitute for the missing document.[^arc02-35]

A stranger in **powered armor** arrived at the mine in a dune buggy and went into the workings. The companions observed him but did not establish his identity or purpose. They took the buggy's **keys** and left in **Fate 4**, with their freed companions aboard. On the road Bertrand told them of the **Lactid Cartel** and suggested that forces were moving toward a nearly empty Slavetown, perhaps while the Principal's soldiers redeployed. It was useful intelligence from a man who had his own interests, not verified control of the city.

Rather than rush toward another uncertain battle, the travelers headed for a **Liberators camp** near the **Deadfingers**. The camp offered shelter and the possibility of allies, though no army had yet promised to march at their command. **Vorel and Bok Bok hunted a bear**; Vorel killed it and brought meat back. **Nash**, angered by an encounter at the camp, discharged a public **Fire Bolt**, alarming friends who feared he might reveal the supposedly concealed settlement. Their growing power made protection and recklessness harder to separate.

Then they encountered **Slippery Brent**, the nervous map seller from Slavetown, now disoriented and possibly caught in the grip of **Pixie Blush**. Some of the companions coerced him and **violently assaulted him**, injuring him as the interrogation became an attack. **Nash objected**, troubled both by the harm done and by what the party was becoming. Brent's confused claims were not automatically reliable, and the episode did not establish his final fate clearly enough to pronounce him dead. A company prepared to face tyrants was beginning to imitate some of the violence it condemned.[^arc02-36]

At the camp they met a **sightless orc** who said he had abandoned the teachings of the **Koromukishi**, even setting aside his old name. He argued for opposition to the Principal and spoke with Bertrand about freeing captives and building resistance. The company weighed an assault on the **Salivating Fields**, stronger alliances, the far-off artifacts and the proposed sealing of Esophageal Pass. No plan had yet been carried out. **Dusthill's enslaved people were still waiting**, and three travelers carried a bargain that would not wait forever.

They chose to call themselves the **Heroes of the Wastes**.

The name described a promise, not a completed victory. They had rescued friends, survived the mine and found allies who might help. They had also left captives in chains, made a pact with an interested power and inflicted cruelty of their own. Whatever they were to become would depend on what they did with the name after this night.[^arc02-37]
