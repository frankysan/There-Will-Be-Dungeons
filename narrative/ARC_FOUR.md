# Arc Four — The Price of a Way Home

**The Wastes, completed fourth story arc — corrected E082 through Hope's departure in E101**

**Full spoilers through the Arc Four portion of E101.** This editorial retelling draws on the linked **processed-ASR episode dossiers**, not audio-verified transcripts. Dialogue is paraphrased; private experiences, NPC explanations and retrospective commentary retain their limits. The story continues from [Arc Three](ARC_THREE.md), with [Arc Five](ARC_FIVE.md) beginning later in the same E101 recording.

## Prologue — After the light

At the bottom of CHIDS, **Vorel, Nash, Hope and Stanley** entered the Traversal Drive's beam together. Their bodies dissolved into light, and the GM declared all four dead. **Sira and Eddie** survived beyond the beam; **Chance** was missing. Neither Earth's fate nor the outcome of the struggle against the Principle was known.

For Vorel, the light gives way to stars, an immense unidentified face, and finally a filthy sewer. He wakes without clothes or equipment. The **Eye of Groxl** in his chest has broken open, sacrificing its active presence to protect him. Nearby floats a black speck. An eye opens inside it.

## I. Strangers in the City of Doors

The speck is **Nash**, reduced to a dragon-metal heart that can see and think but cannot speak. Vorel calls it **Speckle the Cosmic Speck**. When a hungry rat-man approaches the sleeping lizardfolk, Nash learns to move and float well enough to warn him. Survival begins with defending the only friend he can reach.

The sewers hold two more familiar figures: **Hope and Stanley**, apparently dead, being collected for a trade in valuable corpses. Hope's mechanical limbs are gone. Vorel fights to keep the bodies, with Nash ramming their opponents in his strange new form. An agreement with a surviving collector wins them a hovering slab and directions to the surface. The collector names their new home **Sigil, the City of Doors**.

Sigil curves overhead, its streets and roofs filling the sky. At the Welcome Center, Vorel obtains three days of food and shelter; at the **Soggy Feather**, **Bill and Pam** offer warmth and advice. Nash begins growing human pieces from his heart. A new finger is an absurd but tangible reason to hope.

Two strangers join their search for help: **Nothing**, a kenku who communicates through borrowed voices, and **Squirts**, a kobold carrying her infant, Chunks. Squirts leads them to a hidden trickster church. Its priest calls Hope and Stanley **petitioners**, soul-associated manifestations connected to the **Weir**, and proposes a bargain. If Vorel publicly humiliates a sufficiently proud champion, he will try to restore them. The bodies stay in the crypts while Vorel pursues an unlikely rescue through magically controlled fighting marionettes.

## II. A champion made by tricks

The sport is **Big Time Smack'em Brothers**. Vorel, Nash and Nothing enter, acquiring a strategy book through theft, assault and an untested memory-blurring drink administered to its owner. The priest, **Kidigree**, supplies Squirts with devices to sicken opponents. Vorel recognizes the unfairness but continues because Hope and Stanley's return depends upon the bargain.

Vorel and Nash win their opening matches; Nothing and another kenku wait to imitate each other until both forfeit. Vorel's favored high kick carries him through the Royce qualifier, with Squirts' sabotage helping his progress. When the defeated **Ventro** attacks, a terrifying green female figure appears and draws him through a tiny opening in reality. His disappearance is witnessed; his death and destination are not. The companions associate the apparition with warnings about the **Lady of Pain**.

Fame brings reporters, admirers, prospective sponsors and danger. **Crimson Moon assassins** attack the Soggy Feather, vanishing when killed and leaving no useful captive. A poisoned dart threatens Vorel's championship attempt. Medication suppresses its symptoms, while Kidigree's foul potion lets him hear an opponent's intended moves. His victories are real, and so is the cheating that helps produce them.

Vorel defeats the **Mind Master**. Kidigree secretly substitutes a clean sample during the competitors' drug test; another player's disqualification sends Vorel directly to the final. There he beats **Kanja**, then activates the priest's device. The champion appears to contain a tiny workforce operating his body. The crowd turns on its idol, although no independent examination establishes whether the revelation itself was a trick. Vorel receives the title, trophy and **five thousand gold**.

The price reaches beyond the arena. Through Kidigree's mirror, the party watches the Mind Master and Crimson Moon associates threaten Kanja with ruin and death. Kidigree collects the fallen champion's tears for the promised ritual. Later, a newspaper reports Kanja dead. These connections are disturbing, but they do not identify his murderer or prove who ordered the attack on Vorel.

Nothing also carries a private obligation: a paid commission to abduct a prominent Sigil resident and deliver him through a portal to the Abyss. It is accepted, not shown completed. The people helping Vorel save his friends have dangers of their own.

## III. The friends who come back

Kidigree admits that he considered keeping Hope and Stanley merely to make a joke of Vorel's trust. Instead he keeps his word. In the **Hall of Reflections**, Hope and Stanley each perceive an opening from bodiless darkness and choose to approach it. They walk out of mirrors alive, while their original corpses remain strapped to slabs. Hope has natural limbs again, but neither returnee has recovered the equipment lost with the beam.

Vorel is relieved without being certain that a trickster has returned the same friends. Later, spectrometer examinations show living auras and hidden writing: the claim of the **Weir** remains. Resurrection has not visibly annulled their contract. Squirts stays with the chapel; Nothing's separate business remains unresolved as the old party reunites.

Nash's inability to cast spells ends through an explicit **GM correction**, not the purchase of a spell key. He casts Fire Bolt normally. After eight days his body finishes regrowing, with his old injuries still present. His former thermal underwear has fused into him, retaining its cooling and limited flight. Vorel installs a professional marionette station at the Soggy Feather, but his dream of buying the inn remains a dream.

The question becomes whether to leave. **Stanley** finds food, comfort and possibilities in Sigil that Earth seldom offered him. **Hope** remembers her kingdom; **Nash**, his wife and expected child; **Vorel**, their people, friends and unfinished obligations. They agree to search for a route back without knowing what time or disaster may have done to home.

At the **Portal Regulatory Agency**, clerks distinguish their planet Earth from the Elemental Plane of Earth and find a recorded stable portal. Its steward is **Tobacho Fleur**, a powerful figure in the Hive. A return to the sewers also yields **Scully**, Hope's pistol, and a lead connecting its missing twin to Tobacho's network. Nash's umbrella briefly opens another portal, into an alarming creature-filled darkness; it supplies no evidence of a route to Earth.

## IV. The dragon's price

Disguised as **Lady Despair and the Rambunctious Bunch**, the four enter the Hive. At its checkpoint, the Harmonium threatens never to let them leave; a prisoner faces hanging merely for attempting escape. They pass without rescuing him. Sigil's comforts have a boundary, and the companions are crossing it.

In a club, the scarred tiefling **Spatulo** gives Hope directions to Tobacho's cantina. Tobacho proves to be an enormous blue dragon attended by armed retainers and a speaking golem. The party pays for an audience and offers **Scully** as a gift. In exchange for retrieving a valuable item, the dragon promises the location and means of using his Earth portal. Mentioning Spatulo nearly ruins the meeting: the informant is Tobacho's enemy.

Tobacho's broker **Jelver** tests the four against trolls in a magically staged canyon. Their injuries are real, even though the landscape dissolves afterward. Having passed, they learn the target: **Zorander's Fist**, held by paladins in the besieged **White Tower** of the Outlands.

A kidnapped bishop provides the crucial complication. The relic is inside a sacred box, while its pendant-key hangs around **High Cleric Thrun's** neck in Sigil. Stanley deceives the prisoner into trusting him, extracts the information and leaves him captive. The bishop's account describes a tower whose walls admit only good souls. Jelver proposes an airship approach and a teleporting leap through the siege. The plan has not proved that all four can enter.

With Tobacho's writ, the party actually gets back through the Hive checkpoint. New priestly disguises and temporary magical appearances carry them into the **Unwavering House**. During a blessing Stanley steals the pendant and substitutes an imitation. Hope's religious performance almost traps her at the temple for examination; Stanley changes an attendant's memory to secure her release.

The promised supplies arrive: teleportation scrolls, equipment, firearms and ammunition. Hope receives Scully again alongside the pistol called **Fox** here and **Mulder** in earlier research; the naming discrepancy does not establish a third weapon. Vorel carries the borrowed greatsword **Shatter**. At a hidden hangar, an airship waits to carry them toward the price of going home.

## V. Four leap, three arrive

Before **Traveler's Twin** departs, the Mind Master appears with Crimson Moon escorts and a gift for Vorel. Nash identifies an **Amulet of Health**, which Stanley wears. The halfling's involvement in Tobacho's arrangements is now explicit; the earlier assassination order remains unproved.

The airship passes from Sigil into the open sky of the **Outlands**. Through a telescope, Stanley sees White Tower and the army surrounding it. Then winged attackers and wyverns strike the ship. Gnome crew members die. Nash becomes a black dragon, catches Hope after she falls overboard, and carries her as she fires into the battle. Vorel kills a wyvern below decks, but the defenders cannot keep their damaged vessel aloft.

With a sending stone as their only promise of later extraction, the four jump. Stanley casts Dimension Door with Hope; Nash casts with Vorel. **Only three arrive inside the tower**. Its ward rejects Hope, throwing her outside into the besiegers' hands.

**General Carthax** threatens to kill her, but **Ulvert**, who recognizes her from the UBI facility, sees an opportunity. The demons and devils have temporarily allied to seize the Fist. They offer to make Hope good enough to enter, then demand the relic. She negotiates for passage to Earth with her friends, at the right time and without tricks. A mouth-faced creature performs the magic; the GM declares her **lawful good**, an effect she must sustain through her conduct.

Inside, Stanley's claim that reinforcements have arrived wins a brief welcome from the paladins and their acolyte **Walnut**. Hope rejoins the others and admits what happened. Suspicion becomes violence when Vorel refuses to surrender Shatter; Stanley heals a wounded knight and stops the confrontation. All four finally submit to the defenders' judgment.

## VI. A different bargain in White Tower

The **Knight Commander** sentences them to death for their admitted scheme. Stanley wins a reprieve by proposing a different destination for the relic: **Mount Celestia**, where its defenders already hoped to take it. The commander insists that her own people accompany any transfer. The four have escaped immediate execution by jeopardizing Tobacho's bargain.

The commander says the Fist contains a hand capable of tearing planes apart, and recounts Zorander's disastrous pursuit of divine approval. Her history is testimony; the chest stays closed. Stanley admits stealing its key, which she inspects and returns so it remains separate. A proposed golden blessing-totem is another object, distinct from the Fist. Meanwhile, word from the captain confirms that the airship survived its bad landing but needs repairs.

Vorel resents Stanley's decision to commit them to the siege. Nash says he trusts Hope and wants his family. Hope hears a private voice demanding the Fist. Before they can agree what to do, sickness overwhelms the tower. Stanley, Nash and Vorel cannot recover through rest; ordinary restorative magic does not cure them. Hope alone remains well. The commander, now known as **Alentris**, falls ill too and accuses Hope of bringing the plague. Stanley persuades her to leave Hope in his custody while they seek a cure.

The aged cleric **Crudmund Hordweld** remembers **Incubatrix**, a sickness supposedly killed and reanimated as a curse centuries earlier. His journal lies below, in a library choked by black clouds. The companions witness a dying man dissolve into another cloud. Hope can enter the darkness unharmed, where the private voice urges her to take the relic and abandon everyone else.

She and Stanley find Hordweld's journal. A remedy labeled **“shale disease”** prescribes cradling the patient, a Celestial incantation and broth. Rocking alone fails. Walnut writes the words phonetically; Vorel and **Gregor** make chicken broth. Together they treat Alentris, then Stanley, Vorel and Nash. Black clouds leave the patients and vanish. The group and the recovering commander carry the cure through the tower until the GM confirms its surviving residents healed. Earlier deaths remain losses, and the successful treatment does not resolve the conflicting disease names or identify its sender.

Stanley and Alentris become lovers. Hope refuses the voice's demand to let her friends die and finally tells them what she has been hearing. The defenders kneel in thanks, and Stanley apologizes to Vorel for changing their mission without consent. Saving the tower has brought the companions closer to its people, without securing their road home.

## VII. The rescue that becomes a battle

The party searches the restricted **Monstrous Compendium** without identifying Hope's unseen adversary. Walnut's stories about the Fist and planar powers remain hearsay. Alentris formally withdraws the death sentence. A replacement airship is reported, and celestial riders are expected to collect the relic; Stanley persuades the commander to meet them on the roof rather than fight through the gate.

The rendezvous becomes a battle. Armed with new weapons and Hope's flying broom, the companions reach the rooftop while **Alentris carries the still-closed floating chest**. Nash takes dragon form, but an attempt to Enlarge Vorel breaks his concentration on Banishment. The returning enemy occupies his space and becomes horribly joined to his body. His unstable magic spreads further chaos through the fighting.

Celestial riders arrive and briefly take custody of the chest, but an enemy's Mass Suggestion disrupts the rescue. Vorel attacks a rider under its influence; Stanley saves her fall. Alentris recovers the dropped chest, then loses her mount to an archer and falls outside the tower herself. Another magical surge changes day to night and places an immense **moon with a visible city** above the battlefield. The GM identifies it as a random-table result, not a planned revelation.

Stanley reaches Alentris and saves her, returning her and the chest to the roof. Nash loses his dragon form, freeing him from the headless demon's remains. Three more airships appear, bringing attackers who demand the relic. A **four-armed minotaur** strikes Alentris unconscious and takes the chest over the parapet.

What follows is a struggle through falling bodies, reversed gravity, teleportation and pursuit. Vorel unmasks a hostile caster, whom **Hope recognizes as Spatulo**; the others do not automatically share that recognition. Nash and Hope reach ground level, while a Fire Storm drops Stanley and Alentris unconscious. Vorel seizes the key from Stanley's belongings, opens the drifting chest and takes the gauntlet. **A severed hand lies inside it. He does not put it on.** Hope privately hears a summons, and Vorel hears dark whispers. Neither has yet demonstrated submission to the relic's power.

## VIII. The hand and the road apart

Stanley revives through a miraculous death save, heals Alentris and gets her inside. Outside, Vorel is compelled to drop the exposed Fist. Hope's broom retrieves it, but the tower's ward refuses the loose relic; only its closed enchanted case can pass. Hope catches it and hears an instruction that the others cannot hear.

She cuts off her right hand and joins the **severed Hand of the Voice** to her arm. A shockwave scatters the combatants and her wounds disappear. In still time, a hooded presence names her his champion. Asked what she desires most, Hope answers **godhood**. The figure also restores the **Eye of Groxl** in Vorel's chest.

The new Hand changes Hope's strength, appearance and powers. The separate **Fist gauntlet** falls away, and Vorel returns it to the opened chest. Hope kills a demon with **Finger of Death** and raises it as an undead mount. The army recoils. Her companions have survived, but the power that saved them has made their reunion something darker.

The Voice calls Hope away, warning that companions she leaves behind may be absent for a long time. **She chooses to go alone** and tells Vorel to return to the tower. On the commanders' hill, Carthax and Ulvert acknowledge their failure to capture the Hand. **Orcus orders the demons to withdraw**, ending White Tower's siege and dissolving this temporary coalition, while the wider Blood War continues.

Ulvert offers an invitation from **Asmodeus to the Kaiser Casino**. Spatulo warns that Tobacho's claim still matters, and Hope permits him to accompany her. The Voice passes through a stone arch opening onto a path through fire. Hope follows; Spatulo crosses behind her. The portal closes.

**Nash, Stanley and Vorel remain at White Tower. Hope has left them.** The promised return to Earth has not occurred. This departure, completed at approximately **01:57–01:59 in E101**, closes Arc Four. The casino journey later in that same recording opens [Arc Five — No Place Like Home](ARC_FIVE.md).
