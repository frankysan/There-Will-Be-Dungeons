# The Wastes — Season Two: A Vessel for Hope

## Four days in the wreckage

After the destruction of Elhandar, the four companions linger at Stanley's family villa amid the debris of the space station. Three days pass while he waits for his father's enchanted mask and cottage to complete their bond. Hope gathers provisions, Vorel buries those who did not survive, Nash eagerly pesters Stanley, and Stanley retreats into grief. The old fellowship is close to dissolution. Their loved ones, meanwhile, have been evacuated toward the elven haven on Mars.

An unexpected visitor crosses the dunes: Monsoon Cabbage, a vampire from their troubled past. He carries Softhead in a cage as a living source of blood. Monsoon says he once journeyed with Stanley's father, a different Nash, Groxl and the Principle, and claims to know how the Principle may have become something even more dangerous. His history cannot be taken at face value. But he offers a concrete service. With the companions' wary consent, he extracts Stanley's remaining Soulbroker curse into a hostile creature. They battle it inside the cottage, and Hope strikes it down. The game's ruling is decisive: **Stanley is free of the Weir obligation and retains the bargain's benefits.** The broader Soulbroker and Principle threats remain.

At last, the cottage awakens. Wooden spars and sails rise out of the sand. Ghostly elves man a ship into which the old home has been woven, and a peg-legged stranger welcomes Stanley as his nephew.

## The ship that remembers

The stranger introduces himself as **Sever**, Stanley's uncle and first mate. The ghost crew, magically bound to the ship and unable to touch the living, shows the companions its machinery, cabins and controls. Far beneath the engine, an unusual chamber holds a carved face shaped like Stanley's father. A voice from the wood declares itself to be Delit Ornath: he says that he gave up his mortal body and infused his life into the ship. He also tells Stanley that his mother died. The voice is real to the experience of the characters; the complete history of that sacrifice is still a story they have heard, not one they have witnessed.

Freedom changes the party's decision. Stanley no longer insists that anyone follow him, but argues that the harm they failed to prevent would likely have been worse without them. Hope chooses to pursue whatever purpose keeps drawing her back. Nash longs for his family. Vorel cannot dismiss the fate of Razak Val. Rather than part, they agree to call on Vorel's people before traveling to Mars.

## Earth beneath the sails

The ship makes its **first actual flight**, soaring over the damaged lands. Tietkalla can still be seen in the distance, its surviving walls scarred and its streets apparently quiet, but the group does not stop to determine who remains. They land instead in the canyon of Razak Val.

Against Vorel's darkest expectations, the settlement lives. Survivors emerge, kneel, and recognize him as Great Fang. Some tell how the invading worms and attackers fell dead during the fighting; neither they nor the returning heroes can yet provide a comprehensive account of what happened. Vorel's reunion offers hope, though not a restoration of every house or fallen comrade. When he prepares to leave, followers ask why their leader must depart without them. A substantial group takes up their weapons and boards the ship, determined to accompany him beyond Earth.

The strange vessel ascends into the void. An enchanted shield holds the deadly vacuum outside, while its ghost crew guides the mortals and nervous lizardfolk across interplanetary distances in roughly an hour. Mars appears as a brown world with a concentrated patch of green. Within that engineered jungle haven, the ship lands. **Stanley formally gives it the name Fate Six**, now a real christening rather than a tentative joke about their previous vehicles.

## A home on Mars, a missing engineer

Nash finds Angelashi and their four children alive in the colony and embraces them. Their reunion is genuine, but the safety of their new refuge is uncertain. Angelashi fears that the gathering war could follow them even here. Nash speaks privately of another world, Planet Nash, as a possible place to live. Neither family nor vessel is sent there.

The Elhandari leader welcomes Vorel's lizardfolk as possible allies and directs the companions to an eccentric physician, **Dunglestar Ravensbread**, who once loved the missing engineer **Tungsten Wheel**. Dunglestar's Theatre of Mortal Bodies is filled with unnerving anatomical displays. His grief at Tungsten's absence gives the group access to the engineer's workshop, where they encounter something their travels have until now only promised: **a partly constructed Rubistic Triangulosphere**.

It is enormous, intricate and still. Its missing rare ingredient, known in the current transcripts as *Gluptwarp* or similar, is needed to prevent disastrous overheating. A message apparently sent by Tungsten points toward **Blast Belly Rock** and a possible source of the material at **Pentel Weiss**. The travelers notice symbols resembling the ship's navigation keys. The clues offer directions, not certainty. They do not find Tungsten, start the machine or prove how it could save Earth.

Before departing the theatre, Stanley and Vorel persuade the young elf assistant **Twaddle** to seek a place aboard Fate Six helping its intangible ghost surgeon. He writes a farewell note and accompanies them as far as the elven council.

The companions enter the shade of Mars's great elder tree, where an assembly gathers around a map of Earth to plan the war against the Weir. **The council is called to order, but its deliberations are left for the next episode.** The four have recovered a purpose, a ship, allies and the beginnings of a device—not yet a victory.
