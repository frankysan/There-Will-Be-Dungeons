# Arc 11 — The Last War

## I. The final evening

Beneath Mars's great tree, Stanley, Hope and Nash submitted to the Elhandari augmentation cylinder and its eight-hour operation. **Vorel refused**. He would not measure his courage by the elves' machinery and went instead to his lizardfolk people, leaving the fourth pod empty.[^arc11-1]

Some seventy-five lizardfolk lived in earthen homes outside the elven settlement. Vorel proclaimed the Courtship of the Fang, challenging prospective partners to a test of strength. The rite continued until he was nearly spent. The count varied in the telling; no marriage or child was confirmed. His choice would have consequences beyond the gathering.[^arc11-2]

The surgery succeeded. Stanley received enchanted rapier and leather armor, Hope a rapier, shield and half plate, and Nash a staff and Cloak of Protection. They collected a sword and shield for Vorel without a witnessed handover. His refusal stood, and neither Nash's dragon-metal heart nor Vorel's Eye of Groxl had been removed.[^arc11-3]

That evening Stanley addressed his people about the worth of moments of joy before an uncertain tomorrow. Music and wine followed. Hope, grieving those she had lost, chose the friends and future still before her. Union Wheel found Vorel at the feast, took his hand and taught him a simple dance while he ate.[^arc11-4]

Nash returned to Angelashi and their four children. She asked where they might live if he survived; he imagined Earth, Mars or a quieter world. When he spoke as if his return from death proved immortality, she challenged him. Sprout still murmured from Nash's shoulder, and Angelashi asked for privacy.[^arc11-5]

Monsoon Cabbage came to Hope in the armor of a vampire knight. If they won, he said, he intended to leave their world rather than live forever among people his hunger endangered. It was an intention, not a departure already made. Hope returned to the celebration.[^arc11-6]

When Union heard of Vorel's Courtship, she left the dancing visibly hurt. Their bond was wounded, but no final separation was spoken. The revellers interrupted Nash's family evening for a commemorative photograph. By morning Stanley and Hope were hungover, Vorel had been allowed restorative rest, and Union had returned to duty, still watchful.[^arc11-7]

Stanley cast ***Heroes' Feast***, consuming one jeweled bowl to summon breakfast for the four friends and Union. They ate. Their illness lifted, and temporary protection against fear, poison and injury strengthened them for the coming fight. The feast did **not** erase Nash's engineered heart or Sprout. The rejuvenation syringes remained unused.[^arc11-8]

The Council of Earthly Defenders assembled beneath the tree. Scout Nobieri described the Solar Mines: Workerberg, the City of the Chosen, fire-driven Slow Peters, armies of the Toyota Host, and four anti-aircraft guns guarding the lava-ringed central mine. Their numbers and vulnerabilities were scout estimates, not certainties.[^arc11-9]

Tungsten and Dunglestar unveiled the completed Rubistic Triangulosphere. Its hovering frame spun into an impossible shape, briefly softening Hope's horns, before they switched it off. Tungsten believed placing the running machine in Earth's lava could undo the damage to magic and seal demonic paths. **Only its local operation on Mars was demonstrated**; Earth's restoration remained untested.[^arc11-10]

The council chose a northern-gate diversion while four groups silenced the anti-air guns: Diontalus northwest, Monsoon northeast, Vorel's lizardfolk southeast and the alternate Nashes southwest. Once the guns fell, Fate Six would carry the heroes and machine to the mine. Other allies would hold the perimeter; freeing enslaved workers remained an aspiration.[^arc11-11]

Diontalus appeared at the council, eager to fight. Officers distributed radio headsets so the scattered forces could report their progress, though enemies might listen. Markers moved across the battle map until the plan was agreed. Bertrand Mould warned that battle could undo even careful strategies.[^arc11-12]

The companions had shared one more evening with those they loved. Their allies were gathered, the machine completed, and the plan agreed. **But Fate Six had not left Mars. No cannon had fallen, and no one had entered the Solar Mines.** Earth still awaited them.[^arc11-13]

## II. Fire at the northern gate

On Mars, the generals argued over the enormous cannons and the metal giants guarding the Solar Mines. A distant dust cloud might be the goblins Bokbok had promised, but no one yet knew. Stanley asked Union what she wanted beyond her oath. She answered that she would stand beside him as guardian of the Elhandari, even after all it had cost her. He could not persuade her to rest or choose a different duty.[^arc11-14]

Vorel gathered the lizardfolk, perhaps seventy-five warriors from scattered homelands, and sent them toward the southeastern anti-air gun. He promised that, **if they won**, they might claim a home in the Solar Mines valley, but forbade them to harm the workers of Workerberg. Nash said goodbye once more to Angelashi and their four children. Hope studied the completed Triangulosphere and passed its controller to Stanley. None had yet reached Earth.[^arc11-15]

The fleet finally lifted from Mars: fifteen Elhandari dropships and the ghost-crewed Fate Six, the companions together aboard her. Ninety-nine alternate Nashes crowded the ship, bewildered by orders for their southwestern objective. Vorel received and handled the enchanted longsword his friends had collected for him. Belowdecks, Stanley confronted the carved face in which Delit Or'Nath, his father, spoke. Delit said suffering had been necessary and free will an illusion. Stanley could not forgive him. Their quarrel remained unresolved as Earth appeared ahead.[^arc11-16]

After the long crossing, the coalition landed in the desert. One alternate Nash became a gigantic hamster and carried the other ninety-eight toward the southwestern gun. Diontalus, Monsoon and Vorel's fighters departed for their separate targets; none had reported a gun disabled. The Nameless One supplied a battered war car: Hope drove, Stanley sat beside her, Vorel manned its roof gun and Nash chained himself behind, floating on the strange power fused to his body.[^arc11-17]

The dust ahead resolved into buses and trucks overflowing with goblins. Upon a moving throne sat Bokbok, crowned and loudly calling himself king. He greeted Vorel, and the ragged goblin convoy joined the Liberators. They reached the towering closed northern gate just as two molten Slow Peters loosed streams of fire. Vehicles burst apart. Hope swung her car clear, taking the fighters between the giants while her allies struggled through the wreckage.[^arc11-18]

Stanley invoked the Mask of Eltanfawr, accepting a cost to his vitality to glimpse paths through the battle. Vorel roared into rage, his aura shielding his friends against flame, and opened fire with the Gatling gun. Nash brought down ***Ice Storm***. The cold proved especially destructive to the molten machines; what Nash had guessed in council was now painfully real. The giants answered with blinding light and crushing blows. Stanley lost his sight, yet trusted the mask and blasted one sentry until it melted.[^arc11-19]

The remaining giant burned through the rear of Hope's car. Vorel survived his leap into the sand; Nash drifted from the ruined towline. Vorel caught the dangling chain and whirled his friend toward the enemy as if he were a living weapon. Hope steered the surviving half-car into cover. Through the radio came uncertain news of scattered allies, but no anti-air gun was confirmed destroyed. Nash's third Ice Storm broke the second giant. Both sentries were gone, but the gate remained shut.[^arc11-20]

Goblins swarmed the cliffs, fighting the Toyota Host above the wall. Burned vehicles lay across the desert. Then a huge green-armored Paladin of the Umbilical Order descended with eight Kuromokishi warriors. The Paladin commanded submission and proclaimed that lizardfolk belonged in servitude. **Vorel refused**. The heroes had won only the first exchange of a war, and another enemy stood between them and the gate.[^arc11-21]

Stanley changed himself into a giant ape and hurled the ruined half-car onto a samurai. Hope cut into the attackers with rapier and Diplomacy, while Nash filled the sand beneath the orcs with ***Stinking Cloud***. Their feast protected him from its poison. Vorel seized the Paladin's beam-firing hands and forced them apart, making the enemy's own light strike its followers. The Nameless One and the Jeweler drove their vehicles through the melee, but the relentless Kuromokishi clung to wreckage and fought on.[^arc11-22]

One samurai crawled away after tearing his own torso free of Stanley's thrown car. The ape finally killed him by hurling the abandoned lower half after him. The Paladin unleashed diseased rats at Vorel's face; his furious heat burned them away. Then he bit the giant suit's visor free. Beneath the metal was flesh pressed unnaturally against the armor. The Paladin called him a slave, but Vorel had already torn the face from the doctrine's armored spokesman.[^arc11-23]

The radios brought fractured reports. **Diontalus claimed to have destroyed** the northwestern anti-air cannon; Monsoon was still engaged elsewhere. Vorel's lizardfolk had found a button near their assigned gun and were pressing it, without proving the weapon disabled. White-robed Nash warned that another of his people was working a great spell. The sky blackened. Meteors fell across the valley, scattering goblins and striking the wall where the Nameless One's vehicle exploded. Whether he could be recovered was not shown.[^arc11-24]

Hope fought her way through samurai, but a mistaken command sent Diplomacy spinning beyond its reach. Nash struck down one sickened foe with a poisonous Chromatic Orb and unfurled dragon wings to rise above the cloud; his later orbs flew wide. The Paladin detached its hands and blasted forward on a rocket pack, carrying Vorel toward a waiting blade. Vorel tore at its exposed wiring, unable to halt the thrust alone.[^arc11-25]

Hope rushed after them. She drove her rapier into a weakness in the armor, caught Vorel by the calf and dug her feet into the ground. The engines strained, but she would not let him meet the samurai's sword. Detached metal hands clawed at her legs. The Paladin's gun wounded her terribly, yet she held on. Vorel drew the enchanted longsword he had carried from Mars and drove it through the creature's damaged machinery. **The Paladin died**, and the rocket went still.[^arc11-26]

Stanley, still in ape form, leapt onto the last samurai and crushed him. **The immediate fight at the north gate was won.** A voice on the radio promised both good and bad news. The companions asked for the bad. Silence followed. The gate was not visibly open. Other guns remained uncertain, the alternate Nashes' fate was unknown, and the Triangulosphere was still aboard Fate Six, far from Earth's heart.[^arc11-27]

## III. The ship and the giant

The withheld radio report came at last. White Nash and Unfortunate Nash said the southwestern force had been overwhelmed. Unfortunate Nash had discovered ***Meteor Swarm*** among his own spells and called it down on the cannon—along with, by their reckoning, ninety-seven fellow Nashes. They had not counted every body. Their gun was reported disabled. Grief and anger swept the original companions, but Vorel raised his enchanted longsword and named it Chrome's Bane.[^arc11-28]

Command now reported all four anti-air guns disabled, a success the companions knew through voices rather than their own eyes. At the gate Bokbok launched himself over the wall in an explosive goblin contraption. Stanley, still a gigantic ape, hurled the fallen Paladin's beeping armor into the barrier. It detonated, breaking it open further. Liberators, giants and goblins rushed through; the northern gate was breached, though the defenders were far from beaten.[^arc11-29]

Hope summoned Fate Six. The four climbed the rope from her ghost crew, and Stanley became an elf again as he boarded. During the flight they recovered what strength they could. As Selle Prime, Stanley sent nine Elhandari vessels to reinforce Vorel's beleaguered lizardfolk, three toward Diontalus and three toward Monsoon. The coalition spread through the Solar Mines valley while Fate Six bore the completed Rubistic Triangulosphere toward the central shaft.[^arc11-30]

A cyclone of lava rose from the heart of the mine. Through it flew an enormous dragon patched with chrome, hauling a colossal egg from the earth. It placed the egg beside the central approach and blasted a passing Elhandari ship with steam. Another vessel fell. Vorel demanded to close with the monster; Stanley ordered the crew to avoid its deadly breath. The promised route to the world's wound was becoming a sky full of metal and fire.[^arc11-31]

Eight fresh Paladins came flying on rockets. Stanley tried to evade them by changing Fate Six back into Villa Or'Nath, a cottage falling through open sky. Its ghost crew vanished for the instant of transformation, leaving Vorel outside the walls and his companions tumbling indoors. The attackers merely followed. Stanley restored the vessel to a ship and brought Vorel aboard unharmed. The Paladins landed or hovered beside the deck, and battle began.[^arc11-32]

Nash became an adult black dragon as Hope fought the boarders with rapier and Diplomacy, again flying under her command. Stanley changed one Paladin into a falling rabbit and sent destructive magic through the armored attackers. Vorel climbed onto Nash's back. The ghost sailors fought as long as they could, though beams and rockets dispersed several of them. The chrome dragon swept toward the bow, and Nash met it with a line of burning acid.[^arc11-33]

The dragons grappled in the air. Stanley’s Heroes' Feast kept the companions safe from the terror of the enemy's presence. Nash enlarged Vorel, who leapt to the chrome dragon and clung to a horn; its wing beat flung him free. Stanley caught his fall with magic. Steam washed over Fate Six, burning her decks and scattering more ghosts. Hope seized an arcane ballista and shot into the creature's plated body while Stanley kept his allies on their feet.[^arc11-34]

At last Vorel drifted close enough to strike with Chrome's Bane. His blade cut into the chrome dragon's body and throat. The enormous beast convulsed and fell toward the lava. For an instant the friends thought the way ahead might be clear. Then a hand larger than the ship closed around Fate Six. Wood, deck and rigging splintered; the ghost crew faded as the vessel was **crushed and destroyed**.[^arc11-35]

The egg opened. From it rose a mountain-sized lizardfolk with a building bolted to his back and cables feeding weapons in his arms and brow. He called himself Groxl, boasting of gods slain. The Eye embedded in Vorel recognized the figure tied to its ancient legend. Whatever the truth of the giant's triumphs, his body and the destruction around him were real. He struck Elhandari ships aside and strode through molten rock.[^arc11-36]

The friends tumbled into empty air. Nash, still a dragon, caught the falling Triangulosphere before it vanished with Fate Six and carried the heavy machine toward solid ground. Stanley slowed the others' descent. Vorel chose to land near the giant's feet, sword ready. The northern wall had fallen, but the very building that should have led underground was fastened to Groxl's back. The machine was saved, **not installed or activated on Earth**.[^arc11-37]

Hope called up to the giant and challenged him to turn against the Principal. Groxl answered that he would settle with the Principal later; first he would destroy her and her friends. His enormous weapon rose. The four stood scattered among lava and wreckage, their ship gone and the Triangulosphere still in their care. **The fight against the giant had not yet begun.**[^arc11-38]

## IV. Inside Groxl

The giant towered above the Solar Mines, weapons and a whole building fastened to his back. His chain blade missed Hope but scattered lava through nearby allied troops. Stanley's Hex failed against the monster's will, though his blasts chipped its scales. With the true mine entrance now lifted into the giant's body, reaching the machine on his back seemed the only way forward. Vorel raged below, his Eye burning with hatred.[^arc11-39]

The enlargement Nash had granted Vorel faded. Nash himself had almost exhausted the power of his black-dragon form; he became himself briefly and transformed into a fresh dragon. Nearby White Nash and Unfortunate Nash tried to bury the giant's toe with earth magic. Groxl's rockets scattered soldiers, and his roar panicked the survivors. The companions and Union, protected by Stanley's earlier feast, held against the fear.[^arc11-40]

Vorel caught Hope and threw her toward the giant's face. Nash met her in the air. When Vorel asked the Eye of Groxl for a weakness, it filled him with hatred and insisted that this thing was an abomination kept alive by a human calculator. It demanded the machinery be removed—even as the Eye's earlier recognition had seemed to identify the creature with ancient Groxl. The contradiction remained unexplained.[^arc11-41]

Nash caught Hope on his back, struck the giant with acid and narrowly flew them clear of snapping jaws. Union fell into the lava while trying to reach Stanley; he drew her out with Telekinesis. He begged her to live as more than his guardian, but her oath still held her. He threw her a headset and stepped alone through a Dimension Door onto the giant's back, where Hope had spotted the machines controlling its immense weapons.[^arc11-42]

Nash tore at the walls of the building embedded in the giant's spine. Pipes, glowing screens and terrified robed figures spilled into view; some fell to the lava below. Hope traced the conduits to a generator that fed the giant's weapons and warned that breaking it might cause an explosion. Nash wrenched it partly loose, leaking dark fluid. Stanley joined with Telekinesis until the machine tore free. This was Groxl's generator, not the world-restoring Triangulosphere. Amid the fighting, the rescued machine's whereabouts remained unclear.[^arc11-43]

Diontalus appeared in the air as a blue spectral figure. He recognized his old foe and cried that the giant should have been dead. Groxl's laser struck the archfey's manifestation, burning it away until **Diontalus visibly disintegrated**. Stanley felt a terrible inner rupture. Whatever such destruction meant for an immortal being's ultimate fate, no surviving body appeared before them. The friends carried the extracted generator away and dropped it into the lava.[^arc11-44]

The monster's chain blade stopped, its guns failed, and the whole structure slid from its back. Yet Groxl remained standing, enormous and alive. Union and Emenil, trying to escape, fell beneath his crushing fist. Their bodies were visibly broken; no one revived them. Stanley screamed and cast Disintegrate into the giant's side, carving away flesh and bone without killing him. Their attempt to save Earth had become a battle they might not survive themselves.[^arc11-45]

Vorel hacked at a toe with Chrome's Bane until Groxl plucked him free and swallowed him. Inside the immense throat, Vorel used his sword to hold himself against the rushing blood, following the heavy rhythm of a heart. Above him, Monsoon gathered White Nash and Unfortunate Nash and carried them out of danger. Hope drew Groxl's attention toward the generator glowing in the lava while Nash remained airborne with her.[^arc11-46]

Deep inside the creature, Vorel found thick **metal cables woven through its heart**. His first strikes shocked him with devastating lightning, but he lived and clung to the pulsing flesh. Nash blinded the giant with an undignified, physical assault of his dragon body rather than magic. Groxl stumbled after Hope's calls and stepped upon the discarded generator. The resulting explosion lit the valley like a new sun, stripping Nash of his dragon form and leaving Stanley unconscious. From inside the giant, Vorel was spared the blast.[^arc11-47]

Even then Groxl stood. Hearing the Eye's command to strike again, Vorel proclaimed himself “Savior of the Universe” and slashed the heart's cables with Chrome's Bane. Metal broke. The monstrous heartbeat stopped. The giant collapsed across the valley, roaring that this would not be the last the world saw of him. His immense body lay defeated. Whether anything of Groxl endured beyond it, none of them knew.[^arc11-48]

Blood hurled Vorel from the falling body. Stanley lay unconscious, while Hope and the now-humanoid Nash were in peril above the battlefield. Union and Emenil remained among the fallen. Fate Six was gone, and the Rubistic Triangulosphere had still not been installed beneath the Solar Mines. The giant had fallen, but the companions' survival remained uncertain amid the wreckage.[^arc11-49]

## V. The dome and the fallen

Vorel opened his eyes beneath the blue sky of the Endless Dunes. The Eye was absent from his chest. Another lizardfolk waited, calling himself Groxl and greeting Vorel as Slayer of the God Killer. It might have been an afterlife, a dream or something stranger; Vorel could not know. He spoke of the man in yellow who had taken him from his clan, taught him the common tongue and left him to sell meat in Dusthill.[^arc11-50]

Groxl told a sweeping history of being grown in a vat, of uniting the lizardfolk as the first Great Fang, of infertility, the Principal, bargains and wars against beings he called gods. He claimed his own stolen eye had been made into the artifact Vorel wore. No witness outside that strange encounter authenticated the account. Groxl urged Vorel to inherit his hatred. **Vorel refused.** He wanted his people to live in the Solar Mines in peace, with a future beyond revenge.[^arc11-51]

The smell of Bokbok broke the vision. Vorel woke on the real battlefield, the Eye back in his chest and Chrome's Bane still in his hand. Monsoon Cabbage had rescued the four companions from the fall, and a ring of allied wagons guarded their unconscious bodies. White Nash healed the survivors, who were alive but badly hurt. Stanley's grief for Union and Diontalus remained. Admiral Chrysorius showed them the completed Triangulosphere, safe behind the wagons but still without a path into Earth's deepest lava.[^arc11-52]

Nash remembered a privileged settlement under glass, where some children from the Solar Mines had been given clean clothes and comfort. He and White Nash spoke of a childhood bully named Dick Pendleton, and a book once stolen to humiliate him. Monsoon had seen the dome from above. The companions chose infiltration instead of another battle in the open valley. They took White Nash, Unfortunate Nash, Bertrand the Jeweler, five goblins and two Elhandari; the other commanders stayed to defend the battered coalition. Chrysorius quietly entrusted Vorel with an empathy grenade to use with care.[^arc11-53]

An Elhandari vessel carried them over the dome. Bertrand jumped first; the others formed a circle and descended with parachutes. Two Nashes failed to open theirs, and **Stanley's Feather Fall** saved them. On the quiet suburban road stood pickup trucks and houses unlike anything in the Wastes. Children had working local phones and internet. Vorel demanded a library; a suspicious resident instead pointed them toward Walmart after Stanley cleaned his car with magic. The Nashes remembered their stolen book at last: ***Moby Dick***.[^arc11-54]

The store was shuttered under a warning to remain indoors. Vorel bent its doors, and Stanley **shattered** the lock. Within, vegetable peelings lit the dark aisles while the companions listened to hidden breathing. Stanley called for the occupants to emerge. A man with a human torso fixed to tank treads rolled into view, followed by Toyota Host soldiers and Kuromokishi. He removed his mask: Dick Pendleton, Nash's old school tormentor, now styling himself General Motors. His claim that the Principal favored Nash was still a bitter enemy's account, not proof of parentage.[^arc11-55]

The store erupted in violence. Stanley sent a destructive wave through the attackers. Hope ordered the Elhandari to throw empathy grenades, and General Motors began to weep over the cruelty of his childhood even as he faced the friends. Nash launched a real Fireball, killing still more troops. Vorel fought among clothing racks while goblins scattered beneath gunfire. Then a shot came from behind the heroes: Bertrand had shot White Nash in the head. He turned on Unfortunate Nash, but his pistol jammed.[^arc11-56]

Unfortunate Nash responded with another Meteor Swarm, this time inside Walmart. Four blazing stones tore through the roof. **Bertrand died** in the destruction, and Stanley was caught at the edge of the blast. White Nash did not rise. Hope sent Diplomacy against another enemy, but it spun beyond her control; she fought on with her rapier and killed General Motors, still crying under the grenade's effect. The remaining Toyota Host broke ranks and fled. This victory did not undo the betrayals.[^arc11-57]

Vorel seized a fleeing soldier alive. Hope demanded the route to the Principal and, when he refused, killed him. Stanley traced the retreat of the Kuromokishi to a rear stockroom beyond the grocery aisles. There they found a freight elevator. The four companions, Unfortunate Nash and two Elhandari stepped aboard and descended beneath the dome. They had found a path toward the enemy, **not yet met the Principal or restored Earth**.[^arc11-58]

## VI. Beneath the classroom

The freight elevator carried them beneath Walmart, into a cavern of cables and stale air. The others looked to Nash, who remembered the Solar Mines but could not promise the passages were unchanged. The Rubistic Triangulosphere was now with the descending party, floating within its magical protection. How the heavy machine had been transferred from the allied wagon circle to their company had not been shown; its presence was established, its final destination still distant.[^arc11-59]

Stanley went ahead and saw three fleeing Kuromokishi board a railcar. His Message brought the others after him. In an engineering chamber, Hope worked the switches until a second small car arrived on the parallel track. They climbed aboard and rattled deeper underground beside a hot pipe. Vorel tested the heat shining around the Eye in his chest; they considered how the Principal might wield the stolen Hand of the Voice, but none had disabled it.[^arc11-60]

At the end of the rail the friends rested briefly. Nash had been badly hurt in Walmart; Stanley healed Vorel with a powerful touch, and they declined to spend a rejuvenation syringe. A hotter passage led past rooms where laborers had once slept in cages. Now headless bodies lay in bloody heaps, scores in the first chambers and many more beyond. Nash remembered workers alive in those rooms. The massacre was recent, but its murderer and purpose were not witnessed.[^arc11-61]

A familiar corridor halted Nash. He asked to see his old classroom alone. Inside stood rows of restrained chairs, headsets and devices for forcing children's eyes toward screens. An old masked instructor recognized him and spoke of beatings, lessons and the Principal's alleged protection. Then he offered Nash his former seat. Nash sat willingly, drawn by the memory of the films he had once escaped into. Straps closed around him and the machine began to shock him.[^arc11-62]

The others heard the instructor singing. Stanley burst into the room and killed him with an Eldritch Blast. Hope tore at the cables and freed Nash. The boy's refuge had been turned into a trap once again. Nash retrieved a VHS tape of a familiar film, then led the group toward the sleeping cells where children had been locked away. His own door resisted Vorel's hands, and Nash's attempt to freeze its lock with Ray of Frost sent ice across his face instead. Vorel warmed him and smashed the damaged lock with Chrome's Bane.[^arc11-63]

Inside waited a dusty bed, a bucket and a grotesque relic Nash had hidden beneath his mattress. The friends tried to laugh, but the room itself remained terrible. In another chamber a bloodstained table stood beneath a carved dragon's face. Nash recognized the view from nightmares and wondered if his **metal heart** had been placed in him there. It was a memory and suspicion, not a surgical record confirming where the operation occurred.[^arc11-64]

They returned to the pipe, where lava flowed overhead through glass. Three Kuromokishi stood before a chamber, weapons lowered. Nash used Minor Illusion to project the Principal's voice behind them, ordering that Nash be let through. The guards bowed and said he was expected. Even so, Vorel and Stanley attacked, and Hope joined with her rapier and Diplomacy while Nash burned the last foe. The guards died, leaving the way open beneath the molten conduit.[^arc11-65]

A song rose from the walls. At first the shapes looked like stones; then the companions saw living heads embedded in earth, their mouths forced into a mournful choir. Nash recognized some former captors, and others he could barely remember. Among the faces Hope saw one she knew at once: her father. He recognized her, worried about her mother and spoke as though he might still return to the family. Hope touched his face, told him she loved them both, then drove her rapier through his forehead, ending the suffering of the head she could see.[^arc11-66]

At the corridor's end stood a handleless door framed by faces. It spoke as the Empty Chamber and asked who sought entry. Nash Maggard announced himself as the Principal's son and asked to pass. The voices said that the door authority must review his request. The door did not open. The friends stood close to their enemy, the Triangulosphere still in their keeping, but the chamber beyond the door remained sealed.[^arc11-67]

## VII. The enemy's accord

The face-lined door remembered Stanley Billings and spoke of an old acquaintance named Dorothy in a faraway black mine. It said Nash's old fines had been paid—by “your father,” according to its records—and slid open. Nash led the others through more singing heads, lighting a strip of vegetable peel along the way. Beyond lay a vast cavern with a lake of lava, a bridge and four robed figures chanting around a vertical beam of molten light. At a desk sat the Principal, waiting.[^arc11-68]

The chrome ruler called Nash his son and welcomed the others into view. He called the four ritualists Dragonsons, Nash's teachers, and claimed the beam was the Solar Mines' hottest point. The completed Triangulosphere floated within reach, but had not been dropped into the lava. The Principal said he had made Nash in a laboratory, engineered his metal heart and sent him away to grow strong. He told of fractured histories and repeated attempts to defeat the Weir, declaring that a universe converted to chrome was the only possible refuge. The heroes had no way to verify that history.[^arc11-69]

Hope suggested a terrible bargain: rather than fight the Principal immediately, let him join them against the Weir. Vorel opposed trusting a ruler who had enslaved and remade living beings; Stanley feared betraying the allies already killed in this war. But the Principal could not reach the Weir alone. In his palm he displayed Hope's Hollow Ring, seized with her stolen Hand. It could open a gate if charged. He would return it in exchange for a temporary truce.[^arc11-70]

They agreed that the surface forces should cease fighting, and that the four companions and the Principal would rest before confronting the Weir together. They would not contact their armies until the confrontation ended. Afterward their old quarrel could resume. The Principal warned that failure meant returning to his plan of chroming the universe; he hinted at gentler terms if they won, but only as a promise. **No signed soul pact was made**. The companions left the Triangulosphere suspended in its protective sphere inside the lava chamber, and Hope kept the controller.[^arc11-71]

In the rooms offered for their rest, Vorel tried to explain to Stanley what made life worth saving: meals, aging, love, pain, laughter, the earth beneath one's feet. He would rather risk death than survive as a piece of eternal metal. Stanley admitted the shame he felt at bargaining with their enemy. Their disagreement did not vanish, but the friends found a measure of understanding. An elderly robed sorcerer who called herself Nana Truffled brought chicken and warmth to Nash, speaking as someone who had known his childhood. Her affection was real in that room, though her account of raising him remained her claim.[^arc11-72]

The four completed a true long rest. In the morning Stanley used his last gem-encrusted bowl to cast a fresh ***Heroes' Feast***, and they ate. When they returned, the Principal placed the charged Hollow Ring in Hope's hand. She opened a gate to the courtyard of Château Weir; the four heroes and their temporary ally passed through. Stanley renewed the foresight of his mask at its cost, and Nash carried a parcel of chicken Nana had given him. Hope closed the gate, exhausting the ring's charge. Ruby stayed behind in the Solar Mines.[^arc11-73]

Château Weir stood beneath a strange sky. The companions entered an empty throne hall, where Quizquaz the Dream Drinker appeared from shadow and demanded that the three old contract signers—Hope, Nash and Stanley—answer for failing to kill the Principal. **Vorel had never signed**, and Quizquaz admitted he was free of that bargain. Stanley sat on the throne in defiance. Skeleton guards rose, but the Dream Drinker retreated to summon someone more dangerous.[^arc11-74]

The Soulbroker, Kitobal Prith, arrived in a robed goatlike form. He said a Worldbreaker seed was planted in Earth to turn the planet into a gate. Under Hope's questions he claimed that he could remove it but would not while the Principal obstructed his kind. The Principal countered with his own account of the Weir as corrupted ancient humans. Both men spoke of world history as if each alone possessed its truth. The companions watched two powers argue over Earth without either promising to leave its people free.[^arc11-75]

Stanley declared that both the Principal and the Soulbroker threatened their lives. Vorel called himself the Contractless and Defender of Earth, refusing a bargain that would turn his home into another being's weapon. Their appeal did not change either adversary. At the cry of “Lemon tree,” the Soulbroker rose, expanding into a cosmic goat-headed figure. Château Weir came apart in a storm of broken masonry and lightning. The companions and the Principal were caught inside it. The Soulbroker's towering shape filled the storm as the companions faced him.[^arc11-76]

## VIII. The Soulbroker and Hell

Lightning filled the broken sky above Château Weir. For the first time the companions truly fought the enormous Soulbroker. The Principal sheltered the five within a chrome dome, and Stanley turned his enchanted guitar into a lightning rod, sharing his magic's protection even with their temporary ally. The Principal wanted Nash to use the storm against their enemy. Nash, uncertain what that meant, pressed his hand to the charged wall. The pain was real; so were fifty-one new Nashes suddenly crowding the shelter.[^arc11-77]

The copies drew more lightning. The Principal released his dome and fired his ten red rings, but the Soulbroker caught and thrust their enormous power back at him. Vorel carried Hope and two copies toward the monster's face; Stanley stepped through a Dimension Door onto its arm. Another electrical storm filled the air with more duplicates until the count reached 3,280 Nashes, each newly made by the strange reaction of metal heart and Weir lightning. These were not the older alternate Nashes who had fought outside the Solar Mines.[^arc11-78]

Nash considered a blinding ***Sunburst***, then abandoned it to spare his friends and chose a devastating ***Fireball***. Thousands of his copies cast the same spell. Their flames engulfed the Soulbroker until his immense goatlike body **disintegrated**. Dying lightning multiplied the copies again, swelling their number to 95,120. No one could tell which would survive or find a way home.[^arc11-79]

The world of the Weir cracked like glass. Rifts pulled countless copies into unknown spaces. The Principal opened an escape of his own and dragged one Nash through, although no one could prove which of the indistinguishable men was the original. Diplomacy helped Hope and Vorel fight their way toward the opening; other Nashes took black-dragon shapes to lift their friends out of the storm. The Principal closed the gate before the vast crowd could follow. The heroes had escaped destruction, but the fate of most copies was unknown.[^arc11-80]

They woke in Hell, bound to stone slabs beneath a ceiling of scorched souls. Nash lay separately on a table. The Principal congratulated the one he called his son, then declared the weapon too dangerous to survive: he meant to kill Nash and reclaim the heart. Hope's struggles failed to release her. The Principal said the Weir's link to Earth had been severed, but the Worldbreaker seed still remained in their world. Nash's heart was still in his chest, and the threatened execution had not happened.[^arc11-81]

A smiling figure in a white dinner jacket entered with a demon attendant. It was Brett, also called Asmodeus, ruler of that part of Hell. He claimed to have loaned the Principal the dragon-metal heart and demanded payment, including the Hand and Eye of the Voice. The Principal produced Hope's stolen Hand and passed it to Brett, who gave it to his female attendant to keep. The Eye of the Voice was missing; it was not Vorel's Eye of Groxl. Brett considered taking Nash's life and another hero as payment.[^arc11-82]

Stanley challenged the bargain. It was the companions who had pursued the artifacts, he argued; the Principal had merely stolen their work. Brett listened. Vorel broke his stone restraints, and Stanley stepped through a Dimension Door to Nash's table. The Principal threatened Brett, but the ruler of Hell dismissed him with a snap of his fingers. **He vanished from the room; his death was not shown.** Another snap released Hope and transformed the chamber around them into a sumptuous casino, although Nash remained restrained for a time.[^arc11-83]

Brett offered Nash an ordinary living heart in return for the engineered dragon-metal one. There would be no more safe assumption of dragon transformations or extra lives. He also wanted the missing Eye of the Voice and the Principal's death; in exchange he suggested freedom and perhaps even a way to save Earth. These were offers, not accomplished restorations. **Vorel refused** to sign away the finite life he valued. Hope resisted another pact; Stanley asked for time to decide together. Brett freed Nash, handed them gambling chips and withdrew.[^arc11-84]

Four friends could walk through the casino, but they were still in Hell. Hope's Hollow Ring had no charge left after Château Weir; its cruel magic would require an evil act to recharge. They considered another bearer, the White Tower and a gate straight back to the Triangulosphere, but did none of those things. The Worldbreaker still waited beneath Earth. They had defeated the Soulbroker only to discover that even victory could leave them before another bargain. **No heart was removed, no contract signed and no escape gate opened.**[^arc11-85]

## IX. A mortal heart

In Brett's casino, the companions weighed the price of returning to Earth. Vorel examined Nash and wondered what remained of him without his engineered heart. Stanley remembered a private warning from the Elhandari cocoon: that Nash, once necessary to save creation, might also have to die. He did not tell the others. Nash worried about his family and what sort of heart Brett might put in his chest. For now, he still had the dragon-metal one.[^arc11-86]

The casino offered pleasures and traps. A clerk directed them toward a performance by Sinatra, and a note naming room 53C slipped into Stanley's hand. When the dead patrons realized the visitors were alive, their welcome became hungry. Stanley persuaded them to seek an invented room instead; the four never saw the performance. Back at the bar Vorel ordered tarrasque steak. Stanley's Dispel Magic turned the first serving into something revolting, while a replacement remained ordinary meat under the same test. The guests passed hours without committing the evil act needed to recharge Hope's Hollow Ring.[^arc11-87]

At last the bartender tired of their restraint and revealed himself as Brett. He had been watching them the whole time. He offered to send them home if Nash surrendered the metal heart; the replacement, Brett said, would be grown from Nash's own flesh. He tried once more to draw Hope and the others into service. **They refused another contract.** Nash was frightened, but he did not resist when Brett reached into his chest.[^arc11-88]

The god tore out the **dragon-metal heart**, veins and sinew trailing after it. Nash remained conscious through the terrible pain. Brett drew tissue together into a new living heart and closed Nash's wound. The mechanical heart was now in Brett's possession. With another snap, the four companions stood under the hot sky of Earth, alive and free of Hell, their old enemy still unaccounted for.[^arc11-89]

Nash tested what he could. ***Fire Bolt*** still sprang from his hand, and his strange levitation remained. He did **not** try changing into a dragon, so its fate was still uncertain. The scars and peculiarities of his body persisted. Stanley touched the Mask of Eltanfawr, calling for what Groxl had crushed. Against expectation, Fate Six reappeared above them and lowered a ladder.[^arc11-90]

Uncle Sever and Gorgeous George were aboard as ghostly sailors once more, but empty stations told of permanent-seeming losses. Sever mourned Crooked Hand Row, whom he said had truly vanished. Hope took the helm in the missing navigator's place. The ship flew toward the Solar Mines. As they approached, reports brought them farther north to land split by lava and purple corruption. The Worldbreaker was growing within Earth; the Soulbroker's defeat had not cured the planet.[^arc11-91]

At the Solar Mines, Elhandari forces held the surface, tending the wounded and guarding captured residents of the dome. Amanthalodar and Admiral Chrysorius greeted Stanley as Solely Prime, expecting victory. Stanley told them the truth: **the Triangulosphere had not been activated**, and the Principal was not defeated. Amanthalodar recoiled when he learned the friends had joined the Principal against the Soulbroker and gone into Hell. He withdrew to consider the breach of trust, without stripping Stanley of his rank.[^arc11-92]

Chrysorius saw another danger. If they used the Triangulosphere now, it might silence the very magic needed to fight the Worldbreaker. The great machine could wait while they learned how to defeat the creature beneath Earth. The friends considered seeking distant experts and libraries for answers. Instead Stanley ordered an Elhandari team to secure Ruby underground and watch the surviving sorcerers. Chrysorius accepted the mission and prepared to send his people below.[^arc11-93]

The four were back on Earth with a ship again, but not the ship or bodies they had once known. **Brett held Nash's old heart**; the Principal was missing; the Worldbreaker still tore open the land; and Ruby lay unactivated in the depths. Their next battle had become more urgent than the victory they had already won.[^arc11-94]

## X. The uneasy alliance

The Elhandari now guarded Ruby in the Solar Mines' inner chamber, still unactivated. Outside, the land split into fissures of red and purple fire. The companions knew that using the machine could quiet Earth's magic before they had defeated what grew beneath it. Hope took the restored Fate Six north to examine the damage. An object shot past the vessel and threw her and Nash overboard. Nash's peculiar floating saved him; Stanley steered down and cast **Feather Fall**, and Vorel caught Hope before the ground could claim her.[^arc11-95]

They found a narrow metal rocket in the sand. Its door opened upon a slim, younger Principal, his once-imposing chrome body diminished and its red rings absent. He built a little campfire and claimed Brett had destroyed his former body in Hell, leaving him to reform in this state. The new body stood before them, but the details of his escape were his story alone. Stanley contemplated capturing him rather than trusting another temporary bargain.[^arc11-96]

The Principal invited Nash to test whether he could still become a dragon. Nash tried. His body swelled into raw, half-formed flesh, without the scales or power of the black dragon; then the change failed and he shrank back. The attempt was real. The Principal called the barrier fear, but could not prove the cure. He asked about Nash's children and spoke as if he might meet them. **Nash refused to bring his family near him.**[^arc11-97]

Vorel returned to the campfire with terms of his own. The Principal could help defeat the Worldbreaker, but afterward he must answer for what he had done, perhaps in Pentel Vice. Vorel would watch him like a warden. The Principal accepted the provisional arrangement. It was parole, not forgiveness, acquittal or another magical pact. Their enemy had become a monitored ally because Earth might not survive without his knowledge.[^arc11-98]

He called the buried creature a tarrasque, a swift, armored destroyer from beyond their plane in this telling. If it could be made to fail its resistances, Nash's Banishment might return it home—but only after the spell held long enough. The companions weighed other spells, obstacles and traps. None had been cast on the monster yet. The Principal unpacked five hovering windboards from his rocket. Hope tested one over the lava, finding a way to move without trusting Fate Six to survive another giant.[^arc11-99]

They planned to lure the creature through the Leaden Fingers, where mountains and the Stormlands might serve as weapons. Stanley wanted another ***Heroes' Feast*** before the fight, but his last ceremonial bowl had been consumed. He and Hope flew off to practice and seek supplies; Stanley received an enchanted crossbow, but no new bowl was shown purchased. Nash, against every instinct, sat opposite the Principal and hummed as the older man tried to teach him his lost transformation. No scales appeared.[^arc11-100]

Vorel took Fate Six to the ridges, where he found narrow canyons and a violent storm beyond. He brought his lizardfolk warriors to the work, then sought King Bokbok in a roaring goblin celebration. Bokbok was alive and greeted him with affection. Together they loaded gunpowder and explosives onto the ship and chose three chokepoints for traps. The goblins placed the charges, and Bokbok gave Vorel a three-button detonator. The devices were ready, **not yet exploded**.[^arc11-101]

Far away, in the open lava field, gray plates of hardened shell began to rise from the molten earth. For the first time the Worldbreaker's physical emergence could be seen, but no complete creature or attack had yet appeared. The companions had a desperate plan, a dangerous provisional ally and three waiting traps. **The fight had not started.**[^arc11-102]

## XI. The hunt begins

Vorel returned to the Leaden Fingers and concentrated the mountain traps into one narrow canyon. Goblins buried explosives along the ridges while his lizardfolk readied grappling lines and shields. The trap was meant to slow the Worldbreaker long enough for the fighters to reach it, not to destroy it by fire alone. Far away Hope received a new enchanted crossbow, and Stanley obtained the jeweled bowl needed for another magical feast. Ruby remained underground with the Elhandari, waiting.[^arc11-103]

At the Principal's camp Nash tried again to understand the part of himself that had survived the loss of the metal heart. His shoulder's tiny eye and mouth stirred, frightened that he might cut them away. The Principal said they were part of Nash, though Nash could hear a voice of its own. Nash told the little creature he loved it. It wept and answered him with love. The Principal called that progress, but **no dragon scales appeared** and no transformation was restored.[^arc11-104]

The companions met again by the rocket. Stanley cast a fresh Heroes' Feast from the newly obtained bowl, and the four friends ate it. The Principal, who fed upon metal, could not share the mortal meal. Nash offered food to the mouth on his shoulder, which tasted it with delighted wonder. The companions spoke of life after the war: Hope's people, Nash's children, Vorel's hoped-for homeland and Stanley's displaced Elhandari. These were promises to themselves, not victories already won. They rested until dawn.[^arc11-105]

At sunrise the Principal flew above the lava on a windboard and blasted heat into it. The ground rolled. A shell rose, its plates turning like drills around a terrible mouth of rotating teeth: the Worldbreaker emerged completely, larger and faster than any ordinary beast. Hope, Stanley, Nash and the Principal moved on separate flying boards; Vorel remained with the ambush. The first arrows and spells cut at the creature's armor as the battle began.[^arc11-106]

Hope's new crossbow struck. Stanley's Eldritch Blasts ricocheted or vanished against the spinning shell. Nash found a weakness with a ***Fireball***, while Stanley's Hex and Mind Sliver further tested the monster's defenses. The creature's claws raked the Principal's weakened chrome, and its terrifying roar could not frighten the companions because of the feast. Even so, the giant mouth swept up Hope and the Principal. They were alive but trapped among grinding teeth.[^arc11-107]

The Principal caught Hope's hand and opened a portal to Sigil. They escaped the mouth into a sky above that crowded city, then opened another portal back to Earth. This was his magic, not a renewed charge in Hope's Hollow Ring. Outside, Nash's Chromatic Orb struck only armor, but Stanley's Disintegrate tore away part of the shell and exposed vulnerable purple flesh. The Worldbreaker turned toward Stanley and chased him north, carving a trail through the broken land.[^arc11-108]

Nash's next Fireball scorched the exposed body. Stanley considered a Prismatic Wall but kept it unused. The powered boards had a faster setting, and the riders tried to hold the creature's attention on the road toward Vorel's canyon. Then **Hope's board failed**. She could see the broken belt but could not open its housing in time. Stanley stepped beside her through a Dimension Door with his own board. Hope climbed aboard and took the controls as her damaged craft fell into the Worldbreaker's jaws.[^arc11-109]

From the ridgeline Vorel saw three remaining working windboards bearing four riders: Hope and Stanley together, Nash and the Principal on their own. Behind them came the immense creature, grinding through the valley toward the hidden goblins and lizardfolk. Bokbok passed the signal to be ready. Vorel gripped the detonator fixed to his shield. The Worldbreaker was almost at the narrow place—but **no charge had yet exploded**, and the trap's fate belonged to the next battle.[^arc11-110]

## XII. The price of the trap

The Worldbreaker entered Vorel's canyon. Its presence sent most of Bokbok's goblins fleeing, though the lizardfolk stood fast beneath their protective helms. Vorel waited until the monster filled the narrowest passage, then pressed the detonator. Explosives tore the walls apart and buried its legs in stone. The blast left its armor unscathed by fire, despite the wounds earlier flames had seemed to inflict. Yet the falling rock slowed it.[^arc11-111]

The lizardfolk swung from grappling lines onto the vast shell. Vorel leapt among them with Chrome's Bane, cutting deep enough to draw black fluid while ordinary blades glanced away. Nash enlarged him; still small beside the creature, he directed his warriors to bind the monster's legs with their ropes. The Worldbreaker shook the canyon and killed several fighters, but the tethers made it **fall prone**. The momentary victory cost Vorel's people dearly.[^arc11-112]

The Principal twice tried to master the creature with ***Dominate Monster***; its defenses refused him. Stanley sent a Disintegrate against the armored body, but its magic was stopped and turned into a devastating shock across the shell. Lizardfolk still clinging to it dissolved, along with nearby goblins. Vorel survived the force, wounded and furious, while Bokbok fled alive. The survivors could not simply climb the monster again; its armor had become a deathtrap.[^arc11-113]

Nash sought to **banish** the Worldbreaker. The first attempt failed when the creature resisted. The Principal, now openly hostile to losing his prize, struck toward Nash with magic that Stanley countered. He wanted to keep the monster for some purpose of his own. Chrome energy dragged it north, but Vorel leapt onto the Principal's flying board and grappled him, breaking his hold. The temporary alliance had ended in the middle of the battle.[^arc11-114]

Stanley cast ***Polymorph***. The huge shell and rotating mouth vanished, leaving a living snail in the sand. Stanley caught it on Hope's board. Nash tried Banishment once and failed again, then, as the companions held their enemy away, cast it successfully. The snail disappeared from the world. It was not yet victory: Nash had to keep concentrating for a full minute for the exile to endure.[^arc11-115]

Stanley raised a brilliant ***Prismatic Wall*** to protect them. Hope struck at the Principal and urged Vorel to **hurt him, not kill him**. Nash's acid wounded the chrome; Stanley countered another lethal spell aimed at Vorel. But Vorel seized the Principal and pushed him through the wall's colors. Fire, acid, lightning and cold tore through his frame until that body dissolved into liquid chrome, coating Vorel's scales. A visible incarnation of the Principal had died, but his larger fate remained unclear.[^arc11-116]

Above them the morning sky cleared to reveal twelve plumes rising from the Moon. Rocket pods descended around the radiant barrier, and twelve more Principals stepped out. Hope turned on Vorel in anger; she had warned him not to kill the first. The companions were hurt, the monster's shell was gone from sight, but **the banishment minute had not finished**. They had to defend Nash's concentration against twelve newly arrived enemies while Ruby remained unactivated beneath the Solar Mines.[^arc11-117]

## XIII. The debt comes due

The twelve new Principals pressed toward Stanley's Prismatic Wall. Chrome from the body Vorel had destroyed had hardened across his scales, making him more difficult to wound, though its meaning remained uncertain. Nash had to keep concentrating on the absent Worldbreaker. He spoke to the attackers of Angelashi and their four children, hoping they might remember they were grandparents before they remembered conquest. For a moment the words held them; then their old hunger for power returned.[^arc11-118]

The Principals attacked the wall's colored layers with cold, thunder, missiles and portals, stripping defenses away. Stanley stepped outside its shelter to strike at a chrome body, and Nash melted another with acid. Vorel's bluff that he could destroy the Moon with a ludicrous borrowed umbrella fooled no one; the object broke harmlessly against the metal. Hope hovered nearby on her board, ready to take Nash away if the wall failed.[^arc11-119]

Stanley drove one of the Elhandari rejuvenation syringes into himself. Its magic restored his spent strength and spellcasting in an instant, while its promised cost in years remained ahead of him. Vorel hurled two more attackers into the remaining indigo and violet lights. Some were trapped, others blinded, and the others closed again. Monsoon Cabbage arrived in dark vampire armor to fight beside them. Behind him Fate Six crashed into the cluster of chrome bodies, destroying herself a second time to scatter their enemies. No new return of the ship was shown in that battle.[^arc11-120]

Stanley released the distant snail from ***Polymorph*** and made Nash invisible with another spell. Hope lifted Nash high into the sky on her functioning windboard. Their task was not to fight but to survive and keep the banishment intact. Below, Vorel carried Stanley, and an attacker found a path through the dwindling wall. Stanley countered its Disintegrate before it could strike. The minute had not yet passed.[^arc11-121]

With scarcely any strength left, Stanley cast ***Wish*** to make a Simulacrum of himself. He wanted the copy to bear the spells of a rested Stanley, not the exhausted original. The dangerous change was allowed, but the spell exacted a price: his strength fell to almost nothing for days, further magic risked pain, and he might never cast Wish again. That last risk was unresolved. Then the copies repeated the magic until one hundred fragile Stanleys crowded inside the protective wall.[^arc11-122]

The new selves could not pass freely through a barrier that had been woven for the original companions. One copy tore at a Principal with Disintegrate. Vorel, struggling beneath the crush, cut off the enemy's arms with Chrome's Bane, and the others finished the body with blasts of force. Several of the artificial Stanleys dissolved in the effort. The strange army was real, but hardly an immortal legion.[^arc11-123]

High above the battlefield, Nash endured the final moments. Nash held the spell through the final moments of the required minute. The Worldbreaker was **permanently banished from Earth**. Hope embraced the victory. Below, the remaining Principals collapsed into liquid chrome. Their last threats about the gods were a dying enemy's claims, not a future they had proved. The world was safe from the monster—but Ruby had still not been used.[^arc11-124]

A door of red and black flame appeared among the fallen chrome. Through it came a goatlike infernal collector who resembled Brett. He gathered luminous energy from the bodies, calling it the Principal's soul and saying the bill was due. No one fought him. He vanished into his own realm, leaving the companions alive among splintered planks, exhausted magic and the remains of their victory. **The great enemies were defeated, but Earth still bore its wounds.**[^arc11-125]

## XIV. The world after

The desert quieted around the fallen chrome bodies, but the Earth's wounds remained. Stanley learned he had not lost the power to cast ***Wish*** forever. Admiral Chrysorius reported that Elhandari troops had reached the Moon facility, though its inner door was sealed. In the Solar Mines, Amanthalodar said the Rubistic Triangulosphere was ready for Stanley's command. Stanley refused to decide the fate of Earth's magic alone. He asked Nash, Vorel and Hope what future they wanted for their home.[^arc11-126]

The machine could quiet the mana wave that had changed Earth, taking much of its spellcasting with it. Nash chose a world in which Angelashi and their four children might live without the Worldbreaker. Vorel feared leaving Earth weak before other gods and devils, yet wanted its people free. Hope agreed that her world deserved its own future. Stanley gave the order to put the device in. The insertion was not shown in the depths, but what followed proved it had truly worked.[^arc11-127]

Before they could see that renewal, Monsoon Cabbage approached Hope. His ancient oath to guard Earth was fulfilled, he said, and he would no longer live on the hunger that had harmed so many. He opened his protective helmet and stepped into full daylight. The vampire knight **burned and died** beneath the sun. Hope gathered his helmet. It was a death he had chosen, not another threatened departure or mysterious disappearance.[^arc11-128]

The land trembled with release. Translucent dragons in many colors rose over the desert, casting harmless spectral fire across the sky. Then rain fell. Grass and flowers pushed through the dust, trees spread across the Wastes, and waterfalls streamed down the mountains. **Earth had been restored**; the old mana-wave magic no longer worked there. Vorel wept as Stanley whispered that they had done it. Their victory had become something living beneath their feet.[^arc11-129]

Only a handful of Stanley's fragile copies remained. They healed their injured friends and dissolved like snow. An Elhandari vessel came to collect them with King Bokbok, alive and delighted by the new green world. Nash tried to summon fire and magical protection; neither spell answered him on Earth. His strange floating still endured. Bokbok declared himself a Master Magician, pleased that his very ordinary explosives would still work. The company boarded the Elhandari ship for the Moon, where their enemy's last machines had not yet been destroyed.[^arc11-130]

In a lunar complex, they passed human shapes trapped in stone and thirteen opened chrome creation tables. No one found a confirmed phylactery. Nash could not cast even the simplest spell there, though he could still float. His shoulder's small mouth suggested the sealed door might recognize him. Nash placed his hand into its receptacle. The machine called him a Principal byproduct and opened. Beyond waited a chamber of still chrome faces and a raised platform. When Nash stepped upon it, he vanished from his companions' sight.[^arc11-131]

The faces awakened around Nash in a place his friends could not see. They called themselves the Council of Faces, minds of ancient humanity preserved within machines, and claimed a duty to defend Earth from gods and other peoples. The former Principal had failed them; they would make Nash his successor. Against his will, they transformed him into a new tall chrome figure, stripping away his red eye, tiny hand, shoulder mouth and other bodily peculiarities. They ordered him to kill his friends to protect their secret. **Nash refused**, though he pretended compliance long enough to escape their grasp.[^arc11-132]

A chrome man with Nash's face appeared where the old Nash had stood. The companions raised their weapons, fearing another Principal, until he called out that it was him and warned them to flee. He opened the doors, and the friends and Elhandari planted Bokbok's explosives throughout the hidden complex. Nash would not let the Council build another tyrant in his likeness. From the departing ship he pressed the detonator himself. The lunar base burst apart; the Moon itself remained intact.[^arc11-133]

As the facility died, the chrome body that depended upon it began to soften. Nash asked his friends to care for Angelashi and their children. He managed one final gesture before melting into a silver pool. **Nash Maggard died.** Stanley knelt beside the remains and saved a fragment of chrome in a glass vial. This was not a trick, another lost dragon shape or a return to his old metal heart. Their friend was gone, and they would have to decide whether they could bring him back.[^arc11-134]

On the restored Earth, Vorel's surviving lizardfolk prepared to rebuild Razakval, and Hope offered a new home at T'ietkalla to people beyond her own race. Over the Solar Mines the companions raised a stone monument to Nash. At a council of the victors, Stanley argued for mercy and useful work rather than slaughter: the Dragonsons could be watched, the Kuromokishi could join Vorel's people, and the surviving Toyota Host could be settled among the rebuilding communities and Elhandari. They entrusted allies with a mission against remaining hostile works at Clench, while the survivors began to rebuild.[^arc11-135]

After four days Fate Six had been reconstituted again. The four-day account of Stanley's recovery did not neatly match the earlier seven-day cost of his ***Wish***. Hope and Vorel sailed with Stanley to Planet Nash, carrying the vial while Nash was still dead. Beyond Earth's quieted mana field, Stanley could feel his magic again. He made another dangerous ***Wish***, this time to perform ***True Resurrection*** beyond the spell's ordinary safe limits. The spell succeeded. The chrome became living flesh, and Nash climbed out naked and bewildered, his red eye, small arm, shoulder companion and familiar body restored. Stanley had risked losing Wish again, but retained it.[^arc11-136]

Nash ran to Angelashi and their four children. He embraced them, then thanked Stanley for returning a husband and father to his family. He hugged Vorel, knelt before Hope as his queen and said farewell to the friends who had traveled so far with him. His final little rhyme was a goodbye, **not a spell**. He chose to remain on Planet Nash, where the life he most wanted waited beside his children.[^arc11-137]

The other three returned to green Earth. Vorel set off with his lizardfolk and the Kuromokishi toward Razakval, still a journey and a rebuilding, not a city magically restored overnight. Stanley gave him one last title from the friends who knew him best: Friend. Vorel accepted it and made it his own, **Friend Till the End of His Days**. He went into the forest carrying the name with more care than any of his old boasts.[^arc11-138]

At T'ietkalla, Hope's surviving keep stood amid new trees. She intended to rebuild an open city and see her friends again. Stanley gave her the Mask of Eltanfawr, which could grant her command of Fate Six after attunement. She reshaped the mask into a crown and wore it as she returned to her people. Stanley continued to Mars, where the Elhandari gathered. He spoke of belonging to more than one world and let each choose where to live. Amanthalodar opened a real portal to the Feywild, and Stanley crossed with those who wished to go.[^arc11-139]

Far away on Planet Nash, Nash sat with Angelashi watching their children. A cupboard shook. He approached it cautiously and opened the door. Another man who appeared to be Nash Maggard burst out, seized him and warned that it was not over—someone was coming. No one knew who the double was or whom he feared. The friends had fought their way home, but this peace carried one last unanswered warning.[^arc11-140]
