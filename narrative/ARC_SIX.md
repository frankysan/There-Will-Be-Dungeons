## Season Two — A Vessel for Hope

### Four days in the wreckage

After the destruction of Elhandar, the four companions linger at Stanley's family villa amid the debris of the space station. Three days pass while he waits for his father's enchanted mask and cottage to complete their bond. Hope gathers provisions, Vorel buries those who did not survive, Nash eagerly pesters Stanley, and Stanley retreats into grief. The old fellowship is close to dissolution. Their loved ones, meanwhile, have been evacuated toward the elven haven on Mars.

An unexpected visitor crosses the dunes: Monsoon Cabbage, a vampire from their troubled past. He carries Softhead in a cage as a living source of blood. Monsoon says he once journeyed with Stanley's father, a different Nash, Groxl and the Principle, and claims to know how the Principle may have become something even more dangerous. His history cannot be taken at face value. But he offers a concrete service. With the companions' wary consent, he extracts Stanley's remaining Soulbroker curse into a hostile creature. They battle it inside the cottage, and Hope strikes it down. The game's ruling is decisive: **Stanley is free of the Weir obligation and retains the bargain's benefits.** The broader Soulbroker and Principle threats remain.

At last, the cottage awakens. Wooden spars and sails rise out of the sand. Ghostly elves man a ship into which the old home has been woven, and a peg-legged stranger welcomes Stanley as his nephew.

### The ship that remembers

The stranger introduces himself as **Sever**, Stanley's uncle and first mate. The ghost crew, magically bound to the ship and unable to touch the living, shows the companions its machinery, cabins and controls. Far beneath the engine, an unusual chamber holds a carved face shaped like Stanley's father. A voice from the wood declares itself to be Delit Ornath: he says that he gave up his mortal body and infused his life into the ship. He also tells Stanley that his mother died. The voice is real to the experience of the characters; the complete history of that sacrifice is still a story they have heard, not one they have witnessed.

Freedom changes the party's decision. Stanley no longer insists that anyone follow him, but argues that the harm they failed to prevent would likely have been worse without them. Hope chooses to pursue whatever purpose keeps drawing her back. Nash longs for his family. Vorel cannot dismiss the fate of Razak Val. Rather than part, they agree to call on Vorel's people before traveling to Mars.

### Earth beneath the sails

The ship makes its **first actual flight**, soaring over the damaged lands. Tietkalla can still be seen in the distance, its surviving walls scarred and its streets apparently quiet, but the group does not stop to determine who remains. They land instead in the canyon of Razak Val.

Against Vorel's darkest expectations, the settlement lives. Survivors emerge, kneel, and recognize him as Great Fang. Some tell how the invading worms and attackers fell dead during the fighting; neither they nor the returning heroes can yet provide a comprehensive account of what happened. Vorel's reunion offers hope, though not a restoration of every house or fallen comrade. When he prepares to leave, followers ask why their leader must depart without them. A substantial group takes up their weapons and boards the ship, determined to accompany him beyond Earth.

The strange vessel ascends into the void. An enchanted shield holds the deadly vacuum outside, while its ghost crew guides the mortals and nervous lizardfolk across interplanetary distances in roughly an hour. Mars appears as a brown world with a concentrated patch of green. Within that engineered jungle haven, the ship lands. **Stanley formally gives it the name Fate Six**, now a real christening rather than a tentative joke about their previous vehicles.

### A home on Mars, a missing engineer

Nash finds Angelashi and their four children alive in the colony and embraces them. Their reunion is genuine, but the safety of their new refuge is uncertain. Angelashi fears that the gathering war could follow them even here. Nash speaks privately of another world, Planet Nash, as a possible place to live. Neither family nor vessel is sent there.

The Elhandari leader welcomes Vorel's lizardfolk as possible allies and directs the companions to an eccentric physician, **Dunglestar Ravensbread**, who once loved the missing engineer **Tungsten Wheel**. Dunglestar's Theatre of Mortal Bodies is filled with unnerving anatomical displays. His grief at Tungsten's absence gives the group access to the engineer's workshop, where they encounter something their travels have until now only promised: **a partly constructed Rubistic Triangulosphere**.

It is enormous, intricate and still. Its missing rare ingredient, known in the current transcripts as *Gluptwarp* or similar, is needed to prevent disastrous overheating. A message apparently sent by Tungsten points toward **Blast Belly Rock** and a possible source of the material at **Pentel Weiss**. The travelers notice symbols resembling the ship's navigation keys. The clues offer directions, not certainty. They do not find Tungsten, start the machine or prove how it could save Earth.

Before departing the theatre, Stanley and Vorel persuade the young elf assistant **Twaddle** to seek a place aboard Fate Six helping its intangible ghost surgeon. He writes a farewell note and accompanies them as far as the elven council.

The companions enter the shade of Mars's great elder tree, where an assembly gathers around a map of Earth to plan the war against the Weir. **The council is called to order, but its deliberations are left for the next episode.** The four have recovered a purpose, a ship, allies and the beginnings of a device—not yet a victory.

### Enemy Mine — an army and a crown

The council's assembly is no ordinary gathering of elves. The old Nameless One, leader of the Liberators, has returned alive; a cyclops and a regal birdfolk king speak for other distant groups. Nobieri waits beside the companions, and Bok Bok arrives speaking better Common than anyone remembers. Vorel proclaims himself the greatest of all Vorels his audience might encounter. Stanley notices a hooded vampire slip into the rear seats before Monsoon Cabbage acknowledges his presence.

Amanthalodar speaks of the years his people have spent studying Earth's hidden wounds. He attributes the last hope of a cure to Delit Ornath and, more strangely, to an older Nash who supposedly traveled backward through adjacencies. The current Nash has no such memory. What matters most to the council is a great machine that must reach the heart of Earth. The wounded planet still holds a growing creature, and closing the Weir's opening may not stop it.

The **Solar Mines** emerge as the most plausible passage to the core. Their name changes Nash's demeanor. He describes a childhood of learning in clean upper halls, followed by his banishment into the deadly depths once his worth to the breeding program had been judged inadequate. He recalls laboring beside other prisoners under the watch of terrible metal-magic Slow Peters, hearing decrees from a masked Emperor With No Name, and escaping with help from people who never lived to see freedom. The council takes his warning seriously. A plan begins to form around infiltrating the mines from behind while the army attacks their defended entrance.

The fragile coalition counts elves, lizardfolk, giants, birdfolk and Liberators, but Hope refuses to conscript those refugees who have already lost so much. Nobieri alone offers to fight. Bok Bok promises to seek a goblin army—if he can first become its king. Monsoon steps out of the shadows to offer a vampire knight's service. Stanley asks the assembly to consider one more dangerous ally: **Diontalus**, the archfey who once controlled his body. The elf leaders resist, but Stanley argues convincingly that the end of the world allows no comfortable reserve of unspent resources.

Then the terrible image is revealed. The **World Breaker**, the elves believe, is a corrupted **Tarrasque**. It remains somewhere beneath Earth's crust, beyond any force assembled in the hall. Airships, ground vehicles, buried approaches and scattered recruiting missions are debated for a future war. Nothing has yet been won, but the allies agree to make themselves ready while the four seek the missing engineer and the part that might finish his machine.

Before the gathering disperses, Amanthalodar announces that the elves have unanimously elected **Stanley their first new Selle Prime** since the Scorching, despite his half-elven origins. Stanley accepts. The great tree enfolds him in living branches, and he hears a voice meant for him alone. It speaks to his search for a home, his doubts, and a choice still ahead. Most disturbing of all, the tree says that he will need Nash, yet that Nash must die. It is a prophecy, not a death witnessed or an obligation Stanley has accepted.

When the branches open, Stanley has changed: more elven, silver-eyed, luminous and strangely beautiful to different beholders in different ways. The elves kneel, though he bids them rise and says he must still earn their faith. A young spear-bearing guardian takes her place beside him, claiming the missing Tungsten Wheel as her uncle. The council adjourns with an army to prepare, a vessel waiting on Mars and a newly crowned leader burdened by knowledge he has not shared.

### Captain Confusion — the council of Nashes

Still newly crowned, Stanley struggles with the affection of his people. His guard, **Union Wheel**, promises to protect him on his search for her uncle Tungsten, although she knows nothing of his location. Stanley leaves Amanthalodar responsible for Mars while he journeys away. At the harbor the ghost crew of Fate Six prepares a ship sustained by his father's enchanted life, while Vorel builds himself a bed of sand and wood. Twaddle boards for a future apprenticeship. Hope briefly visits Nobieri, who cannot name the Voice inhabiting her Hand but speculates that sufficiently strong shielding might contain a magical backlash should she try to sever it. The Hand remains.

The journey plans change before takeoff. Nash's wife **Angelashi** and their four children come aboard, and Stanley remembers the refuge he promised them: **Planet Nash**, the bizarre pair of asteroids joined by a great tower. Crooked Hand Row coaches Hope in the celestial helm, whose dialed coordinates open a crack through the fabric of reality. Fate Six surges through shimmering rift space, and after several hours reappears near the tower. It is Hope who guides them out of the rift; for the first time the ship's strange interstellar transit is more than just a captain's promise.

In Tower Nash, the talking stone face of another Nash recognizes them. At first he is uncertain of his place in the great tangle of alternate histories. He says that in his own story a desperate journey into the Solar Mines failed, a vortex of Nashes swept him into another strand of reality, and he encountered Stanley's father. Aging, he asked Delit to preserve him as a tower so he might still counsel his younger selves. The group hears the tale, but no one has seen that past themselves.

Tower Nash projects seven other Nashes, each in a differently colored cloak. They bicker, boast, despair and compare their plans for the Solar Mines. White Nash says an assault by air met disastrous batteries and ended with his world's defeat. Green recalls a similarly ruinous approach carrying a great machine. Others have not yet made their attempts. A few speak of dragon forms, one of attempting to charge with a dinosaur, and one of excavating tunnels. Nash discovers that his marriage and children mark his story as different from any he hears. Vorel invites alternate Vorels to appear, turning the council into a competitive parade of lizardfolk ambition.

The conversation gives the heroes warnings but no ready-made army. Stanley suggests Angelashi might be more persuasive with the tower's other inhabitants than Nash himself. She agrees to stay with the children and explore whether alternate Nashes could come and defend their Earth. On an upper floor, Nash visits **another Pod**, living happily with her pixie husband Roger and their children. It is painful precisely because she is **not** the Pod he lost.

Hope encounters a different **Tedna**, now a masked and scarred warrior calling himself **Badass T**. After he insists on joining their expedition, she reluctantly allows him to accompany them later. He returns **Smokey's belt** and its ammunition to her. Then Angelashi sees what the others have missed: this Tedna somehow arrived from a different reality. If one man could cross the distance between worlds, perhaps an army of Nashes might do the same. The question hangs in the air as the episode ends. **No other Nash has yet crossed into their world, and Fate Six has not left Planet Nash.**

### That's What The Blob Said — the herald and the bank

Angelashi's question still hangs in Tower Nash: how did the other Tedna find his way here? He points not to himself but to the talking tower, whose memory is scattered across years and alternate lives. Before he helps, Tower Nash must untangle a smaller crisis. Two managers of Vorel's Sandburgers, a Tungan and a blue Triton, both believe themselves in charge. Vorel proposes a contest of cooking, and the tower calls an old associate by flashing a light into the stars.

The answer rides toward them on a board of brilliant white light: a green-skinned, oddly serene herald named **Zolfin Sol** (the spelling is still provisional). Offering Nash an orange and speaking with extraordinary familiarity, he says he serves **Chronos**, ailing god of time. He and Tower Nash, he claims, drew a version of Tedna from another history to help the heroes escape Sigil. What is happening on Earth threatens more than one world: the herald speaks of time itself falling ill, of innumerable attempts by innumerable versions of the party, and of creation's approaching collapse. They hear him, but none can independently test his enormous account.

Could two hundred Nashes help defend Earth? The herald has the means to help summon some of them, but also warns that many Nashes collected together may be dangerous. His attention shifts to **Hope's Hand**, whose hidden Voice tells her privately to cooperate for now, saving any attempt to overpower this visitor for another day. The herald calls the Voice a **Lord of Secrets**, a once-mortal wizard with an insatiable hunger to control everything. Hope hears a great deal she keeps to herself.

At the sandburger factory Vorel tastes two experiments, one built around seafood and the other around cat food, each seasoned with sand. The others are horrified. Vorel refuses to pronounce one chef a universal victor and tells them to ask their customers which offering they actually want. He refuses the position of manager, suggesting an alternate Vorel might have time to run the business; the herald may help later, but no duplicate Vorel walks through a portal today. Nor do two hundred Nashes arrive. Angelashi asks to raise the children away from such an army and returns with them to Tower Nash. The heroes say their farewells.

This time the party truly sets out. **Badass T boards Fate Six**, as does Stanley's elf guardian. Hope guides the ship into rift space toward **Blast Belly Rock**. During the voyage she tries to understand this battle-hardened Tedna, a man now in his thirties, no longer the sheltered follower she knew. Later the four old companions share elven mead in the captain's quarters, served by the ghostly Gorgeous George, and enjoy for a moment the familiar comfort of being together on the road.

After a day's travel the ship docks at a Swiss-cheese-like asteroid, its ledge occupied by the nearly deserted mining town of **Burp**. Stanley warns Badass T in strikingly harsh terms to obey him. In the **Wet Whistle** tavern, an innkeeper called **Ailboon Hardbottom** is reluctant to discuss the engineer they seek. Stanley produces his guitar and turns their extraordinary adventures into a performance so moving that the room falls under his charm. The keeper grows warm, feeds them tough local rock dog and recalls an elf who, long ago, came each day to wait for another visitor. The elf may have been Tungsten and apparently worked at the **Bank of Burp**, but the barkeep cannot swear to his name.

Badass T agrees, under warning, to remain as lookout in the tavern while the others find the abandoned bank. They pass a guarded gate, cross a silent district and encounter a strange rock dog outside the bank doors. Vorel kicks those doors apart, and Hope ties them shut from the inside. Small luminous yellow blobs cling to the abandoned tellers' floor, walls and ceiling. Nash cannot resist investigating one with his nose, finger and finally tongue. At first he seems unharmed, then one blob climbs his boot and steals **his intelligence**. Suddenly it can speak. Another steals **his dexterity** and scurries away.

Their friend is still himself, but bewildered and badly diminished. Nash lashes out with acid; Hope stabs the creatures; Vorel uses the Eye of Groxl to examine their defenses. Stanley fires blasts of force across the teller cage, annihilating the clever blob and the swift one. They defeat every ooze, but the magic they stole does **not** flow back into Nash. At the end of the struggle he struggles to explain to his companions what has happened. The search for Tungsten has reached the bank, but the most immediate trouble is now Nash himself.

### Schrödinger's Nash — a vault and a dragon

Nash enters the next room of the old bank with almost no wits or coordination left to him. Yet his strange magic remains. He sings an awkward verse about having once been smarter and makes Vorel smaller, enabling the lizardfolk to squeeze past the teller bars and unlock the way from within. Nash tastes the burnt remains of the clever ooze, hoping his lost mind might somehow return. Nothing changes.

Behind the teller hall the party searches office after office, collecting obscure words from abandoned possessions: *tapioca*, *iron*, *plow*. Stanley uncovers the first dependable proof of their quarry: a **signed agreement employing Tungsten Wheel as a teller for ten gold a week**. A ledger points toward Tungsten's own desk and its small key. His niece Union Wheel is no closer to meeting him, but at last they have a trail that is more than a tavern keeper's memory.

Their discoveries have a cost. As Stanley searches beneath an old horned statue, another yellow blob clings to his face and steals **his wisdom**. He destroys it, but killing it accomplishes no restoration. Desperate to reach whatever lies below the bank, he strains his back trying to lift the massive statue. Vorel, frustrated with their puzzle, breaks an old celestial globe and discovers a string that moves the monument aside. The hidden stair is finally theirs.

The old vault recognizes the words they collected as its security passphrase. Stanley steps through the locked inner bars by magic and finds a matching box for Tungsten's key: **724**. There is also a treasury of gold and gems, irresistible to someone now bereft of judgment. He attempts to gather a ridiculous fortune, scattering coins as he teleports back. Hope opens Tungsten's deposit box and takes a parchment bearing **coordinates for the ship's helm**. Their destination remains a mystery, but they are finally holding something the engineer chose to leave behind.

Their escape from the bank leads into another danger. A band of masked orc marauders has occupied Burp, taken prisoners, and resolved to steal Fate Six. The leader carries a dwarf lookout's severed head as warning. Stanley answers the threats with devastating magic; Hope moves among the enemy wearing an orc's appearance; Vorel hurls himself into their midst. Nash becomes a great **adult black dragon**. Within that form his mind is suddenly sharp again, though the stolen intelligence of his ordinary body has not been returned. He crushes enemies beneath his weight and bathes others in acid. Vorel leaps from the dragon and strikes down the marauders' leader.

Three orcs throw down their weapons, and Stanley accepts them as new members of his ship's crew. They point the heroes to cages holding the tavern keeper, other survivors, and **Badass T**. Hope opens the cages and releases the prisoners. The frightened townspeople retreat at the sight of the great black dragon until Hope calls out that he is a friend.

Fate Six still waits at Blast Belly Rock. The party possesses a contract, a key, mysterious coordinates and a chest of uncertain wealth. They have gained three unexpected sailors and lost a measure of their own faculties. **Nash remains clever only in dragon form; Stanley's stolen wisdom remains unrestored; Tungsten is still missing.**

### Brace for Impact — the prison beyond the nebula

The crew leaves the spent mines of Blast Belly Rock at last. Three orcs recently spared by Stanley carry the heavy treasure chest aboard Fate Six, only to draw their swords in fear when the ship's ghost sailors come to greet them. Stanley persuades them to lay down their weapons. The ship pulls free of its moorings, and Hope and Crooked Hand Row take Tungsten's recovered coordinates into rift space.

Nash stays in his immense black dragon form for a while, because the mind he possesses there is much sharper than the one the yellow oozes left behind. Ailboon has promised that the effects of those little monsters will fade after a good rest. Nash asks Vorel to help him sleep when he transforms back. But Vorel secretly tells Stanley that his lost wisdom has made him admirably fearless, and that he need not hurry to recover. Below decks, the ghost elves teach the captain and Vorel to play poker. They gamble for hours, winning and losing about the same amount, while the befuddled Nash is briefly coaxed away from bed before Uncle Sever returns him to safety.

At his cabin Stanley meets a stern Union Wheel, troubled by his cavalier conduct. A spell of **Lesser Restoration** clears the unnatural haze clouding his wisdom, though it leaves him tipsy for the moment. During the night the ship's mortals finally rest, and **Nash awakens with his intelligence and dexterity restored**. For the first time since the bank, the two companions are themselves again.

The next morning the ship leaves rift space far from recognizable landmarks. A vast purple and pink nebula boils with lightning, and there is no apparent city or prison in sight. Stanley orders a laser pigeon dispatched into it with a brief request for an answer. None comes. He sends the ship onward anyway.

Hope learns to navigate the nebula by moving from one pocket of safety to another, with fierce turbulence and lashes of electricity between them. Great three-eyed, three-mouthed whales drift through the luminous clouds. They seem to mean no harm, and might even delight in the lightning, but their proximity conducts terrible shocks into Fate Six. One of the new orcs is fatally battered by the motion; the other two are lost overboard in the storm. Stanley tries to frighten a whale away with a shrieking megaphone, and succeeds briefly. **All three of the crew's newest recruits are gone.**

Hope remembers the little jack-in-the-box compass the clowns gave her. Its pointing hand swings toward the northeast, guiding her across dangerous stretches until even the whales flee from a black hollow in the colored clouds. There hangs a blue-burning sphere no larger than a man, and inside it, strangely distant, turns a dark pyramid. Nash puts on one of Uncle Sever's enchanted glass breathing helmets and flies out to investigate. He crosses the sphere alone and finds a second expanse of space in which the pyramid is miles tall, marked with glyphs and guarded by an overwhelming sense of dread. He decides against entering alone and returns to the ship.

The apparent size of the doorway proves misleading: **Hope guides the entire Fate Six through the little sphere**. Crooked Hand Row helps record a new set of coordinates to find their way back out. The ship docks beside the towering pyramid. The living party—Hope, Stanley, Nash, Vorel, Union Wheel and Badass T—disembarks, leaving the ghosts to prepare for a swift departure.

Inside, a long gray hall lined with faceless geometrical figures leads to a great circular intake shaft. The mysterious Voice speaks **privately to Hope**, claiming this is the prison of the cosmos's most dangerous beings and that they must speak with the warden or discover secret entrances. The great statue guarding the shaft wakes and names the place **Pentel Weiss**, but will tell the visitors nothing of prisoners: none is registered as one of the authorized collectors. Even Hope's attempted use of Tungsten Wheel's name opens no door.

When the warden explains that the shaft receives inmates, the others look to Badass T. He protests the indignity and reluctantly supplies his old surname, **Garfield**, but the warden does not recognize him as a wanted criminal. Still, when Hope asks him to try, the scarred warrior draws both knives and hurls himself into the darkness. The sound vanishes with him. **No one knows what waits below.**

### No Magic Allowed — the intake and the hungry strangers

The others stand over the black intake shaft where Badass T disappeared. Stanley tries to speak to him with magic, whispering a message into his megaphone, but the spell gathers like a sneeze that will not come. The great stone keeper warns that **magic is forbidden in Pentel Weiss**. Even the power of the spell is spent. Vorel drops a lit lantern into the darkness, and Stanley follows it with two unwanted swords; the keeper's warnings grow sterner until every golem in the hall threatens to attack.

The party withdraws rather than challenge the statues. They fly Fate Six slowly around the vast pyramid, searching for another entrance along its perfectly cut gray masonry, finding nothing after hours of careful looking. Hope's curious clown compass only points to the pyramid itself. Returning to its intake, they finally summon the warden: a frail, gray-haired man who appears without walking, bears a frightening scar around one eye, and refuses to tell them anything without collector credentials. **Hope wonders whether that wounded eye is the one sought by her Hand**, but the suspicion proves nothing. The warden vanishes as suddenly as he came, and the golems order them out.

A new plan forms aboard the ship. Vorel puts on an officer's coat and picks up an ordinary sword; Nash discovers his Rambunctious Bunch street clothes count as armor he cannot properly use and exchanges them for hooded attire. Stanley equips his studded leather. Hope privately asks Stanley's wooden father if he can help. The voice knows ancient rumors of Pentel Weiss and remembers **recruiting Tungsten Wheel to build the Triangulosphere**, but knows no secret way inside. Disguised as best they can, the companions race back toward the opening. **Hope's magically altered human appearance dissolves at the archway**, yet the silent guardians let them through.

Then the four heroes and Union Wheel hurl themselves into the darkness. They fall through twisting, soundless space before dropping into an icy subterranean pool. Nash catches his little glowing food peeling as it slips from his head; though dim, its old light survives the magic-suppressing prison. The five scramble toward a distant stair when a monstrous, tentacled head rises from beneath the black water. Vorel meets it first, but his ordinary sword merely skids off its hide. Stanley's blasts and Nash's offensive magic fail utterly, and Hope's conjured darkness does nothing. But Vorel can still summon flame with his rage, and Nash's strangely altered body can still float.

Tentacles seize the heroes, teeth emerge from the water, and **Badass T suddenly appears alive on the ledge**, accompanied by strangers, casting a rope toward Stanley. Vorel fights through the grasping arms and discovers that *Diplomacy*, Stanley's enchanted sword, can actually pierce the creature. He leaps back into danger to drive it home. Meanwhile, Nash is dragged into the vast circular mouth and swallowed. Hope drops onto its teeth, reaches down, and with a tremendous effort **pulls Nash out alive**, his slow-floating body drifting clear of the monster. She then clings to Vorel as he swims for shore. With the help of the rope and the waiting strangers, the party escapes, leaving the hungry creature alive in the pool.

A talkative halfling called **Zipty** welcomes them. He introduces fellow inhabitants of Pentel Weiss and leads them into a bare camp around a small fire. Badass T complains that the others took hours to follow his reckless descent. The strangers say they are innocent people mistakenly imprisoned, cannot age, and need not eat here—but the sight and smell of the party's food excites them terribly. Hope offers rations. They cast aside everything but the meat.

Stanley asks after Tungsten Wheel and gets evasions, half-promises and more appeals for food. An ancient woman lies under a blanket, insisting she cannot hear unless they come close. An orc woman broods beside a lever. The conversation sours, and Hope threatens the group if they will not stop hiding answers. **The orc pulls the lever and a gate slams shut behind the adventurers.** The ragged companions transform before their eyes into monstrous animal-human forms: a rat, a boar, a bear, a tiger and an elephant-like giant. The trap has sprung—but **the confrontation has not yet begun**.

### The Call of Meats is Strong — rescue inside the prison

The hungry prisoners become a monstrous hunting party. Tiny Ziptyree stabs at Stanley, Torbeer the enormous werebear seizes Badass T, and Grandma Tilly, their apparently helpless elder, prowls as a werewolf. Their wounds close around ordinary blows, but Hope's enchanted dagger and Stanley's *Diplomacy* strike true. Nash tries to release fire from a *Chromatic Orb*, only to feel the prison smother the spell before it forms. The power is nevertheless spent. As he takes to the air on his body's strange built-in flight, Vorel wrestles Tilly into the little stone pool and holds her head beneath the surface, refusing to yield even as the raging elephant-like Rugrish tramples him.

Hope kills the wererat Ziptyree; Stanley cuts down Morton, the wereboar. Torbeer savages Badass T and finally hurls his body across the chamber. But the prisoners do not win. Tilly escapes Vorel's grip, still alive, reverts to the old woman the group first met and calls her family to surrender. The wounded survivors kneel or stand down. Stanley runs to Badass T, who is unconscious and bleeding, and stabilizes him. Beneath the mask, new fur has begun to grow. Tilly says the bear's bite has passed a curse: soon the young man will hear the same call of meat that has tormented them for centuries. She offers to keep and teach him. After a fraught argument, the companions leave their unconscious friend in her care, intending to return for him. It is a decision made under constraint, not proof he will be safe or cured.

Torbeer repays the survivors' mercy by opening a concealed passage, leading the four adventurers and Union through silent ranks of arcane golems toward a domed intersection. At its center stands an altar marked by circular patterns and a triangular recess. The party sees no fitting key and leaves it untouched. Guided toward the habitation area of the mages, they meet an elderly bearded sentry who grudgingly admits them to a crowded, improvised settlement. Stanley's booming megaphone announcement asks for Tungsten Wheel. A white-haired elf rises. **Union runs to embrace her uncle, alive at last.**

Tungsten leads them to his tiny cell, its walls covered with his calculations, but the settlement's elected spokesman Rolston resents their secret conversation. Tungsten learns that the half-elven Stanley is now Selle Prime and that his unfinished machine is urgently needed to save the planet. He explains what he believes to be the prison's dampening field: newly conjured arcane effects fail, while some magic inherent in an object or living form persists. Stanley finds that even *Diplomacy* cannot obey its usual command to fly from the scabbard, though its blade has wounded the prison creatures. Tungsten speaks of the old collectors who once ruled these passages, of rings said to free them from suppression, and of a creature from beyond that may have trapped one. This is **a lead and a prisoner's theory**, not an item found. He also suspects that someone revealed his hiding place on Blast Belly Rock, but he cannot know who betrayed him.

Tungsten gathers his notes and chooses to go with the rescuers. Stanley instructs Union to guard her uncle first. Yet the inmates block the common room, their council demanding every possession the newcomers carry. Stanley's fierce warning persuades most to clear a path, but Rolston holds his ground. Stanley passes Vorel an Elhandari empathy grenade, and the great lizard uses it as an improvised club, knocking the magistrate to the floor. No magical detonation follows. The six travelers step into the next corridor under hostile whispers. **They have found Tungsten, but not a way out; Badass T remains behind, and the promised ring is still only a rumor.**

### Big Mouthful of Carpet — voices in the crypt

The passage beyond the mage settlement is lined by towering golems that never move. Tungsten leads the six travelers toward a ward other prisoners fear to enter, its ancient crypts rumored to harbor liches and perhaps a collector's ring. He warns that such a ring might restore access to magic or let them operate the mysterious altar in the prison's central hall. The heroes argue about who should wear it first. Stanley would prefer Nash, whose dragon form and escape magic could change the odds—if only they could use them. For now, Nash must rely on the faintly shining vegetable peel and the peculiar floating power fused into his body. The magical heart that makes him a dragon, the GM says, is a separate implanted object, and that transformation will not function here.

Vorel goes ahead, turning his chromatic scales dark against the dungeon. Every passage seems to lead to tombs. An ancient skeleton lies in an open crypt; inside the first raised sarcophagus, a bandaged, shriveled corpse reminds the others unkindly of Nash. Nash protests that resemblance and casually takes a finger bone, tucking it away. Before long he lends his glowing peel to a skull Vorel has found, making an absurd but serviceable lantern. They pass motionless demonic statues and inspect more old bodies. No one finds a ring.

The other route is carpeted, to Vorel's fascination. He earnestly asks what furnishings each companion would prefer in death and concludes they must take the way of the carpets. They spend long minutes searching the crypts there before the floor comes alive beneath them. One rug engulfs Nash, another wraps around Stanley, squeezing out their breath. Vorel tears Nash free, bites into the cloth and rushes between the divided companions; Stanley struggles to escape. Hope's enchanted dagger finds the living threads, and in a private moment she learns the strange cold she carries through her Hand has not diminished at all. Her cuts spread freezing patterns through the rugs until they shatter. Eventually every attacking carpet lies in pieces. Tungsten, more fascinated than afraid, gathers enough fragments for a future study of how apparently magical inanimate creatures can act where spellcasting fails.

Further in, Nash retrieves a handsome pair of old leather sandals from another corpse, storing them in his bag without putting them on. Still there is no ring. The party finally reaches a chamber with two ancient figures seated in grim splendor, one with a decaying crown embedded in its head. Both appear dead at first. From beyond their room comes an unseen, gentle voice, speaking directly into the minds of the adventurers and inviting them farther east. Tungsten trembles, then begins walking toward it without responding to the others. Stanley catches and restrains him.

Vorel, at the entrance, sees something entirely different from the dark crypt: endless sunlit sand alive with creatures worthy of hunting. He cannot resist. As he walks toward the welcoming mirage, the two seated corpses stir and **warn him away from the door**, their distinct voices suddenly audible. Hope reaches for Vorel's tail but cannot hold him. For a heartbeat the desert vision slips, revealing **humanoid forms caught in black tentacles and a gigantic eye set into the wall**. Then the desert returns and Vorel passes inside, disappearing among the trapped shapes. The others stand outside, their friend beyond their reach. **They have no ring, no clear escape and no certainty what calls from the darkness.**

### Flesh Sauce — song against the impossible

Inside the next chamber, Vorel's fear and helplessness turn into an ideal kingdom. In his mind he sits on a dragon-skull throne over a court of lizardfolk, with food to feast upon and wrestlers to best. Beyond his dream, though, the lizardfolk warrior is merely another body held among a crowd of captives by pulsing black tentacles. Union pulls her spellbound uncle Tungsten out of view, snapping him back to himself, and Stanley goes after Vorel, the blade of *Diplomacy* ready.

At first Stanley's will holds against the welcoming voice. He makes his way among entangled figures and searches their hands for a way out, but every corpse seems to lack the ring the party needs. The tentacles seize him and gently rock him into surrender. His own private dream opens: his father's study, then dinner downstairs with both parents, his friends and children. The dead and distant all come together around the same table. Neither fantasy is real. Hope and Nash hear only silence after their companions disappear from sight.

Two decayed, crowned figures sitting just outside the reach of that psychic influence finally introduce themselves as ancient liches, calling themselves the Maggot Lord and the Obtuserizer. They want a collector's ring as much as the adventurers do and promise unimaginable rewards if the party delivers one. They offer a second, ghastlier route: kill their bodies here, they claim, and their hidden phylacteries will let them reappear somewhere beyond the prison. Nash and Hope refuse to knowingly release anyone who speaks of conquering realms and ruling minds. The liches claim that nothing can defeat the being beyond their throne room by conventional force. Perhaps, they speculate, its maker left some unknown means to undo it.

Hope has a simpler idea. She and Nash tie ropes to the others, blindfold themselves and walk past the threshold without looking at the being. The persuasive voice is still in their thoughts, telling them that endless happiness awaits, but the charming visions cannot reach them through covered eyes. They touch the bound figures in the darkness, finding no rings. Strong tentacles gradually grasp them nonetheless. Nash is pinned near the ceiling; Hope wriggles free again and again, crawling across the tops of immobile people toward a hot, radiating brightness she can dimly sense even through cloth.

After Nash finally escapes, Union ventures in blindfolded to find Stanley, but wrapping cloth over his eyes does not wake him. Hope reaches something immense, soft and slippery: a vast living eye. When she stabs it with her frozen dagger, its piercing telepathic scream tears through everyone nearby. Union collapses unconscious. Hope tries another strike, and another wave of pain follows. Even the others far from the eye can feel the cost. She reaches into the cut and finds only fibers and thick flesh. At last she embraces the creature instead of stabbing again, and its voice quiets long enough to forgive her—yet it keeps urging her to remove the blindfold.

Privately, Hope asks the Voice bound to her Hand whether death-magic could finish the thing. The Voice claims another part of its power waits atop the prison and threatens what will happen if Hope never retrieves it. Instead of instructing another fatal blow, it offers a strange, simple remedy: **sing to the creature**. Hope hums a lullaby. For the first time, the great eye sounds frightened. Nash and Tungsten join in; the chanting breaks Stanley's dream and he sees Union on the ground. He moves to her, restores her breathing to a stable rhythm and takes up his guitar.

Stanley sings the song he wrote about love, shelter and finding one another again. The beautiful false light collapses into the true shape of the great eye, and then the eye itself begins to melt. Every tentacle becomes liquid; the captives fall lifeless into an awful depth of warm **flesh sauce**. Vorel wakes to the sight of his friends singing, the kingdom lost in an instant. The party has finally defeated the thing from beyond—without a spell or a sword stroke delivering the final blow.

Searching the newly accessible corpses, Nash is at last the one who finds a ring. He slips it onto his finger and feels the prison's weight lift, accompanied by a baffling stream of information through his head. It may be precisely the ring they sought, but he has not yet tested a spell. The two liches approach, demanding what they believe was promised. The companions try to persuade them Nash only found a worthless wedding band. As the undead rulers press toward the room, Hope moves to shove them back and Stanley draws *Diplomacy*. **The argument becomes the beginning of another battle**, and the episode ends before the first round is resolved.

### Dust to Dust — the end of a season, not the quest

The undead promise riches, then reveal the prison's cruelty. Archeloft slips back through the crypt entrance and drops a stone wall between himself and the adventurers, abandoning his fellow lich, Egnails the Maggot Lord, with the party. Stanley persuades Nash to hand him their only collector's ring. As soon as he puts it on, his spellcasting comes roaring back: he heals Union and lifts her from unconsciousness. But the sarcophagi shake, and bandaged bodies pull themselves out of the tombs. Above them rises a gold-crowned mummy lord whose gaze petrifies Nash with terror.

Stanley's waves of thunder and light tear through the old wrappings. Vorel borrows the *Corpse Slayer Dagger* and hurls himself into the fight, first wounding Egnails, then protecting the unarmed Tungsten from the dead. Hope's living Hand reaches toward the mummy lord and channels a terrible *Finger of Death*. The magic manifests—but the lord is immune to the deathly energy, and nothing happens to him. The mummies beat Stanley with a wasting curse, knock Union unconscious once more, and finally overwhelm the paralyzed Nash. Nash's final failed death save is not softened by interpretation: **he dies in the crypt**.

Vorel brings the crowned monster down with two savage dagger strikes. Tungsten tends to his injured niece, and Stanley and Hope finish the remaining mummies. Vorel seizes the unarmed Egnails and shoves him bodily into a sarcophagus, closing the lid and sitting atop it. The lich survives, denied both escape and the death that could free him. Once Union is healed again, Stanley presses the ring onto Nash's lifeless finger. There is the smallest movement, but nobody knows whether it means hope.

The sealed room has become a tomb of their own. Hope gathers her companions, the two rescued elves and Nash's body into a circle and **teleports them directly back to Fate Six**. They have escaped Pentel Weiss, at the cost of leaving Badass T behind among the werefolk. Tungsten recognizes Stanley's curse and prepares an antidote with the ghostly Doctor Daffodil, while Hope takes the helm. Privately, the Voice demands that she go back for another artifact high within the prison. She refuses: her friends come first. The ship leaves the prison's dock, crosses back through its portal and prepares for the dangerous purple nebula separating them from the planned journey to Mars.

Belowdecks, Stanley brings Nash before the enchanted wooden face bearing his father's voice. Their conversation becomes more painful than a discussion of magic. Stanley accuses his father of knowing that his family would be lost and his son enslaved, yet permitting it. The voice speaks of the Principle's plans and the Weir's destruction, defending terrible sacrifices as the only road to survival. Those are his assertions, not the last word on the universe. He also says Nash's heart carries a regenerative pattern, implanted in the flesh pits of Clench: a dead body may grow again as long as the dragon-metal heart endures.

With the crew taking a needed rest, Nash's consciousness passes through a fiery inner realm where his dragon self urges him to push outward. By morning an **eyestalk bursts from his dead body's chest**. Vorel understands the frantic blinking, opens the rib cage and frees the small metal heart. It hovers on its own, unable to speak or hold belongings but unmistakably conscious. Nash even summons a spectral hand to make a rude gesture at Stanley. His friends retrieve his equipment. Then Vorel throws the ruined, empty body out into the nebula, where lightning tears it apart.

**Season Two ends with Nash alive in a radically reduced form, Tungsten rescued and the ship away from Pentel Weiss. The current story arc continues into Season Three**: their mission to save creation, the unfinished Triangulosphere, Badass T, the Voice's demands and the Principle remain unresolved.

## Season Three — The continuing Arc Six

**Season boundary only, not a new arc:** [S3E01 — Fly, Fools!](../episodes/S3E01.md) begins immediately after Season Two's ending. The same unresolved threats, characters and mission carry forward. S3E02 continues immediately afterward; **neither session completes Arc Six**.

### A ship caught between Hell and chrome

Nash drifts above Fate Six's deck, little more than a metal heart carrying one impatient eye. If anyone could say how a man who has died twice should feel about being himself, it might be him—but he cannot yet speak. Stanley comes to find him, remembers the companion whose death he could not prevent in the crypt and turns to a new kind of magic. He lowers the small beating engine onto the deck, lifts his megaphone and touches Nash's only eye with the finishing motion of ***Regenerate***.

A fluorescent pink mark blooms where Stanley's finger was. Warmth pulses through the heart. Flesh flows outward in strange little stalks, teeth and lips appear before there is a face to hold them, and Nash begins to talk again. It is not a painless process to watch, although he himself feels surprisingly well. Vorel drapes a cloak over the terrible spectacle, leaving a hole for Nash's eye. The faster restoration promises an end to his helplessness, but it has scarcely begun.

Below, Tungsten tends his exhausted niece Union with the ghostly ship's doctor. Stanley and Hope ask whether the rescued inventor will need one more search before repairing the Triangulosphere. Tungsten smiles with discomfort. He says that the rare material he once left Elhandar to obtain has stayed hidden on his person through all his years of imprisonment. How it can be removed is a delicate subject, and he hopes Dunglestar will help him once they reach Mars. The travelers accept the news with some uncertainty and turn to the helm.

Crooked Hand Row has found a way through the lightning fields. Hope follows his safer course past the wandering space whales, and Fate Six finally breaks into clear space. Mars is ahead—until a vast shadow passes over the wheel. Enormous harpoons strike the vessel's sides. A prisonlike sphere of energy surrounds the hull, and from the other ship a host of armored Hell Knights launches into battle with the elven ghosts. Four familiar servants of Hell leap onto Fate Six behind their resurrected kobold commander, Squirts. Blood Skunk the clown, Steerious Business the minotaur and Doctor Spider have come for Hope, sent by a master who has tired of waiting for her obedience.

Stanley answers with thunder and light, and Vorel meets the minotaur in a contest of flaming blades, furious jaws and stubborn rage. Doctor Spider covers the deck in sickly radiance which torments ally and enemy alike; Stanley disperses it with magic. Squirts falls, rises again and briefly pulls her fellow attackers back from defeat. Nash, who had chosen to grow legs before arms, finds the approaching battle badly timed for his new body. With a leap of magical identity he becomes a vast black dragon—fully furnished with claws and jaws again, but still capable of being trapped in the mind. Doctor Spider imprisons him within an illusory straitjacket. Nash eventually tears himself free, accepting a blast of psychic pain in exchange for movement.

Hope refuses to relent. Vorel fells his raging opposite. Blood Skunk falls and does not rise again. Doctor Spider retreats up the mast behind a ring of flame, raising fallen allies but scorching them as well. When Nash finally fills the crow's nest with acid, Spider is knocked down and the Hell Knights begin losing ground to the ghosts. The defense appears to be working.

Then another portal opens.

The Principle walks onto the deck, bright blue lines crossing his enormous chrome body, each finger ringed with scarlet. His shape rises higher, a crown and a skull forming where a human head had been. The figure proclaims new names and titles that none of the party can yet verify. The hooded Voice appears beside Hope and, for once, sounds frightened: it orders her to flee.

Hope stays. Stanley falls motionless beneath a spoken word of power. She draws her dagger and cuts into the Principle's metal body three times, but cold magic that has conquered lesser opponents barely impresses him. The dragon Nash lunges into the chrome, finding that tooth and claw cannot penetrate it; only a trace of acid burns through. He does not retreat either.

The Principle demands Hope's Hand, claiming he himself ordered Tobacho to recover it and had hoped she would seek its missing piece in Pentel Weiss. Hope holds it up and refuses to give it to him. **The fighting stops only because the story reaches the edge of this episode.** The Hell ship still holds Fate Six, Stanley is stunned, and the Principle stands before them. The quest to restore the Triangulosphere and save creation remains open. So does the **same story arc that began in Season Two**.

### The price of the Hand

Hope had stood her ground before the Principle and the ship had paid for her courage. The chrome giant still held the deck in terror: Stanley could not move beneath the stun of his word, the Hell Knights filled the chains and Nash circled above as a black dragon. Vorel glanced at his immobilized friend, then found another way to fight. He ran straight across the harpoon chain to the attacking ship, scattering Hell Knights into open space, and descended to the gothic cannon room. With a fighter's instinct for triggers and a barbarian's disregard for unfamiliar machines, he turned the infernal guns back upon the Principle. His second shot drove shrapnel into the chrome skull.

Nash roared from above. The dragon's frightful presence threw the Hell Knights into disorder, leaving the ghostly sailors free to cut them down. Then he opened his jaws and poured acid over the chrome figure. It burned: fleshless silver melted and ran. The Principle opened hatches in his body and ejected warm, unfinished humanlike forms, his armor restoring itself as they fell. Their origin was as inexplicable as his power.

The Principle seized Hope by the neck and engulfed the Hand of the Voice in his own liquid-metal hand. She tried to slip free by changing into a slender blond elf, but the chrome fingers followed every contour. An unheard force detonated between the hands. Hope's unnatural Hand was torn from her body and vanished into the Principle's own metal. She collapsed, dying but not yet dead.

The Prince turned to Nash and called him **his son**. He spoke of an old rescue in the desert, the silver road, a new world of steel and a name—**Jesus Chrome**—that Nash was supposedly destined to claim. Nash refused him. Stanley finally broke free of the stun and loosed *Disintegrate* at the extended chrome arm, tearing away a hand and shoulder in a flash. Vorel fired again. Nash's roar kept the enemy army in panic, though the Principle himself would not be frightened. Badly wounded, the silver figure opened a portal, claimed that he had taken what he came for, and offered his supposed son one last invitation: find him in the Solar Mines. Then he escaped with the Hand.

Vorel destroyed the mechanism that held the ships in a bubble of magic. At Stanley's command, Crooked Hand Row sent Fate Six hurtling through a rift. Hope revived under Stanley's healing, and dragon-shaped Nash caught the retreating vessel with his teeth before climbing aboard. The defeated Hell ship dwindled behind them. Only when she woke did Hope understand the full price of freedom: the Hand was gone—but ordinary warmth had returned to her body.

### Sprout and the lives saved

Crooked Hand Row had not taken them directly to Mars. To evade pursuit he steered toward Blast Belly Rock. On the way, Stanley ordered the two surviving enemy officers secured: Doctor Spider and Steerious Business were bound and imprisoned in Fate Six's hidden, warded brig. Their fallen comrades Squirts and Blood Skunk remained dead, although their remains were brought below at Spider's request.

Nash finally resumed his regenerated humanoid form. He and Vorel tried to sleep, speaking anxiously about the Principle's strange claim of fatherhood. A small voice interrupted them. Nash reached back, was bitten and discovered an eye and four-toothed mouth growing directly from his shoulder opposite the familiar extra baby hand. The newcomer could speak, think, feel hunger and fear, but scarcely knew what a name meant. Nash fed it scraps, promised not to cut it out and wondered what his newly grown body was doing.

In another cabin, Tungsten came for Stanley in desperation. Union had taken a devastating blow during the earlier battle and now lay alive but near death, her damaged organs sustained only by Dr. Daffodil's magic. Tungsten urged acceptance, but Stanley refused to abandon another friend. He brought Doctor Spider from the brig. The enemy surgeon demanded his own equipment and, under guard, began the delicate work: a summoned spectral nurse, nimble spider limbs, silk sutures, the healing gun and Stanley's support rebuilt what the Hell Knight's strike had torn apart. After a desperate collapse and more healing magic, Union lived. Doctor Spider earned a promise of release at Blast Belly Rock.

Morning brought breakfast and startling sensations. Hope could taste coffee and feel warmth after so long in the Voice's shadow. Nash introduced his new shoulder companion to the others. He named the timid eye and mouth **Sprout**, who discovered the superior flavor of grapes and wondered whether Nash might be its father. None of them yet knew why Sprout existed. Stanley then offered Hope the same spell he had used on Nash. Pink healing light swirled around the stump; ***Regenerate*** gave her a new, ordinary living arm. She embraced him in gratitude. The stolen Hand of the Voice, however, had not returned.

Fate Six docked at the abandoned dwarven settlement on Blast Belly Rock. Doctor Spider had secretly grafted the dead heads of Squirts and Blood Skunk onto the still-living Steerious's shoulders, an uncanny three-headed figure whose additional faces did not speak. Hope gave Spider her old Hell-supplied necklace and summoning ring to return to Brett, distinct from the collector's ring found in Pentel Weiss. Stanley released Spider and Steerious on the empty rock and warned them not to pursue the ship again. Vorel stared at the ruined kobold head and remembered his lost companion but could not be certain it was the same Squirts.

There were temptations to change course—to recruit an army of other Nashes on Planet Nash, to chase the Principle or the vanished Hand. Tungsten insisted that the concealed component for his unfinished Triangulosphere could be recovered with Dunglestar's help at Mars. The companions chose **Mars first**. As the ship prepared to open its next rift, Union emerged alive on crutches, crossed the deck to Vorel and silently took his hand. He accepted it. Fate Six leapt into the void bound for Mars. **Arc Six continued, with the Principle alive, the Hand stolen, Sprout's origins unknown and the work of saving creation still unfinished.**
