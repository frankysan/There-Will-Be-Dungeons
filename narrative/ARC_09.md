# Arc 09 — The Fall of Elhandar

## I. The forest beneath the stars

**The pirate ship had not gone away.** Beyond the Elhandari vessel's newly sealed hull, Captain Toesalt's ghostly crew still threatened the people who had survived its boarding attack. **Stanley** went with **Vorel** to the bridge while **Nash** stayed near **Angelashi** and their four newborn children. Stanley talked the pirate into withdrawing, but the promise of revenge lingered over the channel. The Elhandari station offered fire support. Vorel agreed, and a shot from the distant refuge struck the departing vessel hard enough to **destroy the ship**. The fate of Toesalt himself, an undead captain, was less certain than the wreckage they could see. Elven crew spoke of sailors swept into space and believed lost. Not every life taken by the boarding had been recovered.[^arc09-1]

Inside the medical room, the barricades came down. **Bok Bok** helped clear the canisters as Stanley and Vorel came to see the family. Angelashi was no longer hiding her **changeling appearance**. She told them she had concealed her nature in Trashburg to avoid being hunted or exploited and said she believed Nash carried something of the same heritage. Nash wanted an answer he could feel in his own body. He tried to change shape into an enormous frog without casting a spell. **Nothing happened.** His dragon heart had made other transformations possible, but this attempt revealed no new ability and settled nothing about his origins. His confusion came out as hurtful remarks about Angelashi's changed face. She asked him to leave. In the corridor Stanley reminded him how readily she had accepted the strange person he already was, and what that trust deserved in return.[^arc09-2]

Then **Elhandar** appeared through the forward windows. Its station stretched in great joined structures through space: a technological cylinder and a **vast glass-enclosed forest**, lit like a green planet caught beneath a dome. The vessel **docked**. The wounded and families disembarked; Bok Bok carried **Chunks**, alive but without his mother. Lyrielle helped arrange Angelashi and the babies upon a floating bed. **Admiral Chrysorius** received Nash, Stanley and Vorel and guided them onto **Rainbow Road**, a current of luminous color carrying passengers into the station. Under soaring lights, thousands of elves greeted the survivors. It was the first actual arrival at the refuge the rescuers had promised, not another view of it from an approaching ship.[^arc09-3]

An elderly **High Elf** sat within a chair held above the floor by moving water. He welcomed Stanley as someone coming home. He took the bard's hands and tried to lead him into an intimate sharing of memories. Stanley **resisted**. The High Elf withdrew instead of forcing his way into Stanley's mind, saying that Elhandar asked each resident to receive an assigned therapist and put the welfare of the station first. The elves told stories of their people before the Scorching and of a great effort to restore damaged worlds. Stanley could listen to those accounts without accepting them as proof of an ancient history he had never seen. The words **“Elhandar first”** sounded less comforting to a man who had spent his life refusing to let others choose his loyalties.[^arc09-4]

The gathering was interrupted by a metal figure stepping from a ring of light. **The Principal**, known to some as **Vladimir**, stood beneath the hall's vaulted ceiling and declared that the recent destruction of **Principal City** was his doing. He claimed he had become something like a **demigod**, offering the elves a future beneath his greater power. Vorel raised his defenses; Stanley reached for **Diplomacy**. The visitor did not begin a fight. Instead, he **grew to several times his height**, drew Stanley toward him without touching him and spoke words meant for him alone: an old friend, presumed lost in the explosion, had survived and would visit. Stanley heard the warning; his companions did not. Whether the visitor truly caused the explosion, had achieved divine rank, or knew the friend's fate was not established merely by his display. He shrank again and passed out through another portal, leaving the elves with a choice they had never requested.[^arc09-5]

The High Elf condemned the Principal's appetite for control and returned to his vision of better lives. **Lyrielle** led the travelers along Rainbow Road toward the forest dome. Humid air and leaves greeted them beneath a roof of impossible scale. **The trees were alive**: Stanley's examination found real growth suffused with pervasive life magic, not painted scenery or a simple illusion. In a clearing beside a lake stood a modest cottage, somehow linked to a family Stanley scarcely remembered. Here Angelashi, the children, Bok Bok and Chunks could rest. Nearby, **Acorn**, Lyrielle's white mink familiar, darted through the room. For the first time since the flight from T'ietkalla, the company had walls that were not breaking around them.[^arc09-6]

The admiral addressed Stanley by a name he had scarcely used: **Valiir Or'Nath**. A scan of his blood had matched Elhandari family records. Stanley acknowledged the name but asked to be called **Stanley**, the person his friends knew. His father, **Delit Or'Nath**, had been an elven scientist who went to Earth and never returned. The admiral offered to search for the father's remains if Stanley would stay and learn something of the people he came from. No search had yet begun, and the promise did not return Delit to the living. Therapists would be **assigned**, Chrysorius clarified, but no one was obliged to submit to an actual session. Stanley chose the cottage and sleep before committing himself to any new Elhandari undertaking.[^arc09-7]

Peace carried another loss inside it. **Bok Bok asked for a funeral for Squirts**. Vorel, who had once taken her ashes into a small vial after Carthax killed her, could no longer produce them. He said he had misplaced or lost them during the fighting on the ship, wondering whether the vacuum or his fiery blade was to blame. No one had seen the loss occur. The absence of the vial could not cancel the fact of her death, and **Chunks was very much alive**. Lyrielle lit a memorial flame. Vorel spoke of the little cleric's courage, and Bok Bok confessed that he would have married her if they had ever reached a gentler life. He threw a burning stick of dynamite into the lake; it exploded without harming the mourners. Then the goblin said he would **care for Chunks as a father**. He wrapped the little one and carried him inside. He could not bring Squirts back, but he could keep her child warm.[^arc09-8]

Nash returned to Angelashi's room and apologized. She apologized too, admitting she had hidden her true face because the world had taught her to fear what it would do to changelings. Together they agreed on names for the four living babies. The one born already in a baby's shape would be **Nash Junior Maggard**. The others, who had first appeared as household objects, would be **Fork, Boot and Pipe**. Angelashi **accepted** the names; they were no longer merely startled descriptions from a delivery room. Two children were boys and two were girls, but no one there assigned every form to a particular sex for the listener. Nash's inherited nature remained a question he could carry while learning to be their father. Exhaustion from the fast-rest pills finally took its toll, and he slept beside his family.[^arc09-9]

Sleep gave Nash no certain answers. In a dream he heard a voice **calling that it was near, hurt and dying**, asking him to find it. The voice seemed to persist after waking, and he sensed a possible contact somewhere in the biodome. **He did not set out to find its owner that night**. Elsewhere Vorel slept among cold fireplace ashes, happier close to earth than upon an elven bed. The companions considered resting for months, perhaps for years, while the world healed without them. What passed was **four days**. The fast-rest debt that had plagued Nash and Stanley cleared, and the company gathered for breakfast on the **fifth morning**. They had not spent another year on Elhandar, learned to command a new ship or repaired the world in their absence.[^arc09-10]

Over food, they weighed what the station offered. A permanent **Teleportation Circle** already stood in one of its science-and-magic districts, Lyrielle said, but no one learned its sigils or traveled through it now. Lyrielle told a story of a former life and an impossible change of form; it was her own story, not a past scene returned to the present. Nash wanted safety for his wife and children. Vorel doubted his right to be called **Fang** when so many of his people were gone. Stanley wanted to understand who had remade the world before charging into another war. **Hope was still absent**. They spoke of the choice she had made, and of the demons that had followed her Hand, without any of them having witnessed her order an invasion of T'ietkalla.[^arc09-11]

**Nobieri** came to the breakfast table with tidings that made the cottage feel less distant from home. He spoke of following the broken trail of the fighting around Razak Val and T'ietkalla, and said **Omnom Cinderborn** had survived and was recovering somewhere aboard the station. **Emenil**, too, had escaped and had been asking after Stanley. Both were **reports**, not reunions. Nobieri offered to seek guidance on the Hand and invited them to visit the tiefling survivors across the forest clearing. He urged Vorel not to surrender his duty as a leader merely because the world had taken away his certainty. As he left, the companions were still sitting at breakfast. Their loved ones might be closer than they had believed, but Hope had not walked into the room, and no promised consultation had yet answered what had become of her.[^arc09-12]

## II. The difficult return

Hope's next road began at a decision that did not sit neatly beside the one she had already made. After the **Council of Clowns** gave her a jack-in-the-box compass, she had taken her infernal company through a **Gate toward Tobacho's Sigil bar**. Yet when her story resumed, **Hope, Steerious Business, Blood Skunk Hendrickson and Dr. Spider** stood in the **empty Council cavern of Pandemonium**, considering another destination. No intervening return to that cavern was shown. Hope still needed a ship to reach the prison the clowns had described, and the **Eye of the Voice** was no more than a promise and a direction. The **copper ring** would have to carry them again, but its strange demand for cruelty had left it without a charge.[^arc09-13]

Hope tried to obtain what the ring required by striking **Dr. Spider**. He endured the blows behind magical protection, and the ring did **not** respond. Spider answered with restraining webbing before the others broke free. **Blood Skunk** then knelt and willingly allowed Hope to **cut off his first outer ear**. It hurt, and the ring awakened. Dr. Spider closed the wound; no lost ear was shown to grow back. Hope opened a Gate to a familiar dock in **Sigil**, the great departure hangar they had used long before their road to White Tower, not the dock where Nash's group had returned after the moon battle. The four stepped into the busy port. There were ships here, but no captain who had agreed to take them to a hidden prison drifting near the **Corinthian Nebula**.[^arc09-14]

At the dockmaster's house they sought a chart, pilot or name that would turn the clown's compass into a route. **Dr. Spider** cursed the dockmaster and trapped him inside **Mental Prison**. The magic **killed him before he could answer alive**. Spider arranged the corpse and used **Speak with Dead**, extracting only fragments: the man knew of phlogiston but nothing useful about their prison or the nebula. A dwarf came calling and was turned away. Hope then tried a quicker escape by opening a Gate toward an Earth destination associated with old travel stories. It revealed **only packed purple ground**; they closed it without crossing. No spell had put them on Earth or aboard a waiting ship.[^arc09-15]

A dwarven trade frigate hung beside the harbor. Its crew refused the strangers' demand for passage. **Steerious killed a dwarf**, and the refusal became a slaughter. Hope crossed the gangway with the Hand's dreadful speed, killing armed defenders who tried to stop her. Steerious attacked with his own weapons and claws; Blood Skunk conjured a furious ape and swarms of wasps; Dr. Spider filled passages with searing light and fire. Many dwarves died. They had not attacked a vessel that was theirs by right or won it through an agreed duel. Their attempt to seize it was violent and plain. When Steerious broke a plank and **fell into the abyss**, the others could not immediately save him. He found a ladder and struggled back toward the dock, hurt but **alive**.[^arc09-16]

Spider set a **Guardian of Faith** near the approach, a grotesque swirling mass shaped like **giggling spectral babies**. It menaced anyone who came close but could not catch a hail of crossbow bolts in the air. The defenders held their vessel. **Blood Skunk** stayed exposed too long under the volleys. His conjured ape fell and he was struck until he **collapsed unconscious**, not dead beyond recovery. Hope opened a Gate beneath him into **Spider's laboratory in Hell** and fled through it. Spider followed and **healed Blood Skunk back to consciousness**. Steerious reached the entrance and dove through last. The ring closed the way to Sigil. They had all survived; they had **not** captured the ship, hired a navigator or found the Eye. The bodies left on the docks could not be recalled when the gateway shut.[^arc09-17]

Dr. Spider led them through the **Arachnoidica Spider Ward**, past workers and helpless figures wrapped in webs, to a train beneath Hell's streets. The station attendant asked for soul coins, then waived the fare for Spider and his guests. They rode to the **Kaiser Casino**, where the smiling host **Brett** received them. He returned **Steerious's greatsword**, the weapon the minotaur had surrendered to Hesterbrook on the River Styx. How Brett recovered it was not shown, nor did the gesture restore every other possession sacrificed for passage. Brett treated Blood Skunk's wounds, but no one established that the missing ears and toe had grown back. He could offer theories about phlogiston and prisoners, not a map to **Pentel Weiss** or a ship to reach it. Hope had spent lives and promises and still possessed only a compass.[^arc09-18]

Back at the laboratory, she tried another form of Gate: **calling the Principal by his name**. **Vladimir actually appeared**, furious at being brought before her. He gripped her throat, ordering her never to summon him that way again, but did not wound her in that hold. He professed ignorance of the prison's location and wondered whether a traveler with Nash's strange relationship to time might help. This was his speculation, not proof Nash could navigate the prison. After the Principal departed, Hope needed another charge to reach her old companion. **Blood Skunk consented to the cutting of his other outer ear**. The wound was real, and the ring woke once more. His earlier missing **left little toe** had not been returned to him by this act. These were **two distinct ear sacrifices** in one terrible day, not a single wound told twice.[^arc09-19]

Hope knew **Nash Maggard** by his true name. She called to him across the planes, and the Gate opened onto a green forest where a little cottage stood. **Nash was pulled bodily from Elhandar into Spider's Hell laboratory**. He saw Hope and her strange allies for the first time since White Tower. She urged him to return quickly; he followed. Together they **crossed back through the open portal into Elhandar**. **Steerious, Blood Skunk and Dr. Spider stayed behind in Hell**. There was no fourth companion waiting beside the cottage to welcome her, and no reconciliation with Stanley or Vorel before the gate closed. But Nash and Hope truly stood in the same place again, alive and facing the consequences of the roads they had taken.[^arc09-20]

The first conversation was not easy. Nash told Hope about **Elhandar's forest**, about **Angelashi**, and about the **four babies** who had been born while she was gone. He named **Fork, Boot, Pipe and Nash Junior**, and Hope tried to understand what she had missed. Nash found **Stanley and Vorel** out hunting with **Bok Bok and Chunks**. They came back armed and wary. Stanley asked Lyrielle to prepare the station's defenses as a precaution; no shield activation or enemy attack followed from that order. The four friends met in the cottage. **Hope began to cry**. Stanley challenged her for carrying the **Hand of the Voice**, for abandoning them and for what had happened to T'ietkalla. Vorel would not accept that they had been helpless without her. Nash urged the others to hear her without violence. Stanley slid an appearance-changing ring back to Hope with the point of his rapier, but **did not say he forgave her or trusted her**.[^arc09-21]

**Chunks** climbed toward Hope and repeated bitter accusations heard from adults, including the thought that she had killed his mother. The child was alive and grieving without being able to know how Carthax's beam had struck Squirts. **Bok Bok** spoke awkwardly of wanting Hope to be a mother to Chunks, while Vorel explained that another **Tedna** had survived in another life as **Badass T**. Neither possibility became a marriage, an adoption or a new romance at that table. Bok Bok said he would remain near the family and help protect the children; **Vorel took Chunks along for the visit to the refugees**. Nash's wife and four newborns remained by the cottage. The fellowship was together enough to walk toward its survivors, but had not found the trust that would make it whole.[^arc09-22]

The path led to a clearing of little houses with a well and a shared fire, where survivors of **T'ietkalla** had been sheltered. **Nobieri and Emenil** came to meet them. Nobieri embraced Hope. She declined a chair set apart for a queen and sat among the others. She had brought the **Hand** home believing that its terrible strength could rebuild or defend her kingdom. Now she said that possessing it had helped draw danger to the city and offered to release the people from their obligation to her. The account was sincere as far as anyone in the clearing could know, but it did **not** establish that she had knowingly ordered Carthax's invasion or every demon that followed. **One man chose to leave with his wife and child**. The others present remained and listened, their loyalty now visibly a choice rather than a command.[^arc09-23]

Nobieri marked the willing with **ash** and asked them to send it into the fire. **Nash, Vorel and Hope** joined the rite; **Stanley refused**, telling the others he was no tiefling and Hope was not his queen. The light changed and the flame rose into the shape of an **armored female tiefling** who called herself **Abegir**. The figure spoke to Hope of leadership as an obligation to **protect living people**, not a pursuit of forgiveness from gods or power for its own sake. It told a history of its people and promised a better fate, but its claims of divine identity and command over souls remained beyond what those gathered could test. **The fiery manifestation was real and visible**. It departed back into the fire, leaving Emenil shaken and Stanley no less skeptical of easy religious answers.[^arc09-24]

The survivors told stories. One ranger spoke of a pregnant companion lost when buildings fell; others described what they had seen in the attack and what they had endured before it. These were **witness accounts**, not new battles taking place around the fire. **Stanley** introduced himself as **Valiir Or'Nath** and spoke of the poverty and disappointments that had shaped him, of his sacrifice and the unkind answer he felt he had received from the divine. **Nash** described the **Solar Mines**, where he said workers had been enslaved and left to die; he recounted discovering that truth and turning his anger on a cruel overseer. His words were memories related to his friends, not a journey backward to the mines. **Vorel**, in turn, recited the titles he had given himself, ending with a determination to be a **Keeper of Stories**. No listener appointed him king or returned Razak Val to life. They could only decide what to carry forward.[^arc09-25]

Asked what they would now call their home, **Hope** gave the clearing a name: **the Harbor**. Its people would be **Harborers**, a family willing to help anyone who had lost their way. **Nobieri proclaimed the choice** before the gathered survivors. It was a real founding of a small refugee community, **not** a restoration of all T'ietkalla or a functioning sanctuary stretching across the stars. Around them stood a few cottages and people who had decided to stay. The work of making that promise true remained almost entirely ahead.[^arc09-26]

Later **Emenil** brought Stanley into her cottage. She apologized for the hope her faith had sometimes demanded of him and **said she loved him**. He was relieved that she lived and grateful for what they had shared, but he could not promise love or a relationship while his own sense of himself remained unsettled. After he left, he **heard a crash inside** and understood that she had broken the objects of her shrine. He had not watched her strike them, nor had a god appeared to renounce her. Elsewhere Vorel caught the **scent of lizardfolk** in the forest. He hoped that it might lead to Omnom or other survivors and **planned to search after supper**. The scent did not deliver a person before nightfall, and the proposed search was not yet carried out. **Hope remained among the friends who had questioned her**. There was still much to answer for, but for the first time since White Tower, all four could choose what to do next from the same place.[^arc09-27]

## III. The key and the first hunt

The cottage in Elhandar's forest had become a refuge for an uneasy fellowship. **Hope** had rejoined **Nash, Stanley and Vorel**, but neither the founding of **the Harbor** nor their shared breakfast could make their old trust whole. When **Admiral Chrysorius** arrived that evening, he brought dinner guests: four elven therapists, each assigned to one of the companions, and a young elf in dark clothing. The therapists offered introductions rather than treatment; Elhandar required an assignment, not attendance at a session. **Lyrielle** prepared the meal while Nash asked questions of the enthusiastic stranger designated to help him. The doctors' elaborate names were difficult for the travelers to catch. One visitor, however, carried something far more important than a proposed conversation.[^arc09-28]

An elven doctor placed a **large, ornate key** in Stanley's hand. She said his father, **Delit Or'Nath**, had left it in Elhandar long ago with instructions that his son should receive it. It would open something within **Villa Or'Nath**, she believed, though no one in the visiting party could point to the lock. Stanley took it as a genuine inheritance, not a promise that someone might search for an heirloom later. The younger elf introduced himself as **Dilo**, fascinated by Stanley's extraordinary magic and his conduct aboard the pirate-struck ship. He wished to become Stanley's apprentice, to learn how a hero judged danger and opponents. Stanley did not bind himself to a student that night. He asked Dilo to return the following morning after he had consulted his friends.[^arc09-29]

The admiral also spoke privately to Vorel. **Omnom Cinderborn** had survived Razak Val, he said, and was somewhere among Elhandar's refugees, though deeply wounded and struggling to live among strangers. Vorel had already scented lizardfolk in the forest. He called into the trees, and at last **Omnom emerged in person**. The warrior's face had been torn open; missing scales, scars and bandages covered him, and casts held injured limbs. He told of resisting demons for nights after his people had scattered. Those battles were Omnom's account, not scenes the companions now witnessed. Seeing a familiar lizardfolk alive brought Vorel relief that lasted only until Omnom noticed **Hope** standing behind him.[^arc09-30]

Omnom lunged at her. **Stanley cast Hold Person**, stopping the attack before a blow landed. **Vorel carried his paralyzed countryman into the trees** and waited for the spell to release him. The accusations came almost at once. Omnom blamed Hope's old rocket attack for the deaths of loved ones at Razak Val, reminded her of the railway station burned in T'ietkalla, and accused her of deliberately leading the demon army into the city. Hope admitted firing the rocket and acknowledged the fire, but said she had believed the demons were drawn by the power of her **Hand**, and that she fled through a portal hoping to pull them away. Her explanation did not erase what had happened; neither did Omnom's accusation prove that she had commanded the invasion. Vorel listened without forgiving everything or declaring judgment upon her.[^arc09-31]

The wounded warrior begged Vorel to kill him, speaking of dishonor and the grief of being left alive. Vorel refused. **Omnom carried memories of Razak Val that no one else could replace**. Rather than answer despair with a blade, Vorel proposed a **hunt on the following morning**, a test of courage and perhaps a way for enemies to stand together without pretending the past had changed. Omnom agreed. He departed to find a place to sleep; no hunting party went out that evening, and Hope had not earned his trust. In the cottage the others turned toward the inheritance Stanley still held, a key with no visible door.[^arc09-32]

They searched the house. **Nash and Vorel** eventually went outside while **Hope stayed beside Stanley**, who remembered an old bedtime poem from his parents. Its words spoke of green fields and **a song at the end of the woods**, a meaning bound up in **Valiir Or'Nath**, the name he had set aside for so much of his life. As he recited, **the key lifted from his fingers** and drifted upstairs. He stopped, and it fell. With Hope singing alongside him, the key rose again into an **invisible keyhole** on the upper landing. Stanley turned it. Gears moved inside the walls, and a stairway descended toward a **third floor that could not have fitted inside the cottage's outer shape**. The secret did not open because the elves had ordered him to accept their version of himself. It opened to the voice of a child his father had known.[^arc09-33]

Stanley and Hope climbed into a **hidden study** whose walls were crowded with star charts, planetary diagrams, an enormous mechanical globe of Earth, and instruments they did not know how to operate. A vast book answered Stanley's name and voice. He opened **the Book of Adjacencies**, Delit's own journals, and found pages that could turn toward a question rather than simply forward. Hope could not read the Elvish writing. An entry addressed to **Valiir** offered a father's love and warnings: Delit wrote as though he knew something of his son's future suffering and his own death, spoke of an anomalous being called **N4SH**, and urged Stanley to resist the **Weir**. He mentioned the **Solar Mines**, the woman he hoped would become Stanley's mother, and a possible meeting beyond death in the **Feywild**. These were real words preserved in a real journal, but the father's foreknowledge and predictions had not thereby become settled history. Stanley let Hope embrace him; it was a small kindness between friends whose larger wrongs remained.[^arc09-34]

A black, jewel-studded **Mask of Elton IV** lay among the instruments. It seemed able to attach to a face without straps and, by its description, to alter the outcomes of future choices at a terrible psychic cost. The instructions demanded **four completed long rests while wearing it** before the power could be used. **Stanley did not put it on or attune it that night**. He studied the book instead. Delit's notes proposed that the Weir had torn at the ordinary boundaries of Earth, making impossible journeys through time and adjacency possible; he speculated that **the Principal's** science had produced beings like N4SH. A marked position on the globe seemed to correspond to the **Solar Mines**. Nothing in the room proved that all alternate Nashes arose by that route. Meanwhile, as Hope left the study, **the hooded Voice appeared to her alone**, demanding that she hasten toward the prison linked to the **Eye of the Voice**. She asked for time. She opened no Gate and recovered no Eye.[^arc09-35]

Morning came after Stanley's night among the pages. **Vorel** fashioned plain hunting tools from wood, bone and the remains of previous kills: a spear for Stanley, a shield and axe for Nash, a blowgun for Hope, and a rough club for the hopeful apprentice. They did not abandon their magical arms forever for the sake of sport. **Pipe**, one of Nash and Angelashi's four infants, seemed to have vanished from the cottage. Nash searched anxiously, then used his strange right-eye sense to look for the child. The missing baby was **in Nash's own pocket, shaped like a pipe**. When retrieved, Pipe turned into a laughing infant. No abductor had come through the night, and no other child was missing. Angelashi remained at home with the children as the hunters prepared to depart.[^arc09-36]

**Dilo returned** in practical clothes and received permission to accompany Stanley **on this outing**, not a lifelong promise of apprenticeship. **Omnom** arrived as well, still scarred and unsteady but willing to hunt beside Vorel. He showed the **Necklace of Fireballs** in a concealed pouch of his scales. Vorel declined to take it; the necklace remained with Omnom. Dilo spoke of their quarry: **Chester**, an immense bear-and-dinosaur creature allegedly bred centuries ago in an Elhandari laboratory, which had escaped and killed hunters. The laboratory experiments and the deaths of earlier expeditions were **Dilo's account**, not evidence the company had inspected. **Stanley quietly warned Vorel** that the Principal had hinted an old enemy might have survived, and that a pupil eager to accompany them could conceal **Diontalus**. Vorel agreed to watch Dilo, but there was no proof of his guilt; Dilo appeared to seek reassurance from Nash.[^arc09-37]

The six set out with **Lyrielle's map**. Vorel insisted that Nash bathe to diminish the scent of his body; Nash actually washed in a basin Omnom helped move, though no spell made him scentless. They met **Fitz**, a tiefling from the Harbor, gathering berries. He offered food and asked them to bring back any strange northern mushrooms for **Nobieri**. No mushroom was collected yet. Talking squirrels begged for nuts and went on their way; an elven fisherman spoke of earlier Chester hunters who had failed to return and offered Nash a piece of chewing grass. None of these witnesses had led the company to a den or shown them the creature. With daylight fading, they chose **a lake camp** over a blind pursuit. Vorel and Stanley caught fish; Dilo prepared supper. Within **Stanley's Tiny Hut**, the company slept until morning.[^arc09-38]

Then the woods woke with a distant bellow. It sounded large, perhaps ursine, but no one had seen its owner. The group moved north together rather than separate, with **Vorel and Omnom** concealed low among the trees and Stanley seeking higher ground. Heavy steps and snapping branches sounded closer. Nash supplied Vorel with a **wax-wrapped fish head smeared in eggs**, and Vorel lit the wax and threw the bait into the brush. It began a small fire beneath a dead tree. **Stanley put the flames out with Prestidigitation**, leaving smoke and the smell of bait behind. Somewhere beyond the leaves, something heavy was moving. **Chester had not yet appeared or been fought**. The company waited with its weapons ready, unaware how much worse this hunt could become than the search for an escaped animal.[^arc09-39]

## IV. The forest's trap and the dragon heart

The smoke from Vorel's extinguished lure drifted through Elhandar's northern woods. **Stanley** glimpsed a patch of brown fur beyond the brush, enough to warn Vorel but not yet enough to name the animal. **Nash held Haste ready** for the moment the battle began. **Hope, Omnom and Dilo** shifted positions, their attempts at stealth imperfect among roots and broken branches. Then the creature stepped into view: **Chester**, an enormous upright bear with thick hind legs, stunted forelimbs, a reptilian head and a crown of great branching antlers. There was nothing gentle in its approach. Stanley became a **Tyrannosaurus rex**, a roar answering a roar, and Nash released the spell upon Vorel. The companions had found the quarry, even if its supposed laboratory origins remained unproved.[^arc09-40]

The battle was swift and savage. **Vorel's flaming sword** opened a cut in Chester's leg; Nash struck with cold magic while maintaining Haste. **Stanley caught the creature in a crushing dinosaur bite**, but it tore free and hurled itself high into a tree. Steel and teeth ripped it as it rose, leaving its insides hanging among the branches. **Hope ran forward and pulled upon the trailing entrails**. Chester and part of the tree toppled onto her. The beast **died in the fall**. Hope was alive but pinned beneath the carcass, barely able to breathe, until **Vorel tipped the enormous body aside** and helped her crawl free. Omnom declared again that Vorel deserved to be the **Great Fang**. It was praise from one survivor to another, not a coronation by a restored Razak Val. Dilo looked shaken, and for a brief moment the hunt seemed over.[^arc09-41]

Six **talking deer** approached to thank the hunters for removing their tormentor. They told stories of frightened woodland creatures, begged for dried fruit and spoke of unusual mushrooms in northern caves. Dilo said that talking animals were hardly strange here. Yet Stanley felt something wrong beneath their manners. He offered food, then **cast Dispel Magic** upon one apparent deer. An **elf with dark veins and shining red eyes** stood where the animal had been. The other five threw off their disguises and attacked. Their gratitude had been bait. **Hope** fought among the spellcasters; **Mass Suggestion** briefly drove her and Omnom to behave like squirrels, while cold and entangling magic wounded and restrained the party. Nash answered with **Chain Lightning** against hostile targets. Dilo seemed to help, striking an enemy Hope had seized. No one had yet seen the signal that would betray him.[^arc09-42]

When Stanley prepared **Destructive Wave**, **Dilo called a warning to the mages**. One produced a wand and enclosed **Hope inside a sealed floating blue sphere**. The captor threw the controller to Dilo, who caught it and **ran west, then turned north toward the mountains**, carrying her prison with him. Stanley's wave tore through the remaining assailants. **All six hostile elves died**, but Dilo had escaped the blast. Hope was alive and conscious within the moving sphere. She tried to **Teleport** out using her Hand and **failed**, spending the magic without breaking the prison. **Stanley became a giant eagle** and flew ahead to find the route, then returned to guide Vorel, Nash and Omnom toward a cave high in the rocks. The old suspicion about Dilo was no longer merely distrust: his **betrayal was witnessed**. His true master, however, had not yet revealed himself.[^arc09-43]

The pursuers entered a damp cavern crowded with enormous glowing mushrooms. Nash cut and **ate part of a blue one** against his companions' warnings. Its light began shining from his mouth, dazzling anyone who looked straight at him. He used the strange glow as a lantern while they advanced. The cave held tracks of something very large and old, but no proof Chester had returned to life; its corpse remained in the forest. At the next chamber **Dilo stood above them with Hope imprisoned beside him**. He demanded either her body and the **Hand of the Voice**, or Stanley's body in exchange for her safety. Stanley attempted to dispel the sphere. **It remained intact**. The captive watched from within as more spherical devices appeared beside her, **empathy grenades** that might destroy her will before anyone could reach her. Dilo's voice now carried a familiarity too terrible to ignore.[^arc09-44]

**Diontalus** acknowledged his name. He claimed the **Principal** had helped free him and said he wished to return to the Feywild in a body entirely his own. Hope, he argued, would not survive the use he had in mind for her. These were threats from an enemy, not a bargain proven safe for anyone. Stanley offered a **duel** instead. He asked **Nash, Vorel and Omnom** to stand aside, though none promised to watch forever if their friend was taken. Stanley became a giant ape and struck Dilo's body. **The young elf fell motionless**, and a separate **blue spectral figure of Diontalus** emerged in his place. The apparently eager student had been a vessel for an enemy. Whether the elf had ever consented, or could be saved, remained unknown; he showed no breath or movement afterward.[^arc09-45]

Diontalus fought with a huge **ethereal scythe** that left Stanley weak in body as well as wounded. Ordinary fists could not reliably hurt him. A radiant blast did, but the enemy kept coming. Stanley abandoned the one-on-one contest, reached Hope's ledge by magic and seized the prison's controller. He broke the wand, yet **the sphere did not open**. He shouted the agreed **“lemon tree”** signal for help. **Nash became an adult black dragon**, and **Vorel** charged with rage and burning steel. **Omnom** tried to grapple the spectral adversary but fell across Vorel instead. Tangled roots bound them as the fight spread through the cavern. Hope worked inside her prison, defusing grenades among those that crowded her, while at least one she touched flashed and blinded her. She had **not escaped**.[^arc09-46]

From Omnom's concealed pouch **Vorel took the Necklace of Fireballs** and threw the entire chain at Diontalus. Its remaining beads burst together in a tremendous blaze, scorching enemy and friends and burning away the roots that pinned the fighters. The necklace was **spent**, not returned to Omnom for another use. Diontalus answered with a chain of destructive lightning. **Omnom was killed outright**. When the smoke cleared, **only his severed feet remained**. No healing spell could make an unhurt body from what the companions had lost. He had returned to Vorel in E118 wounded but alive, had joined the hunt seeking some measure of honor, and now died fighting in a cave far from his ruined home. Hope still watched from her blue cell, unable to join the grief gathering outside.[^arc09-47]

A **scarred black dragon** burst from deeper in the mountain and threw itself upon Diontalus. The stranger managed to seize the spectral foe and hurt him, even as the scythe tore into its own wounded body. At last **Diontalus retreated through solid rock**. He was **not killed or permanently bound**. The battle had gone too far for Nash. In dragon shape he lost control, fire and acid lashing out against his friends, and **Stanley collapsed unconscious**. Nash too fell. **Vorel spent Stanley's healer's kit stabilizing him**, then sheltered the survivors and gathered the remains of Omnom beside Dilo's motionless body. The captive Hope was alive, still rolling within her sphere. The unknown dragon, carved and scarred by past captivity, used its failing strength to **heal Stanley, Nash and Vorel**. It did not restore Omnom or open Hope's prison.[^arc09-48]

The dragon identified itself as the prisoner the companions had encountered long ago in the desert of Earth. It saw something familiar in **Nash's engineered heart**: the trace of **its mother**. Nash called into his own chest. The dragon shape opened around a great mechanical heart, and a **golden spectral mother dragon** emerged to comfort her wounded son. Their names came through the air in sounds the companions could scarcely fix, but the embrace and the lullaby were real to those who saw them. The dragon spoke of ancient battles, the **Weir**, and people who had made pacts that ruined Earth. He blamed **the Principal** for more of that damage, though even he could not reconcile every part of the story he told. The creature claimed Nash had been manufactured through experiments using material from many lives. **His account was not a recovered laboratory ledger**, nor did it erase Angelashi and the four children who were Nash's family now.[^arc09-49]

Before dying, the wounded dragon offered **his own natural dragon-metal heart** for a remedy. He described a device called a **Rubistic Triangulosphere**, built by skilled engineers around the heart and carried to **Earth's hottest point**, where it would supposedly descend to the center and seal away the Weir's influence. **No blueprint, working machine or test existed in the cave**. The dragon had entrusted the companions with advice and a possible component, not a completed salvation. Nash asked the spectral mother what he meant to her. Her answer came as feeling and touch more than words; it did **not** establish that she was Nash's biological mother. Then the black dragon **died**. Its body went still, and the golden apparition withdrew into Nash's manufactured heart. The two hearts were never the same object, and the proposed heart for the machine still lay in the fallen dragon's body.[^arc09-50]

**Stanley pressed the controller's detonator**. At last Hope's sphere vanished, leaving its grenades to tumble harmlessly to the stone. **Hope landed free and alive**, long after the fight she had been forced to watch. Nash told her what the dragon had said; she had **not heard those explanations through the sealed prison**. The broken controller rejoined into a functioning wand when Stanley brought its pieces together. **Vorel carefully extracted the enormous natural heart**, a black, glassy mass far too heavy to carry casually. The work took hours. It was not placed into Nash or delivered to Elhandar. Nash's smaller manufactured heart remained alive inside him, bearing the spectral mother's withdrawn presence without transferring her into the new relic. The corpse in the chamber gave up no other clear promise.[^arc09-51]

Farther back in the cave the company found a **tall black metal spike** amid discarded scales and dried blood. Merely approaching it hurt their heads, and even magic could not easily lift it. They did not identify its maker or learn whether the dragon had once carried it inside his own body. Rather than leave the extracted heart visible, **Nash shaped an inclined hollow in the earth**. Stanley used the repaired wand to guide the huge heart in its resilient sphere beneath the spike, then released it. Nash **closed and disguised the soil**. The heart was **buried and hidden in the mountain**, not transported to Earth or offered to an engineer. Afterward the dead dragon's remaining body **rose and dissolved in light**, leaving no bones behind. The party gathered mushrooms and a few other traces and returned toward the place where Chester had fallen. Omnom and Dilo did not similarly vanish or revive.[^arc09-52]

The path took them past the bodies of the six false deer. Vorel took a bit of fine elven cloth and one of Chester's teeth, but no papers identified who had supplied the mages or why. Hours later the companions reached **the Gatherers' Lodge**, a group of timber buildings by water. Two **elven ghosts** greeted them, moving furniture and offering rooms, water and brightly colored sweets. One went to verify their claim that Chester was dead; he had not returned before they settled inside. Their strange hosts had not attacked them. Nash distrusted the candy, and **Hope changed her appearance to pretend it had transformed her**. The jest became a mock struggle in which Stanley and Vorel joined, not a duel to the death. **No full overnight rest took place before the episode ended**, and nothing had been built from the dragon's heart. The companions had reached shelter with a possible plan, the memory of Omnom, and **Diontalus still somewhere beyond the walls**.[^arc09-53]

## V. A promise under the Fishbird

The **Gatherers' Lodge** was quiet enough for the companions to quarrel over sweets. **Hope**, disguised to fool **Nash**, pretended the ghostly hosts' candy had transformed her. **Stanley** joined the game and pressed another bonbon into Nash's mouth. Nash resisted until **Vorel** made him swallow; then the frightened wizard ran outside and **vomited the candy back onto the grass**. Nobody was poisoned or turned into a ghost. The two elven spirits tending the lodge offered water and food. After all they had survived in the mountain, their foolish wrestling was a brief way to feel ordinary again.[^arc09-54]

One of the ghosts returned from the hunting ground and reported seeing **Chester's corpse** and the six dead spellcasters who had worn the shapes of deer. He suspected the elves belonged to a local council of mages, but could not verify their allegiance. He said he had **sent word to the High Elf**; no answer arrived at the lodge. The household offered a **noble speeder**, a wooden vehicle raised by unfamiliar magic, that the travelers might borrow. They discussed the device the dying dragon had suggested, a construction built around the massive natural heart still **buried in the mountain**. **Vorel named the imagined machine Ruby**. It had a name, but no engineer, blueprint or completed part. Nash spoke of scattered childhood memories; neither those recollections nor Stanley's father's journal established that Nash and **the Principal** were the same person.[^arc09-55]

**Hope and Stanley** stepped outside with beers to inspect the speeder. Hope touched its controls and launched herself from the seat as the runaway vehicle **broke through the barn wall into the trees**. It survived the accident. As they recovered it, Stanley spoke of **White Tower** and how harshly he had judged her choice. He had accepted a bargain of his own when he feared his friends were lost; it was harder now to condemn her for every compromise. **He apologized and welcomed her back** without trusting the Hand's patron. Hope believed removing the living Hand would kill her, but nobody tested that claim. She suggested her travel ring might help locate **Delit Or'Nath**, Stanley's missing father. **No ring was used or father summoned**. They rode the speeder together and returned to the lodge, their renewed friendship real but not a solution to the world outside.[^arc09-56]

The four companions **completed a long rest without attack**. Yet the injuries left by Diontalus's scythe did not heal: **Stanley and Vorel** still felt their drained strength. Next morning Hope drove the borrowed speeder toward **the Harbor**, Stanley and Vorel balancing aboard while Nash **floated behind in a carefully tied harness** using his own enchanted garment. They arrived safely rather than crash or fly to Earth. **Nobieri** received the bag of unusual **mushrooms** Hope had collected in the cave. He warned that one specimen of each might not be enough and asked permission for a cautious investigation. Hope agreed. **No new expedition actually departed or tested a preparation**. While trying to release Nash's harness, Hope hurt his finger; Nobieri set it right with a prayer but **did not restore the lost hit points or cure the scythe damage**.[^arc09-57]

In **Emenil's cottage**, Stanley warned that **Diontalus** might target the refugees, though neither he nor the others knew how many servants the archfey controlled. Emenil refused to abandon the station that had offered them sanctuary without first warning its leader. Hope and Stanley agreed that **the people of the Harbor should remain in their homes**, while the party sought to draw danger away. Emenil offered something other than escape: a handful of precious **fish flakes** left by her late husband, a priest of **Malhya, the Fishbird**. She disrobed and entered the lake to seek communion, and Stanley followed. The others were invited. **The episode ended before the rite took effect**, before a Fishbird appeared or anyone's lingering injuries were healed.[^arc09-58]

When the rite continued, **Emenil and all four companions** swallowed flakes from her locket and discovered they could **breathe underwater**. They joined hands on the lakebed, then the familiar floor gave way to an apparently endless depth. A huge **kraken** passed them without a fight. A laughing, rainbow-scaled winged fish as large as a whale arrived and shaped a **platform of solid water** beneath their feet. Emenil called it **Malhya**. Whatever its authority among the gods, the visitor and its strange acts were visibly present. Stanley argued with it over the sacrifices he had made, and **vanished into darkness**. Nash demanded his return and **vanished too**. Neither had been shown killed; neither heard the conversation that followed while he was gone.[^arc09-59]

**Vorel and Emenil** pleaded for Stanley and Nash to return. Malhya finally relented; both **reappeared alive**, shaken but present. Stanley apologized. Vorel offered the visitor a view of the **Eye of Groxl**, whose anger he felt rising within him. Malhya recognized something dangerous in it, but its cryptic warnings did not settle the Eye's divine history. With Vorel's permission it touched him and **drew away blue strands of Diontalus's harm**. It did the same for Stanley. **Both were actually cured of the ability losses** that sleep had failed to heal. The Fishbird offered no new power over the Eye and no promise that Diontalus could not hurt them again.[^arc09-60]

Malhya could not simply hand them a finished **Ruby**. It said an **engineer capable of impossible work** would be needed and directed them to **the High Elf and his court**. The engineer's whereabouts remained vague; no blueprint or instrument to cool Earth appeared. The watery expanse dissolved and the five returned to the **Harbor lake**. **Emenil stayed beneath the surface**, but answered Stanley's magical message and asked for time. **She remained alive**. Nobieri listened to the travelers' account and **gave Hope a cellstone**. The refugees remained in their own clearing as the four prepared to question the elves. No one had gone to collect the buried heart or completed its proposed machine.[^arc09-61]

At **Villa Or'Nath**, **Angelashi** welcomed Nash. Their four children and the household were ready to travel. **Lyrielle** agreed to accompany them after a limited warning about Diontalus, and Stanley took his father's **ornate key**. They left the speeder at the cottage, walked for hours and followed **Rainbow Road** to the central hall. **Angelashi, the children, Lyrielle and the smaller companions waited at reception** while Nash, Hope, Stanley and Vorel entered the High Elf's chamber. An inconsistent spoken roster seemed to name **Squirts** among the travelers, though her death and memorial had occurred and **no resurrection was shown here**. **Chunks** was the living child known to be with the family. Their presence at the station did not give them knowledge of every private word the four would now hear.[^arc09-62]

The **High Elf** listened as Stanley described Diontalus, the dead mages and the danger of staying. The elf told his own history of the archfey, describing ancient campaigns against demons and Elhandar, and a prison from which Stanley might inadvertently have helped release him. The testimony was not an independently witnessed history. The High Elf offered a **meeting rather than another battle**. With the group's assent he summoned **Diontalus in ghostly form**. Stanley asked him to return to the Feywild and leave the elves alone. Diontalus wanted Stanley to accompany him, stay in his court and abandon obligations that bound him to the Wastes. The same being who had killed Omnom now spoke as if his quarrel with Stanley were an argument between old acquaintances.[^arc09-63]

The hooded **Voice appeared to Hope alone**, demanding that she kill Diontalus and take his power. She **refused twice**. It supplied a supposed phrase that could undo an archfey, but Hope neither spoke it to Diontalus nor tested the rule. The other companions did not hear this private exchange. **Stanley** argued that he still owed help to his friends, had yet to face **the Principal and the Weir**, and could not abandon them. At last he won a narrow promise: **Diontalus agreed to return to his own Feywild court and wait for Stanley**, and to help against the Principal when called. The threat remained inside the bargain—if Stanley failed to come, or died first, **the elves of Elhandar would suffer**. Stanley accepted the conditional accord rather than surrender his body or leave immediately. **No departure or battle against the Principal was shown before the scene ended**.[^arc09-64]

The four had gained a reprieve but not safety. Beyond the audience chamber **Angelashi and the children still waited on Elhandar**, their refuge intact for the moment. **Emenil** was alive at the Harbor lake, **Omnom** was dead, the dragon's **natural heart remained buried** and the machine called Ruby existed only in hope. Stanley had spoken for his friends against an enemy he could not destroy. Diontalus had promised to wait, but his words carried the menace of return. Neither a new oath from the High Elf nor a visible journey into the Feywild resolved whether he would keep the terms. The companions still needed someone who could build what the dragon had imagined. And the station around them had no reason yet to expect the fleet moving toward it.[^arc09-65]

## VI. The broken refuge

The accord with Diontalus had not ended the companions' need for help. Still in the High Elf's chamber, **Stanley** asked who might build the impossible device around the buried dragon heart. The elf named **Tungsten Wheel**, an engineer said to have shaped the station's spire and shipyards before vanishing decades earlier. He believed someone close to Wheel might be found on an **Elhandari terraforming platform on Mars**, though neither the person's name nor the engineer's present location was certain. He gave **Nash real teleportation-circle glyphs for Mars and Elhandar**. No circle was cast, no engineer found and no Ruby built; the natural heart remained hidden beneath the black spike.[^arc09-66]

**Diontalus** had more to say before the company left. He pressed Stanley to come to the Feywild once their business was done and handed him a dark **wooden ocarina**, promising to answer its call. **Stanley received the instrument**, but its summoning power remained untested. Vorel demanded an explanation of the attacks they had survived. Diontalus claimed responsibility for the **deer-disguised mages** and even for the **ghostly pirates** that had assaulted Angelashi's ship. His admission did not make his bargain trustworthy. The archfey had agreed to wait, not to surrender the power with which he threatened the people around Stanley. Their uneasy conversation ended when alarms rang through the station.[^arc09-67]

A **castle escorted by a vast airship fleet** appeared beyond Elhandar's glass. Blue projectiles struck the defenses, the shields failed and evacuation orders sounded. **Armed mercenaries teleported into the audience chamber**. At their center stood a man with **three speaking heads**, draped in purple and hovering on a disk. He introduced himself as **Henatar Grape**, though the pronunciation was uncertain, and claimed that the **Mind Master** had contracted for **Hope and Vorel**. He alleged that **Tobacho Fleur** had been betrayed to the **Lady of Pain** and imprisoned; the companions had not witnessed such a transfer of power. Even the question of which captive was wanted alive was muddled in his demands. He had brought soldiers and a fleet, not proof of his employer's story.[^arc09-68]

**Stanley answered with Destructive Wave**. The High Elf drew living vines around himself, raised enormous trees and turned wind against the attackers. **Hope and Vorel** fought toward the commander; **Nash** rained lightning down on the soldiers. **Diontalus actually joined the defense**, cutting through mercenaries with the same scythe that had maimed his former opponents. The berries the elves had offered before their earlier audience warmed in the companions' bodies and granted a burst of speed, not a permanent new ability. More enemies arrived. The High Elf reflected incoming missiles toward the fleet, but the bombardment continued and **Elhandar began an uncontrolled descent toward Earth**. He told Stanley to save his family in **Villa Or'Nath**, where Delit's secrets might shelter them.[^arc09-69]

Nash considered drawing a **Teleportation Circle** with the glyphs he had just received, but the violent motion of the station made the task impossible; **he did not cast it**. As the companions withdrew, **Diontalus warned Stanley and vanished**. Outside, a **blue light attacked several enemy ships**, visibly fulfilling at least part of his promise to help against the fleet—not his future pledge to oppose the Principal. The four reached **Angelashi, Lyrielle, Bok Bok, Chunks and the four children** waiting in reception. A hurried spoken reference to Squirts among them was **corrected to Chunks**. No one aboard Elhandar saw a restored Squirts at this moment. The household crowded into a descending lift as the spire shook around them.[^arc09-70]

The **lift cable snapped**. The car fell through the shaft, pressing its passengers toward the ceiling. **Stanley cast Feather Fall**, but the heavy carriage continued falling around them. **Vorel burned at the ceiling with his sword** while Hope weakened the metal and Nash, Lyrielle and Stanley reached with separate **Mage Hands**. The first attempt failed. With another effort Vorel cut a sufficient opening, and the protected passengers escaped the shattered lift before its last impact. **No member of the household died in the elevator**. They followed Rainbow Road to a row of parked **noble speeders**, using three craft to carry the children and injured. When a massive beam fell toward them, **Lyrielle cast Wall of Force overhead**, making a brief safe path to the cottage.[^arc09-71]

Stanley brought them into **Villa Or'Nath** and unlocked the impossible upper floor with his father's key. **Seats and restraints emerged** as the hidden chamber responded to the danger. Angelashi secured the four children, Bok Bok and Chunks found places among the others, and the companions fastened themselves in. The station tore around them. Metal shrieked, fire flashed beyond the windows, and a vast **impact** shook the room. The scene ended in darkness. **None of the named household had yet been shown dead**, but neither the extent of the station's destruction nor the fate of the other refugees was known. They had reached shelter, not an escape vessel already flying clear.[^arc09-72]

Far away, the scene shifted to **Hell**, where **Steerious Business, Blood Skunk Hendrickson and Dr. Spider** answered a summons from **Brent**. He was displeased that they had lost Hope and had brought **Mysteriosa** to help him correct the failure. He claimed that he had found **Squirts's soul** in a soul stream and made arrangements to restore her. A medical pod drained; a small kobold within **opened her eyes**. Brent introduced her as **Squirts, newly appointed captain of a group assigned to find Hope**. This was an actual return in a **separate later scene**, not a reversal of her death beneath Carthax, not proof that she had traveled with the Elhandar family, and not a reunion with her child **Chunks**. Her memory, consent and plans remained unseen.[^arc09-73]

When the story returned to **Villa Or'Nath**, the hidden chamber had held through the first crash. **Nash, Angelashi and all four children**, Stanley, Hope, Vorel, Lyrielle, Bok Bok and **Chunks** were alive. Chunks hung by his diaper from a chandelier until Lyrielle lowered him with **Mage Hand**. Below, they found wounded **Harbor tieflings** who said elves had collected their people and brought them inside before the impact. The precise method was unknown. **Nobieri and Emenil** were helping the injured. Rubble blocked the doors and windows, leaving the cottage buried in the station's broken structure. Stanley healed one refugee while the others searched for an exit. There was no safe teleportation out, and no evidence the entire Harbor had escaped unhurt.[^arc09-74]

Stanley opened **the Book of Adjacencies** to learn what his father had hidden within the cottage. Delit's writings described **Villa Or'Nath as a spelljammer**, a disguised ship capable of shrinking with its inhabitants and flying through space. **The Mask of Elton IV** could reportedly control it. Stanley **put on the obsidian mask**, reshaping it to fit his face, and sensed the controls through its moving gems. He tried to raise the house. It **creaked but did not fly**. The mask required **four nights of attunement**, and the half-elf had not completed them. The villa had not changed into a ship or diminished to a coin; its astonishing potential was still trapped inside a wall of debris.[^arc09-75]

**Bok Bok** produced a strange device that Lyrielle identified as a reusable **beauty bomb**. When Vorel activated it, music and magical finery transformed the refugees' clothing, briefly filling the dark house with an absurd celebration. It did not fix the building. Soon the wreckage shifted and sunlight appeared at an upper window. **Vorel climbed through**, saw **Earth's desert** below scattered fragments of Elhandar, then returned to report the opening. The companions considered waiting for the injured to recover. Before they could choose, an enormous burning object and the attacking **castle ship** fell toward the wreckage. Hope warned everyone to take shelter. The second impact spun the villa through stone and metal. **Several tieflings died**, though no reliable total was given. The household and children survived, battered but still together. Stanley used healing magic on the gravely wounded.[^arc09-76]

At last the cottage stood upright on sand. Outside, **Diontalus** fought the three-headed mercenary, whose hovering disk bore an enormous **electric guitar**. Its chords drove the archfey into a crater, where he lay **motionless but not confirmed dead**. The castle fell into the wreckage in another explosion. The mercenary's six blue spheres intercepted spells as he turned his music upon the villa. **Hope and Vorel** rushed forward. An attempted **Teleport** did not move them, and the guitar's compulsion forced the two friends **to strike each other against their will**. The enemy was not merely threatening their house; he was making its defenders into weapons. Nash took on his **black-dragon shape** while Stanley rallied the others with magic and **Diplomacy**.[^arc09-77]

Nash's **acid breath** passed the magic-catching spheres and burned the man on the disk. **Stanley used Telekinesis to seize the guitar**, breaking its hold over Hope and Vorel. The instrument fired a spell when he tried it, but another sphere swallowed the blast. The mercenary tried to flee. Nash climbed above him, drew a second breath, and sent acid through the floating disk. It **broke**, dropping the three-headed man to the sand. **He lay limp and motionless**. Nobody had yet confirmed a death, taken his surrender, questioned him or recovered a body from Diontalus's crater. The family remained in a damaged house in an Earth desert while Elhandar's wreckage burned around it. The battle had stopped, but the fate of their refuge—and what the enemy still knew—would not be resolved until the next played chapter.[^arc09-78]

## VII. Burrow: the night on Earth

The three-headed commander was still breathing. **Henatar Grape**, scorched by Nash's acid and the fall of his disk, lay in the sand beside the shattered remnants of **Elhandar**. **Nash**, still wearing the immense shape of a black dragon, put a foot upon him and demanded to know who had sent him. Henatar called it a contract from the **Mind Master in Sigil**. He had come for Hope and Vorel, he said, and had not expected to find **Diontalus** defending the station. **Stanley**, carrying the mercenary's stolen **electric guitar**, stabbed the captive's leg with **Diplomacy** without trying to kill him. The possibility of sparing the prisoner was discussed. No promise of safety was made. The enemy who had hurled the family from the sky was alive, beaten and begging, and the companions now held the choice of what to do with him.[^arc09-79]

Nash chose violence. He tried to tear away one of the man's heads without ending his life, but when the neck finally gave, **Henatar took his last breath and died**. Nash swallowed the severed head. This was **a killing after the mercenary had yielded**, not merely a delayed result of the acid that had broken his disk. Later Nash removed a second side head, leaving the central face on the corpse. **Hope** searched the fallen commander's things. A robe and pieces of the flying disk lay close by; the blue spheres that had swallowed spells were scattered in the sand. When she reached for one, it **shocked her and flung her backward**, destroying itself in the process. She had not safely gathered the set. The guitar remained in **Stanley's hands**, though he had not learned all its dangers or powers.[^arc09-80]

Beyond the wreckage lay a blue shape in a crater. **Diontalus** was motionless where the mercenary's music had brought him down. Stanley knelt and **cast Healing Word**. The archfey's eyes opened. He stood, able to speak and walk again, still separate from Stanley's body. He complained about his lost opportunity to take the bard as his own vessel and asked after the defeated commander. Stanley had **actually restored him to consciousness**; no pact was thereby fulfilled, nor was Diontalus transformed into a harmless ally. Across the desert **Elhandari rescue ships landed**. Surviving soldiers searched the debris, and the **High Elf** and **Admiral Chrysorius** joined the gathering. The loss of the station was terrible, but some of its people had survived to come down from the sky. The elf spoke of withdrawing those who could be saved to their **terraforming platform on Mars**. The four original companions intended to remain on Earth for now.[^arc09-81]

Stanley told the rescuers where the dying black dragon's **natural metal heart** had been buried. The hiding place in the mountain had been overturned by Elhandar's fall, so the High Elf sent people to **search the wreckage and retrieve it**. At first that was only an order. Later **Chrysorius returned with confirmation that the heart had been found and loaded for the journey to Mars**. It was the **separate natural heart**, not Nash's engineered heart, which still beat within his dragon body. The High Elf offered a path toward **Tungsten Wheel** through a contact on Mars whose name sounded like **Dunglestar Ravensbread**. The engineers and their design remained out of reach. As practical aid, the elves furnished **four noble speeders** and **clamped them to Villa Or'Nath**. Neither the villa nor its mask was yet ready to fly as a spelljammer. The companions had gained transport and an intact component, not an assembled Ruby.[^arc09-82]

**Diontalus** approached the mercenary's corpse. With his spectral scythe he drew out a visible thread of **blue soul-like light**, consumed it and left the body dry and gray. Whatever that meant for Henatar beyond death was not something the companions could test. The archfey walked away, still living, still waiting for whatever Stanley might owe him in the Feywild. **Stanley then questioned the corpse through Speak with Dead**, receiving short answers from the remaining mouth. Henatar had been told to reach Earth; his identification of the party aboard the falling station had been an **educated guess**. He said the Mind Master was using **M-sports publicity** to malign Vorel and had taken **Tobacho's chair in the Hive**. He mentioned a castle console that might once have allowed a return to Sigil. These were the dead mercenary's answers, not independent proof that Tobacho was captive, that the console survived the crash or that his former employer now controlled all of Sigil.[^arc09-83]

**Hope** ordered the surviving **Harborers** to board the Elhandari ships. Their homes had been shattered and Earth offered little protection from another assault. **Angelashi** brought all **four living children** aboard with **Lyrielle** and **Bok Bok**. **Nash** spoke privately to his wife before the separation. She feared what another long absence might do to their family. He told her that staying with him would put them within reach of mercenaries who now seemed willing to break cities to reach the companions. He promised to find them again; **he did not accompany them on the ship**. The fate and whereabouts of **Chunks** in the spoken departing roster remained troubled by a contradiction: an account named **Squirts** alongside Bok Bok even though the little cleric's separate return had been shown **in Hell, not on Elhandar**. Nothing played showed that Hell's Squirts had joined the Mars evacuation. The companions could say goodbye to those visibly boarding without claiming that every name in a muddled roll call had been reconciled.[^arc09-84]

Hope tried to power the **copper Gate ring** that had demanded cruel acts from her. She carved infernal markings into **Henatar's already-dead body**, but the ring gave her nothing. She then attempted to profane the body of a **tiefling killed in the villa's crash**. Again the magic refused to charge: defiling someone already dead did not meet its demand for harm. **No living refugee was hurt and no Gate opened**. Hope stopped, wept, apologized and **buried the tiefling**, continuing to tend the dead rather than feed the ring with another act of violence. Nearby Vorel, exhausted by failed homes and repeated invasions, spoke angrily of killing anyone who stood in his way. Those words were rage, not a massacre he carried out. The desert around them held enough bodies already.[^arc09-85]

At last **the Elhandari ships lifted off**, carrying the rescued Harborers, Nash's family, Lyrielle, Bok Bok and the **recovered natural dragon heart toward Mars**. The High Elf could promise shelter there, not guarantee the refugees would be safe forever. **Nash, Hope, Stanley and Vorel stayed on Earth** beside Villa Or'Nath. No one had found Tungsten Wheel or built the Rubistic Triangulosphere. The wreckage of Elhandar lay across the desert, and the house remained battered but upright, its hidden machinery still waiting for the mask's full attunement. For a little while the four had no audience to reassure and no children to carry away from danger. They stood with the cost of the rescue written across the sand.[^arc09-86]

Stanley took the guitar apart from the others and **played it quietly**. Its notes carried unnaturally far, but did not unleash the destructive music that had forced Hope and Vorel to strike each other. **A familiar goat-like figure** appeared behind him: **the Soulbroker**, whose name was given in several uncertain forms, including **Kitobol Prith**. He spoke **to Stanley alone**. He claimed that of the original companions only Stanley still owed him a soul-bargain; Hope, Vorel and Nash, he said, stood outside his claim for different reasons. He described **the Principal** as an old man of Earth who had sought help against dragons and broken a bargain, then offered a history of the **Weir**, beings he said came from a place between creation and the Far Realm. The Soulbroker's tale of betrayed contracts, divine ambitions and a universe fed on living souls came from a creature who hoped to profit from Stanley's obedience. Stanley heard it; his friends did not yet know what had been said.[^arc09-87]

The visitor called his design a more just order, but its price was terrible. The Weir, he said, needed the energy of the living to survive and intended to **convert Earth** for its own use. He claimed the Principal had once held it at bay, only to weaken that protection by pursuing godhood. **Stanley resisted the promise**. The Soulbroker threatened the consequences of their unfinished agreement, then **raised his arms**. A vast **purple tear opened in the sky**. Through it came an immense dark creature, covered in gear-like scales and armed with **six digging limbs**. It fell far out in the desert and **burrowed into the ground**, so large that the earth itself seemed to rise around it. The claim of a conversion was testimony; **the rift and the creature's arrival were visibly real**. Then the Soulbroker vanished. Whatever the Weir truly was, one of its terrors had reached the world.[^arc09-88]

A **wall of sand** came racing toward the cottage, threaded with pink-purple lightning. Nash spread his **black-dragon wings** and lifted off. **Hope and Stanley** climbed onto him, and **Vorel** joined as the storm struck. For several minutes they flew over the churning desert while the ground split below and the creature's vast back disappeared beneath it. **The burrower was not killed, contained or followed into the depths**. At last the wind weakened and the four returned safely to the ground. **Villa Or'Nath still stood**, partly banked by sand. Only then did **Stanley tell the other three** what the Soulbroker had said in private. They could now weigh the claims of the Weir and the Principal together, but knowing a threat's words gave them no weapon with which to stop it.[^arc09-89]

Around the sheltered house they argued over the paths ahead. **Ruby remained unbuilt**. The dragon's natural heart was going to Mars, where a contact might help them find **Tungsten Wheel**; the device's promised ability to seal Earth was still an untested proposal. **Hope** told the companions of her travels after White Tower: the Hand, the powerful beings who had courted her, Tobacho, the **Council of Clowns**, and the lead toward the **Eye of the Voice**. The ring she carried was still **uncharged**. **Vorel's supporters from M-sports** might offer a way back into Sigil despite the Mind Master's campaign against him, but talk of making him **king of space** was jest and ambition, not an actual coronation. Stanley sent a message to the High Elf and learned only that the Mars contact was already there; **the missing engineer had not been found**. They discussed remaining a few days to finish attuning the villa's mask. **Those extra days had not yet passed**, and the house had not taken flight.[^arc09-90]

Night finally came. **Nash returned from dragon shape**, the others made what shelter they could, and **all four completed a peaceful overnight rest** on Earth. The unseen burrower had not returned in that interval. The companions were **now level thirteen**, alive and together at the end of the first long season of their story. **Angelashi and the children were bound for Mars**, Diontalus was abroad, the Soulbroker had opened a path for the Weir, and the Principal still loomed over a damaged world. No mission among those dangers had yet been completed. Beside the wrecked Elhandar and the stubborn little villa, the four friends had survived another night and still had to choose where to go when morning came.[^arc09-91]
