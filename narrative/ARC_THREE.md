## Prologue — After the light

At the bottom of **CHIDS**, beneath the Wastes and amid the monstrous machinery of the **Traversal Drive**, four friends made a choice that should have ended their story. **Vorel**, **Nash**, **Hope** and **Stanley** entered the beam together. After their bodies dissolved into light, the game master declared all four dead. **Sira and Eddie** were alive beyond the beam; **Chance** had been swept away. The fate of the Drive, Earth, and the Principle was still unknown.

There is no ordinary waking from what follows. For Vorel, the white light becomes an experience without a body or a horizon. He sees himself drift away and passes through a vast field of stars. An enormous, motionless, white-haired face hangs before him, too immense to understand and never identified. A tiny black speck travels near him. What seems like an eternity may be no measurable time at all.

Then the stars collapse into a filthy sewer. Vorel awakes exhausted, bare of clothes and equipment, with moss, rubbish and insects where there should be his friends. His familiar **Eye of Groxl** has split like an egg. Something within it has given itself to shield him from the passage, leaving a membrane that peels away from his scales and a silence where its presence used to be. He survived, but not without a sacrifice of his own.

The black speck is still there. Something red and living pushes from one of its openings. An eye opens, and someone looks out.

## I. A voice without a mouth

Inside the little dark object, **Nash Maggard** can see and think. He cannot explain himself to Vorel. What remains of his physical existence resembles a heart made of dark metal or stone, suspended between life and machinery, without a mouth, arms or legs. Vorel calls the baffling thing **Speckle the Cosmic Speck**. Nash must make do with blinks and awkward motion.

The problem becomes urgent when a hungry, hooded ratman approaches the sleeping lizardfolk with utensils and a plan to eat him. Nash learns to scrape and then **float**, struggling to drive the intruder away. The attack wakes Vorel, and the creature retreats into the refuse. It is a poor first encounter with what the locals call a city of infinite possibilities.

The ratman, **Jarver**, later guides Vorel toward other inhabitants of the sewers. There, masked collectors are loading valuable-looking bodies onto a hovering slab. They are the two people Vorel least expected to find: **Hope and Stanley**, motionless and apparently dead. Hope is missing her mechanical arm and leg. Stanley is cold and lifeless. Vorel will not permit them to disappear into the local trade in corpses.

What follows is desperate and ugly. Vorel uses his changing scales to surprise a collector, then fights the others for possession of the bodies. Electrical weapons flash through the sewer water. Jarver kills one of the collectors, while Vorel restrains or knocks out others without the same intention to kill. Nash cannot shape a spell without hands or a voice, but discovers that he can **ram someone with the heart that contains him**. The party wins not by restoring the dead but by preventing their removal.

The surviving collector, **Binch Lory**, explains that bodies recorded in the **Dead Book** have a price with the **Dustmen**. Eventually he and Vorel come to terms: Vorel will take his friends and borrow the hovering slab; Binch can inspect the strange site where Vorel appeared. Binch names the place **Sigil**, the **City of Doors**, and sketches a route toward its Welcome Center. He says portals can connect the city to other worlds. Whether one leads home is another matter.

For the first time since the beam, Vorel has a concrete task: keep Hope and Stanley together long enough to find someone who might help. It is the sort of duty he understands, even in a place he does not.

## II. The city that curves overhead

Outside the sewers, the world opens in the wrong direction. Sigil's streets, roofs and windows curve away and continue **overhead**, so that the sky is more city. Its inhabitants barely pause for a naked lizardfolk carrying two bodies on a floating platform, with an eye-bearing stone heart keeping him company. To Vorel, who has crossed deserts, raised kingdoms and fought among dragons and demons, the ordinary business of this impossible metropolis may be the strangest sight yet.

On the way, Nash grows a **small human finger**. It is grotesque, painful, and almost useless, but it offers hope that the change may continue. At the **Welcome Center**, Vorel has to describe himself as a newcomer with no possessions and no money. The clerk records his skills, his beliefs and his desperate need for lodging. She also registers the floating heart as **Nash Maggard of the Solar Mines**, treating Vorel's extraordinary explanation as just another administrative inconvenience.

The vouchers buy **three days of meals and shelter**, not a return passage, not a new life, and certainly not the resurrection of Hope and Stanley. The clerk suggests the Dustmen and a possible cleric. Vorel takes the bodies instead to the **Soggy Feather**, where **Bill** tends the bar and **Pam** provides food. Bill offers hot drinks, stories, advice about work and the small comforts that can matter in a city built to swallow newcomers. There is no immediate way for Vorel to earn the gold a miracle might require.

Two strangers notice the party. One is a dark-feathered **kenku**, wrapped in travel-worn clothes and a long-beaked mask, speaking mostly by repeating what other people have said. The other is a tiny, energetic **kobold** wearing scraps of improvised armor and carrying an implausible utensil-built weapon. Neither is one of the companions on the slab. The shape of the party has already changed, though its surviving members have not decided what that means.

## III. The church that cannot stay in one alley

The kobold calls herself **Squirts**. She carries her infant child, **Chunks**, in a shoe attached to her clothing and has an enthusiast's devotion to tricks. The kenku is called **Nothing**. Where Vorel demands direct answers, Nothing has borrowed voices, gestures and silence. Bill employs the bird as a guide; Squirts knows a place where the dead might conceivably be brought back.

She leads Vorel, Nash and Nothing through prosperous streets and increasingly ragged quarters to a dreary concrete church down an otherwise empty alley. Its door responds to a thoroughly undignified signal. Beyond the shabby exterior are unexpectedly extensive halls, strange attendants and a birdlike priest devoted to a trickster power. Vorel brings Hope and Stanley before him and explains what the beam took.

The priest examines Stanley through a crystal apparatus and sees markings that are not visible to ordinary eyes. He calls **Stanley and Hope “petitioners”**, manifestations associated with souls rather than conventional bodies, and connects them to **the Weir**. He has reason to fear what that connection signifies. For Vorel, the distinction matters less than one question: can he get his friends back?

The priest cannot simply promise a resurrection. He proposes a bargain instead. The god he serves delights in **public humiliation**, especially when someone proud or powerful is made ridiculous before people whose admiration he expects. If Vorel helps arrange the right humiliation, the priest says he will attempt to obtain what he needs to restore Hope and Stanley. At first he does not even know whom the trick should target. Vorel accepts the possibility because it is something to pursue, not because his companions have already been saved.

The bodies pass into the church's **protected crypts**. No ritual is performed. When the travelers leave and look back, the alley and the church have vanished from view, as if that little part of Sigil can never be relied upon to stay where it was. Squirts is not surprised.

A night's rest brings a startling change for Nash: **a mouth and a functioning hand** emerge from the heart. He can finally speak to Vorel, even if his form remains absurd. The next morning three **Pelor-affiliated Welcome Center visitors** arrive with charity and suggestions of regular work. Squirts distrusts them. They speak of factions, laws, the **Lady of Pain** and a city where powers cannot simply impose themselves; those are their descriptions of Sigil, not direct encounters with the Lady. Vorel hears offers of apprenticeships, pit fighting and bounties, but he wants neither a career in a new city nor a permanent place in it. He wants his friends and his world.

Bill's alternative is improbable but more appealing: a competition of magically animated fighting **marionettes** called **Big Time Smack'em Brothers**. It promises money to the victor. **Vorel, Nash and Nothing register**; Squirts does not. The group visits the venue, learns that practice costs money they do not have, and searches the city's libraries for a champion's strategy book. Nash finds it—with someone already reading it.

## IV. No book for the innocent

The halfling reader refuses to share his copy of the celebrated marionette guide. Nash, now blessed with a mouth and a talent for causing trouble, first argues and threatens, then lures the reader outside with the promise of a confrontation. While the two prepare to settle their disagreement, **Vorel steals the book** and Nothing hides it. The reader realizes the deception and gives chase.

The resulting escape is scarcely noble. Vorel uses his camouflage to wait in an alley, strikes the pursuing halfling **nonlethally**, then picks him up and carries him away with the stolen guide. The companions make sure he is still breathing. Squirts leads them once more to her hidden church, which appears in an alley different from the one they used before.

The priest supplies petals for a preparation that is supposed to **blur memory**. Squirts crushes them, mixes the powder into a homemade drink and administers it to the unconscious man. No one tests what he will forget. The group leaves him near the library, having acquired the wisdom of their sport by rather less admirable means than studying quietly.

This time the priest has found his target: the young, celebrated, apparently undefeated champion whose name is associated with the very book they took. His defeat at the hands of **Vorel, a complete novice**, would be a humiliation worthy of the church. But the priest's definition of a fair chance is dangerously convenient. He offers to steer Vorel toward easier matchups and gives **Squirts small devices intended to make opposing players sick**. Vorel recognizes the difference between tricks and fair competition. He nevertheless continues toward the contest, because the bargain is bound up with the fate of his friends.

Back at the Soggy Feather, the group spends much of the day studying the book. They learn how to make the marionettes leap, kick, block and stun one another, and how a decisive **Big Time Smackeroo** can throw a defeated opponent out of play. Reading grants them enough familiarity to attempt the game, though not years of practice. What seemed a desperate way to make a little money is becoming a staged affront to the city's most celebrated player.

## V. A job the others do not hear

Not everyone at the Soggy Feather has the same reason to stay in Sigil. During dinner, a man called **Pelvis** arrives wearing a neat hat and showing off a grin of gold and diamond teeth. Nothing recognizes him. He is a **fixer**, a man who connects those with dangerous work to those willing to perform it. He knows Nothing from earlier jobs and uses a name resembling **Ebon Skulker**.

Pelvis draws the kenku aside. He offers **three times the usual fee** and a note directing Nothing to a magic shop. The details are for a client to provide. Nothing agrees, but the rest of the company does not hear the private arrangement. Asked about the visitor, Nothing supplies little beyond the word *job*. It is not enough to reveal what has been promised.

Before dawn, after another night's rest, Nash acquires **another hand and forearm, and a dangling foot**. He is becoming more human piece by piece without ceasing to be a floating heart. Nothing has gone elsewhere. At the address from Pelvis's note, a strange old **githyanki** shopkeeper produces a box containing a small marble pyramid. Nothing activates it in a private room and steps into a space where an anonymous client speaks through a screened partition, unseen and with a disguised voice.

The commission is not ordinary burglary. Nothing is to **abduct a prominent man from Sigil's high quarters**, take him to an address in the **Hive**, and use a portal to **deliver him into the Abyss**. The client allows **no more than seven days** to finish the job, and warns that the portal will permit only **one hour to return** once used. The target's name and faction are uncertain in the machine transcripts; one recognizes the faction as **Godsmen**, another as **Guardsmen**. Whoever this person is, Nothing has accepted an order that has nothing to do with learning marionette fighting.

Nothing returns to the inn. The others prepare for their tournament without being shown the secret client's instructions. **No abduction has occurred.** Two debts now hang over the new company: one made in the hope of recovering the old party, another whose cost has yet to be seen.

## VI. Three marionettes and an uncertain victory

The **Royce qualifying venue** is crowded with competitors, controllers and the expectant noise of a popular sport. Guards and an elven examiner screen entrants for magic. Vorel passes. Nash, despite looking like an impossible enchanted anatomical specimen, is admitted after the scanner detects nothing objectionable. Squirts' magical stone attracts attention, and the guards turn her away. Nothing avoids the screening by stealing a small object from confiscated property, planting it on another entrant, and provoking a fight at the gate. While the guards deal with the disturbance, the kenku slips inside.

Squirts finds another route. The trickster priest is present **disguised as a human** and ushers her through a side entrance. He intends to interfere with the competition; Squirts has her own tools for the task.

An announcement changes the players' preparations. A problem with the **modron-built marionettes** means the qualifiers will offer just three fighters: **Bland**, **Kung** and **Rubles**. The favorite characters some entrants studied are unavailable. An anonymous benefactor has also added **four thousand gold pieces** to the advertised prize pool. The money grows more tempting, but it has not yet been won.

Vorel's first opponent is an elderly **gnome**, a friendly competitor more interested in enjoying the game than overturning the sport's hierarchy. Before they sit down, **Squirts secretly pricks the gnome with one of the priest's sickness devices**. He notices something strange and gradually struggles to maintain his concentration. Vorel plays **Kung**, a lizardfolk fighter mocked as a beginner's choice, against the gnome's **Rubles**. It is hardly a straightforward victory. The gnome strikes first, builds dangerous stagger and nearly sends Vorel out of the match. Vorel recovers with a biting attack and carefully timed blows, finally reducing his opponent's fighter to defeat.

He has **won his opening match**. He has also benefited from a deliberate act of sabotage against a man who greeted him kindly. The gnome, feeling ill, suspects that a kobold interfered. Vorel has advanced, but the manner of the advance is already part of the story.

Elsewhere Nash faces an elderly orc woman who has come to enjoy the same tournament. Unable to choose his planned fighter, Nash takes **Rubles** against her **Bland**. The match becomes an education in blocks, counterattacks and costly mistakes. An early attempt at a dramatic knockout fails, and the opponent punishes him for it. Nash persists, builds her stagger past the threshold, and finally lands the **Big Time Smackeroo** that sends her marionette flying. She smashes her controller in frustration. **Nash, too, has won the first round.** Unlike Vorel's opponent, hers is not shown to have been poisoned or otherwise manipulated.

Then comes Nothing's game. The kenku faces **another kenku**, whose instinct is also to mimic. One takes Bland; Nothing chooses Kung. Neither will move first. Each waits to copy the other's controller action. Seconds stretch into silence as the timer drains away. When it expires, the game declares **both competitors forfeit**. Two wins and a double timeout: an absurd beginning to the hoped-for rescue.

The famous champion has not been faced. No tournament prize has been paid. The cleric has not performed the promised ritual. The real consequences of the sickened gnome, the stolen book, and Nothing's secret agreement remain beyond this opening stretch of Sigil.

## VII. Always high kick

As the qualifier wears on, the marionette stations begin to vanish from the floor. Competitors who arrived expecting a day of games become spectators, and **Vorel and Nash survive round after round**. Vorel's opponents have often seemed ill; Nash has found many less threatening than he expected. Neither can tell how much of their progress belongs to skill, luck or Squirts' unseen work. By the lunch break only four remain.

One of them is a quiet gray-skinned elf whose hood obscures her face. Another is **Ventro**, an immense alligatorlike man adored at Royce Gymnasium and accompanied by a gang of swaggering supporters. His victory streak is local to this venue; the greater champion whom the priest wants humiliated is still **Kanja**, waiting somewhere beyond the qualifiers. Ventro seizes the competitors' dining room as if it belongs to him. He insults Nash, boasts about his victories and threatens anyone who refuses to be afraid. Squirts responds in the language of her calling: she secretly puts **two of the priest's sickness needles into Ventro's leg**. He catches her despite her attempt to disappear, and eventually **kicks her and Chunks across the room**. The violence does not end his tournament entry. The others hold themselves back, hoping he will be disqualified instead of dragging them into a street fight.

The announced pairings provide a stranger ordeal: **Vorel must face Nash**. For all the terrible things the two have survived together, Vorel resists the idea of shaming a friend for sport. Nash reassures him that marionettes are only a game; he even offers to lose deliberately. Squirts has no such hesitation about bending the odds and **sticks Nash with a sickness needle**. But Nash withstands the poison. Their match proceeds without a declared surrender.

Nash chooses the monk **Bland**, Vorel the lizardfolk **Kung**. They test one another with punches, ducking kicks and feints. Both miss repeatedly, to the bewilderment of a crowd that has seen sharper matches. Eventually Vorel finds his rhythm. He dazes Bland, strings together the jumping kicks he favors, and prevails after Nash briefly breaks out of the combination. **Vorel wins**. Nash takes the loss without resentment, leaving the stage to watch his friend.

The other match has already gone to Ventro. He adds insult to victory by **kicking the defeated elf from the stage**. No official intervenes. His popularity seems enough to excuse conduct that would ruin an outsider. Now the crowd roars for a match between the local champion and an almost unknown lizardfolk, a newcomer who started playing only the day before.

Ventro knows Vorel likes Kung. When the marionette selection opens, he seizes the fighter first, expecting the novice to collapse without his favored choice. **Vorel chooses Bland** instead. On the screen the monk meets the lizardfolk in a flurry of blows. Vorel learns quickly, using a stunning strike to open a combination, then **jumping into one heavy high kick after another**. Ventro struggles to escape the blows. His fighters' remaining strength disappears. The champion at Royce Gymnasium has lost.

Ventro cannot accept it. He lifts the game station and tries to crush Vorel with it.

## VIII. A warning without a voice

Nothing reacts first, pitching a hammer at Ventro's arm. The shattered rhythm of the threatened blow buys Vorel time. **Nash tries to make him larger**, hoping a spell can protect him, but something prevents the magic from taking shape. Squirts has no comparable trouble: she commands Ventro to kneel, and the furious champion finds himself **groveling and praising Vorel against his will**.

The crowd's favorite has brought his entourage. They pour over the barriers with clubs and axes. Nothing scatters **ball bearings** into their path, sending some sprawling. Squirts' magic takes the fury out of others, though not all; her hastily made crossbow drives a blade into Ventro's foot. Nothing attacks with swords, trying to subdue him. Nash keeps attempting magic, only for the same unknown force to silence each effort. He resorts to punching a thug with one of the awkward hands grown from his heart. Vorel stands amid blows, raging, gripping one attacker and using him as a weapon against the rest.

The tournament has ceased to resemble a game. A mob watches the violence rise from a stage where the rules were supposed to prevent exactly this. Ventro recovers his axes and turns them on Nothing.

Then the entire arena goes quiet.

A **towering green figure shaped like a woman** has appeared where there was none. Every spectator has the unmistakable impression that its gaze falls upon them. Nobody hears a voice, yet the warning is understood. Ventro tries to protest: it was the others, he says; they cheated; he did nothing wrong. His defiance comes too late. His body begins to collapse toward a hole in reality scarcely large enough to see. He twists and cries out while something draws him through it, until **he is gone**. The figure vanishes as suddenly as it arrived.

The companions have heard the name **Lady of Pain** in Sigil, always with an admonition not to attract her notice. What they have just witnessed fits the terror behind those warnings, though the figure has not given her name. **Ventro's disappearance is certain; his death or destination is not**.

His entourage scatters. The spectators recover their voices and cheer for Vorel, the unlikely **champion of the Royce qualifier**. A much larger final event remains, and the overall champion **Kanja** has not been faced. But for this one afternoon, Vorel has conquered a realm that has nothing to do with kingdoms, gods or dragon engines: a world of marionettes, applause, and the embarrassing power of a jumping kick.

## IX. The price of being famous

Victory travels faster than the companions do. A reporter from **M Sports Weekly** wants Vorel's portrait, his history and the secret of a game he has barely learned to play. Small winged creatures paint his likeness while admirers crowd forward. He names **Mighty Squirts** as his team, with **Squirts** as its manager. The answer is good enough for a story, though there has been no formal team registration. Vorel recommends reading books and relying on the high kick. The journalists are delighted.

Merchants are delighted too. **Arcane Spice** offers a monthly fee for wearing its branding; a maker of marionette controllers offers even more. But the contracts trouble Vorel, whose friends' earlier promises to a supernatural broker brought them to this desperate place. He listens to the offers and **signs nothing**. He would rather be poor than find his name caught in another agreement he cannot escape.

Some attention is harder to fend off than a contract. Admirers want his time, affection and company, and do not always listen when he refuses. Vorel gives the stolen strategy book to a young lizardfolk admirer and is soon leading a crowd back toward the **Soggy Feather**, where Bill and Pam barely know how to cope with so many customers. **Nash and Nothing help serve** the crowd and **earn actual wages and tips**; Nothing also tricks drinkers into surrendering extra coins. Squirts collects a few strange little treasures whose uses are not yet known. For the first time since the beam, the survivors have at least a little money of their own, though none of it is the promised grand tournament prize.

Vorel himself has gained little but admirers he does not want. When several refuse to leave him alone, he tries desperate distractions and finally **leaps through an upper window** to escape. He returns only after the inn grows quiet. Nothing lets him inside; the companions retreat to the small, strange peace of their room. For one evening, the Soggy Feather has been a place of celebration rather than a refuge for the homeless.

The peace does not last.

Late in the night, Nothing's **warning sword begins to vibrate**. The kenku hears movement above the room and cries out to wake the others. They listen into the dark. Then **two armed, ninja-like figures burst through a window** and rush toward them, swords drawn. Their faces, employer and purpose are still unknown when the episode ends.

The newly famous champion has a larger tournament ahead of him. His friends have neither been restored nor forgotten. And Sigil, which offered the party a safe place to sleep, has just shown that even a locked door may be no protection at all.

## X. The moon on the headbands

The swords that crash through the **Soggy Feather** windows are not there to challenge Vorel in a game. Two masked strangers roll into the bedroom wearing black and white, with a red moon and howling wolf on their clothes. When they find the lizardfolk's bed empty, they descend on **Nothing**, who has already been warned by the sword she keeps close to her chair. Her twin blades cut down the first intruder. For an instant there is a look of terror behind the mask, and then the assassin becomes **dust**, leaving only the empty costume.

More figures arrive through the windows and door. Some have swords, others nunchucks or fans of thrown knives. **Vorel leaps from beneath his bed, pointing toward the ceiling and calling on the Lady of Pain** to punish the attackers. No green statue appears. Squirts tries to turn the dead men's clothes into restraints, then abandons the attempt amid the rush of weapons. Nash, tucked into his bed, calls out the question he has been carrying for days: why can he not make his magic work?

Squirts answers between blows. He needs **a key**.

That is no help in a room full of assassins. It is the strange **dragon-metal heart** itself that gives Nash something he can do. Remembering its old enchantment, he gathers breath in a mouth that should never have grown from a heart, then spits a cone of burning energy across the room. The assailants catch fire. Their weapons still find the party, but the stone-and-flesh survivor can at least fight back.

Vorel pulls a mask from one attacker and finds a mouthless, distorted face beneath. He decides he needs a live captive. He grapples a ninja and tries to keep him alive as the others try to hack off his head. Nothing comes flying across the melee and kills two assailants at his back. A more elaborately dressed woman in the same black uniform finally steps through the window and holds Vorel with a dark power he manages to shrug off. Her hands know how to stop a weapon in motion: when Nothing attacks, the woman twists the kenku's wrist and kicks her into a painful stupor.

Squirts commands the woman to freeze. It works. **Vorel charges at her with a mattress**, knocks her to the floor, then pins her beneath it without intending to kill her. Nash, who has been burning every masked attacker he can reach, lifts a corner of the bedding and breathes again. There is a scream. The mattress falls flat. The captive has vanished like the others, leaving Vorel with nothing to question—and with an expensive mattress he tears to pieces in frustration. Bill, who has run upstairs with a club to help fight off a knife thrower, is not amused.

The inn survives. The attackers do not. But the insignia means something to Nothing. In a private moment, the kenku recognizes the symbol from old underworld warnings: **the Order of the Crimson Moon**, an almost legendary guild of assassins, expensive enough that only powerful clients usually employ them. Nothing cannot simply say all she knows. She arranges clothing, paints a red crescent with blood, imitates coin sounds and watches Vorel and Bill stumble through ridiculous guesses until they finally understand the name.

They know **who the masked killers were**. They still do not know **who paid them**.

Bill objects when Vorel complains that the Lady should have appeared to save him. Sigil's mysterious power is not the group's personal bodyguard, he says; her actions are not predictable, and the city is far from free of violence. Vorel imagines making himself her champion by taking down assassins. Bill advises him to win his tournament, find his friends and get home. For now, at least, that sounds like an achievable ambition.

## XI. The cape, the child and the key

By morning the Soggy Feather has mostly been put back in order. Nash's long sleep has made **a thigh and calf grow behind one of his feet**. He can almost stand on a leg, though the rest of him remains a floating heart with an eye and an assortment of hands. The broken windows and scorched room have not frightened away Vorel's admirers. The dining room is once again full of people who want a piece of the local champion.

Vorel had refused sponsorship contracts the day before, afraid of binding himself to more promises. But **Arcane Spice** offers another possibility: wear its colors for a single day, and receive **two hundred gold**. That is money he can understand, and there is no lifetime oath attached. He tries on a splendid blue cape with animated lettering and begins to feel like a man whose fortunes are turning.

His priestly patron arrives disguised as an old man with a peculiar eye. **Kidigree** has been thinking about the day's larger championship. Sickening opponents will draw too much attention now, and the competitors will be watched for magic. The solution, in his view, is to make **Vorel** better at the marionette game. In Bill's kitchen the priest opens a coat lined with living soil and harvests a hideous pink mushroom, biting plants, and other things no cook should recognize. Nothing puts on a chef hat. Squirts plunges into the hot mixture long enough to lend it some trickster magic. The priest sacrifices a squirrel for its eyes, and the resulting concoction looks almost as dreadful as it smells.

Vorel drinks it.

The priest promises that in the **three o'clock finals**, Vorel will see opponents' moves and patterns with unnatural clarity. He also warns that the officials forbid **performance-enhancing substances** and that no one must talk about what they have done. There is no evidence yet that the potion works. But the possibility of cheating his way to the great champion seems, to this odd new company, better than abandoning Hope and Stanley.

Then the morning takes a turn no one expected. A small lizardfolk boy is still waiting in the common room, begging Vorel to become his father. Vorel tells him to find his own greatness. **Squirts** bites the boy's tail and orders him to leave. The child flails at her, attempting to defend himself. She answers with **Sacred Flame**. Fire runs across his scales, and he collapses. Squirts drags his motionless body through the room, past horrified onlookers, and hides him in the rubbish behind the inn.

An Arcane Spice representative has seen enough. He accuses the party of allowing a child to be killed, **tears up the agreement** and retrieves Vorel's beautiful blue cape. Other sponsors and admirers leave. The money that seemed so close is gone. Even Bill's separate arrangement to promote the same brand at the Soggy Feather may be ruined.

But the boy's story does not end beneath the rubbish. Squirts returns to him, and, remembering a cleric's power that can stabilize the dying, **casts Spare the Dying**. The child opens his eyes, speaks and runs away, badly burned but alive. Squirts tells him his own father saved him and sends him home. The crowd does not see this. The incident is not harmless merely because he lives, and the party's reputation does not recover as quickly as his breath.

An official from the **M Sports commission** arrives to explain the day's schedule. Vorel is expected at the larger finals at **three o'clock**, with an escort around **one**. The official emphasizes strict bans on doping, cheating and magical equipment. Vorel, who has just drunk an illicit performance-enhancing potion, has reason to listen.

Without a sponsor, he decides to make his own. Bill would appreciate a cape bearing **the Soggy Feather**. At the enormous bazaar, a rug merchant introduces him to a tailor who promises the garment for twenty gold. Vorel's celebrity draws business to the stall, and when he returns for the red cape with its brilliant white letters, the tailor **waives the price**. For once, fame has bought him something without a contract.

Nothing leads Nash elsewhere in the same market. The kenku has been trying to tell him what he needs, and here the explanation finally has a shape. An old woman sells curious little charms: **spell keys**. Sigil's magic does not behave as Nash expects because ordinary spellcasting is constrained here. Keys associated with **schools of magic** can unlock those abilities. Nash would need different keys for different spells. **Each costs five hundred gold**, or fifty a month over twelve months if he can pass a credit check and provide identification issued by a faction.

Nash stares at the prices. He has neither the necessary money nor the paperwork. A fortune he once had still waits in a vault back in **Tietkalla**, beyond every door he knows how to open. The vendor cannot help him leave with a key on credit. Nothing has brought him to an answer but not a solution.

So Nash, now an eye, a mouth, two hands, a heart and one strange leg, floats back into the bazaar with the others. **Vorel has a cape and a hidden advantage he has not yet used. The assassins' employer is unknown. Hope and Stanley remain in the crypts. The championship has not begun.** Sigil has given the group new information—and many new reasons to fear what comes next.

## XII. The champion who knew tomorrow's move

On the way back from Sigil's bazaar, whispers trail Vorel's bright new cape. The city remembers the young lizardfolk dragged out of the Soggy Feather, not the fact that **he survived**. Bill is taking down the Arcane Spice sign when Vorel returns. The inn has lost its sponsorship, but Vorel has little time to worry about that now. The great championship begins this afternoon.

There is something wrong with his shoulder. A swelling has grown where a masked assassin's dart struck him in the night. Nash, with the resourcefulness and questionable judgment of a man currently shaped like a heart, **bites it open**. Yellow fluid runs down Vorel's scales. Suddenly Vorel feels heavy, slow and sick. The dart has carried a **delayed poison**, and it could make winning an impossible task.

The official escort arrives. Vorel accuses the championship's other players of sending the assassins, but has no name, confession or evidence to support him. The official inspects the empty black costumes strewn across the ruined upstairs room, recognizes the symbol of the **Order of the Crimson Moon**, and is disturbed by the expense someone has undertaken to kill this newcomer. But recognition is not proof of who paid. The official will help Vorel seek medical treatment at the Colosseum, not condemn a competitor on a hunch.

The **Great Colosseum** rises like a monument above Sigil's streets, surrounded by banners and people. They arrive in a six-horse carriage and pass through a strict magical search. Nothing leaves her warning sword behind. Squirts leaves her strange forehead stone. Nash's heart is itself a source of enchantment, so an attendant clips an **M-shaped badge** to him: he is allowed in as a magically constituted person, even though enchanted equipment is banned.

Eight players wait in the competitors' lounge. An armored warrior sits alone with a drink. A talking **illithid** shares a table with the jovial ghost of **Tubby Flavorbeard**, who calls himself **Tubbs 9K**. A halfling calling himself **the Mind Master** arrives surrounded by admirers. And near a throng of young fans sits **Kanja**, the celebrated champion who has supposedly never lost, sheltered behind a broad mirrorlike visor. He barely acknowledges the outsider everyone is discussing.

The ghost and the illithid welcome Vorel to their table, only to ask whether his little manager killed a child because she was jealous of Vorel's supposed love for the boy. Sigil has built a scandal from bits of truth and invention. Squirts and Vorel deny the romance; nobody can explain what happened in a way that satisfies the curious.

The Colosseum's physician finds the poison in Vorel's body and offers pills that will let him fight **without its crippling symptoms for eight or ten hours**. The poison will come back, he warns, and will require actual treatment later. Vorel suspects another plot. He holds the medicine rather than swallowing it. Nothing listens at the doctor's door and hears nothing but a solitary song. In the lounge Nash chews one of the pills, feels a little better, and persuades Vorel that the medicine may be what it claims. **Vorel takes the remaining tablets**. His strength returns for the tournament, though the infection is still there beneath the surface.

In the announced first round, **Kanja, the armored Arsenad and Tubbs 9K** win their respective games. When Vorel's turn comes, a woman accompanying the Mind Master kisses the halfling, looks over his shoulder at Vorel and for an instant meets his gaze. Vorel knows those eyes. **They were in his bedroom during the assassination attempt.** Whether this woman hired the killers, wore the black mask, survived the vanishing body or only resembles someone he saw is still a mystery. The Mind Master laughs off the suggestion.

The spectators have other concerns. They greet Vorel with chants of **child killer** and a storm of rotten vegetables. Vorel rages and starts batting the missiles away with fire, but officials warn that spells and magic on stage will bring disqualification. He sits down at the marionette controller instead.

The Mind Master chooses **Rubles**. Vorel takes **Kung**, the fighter he has barely stopped playing since his first day. As the models tower above the arena and the match begins, the wretched potion from Kidigree's cauldron finally takes hold.

Vorel's vision trails and splits. More strangely, a voice inside his head begins to **announce the Mind Master's intended moves before he makes them**. The novice hears what his opponent plans, yet must still choose how to answer it. He misjudges some attacks, blocks others and is nearly caught. Then he finds the rhythm of his favorite maneuver: the **jumping heavy kick**, the blow that has carried him through Royce. Rubles staggers beneath Kung's attacks, and a last ducking kick sends the opponent's marionette down.

Vorel has won. **It is a played victory—and the unfair assistance of a forbidden potion is real.** The Mind Master leaves furious, promising that Vorel's career will not survive what he has done.

## XIII. Five thousand gold and the tears of Kanja

The tournament has rules against exactly the sort of advantage Vorel used. **Kanja** can see that something is wrong. He demands that the officials test the outsider, arguing that no one arriving from a portal could dominate experienced players so quickly. Squirts responds by urinating on the champion's leg, and then insists that **all the remaining competitors be tested**, not only Vorel.

The officials send the players to a latrine for a supervised urine sample. A pleasant attendant named **Jimmy** watches Vorel fill his cup. But when the attendant lifts the sample, **his eye changes**, briefly revealing the birdlike priest's true form. **Kidigree** has found another way to help. He pours Vorel's specimen away and replaces it with **his own urine**, then submits the cup under the competitor's name. Nothing about the test can now establish that Vorel was clean.

The resulting spell test lights up one specimen in red. It is not Vorel's and not Kanja's. It belongs to **Tubbs 9K**, the friendly dwarven ghost. Officials say they have found a performance-enhancing substance and **disqualify him**. Tubbs protests that it is difficult enough for a ghost to compete against the living, then floats away. What actually produced his positive result is not investigated further in this scene. His disqualification gives **Vorel an automatic place in the grand final**. Vorel did not beat him in a match.

Kidigree, disguised once more, slips Vorel a **small round device with a button**. First, Vorel must defeat Kanja publicly. Then he must press the button and place the disc at Kanja's feet. The priest wants more than the end of an undefeated streak; he wants Kanja shamed so utterly that the crowd cannot look away. Only after that humiliation, he repeats, can he see about bringing back **Hope and Stanley**.

Kanja defeats the armored challenger in the other semifinal. At last the two most unlikely finalists walk into the arena together: a young celebrity who thinks himself invincible, and a lizardfolk who has scarcely been in Sigil long enough to know the rules.

Kanja takes **Bland**. Vorel selects **Kung**.

Again the potion gives Vorel a strange double vision and the opponent's intended moves reach him before their execution. But **Kanja is far better than the Mind Master**. His strikes arrive quickly, his dazing blows leave Kung vulnerable and his tremendous kicks nearly end the game. Vorel learns to survive by ducking at the right moment and counterattacking with the heavy kicks he loves. Near the end both marionettes are down to their last strength, and the crowd sees Kanja ready one final enormous blow. Vorel knows what is coming.

He ducks, kicks, and **Bland falls**.

Vorel has **beaten Kanja in the played championship final**. The crowd howls in outrage. Kanja cannot accept that a newcomer has ended his perfect career, screaming about cheating as vegetables fly onto the stage. His suspicions are not imaginary—Vorel really has cheated—but Kanja has a secret of his own.

Vorel presses the button on Kidigree's disc and slides it to the champion's feet. A purple light climbs across Kanja's body, exposing what looks like **an entire tiny workforce inside him**: little figures operating wheels, walking along stairs, laboring at desks. The miniature inhabitants look out in panic. Kanja turns to flee. The announcer declares the revered champion to be **a team pretending to be one player**, a violation of the sport's rules. The crowd, suddenly delighted at its former idol's disgrace, turns its vegetables and ridicule toward Kanja instead.

The priest's device may have revealed a literal hidden mechanism, or deceived everyone with trickster magic; the game offers **no independent examination of how Kanja is made**. But the humiliation is unmistakable. Kanja is beaten, exposed to a jeering crowd and gone from the stage in tears. That is exactly what Kidigree demanded.

Then the referee lifts Vorel's hand and names him **overall Big Time Smackeroo champion**. The organizers hand him **two sacks totaling five thousand gold pieces** and a great trophy bearing Kung's likeness. Flying reporters with tiny wings paint his portrait. Overnight the despised outsider has become a hero of the sport, though the sight of a victorious lizardfolk says little about how fairly he fought.

The party returns to the **Soggy Feather** amid another parade. The trophy becomes Vorel's stew bowl while fans celebrate well into the night. **Nash** waits tables, earning a gold piece in wages and eighty-five in tips. **Nothing** mixes honest serving with a lucrative talent for emptying patrons' purses. **Squirts** plays tricks and pockets small change. Through it all Vorel guards his winnings; they are not merely a reward for sport, but the means to repair what happened at CHIDS.

When the revelry ends, Nothing asks for payment for guarding Vorel and fighting off the assassins. They settle on **six hundred gold**. Vorel then gives **five hundred** to Nash for the expensive **spell key** that might let him cast again. The original five-thousand-gold purse stands at **three thousand nine hundred** after those transfers.

They sleep through much of the next day. Nash awakes with **a second forearm and an ear**, two more absurd signs of his body growing back in installments. Bill, meanwhile, has decided that the Soggy Feather cannot continue housing them at the Welcome Center's expense. The free vouchers have run out, the damage is considerable, and the inn needs money. Vorel **pays one hundred gold**: forty for repairs, sixty for thirty days' lodging and ordinary meals. **Three thousand eight hundred gold remains from the championship prize**.

Bill speaks wistfully of retiring with Pam to the **Feywild**. Perhaps, he suggests, Vorel would like to buy the Soggy Feather or become a partner. It is a tempting possibility for a party in need of a home, but **no sale is made**.

Vorel's shoulder is swelling again. The doctor's pills have worn off; the dart poison is still there. This time **Squirts uses her own cleric magic to neutralize it**, and the pain and pustules retreat. Then Vorel asks her to take him to the person who began all this business. He has defeated the champion, pressed the humiliating disc and collected his money. It is time to ask the priest to keep his promise.

The four leave the inn together and follow Squirts through Sigil toward the hidden temple. **Hope and Stanley have not yet been revived**. Nothing's separate commission still waits. But Vorel has done what the trickster demanded, and for the first time since the beam there is a clear path toward trying to bring the old party together.

## An open door, not an ending

The four who entered the beam at CHIDS remain divided by what happened there. **Vorel is now Sigil's overall Big Time Smackeroo champion**, with the genuine trophy and a prize of **five thousand gold paid**. But his contest was aided by **a forbidden predictive potion** and a **swapped urine sample**. He also used the priest's glowing device to humiliate **Kanja**, whose apparent body housed a secret miniature work crew, or something the device made the crowd believe was there. **The public humiliation is performed**; **the promised resurrection remains a conditional next step**.

**Nash** has gained forearms and an ear and now physically possesses money that could buy his first spell key, but **has not bought one or restored ordinary spellcasting**. **Nothing** knows the assassins as the **Order of the Crimson Moon** but not their employer, and carries a separately accepted **Abyss-bound kidnapping commission that remains unperformed**. **Squirts** has cured Vorel's lingering dart poison, while the young lizardfolk she burned is **alive at his last witnessed appearance** despite persistent rumors of murder.

The Soggy Feather is a temporary, paid home for thirty days. Bill has suggested ownership, not sold it. Sigil still offers no verified portal back to Earth, and **the Traversal Drive, the Principle and the Soulbroker pact** remain unresolved.

**This narrative currently ends at E087 as the party sets out toward Kidigree's temple.** No meeting with the priest, actual ritual, reunion with Hope and Stanley, or consequence for Kanja beyond this episode is yet described.
