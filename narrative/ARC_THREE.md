## Prologue — After the light

At the bottom of **CHIDS**, beneath the Wastes and amid the monstrous machinery of the **Traversal Drive**, four friends made a choice that should have ended their story. **Vorel**, **Nash**, **Hope** and **Stanley** entered the beam together. After their bodies dissolved into light, the game master declared all four dead. **Sira and Eddie** were alive beyond the beam; **Chance** had been swept away. The fate of the Drive, Earth, and the Principle was still unknown.

There is no ordinary waking from what follows. For Vorel, the white light becomes an experience without a body or a horizon. He sees himself drift away and passes through a vast field of stars. An enormous, motionless, white-haired face hangs before him, too immense to understand and never identified. A tiny black speck travels near him. What seems like an eternity may be no measurable time at all.

Then the stars collapse into a filthy sewer. Vorel awakes exhausted, bare of clothes and equipment, with moss, rubbish and insects where there should be his friends. His familiar **Eye of Groxl** has split like an egg. Something within it has given itself to shield him from the passage, leaving a membrane that peels away from his scales and a silence where its presence used to be. He survived, but not without a sacrifice of his own.

The black speck is still there. Something red and living pushes from one of its openings. An eye opens, and someone looks out.

## I. A voice without a mouth

Inside the little dark object, **Nash Maggard** can see and think. He cannot explain himself to Vorel. What remains of his physical existence resembles a heart made of dark metal or stone, suspended between life and machinery, without a mouth, arms or legs. Vorel calls the baffling thing **Speckle the Cosmic Speck**. Nash must make do with blinks and awkward motion.

The problem becomes urgent when a hungry, hooded ratman approaches the sleeping lizardfolk with utensils and a plan to eat him. Nash learns to scrape and then **float**, struggling to drive the intruder away. The attack wakes Vorel, and the creature retreats into the refuse. It is a poor first encounter with what the locals call a city of infinite possibilities.

The ratman, **Jarver**, later guides Vorel toward other inhabitants of the sewers. There, masked collectors are loading valuable-looking bodies onto a hovering slab. They are the two people Vorel least expected to find: **Hope and Stanley**, motionless and apparently dead. Hope is missing her mechanical arm and leg. Stanley is cold and lifeless. Vorel will not permit them to disappear into the local trade in corpses.

What follows is desperate and ugly. Vorel uses his changing scales to surprise a collector, then fights the others for possession of the bodies. Electrical weapons flash through the sewer water. Jarver kills one of the collectors, while Vorel restrains or knocks out others without the same intention to kill. Nash cannot shape a spell without hands or a voice, but discovers that he can **ram someone with the heart that contains him**. The party wins not by restoring the dead but by preventing their removal.

The surviving collector, **Binch Lory**, explains that bodies recorded in the **Dead Book** have a price with the **Dustmen**. Eventually he and Vorel come to terms: Vorel will take his friends and borrow the hovering slab; Binch can inspect the strange site where Vorel appeared. Binch names the place **Sigil**, the **City of Doors**, and sketches a route toward its Welcome Center. He says portals can connect the city to other worlds. Whether one leads home is another matter.

For the first time since the beam, Vorel has a concrete task: keep Hope and Stanley together long enough to find someone who might help. It is the sort of duty he understands, even in a place he does not.

## II. The city that curves overhead

Outside the sewers, the world opens in the wrong direction. Sigil's streets, roofs and windows curve away and continue **overhead**, so that the sky is more city. Its inhabitants barely pause for a naked lizardfolk carrying two bodies on a floating platform, with an eye-bearing stone heart keeping him company. To Vorel, who has crossed deserts, raised kingdoms and fought among dragons and demons, the ordinary business of this impossible metropolis may be the strangest sight yet.

On the way, Nash grows a **small human finger**. It is grotesque, painful, and almost useless, but it offers hope that the change may continue. At the **Welcome Center**, Vorel has to describe himself as a newcomer with no possessions and no money. The clerk records his skills, his beliefs and his desperate need for lodging. She also registers the floating heart as **Nash Maggard of the Solar Mines**, treating Vorel's extraordinary explanation as just another administrative inconvenience.

The vouchers buy **three days of meals and shelter**, not a return passage, not a new life, and certainly not the resurrection of Hope and Stanley. The clerk suggests the Dustmen and a possible cleric. Vorel takes the bodies instead to the **Soggy Feather**, where **Bill** tends the bar and **Pam** provides food. Bill offers hot drinks, stories, advice about work and the small comforts that can matter in a city built to swallow newcomers. There is no immediate way for Vorel to earn the gold a miracle might require.

Two strangers notice the party. One is a dark-feathered **kenku**, wrapped in travel-worn clothes and a long-beaked mask, speaking mostly by repeating what other people have said. The other is a tiny, energetic **kobold** wearing scraps of improvised armor and carrying an implausible utensil-built weapon. Neither is one of the companions on the slab. The shape of the party has already changed, though its surviving members have not decided what that means.

## III. The church that cannot stay in one alley

The kobold calls herself **Squirts**. She carries her infant child, **Chunks**, in a shoe attached to her clothing and has an enthusiast's devotion to tricks. The kenku is called **Nothing**. Where Vorel demands direct answers, Nothing has borrowed voices, gestures and silence. Bill employs the bird as a guide; Squirts knows a place where the dead might conceivably be brought back.

She leads Vorel, Nash and Nothing through prosperous streets and increasingly ragged quarters to a dreary concrete church down an otherwise empty alley. Its door responds to a thoroughly undignified signal. Beyond the shabby exterior are unexpectedly extensive halls, strange attendants and a birdlike priest devoted to a trickster power. Vorel brings Hope and Stanley before him and explains what the beam took.

The priest examines Stanley through a crystal apparatus and sees markings that are not visible to ordinary eyes. He calls **Stanley and Hope “petitioners”**, manifestations associated with souls rather than conventional bodies, and connects them to **the Weir**. He has reason to fear what that connection signifies. For Vorel, the distinction matters less than one question: can he get his friends back?

The priest cannot simply promise a resurrection. He proposes a bargain instead. The god he serves delights in **public humiliation**, especially when someone proud or powerful is made ridiculous before people whose admiration he expects. If Vorel helps arrange the right humiliation, the priest says he will attempt to obtain what he needs to restore Hope and Stanley. At first he does not even know whom the trick should target. Vorel accepts the possibility because it is something to pursue, not because his companions have already been saved.

The bodies pass into the church's **protected crypts**. No ritual is performed. When the travelers leave and look back, the alley and the church have vanished from view, as if that little part of Sigil can never be relied upon to stay where it was. Squirts is not surprised.

A night's rest brings a startling change for Nash: **a mouth and a functioning hand** emerge from the heart. He can finally speak to Vorel, even if his form remains absurd. The next morning three **Pelor-affiliated Welcome Center visitors** arrive with charity and suggestions of regular work. Squirts distrusts them. They speak of factions, laws, the **Lady of Pain** and a city where powers cannot simply impose themselves; those are their descriptions of Sigil, not direct encounters with the Lady. Vorel hears offers of apprenticeships, pit fighting and bounties, but he wants neither a career in a new city nor a permanent place in it. He wants his friends and his world.

Bill's alternative is improbable but more appealing: a competition of magically animated fighting **marionettes** called **Big Time Smack'em Brothers**. It promises money to the victor. **Vorel, Nash and Nothing register**; Squirts does not. The group visits the venue, learns that practice costs money they do not have, and searches the city's libraries for a champion's strategy book. Nash finds it—with someone already reading it.

## IV. No book for the innocent

The halfling reader refuses to share his copy of the celebrated marionette guide. Nash, now blessed with a mouth and a talent for causing trouble, first argues and threatens, then lures the reader outside with the promise of a confrontation. While the two prepare to settle their disagreement, **Vorel steals the book** and Nothing hides it. The reader realizes the deception and gives chase.

The resulting escape is scarcely noble. Vorel uses his camouflage to wait in an alley, strikes the pursuing halfling **nonlethally**, then picks him up and carries him away with the stolen guide. The companions make sure he is still breathing. Squirts leads them once more to her hidden church, which appears in an alley different from the one they used before.

The priest supplies petals for a preparation that is supposed to **blur memory**. Squirts crushes them, mixes the powder into a homemade drink and administers it to the unconscious man. No one tests what he will forget. The group leaves him near the library, having acquired the wisdom of their sport by rather less admirable means than studying quietly.

This time the priest has found his target: the young, celebrated, apparently undefeated champion whose name is associated with the very book they took. His defeat at the hands of **Vorel, a complete novice**, would be a humiliation worthy of the church. But the priest's definition of a fair chance is dangerously convenient. He offers to steer Vorel toward easier matchups and gives **Squirts small devices intended to make opposing players sick**. Vorel recognizes the difference between tricks and fair competition. He nevertheless continues toward the contest, because the bargain is bound up with the fate of his friends.

Back at the Soggy Feather, the group spends much of the day studying the book. They learn how to make the marionettes leap, kick, block and stun one another, and how a decisive **Big Time Smackeroo** can throw a defeated opponent out of play. Reading grants them enough familiarity to attempt the game, though not years of practice. What seemed a desperate way to make a little money is becoming a staged affront to the city's most celebrated player.

## V. A job the others do not hear

Not everyone at the Soggy Feather has the same reason to stay in Sigil. During dinner, a man called **Pelvis** arrives wearing a neat hat and showing off a grin of gold and diamond teeth. Nothing recognizes him. He is a **fixer**, a man who connects those with dangerous work to those willing to perform it. He knows Nothing from earlier jobs and uses a name resembling **Ebon Skulker**.

Pelvis draws the kenku aside. He offers **three times the usual fee** and a note directing Nothing to a magic shop. The details are for a client to provide. Nothing agrees, but the rest of the company does not hear the private arrangement. Asked about the visitor, Nothing supplies little beyond the word *job*. It is not enough to reveal what has been promised.

Before dawn, after another night's rest, Nash acquires **another hand and forearm, and a dangling foot**. He is becoming more human piece by piece without ceasing to be a floating heart. Nothing has gone elsewhere. At the address from Pelvis's note, a strange old **githyanki** shopkeeper produces a box containing a small marble pyramid. Nothing activates it in a private room and steps into a space where an anonymous client speaks through a screened partition, unseen and with a disguised voice.

The commission is not ordinary burglary. Nothing is to **abduct a prominent man from Sigil's high quarters**, take him to an address in the **Hive**, and use a portal to **deliver him into the Abyss**. The client allows **no more than seven days** to finish the job, and warns that the portal will permit only **one hour to return** once used. The target's name and faction are uncertain in the machine transcripts; one recognizes the faction as **Godsmen**, another as **Guardsmen**. Whoever this person is, Nothing has accepted an order that has nothing to do with learning marionette fighting.

Nothing returns to the inn. The others prepare for their tournament without being shown the secret client's instructions. **No abduction has occurred.** Two debts now hang over the new company: one made in the hope of recovering the old party, another whose cost has yet to be seen.

## VI. Three marionettes and an uncertain victory

The **Royce qualifying venue** is crowded with competitors, controllers and the expectant noise of a popular sport. Guards and an elven examiner screen entrants for magic. Vorel passes. Nash, despite looking like an impossible enchanted anatomical specimen, is admitted after the scanner detects nothing objectionable. Squirts' magical stone attracts attention, and the guards turn her away. Nothing avoids the screening by stealing a small object from confiscated property, planting it on another entrant, and provoking a fight at the gate. While the guards deal with the disturbance, the kenku slips inside.

Squirts finds another route. The trickster priest is present **disguised as a human** and ushers her through a side entrance. He intends to interfere with the competition; Squirts has her own tools for the task.

An announcement changes the players' preparations. A problem with the **modron-built marionettes** means the qualifiers will offer just three fighters: **Bland**, **Kung** and **Rubles**. The favorite characters some entrants studied are unavailable. An anonymous benefactor has also added **four thousand gold pieces** to the advertised prize pool. The money grows more tempting, but it has not yet been won.

Vorel's first opponent is an elderly **gnome**, a friendly competitor more interested in enjoying the game than overturning the sport's hierarchy. Before they sit down, **Squirts secretly pricks the gnome with one of the priest's sickness devices**. He notices something strange and gradually struggles to maintain his concentration. Vorel plays **Kung**, a lizardfolk fighter mocked as a beginner's choice, against the gnome's **Rubles**. It is hardly a straightforward victory. The gnome strikes first, builds dangerous stagger and nearly sends Vorel out of the match. Vorel recovers with a biting attack and carefully timed blows, finally reducing his opponent's fighter to defeat.

He has **won his opening match**. He has also benefited from a deliberate act of sabotage against a man who greeted him kindly. The gnome, feeling ill, suspects that a kobold interfered. Vorel has advanced, but the manner of the advance is already part of the story.

Elsewhere Nash faces an elderly orc woman who has come to enjoy the same tournament. Unable to choose his planned fighter, Nash takes **Rubles** against her **Bland**. The match becomes an education in blocks, counterattacks and costly mistakes. An early attempt at a dramatic knockout fails, and the opponent punishes him for it. Nash persists, builds her stagger past the threshold, and finally lands the **Big Time Smackeroo** that sends her marionette flying. She smashes her controller in frustration. **Nash, too, has won the first round.** Unlike Vorel's opponent, hers is not shown to have been poisoned or otherwise manipulated.

Then comes Nothing's game. The kenku faces **another kenku**, whose instinct is also to mimic. One takes Bland; Nothing chooses Kung. Neither will move first. Each waits to copy the other's controller action. Seconds stretch into silence as the timer drains away. When it expires, the game declares **both competitors forfeit**. Two wins and a double timeout: an absurd beginning to the hoped-for rescue.

The famous champion has not been faced. No tournament prize has been paid. The cleric has not performed the promised ritual. The real consequences of the sickened gnome, the stolen book, and Nothing's secret agreement remain beyond this opening stretch of Sigil.

## VII. Always high kick

As the qualifier wears on, the marionette stations begin to vanish from the floor. Competitors who arrived expecting a day of games become spectators, and **Vorel and Nash survive round after round**. Vorel's opponents have often seemed ill; Nash has found many less threatening than he expected. Neither can tell how much of their progress belongs to skill, luck or Squirts' unseen work. By the lunch break only four remain.

One of them is a quiet gray-skinned elf whose hood obscures her face. Another is **Ventro**, an immense alligatorlike man adored at Royce Gymnasium and accompanied by a gang of swaggering supporters. His victory streak is local to this venue; the greater champion whom the priest wants humiliated is still **Kanja**, waiting somewhere beyond the qualifiers. Ventro seizes the competitors' dining room as if it belongs to him. He insults Nash, boasts about his victories and threatens anyone who refuses to be afraid. Squirts responds in the language of her calling: she secretly puts **two of the priest's sickness needles into Ventro's leg**. He catches her despite her attempt to disappear, and eventually **kicks her and Chunks across the room**. The violence does not end his tournament entry. The others hold themselves back, hoping he will be disqualified instead of dragging them into a street fight.

The announced pairings provide a stranger ordeal: **Vorel must face Nash**. For all the terrible things the two have survived together, Vorel resists the idea of shaming a friend for sport. Nash reassures him that marionettes are only a game; he even offers to lose deliberately. Squirts has no such hesitation about bending the odds and **sticks Nash with a sickness needle**. But Nash withstands the poison. Their match proceeds without a declared surrender.

Nash chooses the monk **Bland**, Vorel the lizardfolk **Kung**. They test one another with punches, ducking kicks and feints. Both miss repeatedly, to the bewilderment of a crowd that has seen sharper matches. Eventually Vorel finds his rhythm. He dazes Bland, strings together the jumping kicks he favors, and prevails after Nash briefly breaks out of the combination. **Vorel wins**. Nash takes the loss without resentment, leaving the stage to watch his friend.

The other match has already gone to Ventro. He adds insult to victory by **kicking the defeated elf from the stage**. No official intervenes. His popularity seems enough to excuse conduct that would ruin an outsider. Now the crowd roars for a match between the local champion and an almost unknown lizardfolk, a newcomer who started playing only the day before.

Ventro knows Vorel likes Kung. When the marionette selection opens, he seizes the fighter first, expecting the novice to collapse without his favored choice. **Vorel chooses Bland** instead. On the screen the monk meets the lizardfolk in a flurry of blows. Vorel learns quickly, using a stunning strike to open a combination, then **jumping into one heavy high kick after another**. Ventro struggles to escape the blows. His fighters' remaining strength disappears. The champion at Royce Gymnasium has lost.

Ventro cannot accept it. He lifts the game station and tries to crush Vorel with it.

## VIII. A warning without a voice

Nothing reacts first, pitching a hammer at Ventro's arm. The shattered rhythm of the threatened blow buys Vorel time. **Nash tries to make him larger**, hoping a spell can protect him, but something prevents the magic from taking shape. Squirts has no comparable trouble: she commands Ventro to kneel, and the furious champion finds himself **groveling and praising Vorel against his will**.

The crowd's favorite has brought his entourage. They pour over the barriers with clubs and axes. Nothing scatters **ball bearings** into their path, sending some sprawling. Squirts' magic takes the fury out of others, though not all; her hastily made crossbow drives a blade into Ventro's foot. Nothing attacks with swords, trying to subdue him. Nash keeps attempting magic, only for the same unknown force to silence each effort. He resorts to punching a thug with one of the awkward hands grown from his heart. Vorel stands amid blows, raging, gripping one attacker and using him as a weapon against the rest.

The tournament has ceased to resemble a game. A mob watches the violence rise from a stage where the rules were supposed to prevent exactly this. Ventro recovers his axes and turns them on Nothing.

Then the entire arena goes quiet.

A **towering green figure shaped like a woman** has appeared where there was none. Every spectator has the unmistakable impression that its gaze falls upon them. Nobody hears a voice, yet the warning is understood. Ventro tries to protest: it was the others, he says; they cheated; he did nothing wrong. His defiance comes too late. His body begins to collapse toward a hole in reality scarcely large enough to see. He twists and cries out while something draws him through it, until **he is gone**. The figure vanishes as suddenly as it arrived.

The companions have heard the name **Lady of Pain** in Sigil, always with an admonition not to attract her notice. What they have just witnessed fits the terror behind those warnings, though the figure has not given her name. **Ventro's disappearance is certain; his death or destination is not**.

His entourage scatters. The spectators recover their voices and cheer for Vorel, the unlikely **champion of the Royce qualifier**. A much larger final event remains, and the overall champion **Kanja** has not been faced. But for this one afternoon, Vorel has conquered a realm that has nothing to do with kingdoms, gods or dragon engines: a world of marionettes, applause, and the embarrassing power of a jumping kick.

## IX. The price of being famous

Victory travels faster than the companions do. A reporter from **M Sports Weekly** wants Vorel's portrait, his history and the secret of a game he has barely learned to play. Small winged creatures paint his likeness while admirers crowd forward. He names **Mighty Squirts** as his team, with **Squirts** as its manager. The answer is good enough for a story, though there has been no formal team registration. Vorel recommends reading books and relying on the high kick. The journalists are delighted.

Merchants are delighted too. **Arcane Spice** offers a monthly fee for wearing its branding; a maker of marionette controllers offers even more. But the contracts trouble Vorel, whose friends' earlier promises to a supernatural broker brought them to this desperate place. He listens to the offers and **signs nothing**. He would rather be poor than find his name caught in another agreement he cannot escape.

Some attention is harder to fend off than a contract. Admirers want his time, affection and company, and do not always listen when he refuses. Vorel gives the stolen strategy book to a young lizardfolk admirer and is soon leading a crowd back toward the **Soggy Feather**, where Bill and Pam barely know how to cope with so many customers. **Nash and Nothing help serve** the crowd and **earn actual wages and tips**; Nothing also tricks drinkers into surrendering extra coins. Squirts collects a few strange little treasures whose uses are not yet known. For the first time since the beam, the survivors have at least a little money of their own, though none of it is the promised grand tournament prize.

Vorel himself has gained little but admirers he does not want. When several refuse to leave him alone, he tries desperate distractions and finally **leaps through an upper window** to escape. He returns only after the inn grows quiet. Nothing lets him inside; the companions retreat to the small, strange peace of their room. For one evening, the Soggy Feather has been a place of celebration rather than a refuge for the homeless.

The peace does not last.

Late in the night, Nothing's **warning sword begins to vibrate**. The kenku hears movement above the room and cries out to wake the others. They listen into the dark. Then **two armed, ninja-like figures burst through a window** and rush toward them, swords drawn. Their faces, employer and purpose are still unknown when the episode ends.

The newly famous champion has a larger tournament ahead of him. His friends have neither been restored nor forgotten. And Sigil, which offered the party a safe place to sleep, has just shown that even a locked door may be no protection at all.

## X. The moon on the headbands

The swords that crash through the **Soggy Feather** windows are not there to challenge Vorel in a game. Two masked strangers roll into the bedroom wearing black and white, with a red moon and howling wolf on their clothes. When they find the lizardfolk's bed empty, they descend on **Nothing**, who has already been warned by the sword she keeps close to her chair. Her twin blades cut down the first intruder. For an instant there is a look of terror behind the mask, and then the assassin becomes **dust**, leaving only the empty costume.

More figures arrive through the windows and door. Some have swords, others nunchucks or fans of thrown knives. **Vorel leaps from beneath his bed, pointing toward the ceiling and calling on the Lady of Pain** to punish the attackers. No green statue appears. Squirts tries to turn the dead men's clothes into restraints, then abandons the attempt amid the rush of weapons. Nash, tucked into his bed, calls out the question he has been carrying for days: why can he not make his magic work?

Squirts answers between blows. He needs **a key**.

That is no help in a room full of assassins. It is the strange **dragon-metal heart** itself that gives Nash something he can do. Remembering its old enchantment, he gathers breath in a mouth that should never have grown from a heart, then spits a cone of burning energy across the room. The assailants catch fire. Their weapons still find the party, but the stone-and-flesh survivor can at least fight back.

Vorel pulls a mask from one attacker and finds a mouthless, distorted face beneath. He decides he needs a live captive. He grapples a ninja and tries to keep him alive as the others try to hack off his head. Nothing comes flying across the melee and kills two assailants at his back. A more elaborately dressed woman in the same black uniform finally steps through the window and holds Vorel with a dark power he manages to shrug off. Her hands know how to stop a weapon in motion: when Nothing attacks, the woman twists the kenku's wrist and kicks her into a painful stupor.

Squirts commands the woman to freeze. It works. **Vorel charges at her with a mattress**, knocks her to the floor, then pins her beneath it without intending to kill her. Nash, who has been burning every masked attacker he can reach, lifts a corner of the bedding and breathes again. There is a scream. The mattress falls flat. The captive has vanished like the others, leaving Vorel with nothing to question—and with an expensive mattress he tears to pieces in frustration. Bill, who has run upstairs with a club to help fight off a knife thrower, is not amused.

The inn survives. The attackers do not. But the insignia means something to Nothing. In a private moment, the kenku recognizes the symbol from old underworld warnings: **the Order of the Crimson Moon**, an almost legendary guild of assassins, expensive enough that only powerful clients usually employ them. Nothing cannot simply say all she knows. She arranges clothing, paints a red crescent with blood, imitates coin sounds and watches Vorel and Bill stumble through ridiculous guesses until they finally understand the name.

They know **who the masked killers were**. They still do not know **who paid them**.

Bill objects when Vorel complains that the Lady should have appeared to save him. Sigil's mysterious power is not the group's personal bodyguard, he says; her actions are not predictable, and the city is far from free of violence. Vorel imagines making himself her champion by taking down assassins. Bill advises him to win his tournament, find his friends and get home. For now, at least, that sounds like an achievable ambition.

## XI. The cape, the child and the key

By morning the Soggy Feather has mostly been put back in order. Nash's long sleep has made **a thigh and calf grow behind one of his feet**. He can almost stand on a leg, though the rest of him remains a floating heart with an eye and an assortment of hands. The broken windows and scorched room have not frightened away Vorel's admirers. The dining room is once again full of people who want a piece of the local champion.

Vorel had refused sponsorship contracts the day before, afraid of binding himself to more promises. But **Arcane Spice** offers another possibility: wear its colors for a single day, and receive **two hundred gold**. That is money he can understand, and there is no lifetime oath attached. He tries on a splendid blue cape with animated lettering and begins to feel like a man whose fortunes are turning.

His priestly patron arrives disguised as an old man with a peculiar eye. **Kidigree** has been thinking about the day's larger championship. Sickening opponents will draw too much attention now, and the competitors will be watched for magic. The solution, in his view, is to make **Vorel** better at the marionette game. In Bill's kitchen the priest opens a coat lined with living soil and harvests a hideous pink mushroom, biting plants, and other things no cook should recognize. Nothing puts on a chef hat. Squirts plunges into the hot mixture long enough to lend it some trickster magic. The priest sacrifices a squirrel for its eyes, and the resulting concoction looks almost as dreadful as it smells.

Vorel drinks it.

The priest promises that in the **three o'clock finals**, Vorel will see opponents' moves and patterns with unnatural clarity. He also warns that the officials forbid **performance-enhancing substances** and that no one must talk about what they have done. There is no evidence yet that the potion works. But the possibility of cheating his way to the great champion seems, to this odd new company, better than abandoning Hope and Stanley.

Then the morning takes a turn no one expected. A small lizardfolk boy is still waiting in the common room, begging Vorel to become his father. Vorel tells him to find his own greatness. **Squirts** bites the boy's tail and orders him to leave. The child flails at her, attempting to defend himself. She answers with **Sacred Flame**. Fire runs across his scales, and he collapses. Squirts drags his motionless body through the room, past horrified onlookers, and hides him in the rubbish behind the inn.

An Arcane Spice representative has seen enough. He accuses the party of allowing a child to be killed, **tears up the agreement** and retrieves Vorel's beautiful blue cape. Other sponsors and admirers leave. The money that seemed so close is gone. Even Bill's separate arrangement to promote the same brand at the Soggy Feather may be ruined.

But the boy's story does not end beneath the rubbish. Squirts returns to him, and, remembering a cleric's power that can stabilize the dying, **casts Spare the Dying**. The child opens his eyes, speaks and runs away, badly burned but alive. Squirts tells him his own father saved him and sends him home. The crowd does not see this. The incident is not harmless merely because he lives, and the party's reputation does not recover as quickly as his breath.

An official from the **M Sports commission** arrives to explain the day's schedule. Vorel is expected at the larger finals at **three o'clock**, with an escort around **one**. The official emphasizes strict bans on doping, cheating and magical equipment. Vorel, who has just drunk an illicit performance-enhancing potion, has reason to listen.

Without a sponsor, he decides to make his own. Bill would appreciate a cape bearing **the Soggy Feather**. At the enormous bazaar, a rug merchant introduces him to a tailor who promises the garment for twenty gold. Vorel's celebrity draws business to the stall, and when he returns for the red cape with its brilliant white letters, the tailor **waives the price**. For once, fame has bought him something without a contract.

Nothing leads Nash elsewhere in the same market. The kenku has been trying to tell him what he needs, and here the explanation finally has a shape. An old woman sells curious little charms: **spell keys**. Sigil's magic does not behave as Nash expects because ordinary spellcasting is constrained here. Keys associated with **schools of magic** can unlock those abilities. Nash would need different keys for different spells. **Each costs five hundred gold**, or fifty a month over twelve months if he can pass a credit check and provide identification issued by a faction.

Nash stares at the prices. He has neither the necessary money nor the paperwork. A fortune he once had still waits in a vault back in **Tietkalla**, beyond every door he knows how to open. The vendor cannot help him leave with a key on credit. Nothing has brought him to an answer but not a solution.

So Nash, now an eye, a mouth, two hands, a heart and one strange leg, floats back into the bazaar with the others. **Vorel has a cape and a hidden advantage he has not yet used. The assassins' employer is unknown. Hope and Stanley remain in the crypts. The championship has not begun.** Sigil has given the group new information—and many new reasons to fear what comes next.

## XII. The champion who knew tomorrow's move

On the way back from Sigil's bazaar, whispers trail Vorel's bright new cape. The city remembers the young lizardfolk dragged out of the Soggy Feather, not the fact that **he survived**. Bill is taking down the Arcane Spice sign when Vorel returns. The inn has lost its sponsorship, but Vorel has little time to worry about that now. The great championship begins this afternoon.

There is something wrong with his shoulder. A swelling has grown where a masked assassin's dart struck him in the night. Nash, with the resourcefulness and questionable judgment of a man currently shaped like a heart, **bites it open**. Yellow fluid runs down Vorel's scales. Suddenly Vorel feels heavy, slow and sick. The dart has carried a **delayed poison**, and it could make winning an impossible task.

The official escort arrives. Vorel accuses the championship's other players of sending the assassins, but has no name, confession or evidence to support him. The official inspects the empty black costumes strewn across the ruined upstairs room, recognizes the symbol of the **Order of the Crimson Moon**, and is disturbed by the expense someone has undertaken to kill this newcomer. But recognition is not proof of who paid. The official will help Vorel seek medical treatment at the Colosseum, not condemn a competitor on a hunch.

The **Great Colosseum** rises like a monument above Sigil's streets, surrounded by banners and people. They arrive in a six-horse carriage and pass through a strict magical search. Nothing leaves her warning sword behind. Squirts leaves her strange forehead stone. Nash's heart is itself a source of enchantment, so an attendant clips an **M-shaped badge** to him: he is allowed in as a magically constituted person, even though enchanted equipment is banned.

Eight players wait in the competitors' lounge. An armored warrior sits alone with a drink. A talking **illithid** shares a table with the jovial ghost of **Tubby Flavorbeard**, who calls himself **Tubbs 9K**. A halfling calling himself **the Mind Master** arrives surrounded by admirers. And near a throng of young fans sits **Kanja**, the celebrated champion who has supposedly never lost, sheltered behind a broad mirrorlike visor. He barely acknowledges the outsider everyone is discussing.

The ghost and the illithid welcome Vorel to their table, only to ask whether his little manager killed a child because she was jealous of Vorel's supposed love for the boy. Sigil has built a scandal from bits of truth and invention. Squirts and Vorel deny the romance; nobody can explain what happened in a way that satisfies the curious.

The Colosseum's physician finds the poison in Vorel's body and offers pills that will let him fight **without its crippling symptoms for eight or ten hours**. The poison will come back, he warns, and will require actual treatment later. Vorel suspects another plot. He holds the medicine rather than swallowing it. Nothing listens at the doctor's door and hears nothing but a solitary song. In the lounge Nash chews one of the pills, feels a little better, and persuades Vorel that the medicine may be what it claims. **Vorel takes the remaining tablets**. His strength returns for the tournament, though the infection is still there beneath the surface.

In the announced first round, **Kanja, the armored Arsenad and Tubbs 9K** win their respective games. When Vorel's turn comes, a woman accompanying the Mind Master kisses the halfling, looks over his shoulder at Vorel and for an instant meets his gaze. Vorel knows those eyes. **They were in his bedroom during the assassination attempt.** Whether this woman hired the killers, wore the black mask, survived the vanishing body or only resembles someone he saw is still a mystery. The Mind Master laughs off the suggestion.

The spectators have other concerns. They greet Vorel with chants of **child killer** and a storm of rotten vegetables. Vorel rages and starts batting the missiles away with fire, but officials warn that spells and magic on stage will bring disqualification. He sits down at the marionette controller instead.

The Mind Master chooses **Rubles**. Vorel takes **Kung**, the fighter he has barely stopped playing since his first day. As the models tower above the arena and the match begins, the wretched potion from Kidigree's cauldron finally takes hold.

Vorel's vision trails and splits. More strangely, a voice inside his head begins to **announce the Mind Master's intended moves before he makes them**. The novice hears what his opponent plans, yet must still choose how to answer it. He misjudges some attacks, blocks others and is nearly caught. Then he finds the rhythm of his favorite maneuver: the **jumping heavy kick**, the blow that has carried him through Royce. Rubles staggers beneath Kung's attacks, and a last ducking kick sends the opponent's marionette down.

Vorel has won. **It is a played victory—and the unfair assistance of a forbidden potion is real.** The Mind Master leaves furious, promising that Vorel's career will not survive what he has done.

## XIII. Five thousand gold and the tears of Kanja

The tournament has rules against exactly the sort of advantage Vorel used. **Kanja** can see that something is wrong. He demands that the officials test the outsider, arguing that no one arriving from a portal could dominate experienced players so quickly. Squirts responds by urinating on the champion's leg, and then insists that **all the remaining competitors be tested**, not only Vorel.

The officials send the players to a latrine for a supervised urine sample. A pleasant attendant named **Jimmy** watches Vorel fill his cup. But when the attendant lifts the sample, **his eye changes**, briefly revealing the birdlike priest's true form. **Kidigree** has found another way to help. He pours Vorel's specimen away and replaces it with **his own urine**, then submits the cup under the competitor's name. Nothing about the test can now establish that Vorel was clean.

The resulting spell test lights up one specimen in red. It is not Vorel's and not Kanja's. It belongs to **Tubbs 9K**, the friendly dwarven ghost. Officials say they have found a performance-enhancing substance and **disqualify him**. Tubbs protests that it is difficult enough for a ghost to compete against the living, then floats away. What actually produced his positive result is not investigated further in this scene. His disqualification gives **Vorel an automatic place in the grand final**. Vorel did not beat him in a match.

Kidigree, disguised once more, slips Vorel a **small round device with a button**. First, Vorel must defeat Kanja publicly. Then he must press the button and place the disc at Kanja's feet. The priest wants more than the end of an undefeated streak; he wants Kanja shamed so utterly that the crowd cannot look away. Only after that humiliation, he repeats, can he see about bringing back **Hope and Stanley**.

Kanja defeats the armored challenger in the other semifinal. At last the two most unlikely finalists walk into the arena together: a young celebrity who thinks himself invincible, and a lizardfolk who has scarcely been in Sigil long enough to know the rules.

Kanja takes **Bland**. Vorel selects **Kung**.

Again the potion gives Vorel a strange double vision and the opponent's intended moves reach him before their execution. But **Kanja is far better than the Mind Master**. His strikes arrive quickly, his dazing blows leave Kung vulnerable and his tremendous kicks nearly end the game. Vorel learns to survive by ducking at the right moment and counterattacking with the heavy kicks he loves. Near the end both marionettes are down to their last strength, and the crowd sees Kanja ready one final enormous blow. Vorel knows what is coming.

He ducks, kicks, and **Bland falls**.

Vorel has **beaten Kanja in the played championship final**. The crowd howls in outrage. Kanja cannot accept that a newcomer has ended his perfect career, screaming about cheating as vegetables fly onto the stage. His suspicions are not imaginary—Vorel really has cheated—but Kanja has a secret of his own.

Vorel presses the button on Kidigree's disc and slides it to the champion's feet. A purple light climbs across Kanja's body, exposing what looks like **an entire tiny workforce inside him**: little figures operating wheels, walking along stairs, laboring at desks. The miniature inhabitants look out in panic. Kanja turns to flee. The announcer declares the revered champion to be **a team pretending to be one player**, a violation of the sport's rules. The crowd, suddenly delighted at its former idol's disgrace, turns its vegetables and ridicule toward Kanja instead.

The priest's device may have revealed a literal hidden mechanism, or deceived everyone with trickster magic; the game offers **no independent examination of how Kanja is made**. But the humiliation is unmistakable. Kanja is beaten, exposed to a jeering crowd and gone from the stage in tears. That is exactly what Kidigree demanded.

Then the referee lifts Vorel's hand and names him **overall Big Time Smackeroo champion**. The organizers hand him **two sacks totaling five thousand gold pieces** and a great trophy bearing Kung's likeness. Flying reporters with tiny wings paint his portrait. Overnight the despised outsider has become a hero of the sport, though the sight of a victorious lizardfolk says little about how fairly he fought.

The party returns to the **Soggy Feather** amid another parade. The trophy becomes Vorel's stew bowl while fans celebrate well into the night. **Nash** waits tables, earning a gold piece in wages and eighty-five in tips. **Nothing** mixes honest serving with a lucrative talent for emptying patrons' purses. **Squirts** plays tricks and pockets small change. Through it all Vorel guards his winnings; they are not merely a reward for sport, but the means to repair what happened at CHIDS.

When the revelry ends, Nothing asks for payment for guarding Vorel and fighting off the assassins. They settle on **six hundred gold**. Vorel then gives **five hundred** to Nash for the expensive **spell key** that might let him cast again. The original five-thousand-gold purse stands at **three thousand nine hundred** after those transfers.

They sleep through much of the next day. Nash awakes with **a second forearm and an ear**, two more absurd signs of his body growing back in installments. Bill, meanwhile, has decided that the Soggy Feather cannot continue housing them at the Welcome Center's expense. The free vouchers have run out, the damage is considerable, and the inn needs money. Vorel **pays one hundred gold**: forty for repairs, sixty for thirty days' lodging and ordinary meals. **Three thousand eight hundred gold remains from the championship prize**.

Bill speaks wistfully of retiring with Pam to the **Feywild**. Perhaps, he suggests, Vorel would like to buy the Soggy Feather or become a partner. It is a tempting possibility for a party in need of a home, but **no sale is made**.

Vorel's shoulder is swelling again. The doctor's pills have worn off; the dart poison is still there. This time **Squirts uses her own cleric magic to neutralize it**, and the pain and pustules retreat. Then Vorel asks her to take him to the person who began all this business. He has defeated the champion, pressed the humiliating disc and collected his money. It is time to ask the priest to keep his promise.

The four leave the inn together and follow Squirts through Sigil toward the hidden temple. **Hope and Stanley have not yet been revived**. Nothing's separate commission still waits. But Vorel has done what the trickster demanded, and for the first time since the beam there is a clear path toward trying to bring the old party together.

## XIV. The mirror and the tears of a civilization

Vorel has achieved the extraordinary and humiliating trick that bought his friends' return. But there is no familiar chapel where he expects it. **Squirts** leads the group for half an hour, through streets that seem to rearrange themselves, until an alley reveals a sunken building with smooth stone doors. A fart noise from the little kobold opens them.

Inside the **Chapel of Laughter**, an eager priest named **Carr** greets them with a joke: pull his finger and a great noise is followed by little fireworks. His serious expression suggests he believes this a fine advance in the art of surprise. When Vorel asks for Kidigree, Carr leads them down many flights of stairs, past rats and dark corridors, into a room full of mirrors.

The **Hall of Reflections** is built like half an amphitheater. Mirrors of every shape line its rising tiers, each showing a different being under the hood of the single figure at the table below: an orc, a cat, an elf, a noble, a reptilian warrior. **Kidigree** sits at the center with hundreds of empty and filled vials. He is watching one mirror that reflects no one in the chamber. It looks instead into an alley elsewhere in Sigil.

**Kanja** runs into the alley, frantic to escape. Masked figures catch him, and behind them comes the **Mind Master**, the halfling whom Vorel defeated in the first round. The halfling identifies himself as an investor and director in the marionette fighting league, furious that Kanja's ten years of fame and prize money were won through deceit. He orders the exposed champion to repay **one hundred thousand gold in one week**. If the money does not come, his men will kill one of the little people inside Kanja's apparent body every week until none remain.

The guards wear the black and red emblems of the **Order of the Crimson Moon**. Vorel's suspicions acquire a sharper edge: the Mind Master truly works with those assassins, at least in this scene. Yet there is still no proof he gave the order to attack Vorel at the Soggy Feather.

Kanja collapses in terror. Through the mirror Kidigree draws the tears from his eyes, filling little vials with the anguish not merely of a fallen champion but of the crowd of tiny inhabitants inside him. He calls it **the tears of a civilization**. Vorel does not celebrate the cruelty. He has come for his friends.

Kidigree invites Vorel to sit. The priest admits he thought the lizardfolk a fool when Squirts first brought him here, and says that the **Council of Clowns** loves nothing better than a joke at the expense of the dead and those who love them. He nearly decided to keep Hope and Stanley, purely because Vorel had trusted him. But a trickster must be inconsistent, Kidigree reasons, and today the more surprising trick is to **keep his word**. He warns Vorel that such trust may one day kill him. Then he asks Carr to bring in the dead.

Two mirrors are wheeled into the chamber. Two more slabs follow, with **Hope and Stanley's unchanged, inert bodies** strapped upright to them. Kidigree brushes strange liquid over the corpses and chants. For a moment, nothing happens.

Far from that room—in a place without clocks, walls or a body—**Stanley** perceives light. He cannot tell whether a moment or eternity has passed. He sees a small opening, and on the other side, his own lifeless body. He chooses to come toward it.

**Hope** is offered the same decision, separately, within the same impossible darkness. She too approaches the light and chooses to leave.

In the great mirrors, dark mist begins to move. **Hope and Stanley walk out of the reflections** and stand before the living as themselves, able to speak, puzzled by the crowd of unfamiliar faces and the grotesque heart with arms and a leg that calls himself Nash. The **original corpses remain strapped to the stone slabs**. Hope has her full natural limbs again, but the metal and gun components she once carried in her body are gone; older scars remain. Both have come without clothes or equipment.

The priest says the old bodies will become husks and be put away, but does not allow the party to take them. He has performed what Vorel asked. **The two lost companions are alive and present**, and he urges everyone to leave, never to return. Vorel thanks him with careful politeness. For all his relief, he still wonders whether a trickster's mirrors could have made convincing substitutes. Hope and Stanley, for their part, have no answer beyond the simple fact that they feel themselves again.

In the temple's black corridors, Nash angrily complains that he cannot produce even a light. Then something changes—**not because of a key, but because the GM explicitly corrects his previous ruling**. The supposed blanket suppression of magic in Sigil was an error. Nash throws up one of his ragged little arms, and an actual **Fire Bolt** lights the corridor. Nothing quietly takes out a lantern and gives it to Vorel.

Outside the chapel, Sigil's looping skyline astonishes the returnees. The reunited party walks back toward the Soggy Feather, with **Squirts remaining in the chapel** and **Nothing leading them home**.

## XV. New bodies in the Cage

Hope needs clothing. Nothing offers a tunic and pants, while Stanley wraps the red **Soggy Feather** cape around himself like a toga. At the inn, Bill lends Stanley his old lawn-bowling outfit; the half-elf restores some dignity to it with a spell that removes its smell. The restored friends eat soup and drink while Bill marvels at what he had believed were merely corpses on Vorel's floating cart.

Stanley remembers the time away like a dream of nothing. Hope recognizes her own hands and moves them in wonder. The two do not know how long they were lost, or why Vorel emerged from the beam whole while Nash became a heart that regrows itself a little each night. Vorel explains the tournament and his strange new companions; Nash greets Stanley's familiar sarcasm with the relieved irritation of a lifelong friend.

Their reunion changes the traveling party. **Kristen resumes playing Hope**, and **Jon resumes Stanley**. The kobold Squirts remains connected to Kidigree's chapel, where her tricks are appreciated. **Nothing** may come back to the inn, but the kenku has another dangerous matter to address: a commission concerning a kidnapping and the Abyss that she has not completed. The friends have been restored; the people who helped save them have not simply ceased to exist.

The next morning Bill arranges a meal and urges the five to talk seriously. Vorel describes the short span since the beam, the valuable corpses, the priest's bargain and the vast marionette prize. He confesses that he still wants to be certain the two people who emerged from the mirrors are the friends he knew. Stanley questions Nash and Vorel's memories in the old argumentative way, but that familiarity is not an objective test. Vorel has an idea: borrow an **ethereal spectrometer** like the ones once used to inspect the souls of dead petitioners.

There is also the matter of home. Bill explains why some call Sigil **the Cage**. The city is full of **hidden portals** into other worlds and planes; knowing the right door and its key can take someone almost anywhere. The trouble is learning which portal leads where, and who controls it. He suggests the records of the **Clerk's Ward**, where agreements, ownership and official movement are tracked. Perhaps someone there knows a way back to **Earth**, or to the place the companions have come to call the Wastes.

Bill is less sanguine when the companions mention their dealings with the **Weir** and the name of one of its lords. He calls such beings terrifying powers from beyond the outer planes, enough to make even the mighty think twice. The companions have been to dangerous places, he admits, and he may have underestimated them. But neither Bill's fear nor the spectrometer can erase the bargains they carry.

Around the table the old party tells stories it should perhaps have told much earlier. Stanley recalls how the rarity of a half-elf led to the murder of his family and his enslavement. Nash describes the Solar Mines, where a happy childhood under **Master Beltnotch** was eventually revealed to be a cruel preparation for betrayal. Vorel remembers his hatchling days with less sorrow, though he barely knew his parents. Their pasts have shaped the suspicion with which they approach every possible helper in Sigil. Having come back from death, Hope and Stanley have reason to hear them anew.

They decide to build a life here long enough to find a way out. Vorel is rich by their standards, but every gold piece has demands upon it. **Hope needs weapons and ammunition**, Stanley needs his former tools of magic and speech, and Nash still needs time to finish growing his body. The city offers everything, says Bill, but little is free.

## XVI. Shopping for the living

The bazaar has no readily available firearms for Hope, and the friends cannot replace their old equipment in a single afternoon. Vorel has a narrower goal: he wants to examine the souls of his returned companions. At an old arcane bookshop, the owner offers to order a spectrometer, but directs them to a better-equipped merchant across the market.

**Large S's emporium** first appears to be a place for children's toys. Beyond the counter, however, is a showroom that resembles a night sky, with enchanted treasures resting on floating pedestals. The shopkeeper, an extravagantly dressed merchant, produces a pair of **ethereal spectrometer goggles**, adjusting their frame to fit a lizardfolk face.

Vorel looks through them. The living figures before him take on pale luminous outlines; **Hope, Stanley, Nash and the merchant all have a similar white aura**. Nothing appears plainly monstrous or false. Then he peers toward Stanley's neck and glimpses **glowing Infernal writing and Stanley's name**. The return from the mirrors has not erased every mark of the Weir. The merchant catches Vorel conducting a more intensive test than she meant to allow and takes back the goggles. They cost **fourteen hundred gold**, and he does not buy them.

There are other things they can afford. Nash chooses a **skull-shaped arcane focus**. Vorel purchases a **professional sixteen-fighter marionette station** for the Soggy Feather, hoping his championship title can draw paying players and give the party a steady income. **Vorel pays twelve hundred and fifty gold for the machine and Nash's focus together**. The store promises delivery and installation.

The emporium's owner talks of the city's poor with cruel disdain, even imagining throwing them into the Abyss. The party leaves with its purchases and a clearer sense that commercial polish is not the same as trustworthiness. They have no spectrometer and no route home.

Vorel decides that the next step is to **look wealthy**. He brings Hope and Stanley back to **Lohar**, the orc tailor who made his red cape. In front of an eager crowd the champion commissions three outfits: refined finery for Stanley, bold black-and-gold clothing for Hope, and a tuxedo, monocle and cufflinks for himself. Nash is changing shape too quickly to fit, so the tailor asks him to come back once his body is whole. The three suits cost **thirty gold altogether**, half paid now and half on delivery.

After a short span of days, the **marionette station actually arrives at the Soggy Feather**. Bill agrees to have it installed, encouraged by the prospect of customers. The formal clothes arrive too, and Vorel pays the remainder. He imagines one day buying the inn and transforming it into the **Golden Feather**, but imagination is not yet ownership. Nash hopes that **eight days of rest** will give him back the body he remembers. The group considers taking that much time for recovery and shopping, without the session showing the finished result.

The Clerk's Ward still waits. Somewhere among Sigil's countless doors may be the path to Earth. The living friends have found each other; now they must decide what kind of life they can build long enough to find their way home.

## XVII. The heart has a body again

Eight days after the reunited party began planning to live in Sigil, **Nash wakes with his entire body restored**. His red eye, damaged skin and familiar disfigurements are still his, but so are two arms and two legs. He has barely had time to enjoy the discovery before a white bird flies through the window and tries to peck him in the eye. Nash swats it away. It bites Stanley on the cheek. **Vorel hurls a water pitcher at it**, bringing it down, and carefully covers the limp creature with a pillowcase so it cannot escape unnoticed.

Nash parades his newly returned anatomy before his unimpressed friends. The clothes arranged for him during his recovery make him look, in Stanley's estimation, like a schoolboy. Hope and Stanley have handsome new outfits, Vorel a tuxedo, and the **Soggy Feather** now glows with the marionette machine Vorel had installed. Life almost looks settled. Then the day's paper arrives.

**M Sports Weekly** carries a large image of **Kanja** and the headline that the former champion has been **found dead**. The report describes a hanging and fire at his home, with some of the small people who lived within his false body discovered dead as well. The **Harmonium** is investigating. The day before the party saw **Mind Master threaten Kanja through Kidigree's mirror**, supported by men in the black clothing of the **Order of the Crimson Moon**. The news makes the connection frightening, but **neither Vorel nor the newspaper has identified the murderer**.

Remembering the bird, Vorel races back upstairs. The covering has been disturbed and the bird is gone. Perhaps it was only stunned, and perhaps it has flown away to die. Vorel prefers to wonder whether even Sigil's birds can be spies. No evidence settles the matter.

There is a more pressing question now that Nash is whole again: **how do the five get home?** Bill previously suggested the **Clerk's Ward**, where information about Sigil's countless hidden portals may be held. Dressed in finery meant to convince bureaucrats they are important, the companions hire a rickshaw in the Merchant's Ward.

Its puller is a splendidly muscular man called something like **Fanbo Joe**, whose true passion is **urban poetry**. He dreams of being published and invites them to hear his set at the **Dangly Dozen** on Friday night. He also tells Vorel about a gym where he could train. Vorel is delighted by the man's physique and even steps down to help run the carriage, while his companions watch with a mixture of amusement and disbelief. A tentative future evening of poetry is agreed; no one attends it yet.

The **Clerk's Ward** is quieter than their part of Sigil, packed with tall buildings, well-dressed people and offices whose purpose is seldom obvious from the street. They buy frogs on sticks and speak with a vendor who explains that **knowing the whereabouts of a portal** is often more profitable than claiming to own it. A gate hidden from strangers can be controlled in practice even if the Lady forbids formal ownership.

When the group considers what anyone could import from Earth, Nash reveals more about the **Solar Mines** than he has ever cared to discuss. The energy his masters marketed as **solar** often came from **radioactive material** gathered and piled together to produce heat, poisoning workers and killing many of the people he grew up with. He suspects the Principle might have bought it, though he never saw the buyer's name. His companions listen with new horror to a piece of the past that has left marks all over Nash's body.

They walk into the first official-looking building they can find. It turns out to regulate **horse-drawn carriages**, not portals. A helpful clerk draws them a map. Several blocks away stands the gilded **Portal Regulatory Agency of Sigil**, with a revolving entrance so strange that the party watches other visitors pass through before attempting it themselves.

Inside, an attendant recognizes Vorel's name and the group is offered an appointment. There is one cruel condition. **Hope**, a tiefling, is told that **two armed guards must escort her** because the office believes beings of her ancestry cannot be trusted. Hope quietly makes the guards look like her personal entourage. She is not arrested; the ugly assumption belongs to the people enforcing it.

The portal expert **Duerto** meets them upstairs. His assistant is a familiar face, if a face full of tentacles: **Fax**, the illithid competitor from the Big Time Smackeroo tournament. Hope and Stanley remember other illithids from the Wastes and tense as the two enter, but Fax merely recognizes Vorel and seems interested in his marionette coaching business.

The clerks place an extraordinary headset over their eyes and search an archive known as the **Echo**. The word the party needs is **Earth**, a planet—not the **Elemental Plane of Earth**. The steward who knows the passage can be found in **the Hive**, under a name that sounds like **Tobacho Fleur**.

The man does not offer to take them there. The Hive is dangerous, impoverished and home to factions he would rather not deal with. The party has **a steward's name and a ward**, not the address of an open door or a key that will safely carry them home.

## XVIII. The gifts and ghosts of the Dark Below

Outside the agency, **Stanley** asks a question his companions dislike: **what if Sigil is better than Earth?** In this city he can eat a meal without fighting for it; back home, every promise of a better future seemed to end in loss. Hope reminds him that she has a kingdom to care for. Nash remembers his wife and child. Vorel names friends, unfinished obligations and the possibility that **Chance still drifts in darkness**. They cannot say how much time will have passed on Earth, or whether all those they remember are still alive.

The argument grows quieter over an extravagant meal at a restaurant called something like **the Pig's Foot**. They eat potatoes, gravy, cheese and beef, and taste fizzy cola that to Nash seems almost legendary. Stanley concedes that leaving the Wastes forever would also mean leaving people behind. They agree to **look for a way back**, without pretending it will be easy.

But before entering the Hive, Hope has another hope of her own: the guns she lost when the beam took her body. Vorel remembers the **sewer chambers** where he and Nash arrived and the dead bodies of Hope and Stanley were first found. Perhaps not everything they carried has disappeared. They return to the **Soggy Feather**, exchange their fine clothes for working gear and descend into the **Dark Below** near the Welcome Center.

One chamber contains a rotting small-bodied corpse in shallow filth. Vorel remembers fighting the scavengers who were examining the party's fallen bodies, but the corpse is too decomposed to identify. They search the brackish water and piles of rubbish carefully.

**Stanley touches something metallic with his blade**, stoops to retrieve it, and raises a pistol from the sewer. Hope knows it by the markings on the grip. **It is Scully**, one of the guns she thought gone forever. She takes it back, relieved beyond words. There should be another, if the first survived. The search continues.

It yields an object Nash finds much more amusing than a firearm: a metal implement of suggestive shape that, when Stanley discovers a switch, suddenly opens into **an umbrella**. Nash begins opening and closing it in dramatic quick-draw motions, making an absurd game of it. Later he will name it **Barbara**. For the moment it seems only an embarrassing piece of lost property.

The next thing Stanley hauls from the water is far less ridiculous. It is an **ethereal spectrometer**, smaller than the expensive goggles Vorel tried in the emporium but fitted with the same sort of suspended crystal. Stanley cleans and activates them. His companions become pale blue and white auras, their living forms followed by strange luminous traces. They focus on **Hope and Stanley's necks** and see writing no ordinary eyes can perceive.

Their names glow above marks written in **Infernal or Abyssal**. Hope recognizes enough of the language to understand the dread meaning: **their souls are still destined for the Weir**, with a proprietor named and a **reward if found**. Even their return through Kidigree's mirrors has not visibly broken the claim. There is an additional name on Stanley that the companions have not used before; its precise form remains uncertain. Stanley removes the goggles too suddenly and stumbles with dizziness.

Across the room Nash keeps practicing his umbrella draw. He flourishes **Barbara**, and a **purple door appears in a brick wall**. No one expected a portal to open here. The other companions are still studying the marks on their souls when Nash calls them over.

A thrown stone vanishes through the violet surface. Nash tosses the umbrella after it. Then, with the others holding him, he **puts his head through**. On the far side is a dark, hot space with gritty ground. He brings a light to its mouth and casts **Fire Bolt** into the blackness. For a moment he sees walls alive with **slick, many-legged creatures**. That is enough. Nash pulls back and recommends not going in.

The purple doorway fades. Nash reaches in desperately for his precious umbrella and pulls **Barbara** back just before the brick wall becomes solid again. **None of the five has traveled through bodily**; there is no sign that this is the Earth portal the agency recorded, nor proof they could summon the door again in the same way.

At the chamber where Vorel and Nash first appeared, they find a different scene from the one Vorel remembers. **Six or seven bodies** lie scattered among broken scavenger equipment. Something small stirs under the rubbish. It is **Java the Carver**, a rat-man who recognizes Vorel.

Java says the people to whom Vorel sold rights to the arrival site have been **killed and robbed by the Rat King's people**. The rats searched for the vanished arrival portal, too, but found nothing. Java is quick to explain that rats are everywhere under Sigil and will one day inherit the city. Vorel gives him the indulgence of a listener who needs information.

Hope shows the rat-man **Scully** and asks after its twin. Java remembers another strange gun. He says he sold it to a man named **Fedor**, claiming it was a demonic charm that enhanced virility. After **nine gold pieces in bribes**, he reveals the buyer's connection: Fedor works for a great merchant of the **Hive**, **the very same Tobacho Fleur** named by the portal agency.

A gun and a doorway. The party has found two reasons to visit the same dangerous ward, and neither tells them how safely to get there. Java can move unseen through the rat-sized tunnels of the Dark Below, but the tall travelers cannot. The Hive's surface streets are open to them, while its underside may be worse than the danger above. Someone suggests becoming small enough to follow the rats, but no spell plan is workable for everyone.

The five stand among the wreckage, **Hope holding Scully, Stanley carrying evidence their souls are still claimed, and Nash guarding his ridiculous umbrella**, and consider which way to enter the Hive. For now, the homeward portal remains only a record and a name.

## XIX. Dressed to enter trouble

The dark water of the sewer still laps against old stone when Vorel returns to the idea of becoming rats. **Java the Carver** lives in the underworld, after all, and can reach the Hive by tunnels no tall creature could navigate. But Vorel can change only one of them into an animal for a short time. Even Java says the passages are uncertain and dangerous. They cannot reasonably turn four companions into a rat-sized expedition. **The streets will have to do.**

That requires an appearance. Vorel proposes a gang of outlaws tough enough not to be robbed the moment they step into the Hive. Java offers a tailor he could burgle while the owner sleeps. Vorel prefers to pay **Lohar**, the old orc whose skills have already served him. They climb back up from the Dark Below, pass their crowded inn and return to the great market.

Lohar listens to his champion's latest fashion ambition with professional delight. **Spikes? Of course. Belts and clasps? Many. Rope instead of precious chain? The right kind of poverty. A horned, grimacing demon face on the chest? Marvelous.** And in their boots he can conceal a little surprise—steel toes whose sharp metal spikes can flick out at a tap of the heel. In matching clothes they will pretend to be **Lady Despair and the Rambunctious Bunch**. Hope, a real tiefling, is cast as Lady Despair herself. Vorel chooses to be **Toyota Stump**, an enforcer whose vocabulary seems to consist mostly of fists and shouting.

Lohar quotes **fifty gold each** for the four outfits. The metalwork needs two days.

The companions spend the interval in their accustomed ways. **Hope listens for Tobacho Fleur's name in taverns**, but the people she questions turn away. Whatever power Tobacho holds in the Hive is not popular dinner conversation outside it. **Stanley studies old census records**, finding estimates that the Hive houses more people than the rest of Sigil many times over, and that the **Harmonium** attempts to confine disorder behind its boundaries. **Nash drinks coffee and does remarkably little**. Vorel continues coaching at the newly installed marionette machine, surrounded by customers. One promised pupil does not appear: **Fax**, the illithid they met at the portal agency, fails to attend his lesson. Even Tubbs 9K cannot find him.

The GM has one unusual clarification for Nash. **His old thermal underwear has become part of him**. During the terrible journey between realities its cooling mechanism fused to his body, leaving a tiny vent and switch beneath an armpit. He can still **float or fly up to ten feet** despite having grown back his complete human form. There is no outer garment to remove now. When the two days pass, Vorel carries four elaborately wrapped parcels to the Soggy Feather table. They hire a rickshaw. As Sigil's better-kept buildings fade behind them, the sky becomes smog and the air fills with sulfur and dung. Their driver takes **one gold** and will go no farther. Ahead, armored men are building **a gallows**.

## XX. A gate for those who must never leave

At the entrance to the Hive, about a dozen guards are putting up a platform beside **three cages**. Some hold prisoners. Their commander is a mounted Harmonium officer, **Sir Unindra**, who makes it clear that the new arrivals cannot pass without his say-so.

Stanley introduces himself. Nash gives his own name and manages to provoke rather than impress the man. If the visitors are trouble, he reasons aloud, surely the Harmonium would prefer that trouble **inside the Hive rather than among the city's comfortable citizens**.

The captain agrees with a cruel condition: **they may enter, but his men will not permit them to come back out**. One captive, a furry little creature in a cage, cries for help as they pass. ## XXI. The dragon who charges for the way home

They follow Spatulo's directions through streets that grow darker and more crooked. Hours pass. Music and drunken singing finally reach them from an inn across from a well-lit stall. An old green vendor is cooking **fried frogs on sticks**. Hope asks quietly for a cantina. He points toward the alley.

At the far end sits a **burgundy metal door** beneath a small lamp. It has a sliding slot. A strange cloudlike guard opens it at Hope's words: **Lady Despair and the Rambunctious Rascals**. Within are stairs, a crowded room, armed patrons and trumpets.

At the center lies an enormous **blue dragon**, lounging upon a couch designed for his tremendous belly. He wears **a monocle and beret** and smokes a pipe with a leisure not shared by anyone around him. Beautiful companions rest beside him, servants hover, and a **stone golem** stands near enough to speak for its master. The dragon's words are an archaic form of Draconic that the visitors cannot fully understand; the golem makes him intelligible.

This is **Tobacho Fleur**, the very merchant the Portal Regulatory Agency named as steward of the recorded Earth portal. He is no back-alley information broker.

They have found the steward of a recorded Earth portal, given him a beloved pistol and made contact with one of his enemies. **They have not yet heard which item he wants or where his portal actually is.**

## XXII. The canyon that was not a canyon

Jelver leads them deep into Tobacho's underground halls. At one open doorway, **Gerbken Shartlinger—the Mind Master**—sits across a desk from an unknown figure. ## XXIII. The key that is nowhere near the treasure

In a comfortable private office, attendants pour large, sharp-tasting restorative drinks for the wounded. **Stanley, Nash and Vorel recover their strength**. Jelver warns that Tobacho's goodwill will turn quickly if they undertake the mission and fail. The promised unnamed object is at last given a name: **Zorander's Fist**, a gauntlet once carried by a champion from a distant Prime world. ## XXVI. An airship waiting below Sigil

The many-armed driver has been waiting outside. He has more than horses and a carriage to offer: the supplies Jelver promised are **now inside**. She gives them a tour of the decks, cargo space and crew's machinery. She promises the ship will depart soon.

It is **not yet a flight**. The party has stolen its first key, obtained the promised gear and reached an actual ship, but **the White Tower remains under siege far away**, and **Zorander's Fist remains in its box according to the bishop's untested account**. As the episode ends they have not sailed, passed through the enormous portal, opened the chest or returned home. Their next adventure can begin at last.

## XXVII. An invitation from the Mind Master

**Traveler's Twin**. The name is a promise, or perhaps a boast, that the captain makes as the companions admire the vessel waiting on its enormous launch rails. She is a **loxodon**, a towering elephant-person dressed in ornaments and a swaggering pirate coat, with a curved sword that looks small only until she draws it. Her name is difficult to catch through the telling; something like **Meart**. But her confidence is easy to understand: this is a ship built to fly.

Before they can depart, a richly dressed halfling steps out onto the dock. **Gerbken Shartlinger, the Mind Master**, has arrived with **four escorts dressed like the Crimson Moon assassins** who attacked the party before the BMF finals. Vorel immediately distrusts him. He remembers the taunts, the rivalry and the attempt to ruin his competition. The little man is quite pleased by the distrust. It was **he**, he says, who arranged for the ship at Tobacho's request.

He has brought a gift. A black-clad attendant comes forward and opens a box containing a **dragon-head amulet with emerald eyes**, on a handsome chain. Its inscription wishes Vorel a future friendship with Mind Master. Vorel fears the necklace may be a trap. **Nash examines it with magic**, and the GM identifies the thing as an **Amulet of Health**, which will make its wearer's Constitution score **nineteen**. The fighters compare what such a gift might do for them. Vorel has no need to wear it, despite being its intended recipient. **Stanley takes it and puts it on**, feeling at once healthier and more vigorous. The expected strangulation does not come.

Vorel asks how the Crimson Moon can vanish without leaving bodies. Mind Master will not tell him. He claims that to learn their methods one must join their order. He and the escorts depart after a look that makes Vorel more suspicious than before. **The man has been revealed as a participant in Tobacho's present business**, not proved to have ordered the earlier killings.

## XXVIII. A sky that Sigil cannot show them

The captain calls the crew to work. Locks are pulled, ropes tightened and great sails raised. **Traveler's Twin begins sliding along its rails** toward a tremendous ring in the cavern wall. At the last possible moment the empty circle blossoms into **purple and pink light**. The hull glides through—and there is nothing beneath it.

The airship tumbles in **a real blue sky**. Its engines catch, roar and lift it into stable flight. Down below the **Outlands** spread farther than anything familiar from the Wastes: lands of impossible colors, intermingled forests and deserts, waterways and far-away places of war. At their center an immense spire stretches almost beyond sight, with a small circular shape at its summit. The captain identifies this extraordinary landscape and tells the companions about worlds that meet to trade or fight there.

She invites them into her quarters for tea and biscuits called, with little justification, **sky biscuits**. They discuss dying, returning, belonging and what the four mean to one another. Vorel has spent the last few days as a lizardfolk champion, a criminal bruiser and a disguised acolyte. His confusion about the life of a spy is real. The captain reminds him that espionage is built on appearances and deceit; he is reluctant to think of himself as that sort of person.

The original heist still looks suicidal. The party intends to **leap from the airship above White Tower**, cast **Dimension Door** into it, recover the Fist and then somehow return to the sky. The captain doubts the ship can safely wait directly over a battlefield while they search. She **offers them a sending stone** so they can contact her for extraction when ready, and tells them not to call before there is somewhere safe to land.

Through the captain's powerful telescope, **Stanley now sees the White Tower with his own eyes**. It is a stark white shape standing amid black masses that resolve into **a vast besieging army**. **Trebuchets fling stones into its walls**. Something like small creatures crawls over its surface. What the captured bishop told them about the siege is no longer merely a captive's account. But **no one can see Zorander's Fist through the telescope**. Neither the locked box nor the stolen key has been put to its intended use.

An enormous **bang** rocks the vessel. A gnome bursts into the cabin, shouting that they are under attack.

## XXIX. Falling is not the same as flying

A cloud of **winged beastmen** fills the air around **Traveler's Twin**, accompanied by **two enormous wyverns**. The creatures dive onto the decks and slash at the gnome crew. Cannons fire, swords flash and the hull groans under blows from the larger beasts. Many of the little sailors fight bravely; **several are killed, and some tumble from the ship to the distant ground**.

**Nash changes himself into a young black dragon** and flies above the battle, his great wings briefly making the airship look small. It is his own transformation, not another ape form bestowed by Stanley. **Vorel**, first distracted by his cask of ale, spins a ship's winding mechanism with such force that it catches a flying attacker and sends it around in circles. Then he bites the helpless creature. **Stanley uses Mantle of Inspiration** to shield his friends in temporary vigor and urges them into position with bardic magic, before hurling a truly insulting **Vicious Mockery** at the trapped enemy. **Hope draws Scully and fires** into the oncoming crowd.

A wyvern hammers the hull. **Hope is thrown over the stern and catches the railing with one hand**. She hangs in the wind while the entire flying ship streaks away from her. Another enormous blow sends sailors sprawling. **Nash's acid breath** tears through a flying enemy and badly injures a wyvern, which crashes into the vessel, adding another shudder to its wooden frame.

**Vorel's borrowed greatsword Shatter** takes apart a boarder. **Stanley sees a sliver of another wyvern beneath the ship, casts Polymorph, and transforms it into a tiny rat**. The creature drops from sight toward the distant ground. No one watches it land, and its ultimate fate remains a question.

Hope's grip is weakening. A winged attacker creeps along the stern and **slashes the hand holding the rail**. The pain is too great; **Hope falls into the open sky**. Nash sees her go. He dives after her in his **black dragon form**, catches her, and returns her to the fight. Now **Hope rides Nash through the air and shoots from his back**, an improvised aerial partnership that actually works.

Another wyvern has smashed through the vessel into the decks below. **Vorel bursts through the planking after it**. In a corridor choked with dust and smoke he meets the monster's teeth and stinger. It **poisons and wounds him badly**. But Vorel, raging and clutching Shatter, drives the great blade through the creature and finally **kills it**. The gnomes who survive rush to his aid. He has won, but he is **not shown suddenly healed**.

Above, **Stanley continues fighting the boarders**. **Nash's dragon breath recharges**, and he turns acid against more of the winged creatures on the deck, killing two. The party debates whether the acid could also harm the ship; no one has isolated the cause of the vessel's accumulating damage. Hope, firing from the back of a moving dragon, has a much more immediate mistake. **A shot from Scully hits Nash squarely in the back of his head**, hurting him but not killing him. Her very next shot is a **critical hit** that tears an enemy's wings apart and sends it plummeting. The table remembers Scully's special benefit when it strikes critically.

The defenders slowly clear the remaining attackers. It seems they may yet reach their goal. But **Traveler's Twin does not stop shaking**. There is **thick smoke billowing behind the captain**, and the great vessel begins **to tip forward and descend**. The gnomes have driven off the immediate boarders, yet the ship itself may now be their enemy.

The story leaves all four in the air, **not on the ground beside White Tower**. They have a pendant, a sending stone and a dangerous mission. **There is no confirmed crash, landing, chest opening, Fist retrieval or safe road to Earth before the episode ends.**

## XXX. Four leap, but only three arrive

**Traveler's Twin is dying in the sky**. Thick black smoke pours out of the engine as it falls toward the immense army around White Tower. The captain, a weary loxodon who has had quite enough of wyverns, shouts commands to the surviving gnomes and admits the ship cannot be righted before the companions need to leave. **She already gave them the sending stone**. If the ship is repaired, she will contact them. If it is not, she may never call.

**Nash returns from his young-black-dragon form to his own body**. Their escape plan demands that they become four small shapes in the sky, not a visible black dragon. **Stanley takes Hope's hand** and **Nash takes Vorel**, who has managed to recover two remaining barrels of drink. On the lip of the railing the pairs prepare their **Dimension Door scrolls**. Then all four **actually jump**.

They fall past the ship and toward the besieged tower. Getting a scroll open against the rush of air is a struggle, but both readers hold on. As they descend, the **Traveler's Twin sinks behind a distant mountain**, trailing smoke and a dark rising cloud. The four have no time to turn and investigate. The white tower is rushing to meet them.

At last **Stanley casts**, with Hope still beside him. **Nash casts**, drawing Vorel with him. The four disappear into the teleportation magic. **Only three arrive in White Tower's cleaning closet**.

Hope appears instead **outside**, thrown violently against the tower's unseen ward. It burns her as it hurls her away. She draws **the +2 dagger supplied by Tobacho** and tries to use it, with her spiked boots, to anchor herself to the building. The barrier throws her off once more. **She plummets some eighty feet and hits the ground**, hard enough to knock out her breath and chip a tooth, but **she lives**.

Winged doglike soldiers with tridents pick through the stones toward her. Hope cannot fight an army in that condition. She lets them disarm her, rises slowly, and is marched into the camp of the tower's besiegers.

## XXXI. A voice she knows among the damned

The soldiers bring Hope before an enormous armored commander with smoldering eyes, **General Carthax**. He demands to know who she is and why she has come for the White Tower. Hope says she is a queen of Earth and **has come for Zorander's Fist at Tobacho's behest**. The giant wants to kill her.

A strange little flying figure interrupts him. **Ulvert**, a being with a baby's body and an old man's face, **recognizes Hope from their time around the UBI scientific facility**. He remembers enough of her history to believe she may be more useful alive. The **devils of the Nine Hells and demons of the Abyss**, he says, have made a temporary common cause to seize the Fist. Carthax resents having his authority shared.

The ward at the tower **rejected Hope**. To them this suggests that she has an evil heart. Ulvert offers a terrible bargain: **they will make her good enough to enter**, then send her inside to steal the relic on their behalf. Afterward she can abandon her good behavior so the tower throws her back to the besiegers. Hope insists on a price. **They must send her and her three companions to Earth, at the right time, without tricks**. Carthax is reluctant and threatens unspeakable torture if she fails. Ulvert assents, though he admits they have no ready means to bring the other three back out of the tower. Hope asks about **Kitobalpraith**; his name silences the general, but no Weirlord appears.

They summon a woman-shaped creature with **no ordinary face and a mouth where her neck should be**. She whispers words that seem to take physical shape, then sends them into Hope's face. The **GM declares Hope's alignment is now lawful good**. It does **not** simply erase her choices or turn her into an unthinking servant, but she must **behave according to this new alignment or lose its effect**.

The besiegers send her back inside the White Tower. She is **a reluctant, coerced agent carrying an unfulfilled promise to go home**, not someone who has actually delivered the Fist.

## XXXII. Walnut and the wizard's ball

In a vast supply closet, **Stanley, Nash and Vorel** listen to sounds of a game and a prayer service through the door. Hope is absent. Stanley decides there is little benefit in whispering forever. He walks out and announces that **the reinforcements have arrived**.

Two armed knights of **Euphemius**, **Sir Samson** and **Sir Clotchup**, have been playing a game in the beleaguered tower. Nearby, a little robed acolyte called **Walnut** tends to their needs. The newcomers claim to have teleported through the army to save the defenders. The knights are delighted by the tale of such bravery, until they discover the **fourth companion has vanished**. They warn that the tower **rejects evil souls**, and that a lost friend may have been destroyed.

Just then **Hope appears in the chamber**.

The paladins see a tiefling and draw their swords. They assume that she must be evil by nature. **Vorel, gloriously drunk, claims she carries the divine child of a paladin**. His performance is bizarre and unconvincing; **Hope's pregnancy is not a fact of the story**. **Stanley saves the situation by appealing to the defenders' better judgment**, citing Vorel's acts of courage and their shared desire to fight evil.

Then Hope tells the truth: **the enemy commanders found her outside, magically changed her alignment, and sent her in to steal the Fist**. The story terrifies the knights. They demand that all four surrender their weapons until their commander can judge them. Stanley hands over several blades. **Nash's single weapon is a magical ball**, which Walnut takes with a gravity completely at odds with its appearance. The title of the episode is his increasingly incredulous question: **where is his ball?**

**Vorel refuses to give up Shatter**. He headbutts Samson. A second paladin lunges, misses, and **accidentally plunges his greatsword into Samson** instead. The wounded knight collapses. Before anyone can continue the foolish battle, **Stanley casts Healing Word and closes Samson's wound**. Nash tries to seize Walnut and recover his ball, but the little fellow wriggles away. Vorel demands that they finish the fight and take what they came for. Stanley refuses.

More paladins come up the stairs. **The party finally relinquishes its weapons and follows the defenders peacefully**, with Samson now praising Stanley for helping an enemy when there was no tactical reason to do so.

## XXXIII. A box beside the throne, a key in a bag

Deep in White Tower a woman with strawberry-colored hair sits as **Knight Commander**, surrounded by white-armored defenders. Beside her throne is **a strange hexagonal chest**. She hears that four strangers entered without invitation, admitted a scheme with the demons and tried to steal something sacred.

**Stanley explains what home means to his companions**. Hope rules people who need her; Vorel's people are in danger; Nash has a wife who was expecting a child when he left. They came to Sigil, were promised a way back, and became ensnared in a dragon's bargain. Their means are compromised even if their purposes are understandable. **The Knight Commander sentences all four to death**. The guards order them to kneel.

Stanley asks for one more word. Why should the Fist remain here, a prize for the besieging legions, if the defenders hope eventually to move it to **Mount Celestia**? The strangers had an airship and scrolls. **What if they used those resources to take the Fist to the destination the paladins already wanted?** The commander thinks. She **grants a stay of execution**, requiring that any move be accompanied by her people. **Walnut returns everyone's weapons, including Nash's ball**. Vorel gets Shatter back. The party is no longer awaiting immediate beheading, but it is no freer to take the relic to Tobacho.

The commander says **the Fist lies inside the hexagonal chest**, which she guards. She **does not open it**; the travelers do not actually see the hand. She tells a tale of **Zorander**, a desperate paladin who sought the approval of Euphemius, fell for a power contained inside the glove and destroyed his world before being denied divine love. She claims the **hand within the glove can rend whole planes apart**. This is the commander's religious history and description of danger—not a display of the relic's power.

Stanley admits that they have stolen **High Cleric Thrun's pendant key**. The commander takes and inspects it, insisting that **the key must remain apart from the chest**. She also describes a very different gift: **a golden totem shaped like Euphemius's sun-holding fist**, with which they might summon a blessing upon Earth. **The offered totem is not Zorander's Fist**, and the question of who physically accepts it remains unsettled in the transcripts.

At that moment **the sending stone vibrates**. The loxodon captain speaks from elsewhere in the Outlands: she **survived the ship's bad landing**, but **Traveler's Twin needs about a week to repair**. The plan to extract the Fist by immediately returning to the airship is impossible for now. The Knight Commander hands **the key pendant back to Stanley**, again keeping it separate from the relic's chest, and has the party sent to rooms with beds and food while a new plan is made.

The cook, **Gregor**, offers food and potential work. The four eat, but not happily. **Vorel believes Stanley has taken their one chance of going home and pledged them to a siege they cannot win**. Stanley refuses to butcher paladins simply to fulfill the terms of a bargain with Tobacho. Nash, exhausted, **says he will follow Hope**, whatever decision she makes, because he trusts his queen and wants to return to his wife. Hope, newly compelled to conduct herself as **lawful good**, cannot easily voice the choice her secret patrons want.

**Stanley leaves the pendant in his bag in his quarters and goes to sleep**. Nash withdraws as well, and Vorel finds a place helping in the kitchen. Alone with a cup of drink and her anger, Hope hears a small voice **inside her mind**. It demands **the Fist**. She tries to answer, but receives only pressure and a repeated demand. **The speaker is not identified.**

The episode ends with the **key in Stanley's bag, the chest still under the Knight Commander's protection, and the friends inside the besieged tower**. They have not stolen the Fist. The way home is more contested than ever.

## An open door, not an ending

The four who entered CHIDS's red beam are together again: **Hope and Stanley restored through Kidigree's mirrors**, and **Nash fully regrown after eight days**, with his thermal underwear unexpectedly fused to his body, providing **cooling and ten-foot flight** by GM ruling. **Vorel** owns a champion's title and a professional BMF cabinet at the Soggy Feather, but has not bought the inn.

The search for **Zorander's Fist** has finally brought all four friends **inside White Tower**, but not by a single successful infiltration. **Traveler's Twin crashes beyond a ridge** after they jump from it; its captain **survives and later reports a week of repairs**. **Stanley, Nash and Vorel Dimension Door into the tower**, while its ward rejects **Hope**, who survives a heavy fall and is captured by the besieging demons and devils. **General Carthax and Ulvert bargain with her for the Fist**, promising Earth passage for all four and magically **changing her alignment to lawful good** so the tower will let her inside. She rejoins her companions and tells the defenders what happened.

The encounter with **Sir Samson, Sir Clotchup and Walnut** almost becomes a massacre, but **Stanley saves an accidentally wounded paladin**. **Nash's spellcasting ball is confiscated and later returned**. The **Knight Commander sentences all four to death, then stays that sentence** when Stanley proposes helping her **move Fist to Mount Celestia** instead of stealing it for Tobacho. She guards **an actual hexagonal chest said to contain the Fist**, but **the chest is not opened**; the relic's described world-rending power remains her testimony. **High Cleric Thrun's pendant was handed to her briefly, then returned to Stanley**, who later **leaves it in his bag in their assigned quarters**. A different **Euphemius golden totem** is shown/offered for possible use on Earth; its final inventory state requires verification.

The four have been fed and sheltered for now, and the swords have been returned, including **Vorel's borrowed Shatter**. **Vorel resents Stanley's refusal to take the quickest violent path home**, and **Nash says he will follow Hope**. But Hope has an **unwanted lawful-good obligation and a private, unidentified mental voice demanding the Fist**. **No relic has been taken from its chest, no execution carried out, no trip to Mount Celestia made and no Earth portal reached**. That is **E094's true cutoff**, in the tower, as the siege continues. ### Later commentary, separate from the action

In the [December 2019 bonus Q&A](../episodes/BONUS-2019-12.md), **Beau later explains** that Hope and Stanley's apparent bodies are **manifestations of souls interrupted on their way to the Weir**. That adds creator commentary to the trickster priest's explanation, but it does **not** place the knowledge in Vorel's hands at the start of E082, resurrect either companion, or settle the future of the contract. Beau also says he did not plan for **all four** to enter the beam in E081. These are **retrospective explanations**, not extra scenes between E082 and E088. The [December 2019 bonus ASR comparison](../reports/BONUS_2019_12_ASR_COMPARISON_2026-10-22.md) remains **unverified against audio**.

In the **[January 2020 public-release Q&A](../episodes/BONUS-2020-01.md)** (introduced aloud as the **December 2019** bonus), **Beau later calls the E085 arena apparition the Lady of Pain**. This is a **retrospective GM identification** rather than a declaration heard by the participants in the scene. He then **believed Nash's spellcasting abilities remained intact but were subject to a Sigil-specific external restriction**. **E088 explicitly supersedes that as a DM mistake; Nash casts Fire Bolt normally.** Beau also anticipates Nash's gradual bodily regeneration, which is **not shown complete by E088**. This discussion is not a tenth narrative chapter. [Two-ASR comparison](../reports/BONUS_2020_01_ASR_COMPARISON_2026-10-22.md).

In the [March 2020 cast Q&A](../episodes/BONUS-2020-03.md), **Beau explains that Sigil and Planescape supply published D&D material**, while **the Weir and its inhabitants are original to the campaign**, with only an **alluded-to** link to the published Far Realm. He also says **M-sports began as a joke poster**, the actual fighting mechanics written after player interest. **Jon explains the resurrected Stanley is emotionally changed and unsure of returning to the Wastes**, and Beau emphasizes that the chance to revive the two was **specific to the party's choices and Sigil's unusual access to magic**. These are **retrospective production/character clarifications** relevant to the earlier E081–E094 story, **not another chapter of in-world time**. The cast's **Lady of Pain final-boss and Hope-godhood scenarios are playful speculation**, not future facts. [Dual-ASR comparison](../reports/BONUS_2020_03_ASR_COMPARISON_2026-10-23.md).
