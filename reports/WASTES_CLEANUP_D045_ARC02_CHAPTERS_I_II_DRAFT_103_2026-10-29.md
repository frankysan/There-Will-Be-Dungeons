# Chains and Bargains — Arc 02, Chapters I–II

## I. A freedom offered by captors

Across the Wastes came the convoy that had appeared on the horizon as the companions escaped the buried facility. **Nash** and **Stanley** rejoined **Hope** and **Vorel** beside Fate 4, carrying stories of a prison overseen by a voice and of two gnomes lost beneath the sand. They had barely found one another before vehicles were surrounding their moving home. At first they wondered whether these were the goblins who had stolen people from Dusthill. The weapons, discipline and numbers of the newcomers soon argued otherwise.

Their attempt to escape became a running battle. Armed riders closed on the vehicle; a formidable orc bearing a **katana** and firearms forced his way onto the roof. He demanded obedience in the name of **the Principal**, claiming lawful authority that meant little to the injured travelers he was trying to arrest. They fought until they could fight no longer. Their surrender was compelled by force. Manacles replaced their weapons, and Fate 4 disappeared from sight as they were confined to a windowless transport.[^arc02-1]

After days of hunger and thirst they were brought to **Slavetown**. Its crowded streets and impressive businesses stood beside an institution that bought and held people. Among those imprisoned were the very Dusthill residents the party had set out to find. The adventurers could tell them the town's water had been restored. They could not open the prisoners' chains.

A magistrate accused the newcomers of attacking property protected by the Principal's laws. Judgment would be made through an ordeal in the **Rusty Cage**, where the four companions and the elderly traveler they had carried from the desert faced five armed orc initiates. Weapons dropped into the arena; magic and steel decided what the proceedings called justice. The companions survived and defeated their opponents. Yet the triumph at the end of the fight was not the same thing as freedom.[^arc02-2]

Only afterward did the city's authorities perform a public ceremony declaring the four innocent and releasing them. **Lady Saltbubble** and **Sir Chickenfinger**, warriors of the **Koromukishi**, offered an invitation to join their order. Whatever the ritual promised, its judgment applied to the celebrated fighters alone. The Dusthill people remained enslaved.

The company recovered an imperfect but functioning **Fate 4**, accepted directions from a nervous street-map seller called **Slippery Brent**, and saw enough of Slavetown to understand that privilege and desperation lived on adjacent streets. Hope sought mechanical work, Stanley pursued repairs to his arms, and Vorel withdrew from the city's press of strangers. Nash briefly felt relief from an item Hope lent him, but none of them knew whether it had changed what troubled his body.

At the **Buxom Confederate**, a prosperous local patron named **Celibus Stump** congratulated them. He had profited by betting on their unexpected arena victory, and his offers of money and lodging came with a useful new acquaintance. Nearby, Stanley recognized **Talia**, the mother of young **Bruce Bane** from Dusthill. She remembered her son and feared what it would cost to leave. Stanley sought even a night of safety for her, but the negotiation did not free her from her owner or bring her home.[^arc02-3]

As the others followed their separate errands, Nash drank, gambled and confronted a man known as **the Jeweler**. The two moved toward a fight that neither fully controlled. In Slavetown, surviving the arena had proved easier than deciding which bargains could be trusted.

## II. The price of a missing deed

The next days made the distinction between shelter and freedom painfully clear. **Vorel and Bok Bok** found lodgings at a hotel, and Stanley arranged temporary refuge there for **Talia**. She could spend a night away from the establishment that controlled her, but the arrangement rested on negotiation and might be withdrawn. Talia's fear, and Stanley's promise to help, belonged to a private conversation that the rest of the company had not witnessed.

Nash's confrontation with the Jeweler turned physical. A fight that had seemed to be about fists involved magic, and Nash's own **Thunderwave** shook the room. He later emerged injured, drunk and without his staff, wraps, clothing and much of his money. His explanations of what had happened were less certain than the missing possessions. The companions gathered him up as the city's opportunities for work and trade continued around them. Hope took a place at **Owen's garage**, and the others visited shops and workshops, trying to find a way back to Dusthill's captives.[^arc02-4]

**Stump** offered help, but linked it to a **mine deed** he wanted recovered. The Jeweler proposed another use for the same document: a challenge to the powerful **Oilbeards** and the trading system that enriched them. Nobody had produced the deed. Even its description shifted between a copper mine and a gold mine. The Jeweler alleged that Dusthill had been betrayed by a Hillmaster and a gnome resembling **Softhead**; the accusation was grave, and it remained his accusation rather than discovered proof.

Slavetown had once been called **Pristine**, according to accounts the companions heard. The expanding influence of the Principal's soldiers, Oilbeard interests, the city's magistrate and local sheriff had not made it one simple chain of command. The **Koromukishi** followed their own martial role; Stump courted power through money and influence; the Jeweler wanted the mine deed for a competing bargain. The companions might be useful to each of them without being safe in the hands of any.[^arc02-5]

Violence interrupted the negotiations at the Jeweler's establishment, **the Sapphire**. Armed men connected to a group called the **Lactid Cartel** arrived with a robed spellcaster and accused the Jeweler—now addressed as **Bertrand**—of failing to hand over an item. That item was not shown to be the mine deed. Shots turned the building into a battlefield. **Trigger** and **Donk** fought beside Bertrand; Vorel charged through the attackers; Stanley's magic conjured the *appearance* of Quizquaz, not the return of the creature they had destroyed beneath Dusthill. The fighting spilled into the street, where the local sheriff intervened. Bertrand survived after receiving healing. **Donk died**.[^arc02-6]

An enormous one-eyed figure came to the travelers' hotel, shaking its walls and reaching for Vorel. The giant sought a stolen necklace he called his **shiny**. In the confrontation that followed, the sheriff also faced the creature, and clues pointed the party toward a strange man associated with purple clothing and a house on **Thorn Row**. It was too soon to know which names the man had used or who had taken the giant's treasure.

Inside the Thorn Row house, Hope found mutilated bodies, a written warning addressed to **Timber**, and a trap that exploded when she investigated it. A signature resembling **Red Scarlet** did not reveal who had sent the message. The adventurers found a firearm, a Liberators pamphlet and other possessions whose history was uncertain. Hidden stairs led down to chambers smelling of decay and foul ritual work. There they discovered a necklace set with red stones, close to the one the giant described, but its provenance was still unknown.

Among the victims lay **Randall Owen**, the garage owner who had employed Hope. This time the death was directly identified in the scene; her outrage carried her farther into the house. The group encountered an apparent figure that dissolved into deception, then a ritualist who demanded the ruby necklace. He spoke of **Catoblepraithe**, a being he called **the Soulbroker**, as a patron he intended to summon. His threat was not the same as the patron's arrival. When the mutilated dead rose around the companions, their hunt for the deed had become a struggle against a power whose reach they did not understand.[^arc02-7]
