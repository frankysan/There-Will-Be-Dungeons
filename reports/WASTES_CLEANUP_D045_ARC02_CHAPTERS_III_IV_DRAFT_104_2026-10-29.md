# Chains and Bargains — Arc 02, Chapters III–IV

## III. A death that would not stay settled

The dead rose from the mutilated remains beneath Thorn Row, barring the way between the companions and the ritualist who had demanded the ruby necklace. **Tylander Sullivan** spoke from beyond an iron gate, certain that fear and his strange preparations would drive his pursuers away. Spells, an immovable rod and brute strength helped the four withstand the corpses; then Vorel, enlarged by magic, forced an opening through the house itself. Tylander escaped into Slavetown before they could reach him.[^arc02-8]

The town seemed to be emptying. Beyond its streets the giant **Orp** still wanted his stolen necklace. Nash brought it to the cyclops while Stanley tried to keep him calm. Tylander appeared near Orp and turned the giant's uncertainty against the party. Orp knocked Nash from a dangerous height, but **Stanley slowed his fall**; moments later Tylander's magic left the giant breathing but unconscious. Whatever Orp had threatened to do, he was no longer defending himself.

**Hope and Vorel** pursued Tylander around the fallen giant. He struck Vorel with a wasting touch that left the lizardfolk carrying a phantom pain. When Tylander tried to surrender, Hope and Vorel kept striking him. His apparent death was unmistakable to those who had fought him, even if the full nature of his magic was not. Warriors of the **Koromukishi** arrived with soldiers of the Principal's Host, praised the killing and discussed whether Orp should also die. The companions argued for the giant's life. A parchment recovered from Tylander's body resembled the missing mine deed only until it **dissolved into smoke**, leaving them with no document to trade for the Dusthill captives.[^arc02-9]

Orp awoke and, after difficult persuasion and the promise of food, was drawn away from the city. **Nash and Vorel** accompanied him into the desert while **Hope and Stanley** returned to Slavetown. The party was divided again, not by a supernatural barrier this time, but by choices about whom they would protect and how far they were prepared to deceive someone dangerous.

Nash and Vorel sent the hungry cyclops toward traffic at **Esophageal Pass**, persuading him that an ordinary dagger was a magical device through which they could call him. It was a deliberate falsehood and a potentially lethal choice. They did not witness what Orp later did to the travelers they had placed in his path. Then Vorel heard a summons from lizardfolk to the east. Nash went back to Slavetown alone; he did not see the rites that followed.[^arc02-10]

A priestess among Vorel's people spoke of **Fuxi, Father of Fire**, and of an ancient promise or captivity. Vorel experienced a fiery sky, enormous creatures beneath the earth and the struggle of being swallowed and reborn through flame. He could describe that vision, but neither the priestess's cosmology nor the vision's physical location was proven to his absent companions. When he awoke, Bok Bok was near him, and the road back to the others remained open.

In Slavetown, Hope and Stanley found fresh horrors. At **Randall Owen's home**, another mutilated victim lay behind a once-ordinary domestic door. Tylander's house on Thorn Row had become rubble surrounded by dead soldiers. A severed human **arm** stirred in the wreckage and nearly strangled Stanley. They fought it and later burned it. Its movements were plain to see; whose arm it had once been was still an inference.

The enormous **black dragon** from the desert flew overhead. In the street beneath it, Nash's skin darkened into scales and flames burst from his body, harming Hope and Stanley. Nash heard a low voice calling for **“Mother.”** His companions saw the fire, not the private voice; he could not know what the speaker meant.[^arc02-11]

An appointed magistrate came to **Nash, Hope and Stanley** with a story more disturbing than the arm. He said **Tylander had returned**, slain troops and become difficult to kill through infernal bargains. The companions had not seen a resurrected Tylander themselves. The magistrate pointed them toward the **Black Mine**, claiming that a land record connected **Catherine Speckles** to its legendary 999th level. His offer of rewards—and a promise that the Dusthill captives could be freed—was a bargain, not their freedom already accomplished. The three agreed only conditionally, while **Vorel was still returning** from his separate ordeal. A new destination had taken shape, and the party had not yet learned whether the man they killed could be killed at all.[^arc02-12]

## IV. Pixie Blush and a city on fire

By the time they prepared to travel, **Slavetown** had grown eerily quiet. Some of its people and enslaved captives had been moved toward the **Salivating Fields**, according to reports; no one had promised their safety. The company still needed supplies, and some doors were locked against them. **Hope, Stanley and Nash** broke into a general store, then sought weapons and other necessities while the injured and unsettled companions tried to regain their footing.

At an apothecary, a small tiefling offered remedies for Nash's distress. He took a preparation later called **Pixie Blush**. His perceptions slipped almost at once: ordinary people seemed to vanish, objects shimmered and the streets no longer held their shape. Meanwhile, Hope received a card reading and a warning of danger from the apothecary. It was advice or prophecy from one speaker, not a future guaranteed to unfold.[^arc02-13]

Elsewhere **Vorel**, now back in town with **Bok Bok**, met a woman in a striking **red bandolier**. She invited him to join a plan to rob **Forbisher's Holdings**, offering the excitement of another dangerous bargain. Her identity and affiliations were not established by her clothing. Vorel could relay the offer to the others; it did not mean that he or the company had actually robbed a bank. By then Nash had gathered explosives, and his hallucinatory fear was making him difficult to protect.

The three others returned to a locked **foundry** in search of equipment. The owner confronted them with a gun and fired. **Stanley** was badly wounded; **Vorel killed the owner** during the struggle. Whatever the legal rights of a business owner or strangers taking supplies, those consequences were played, not rumors that could be brushed aside. The wounds followed the party into the next crisis.[^arc02-14]

Nash, meanwhile, saw what appeared to be a **green elf**, an encouraging stranger who offered him comfort and urged him toward the explosives shop. The encounter belonged to Nash's drug-altered perception; none of his companions had established the stranger's presence. But the **dynamite** was real. Nash set it alight with a spell, and the resulting blast tore through the district. Fire spread toward crowded businesses, the **Buxom Confederate** and the hotel. The apparent voice that had encouraged him might have been a hallucination; the destruction and the terror it caused were not.

The companions struggled to gather their wounded. They confronted **Celibus Stump**, crossed streets turning dangerous with flame and fought to get Stanley into a vehicle. Vorel attacked a motorist trying to leave the city. **Hope secured a working station wagon** from the garage area; she had **not repaired Fate 4**. They collected Nash and drove toward the Black Mine as Slavetown burned behind them. The full loss of life was unknown. They had not liberated the Dusthill prisoners, and no one could truthfully say the entire city—or everyone still within it—had been destroyed.[^arc02-15]

The desert did not offer an easy escape. The following journey began with Hope driving the exhausted, injured party through worsening wind. Lightning flashed, but the violence around them was **sand**, not rain. The storm buried the station wagon, forcing the travelers to dig themselves free and stay together against near-blind winds. **Vorel** helped pull them out; their next shelter was the hollow carcass of an immense dead desert creature.

Small cloaked figures approached with crude weapons, and one struck Vorel's scales. **Hope's threatening voice in Infernal** drove them away. No one had identified the strangers or shown that they were killed. The party remained beneath the dead creature, waiting for the storm to clear and unable to tell whether the winds would smother Slavetown's fires or spread the ruin farther.

The road that had begun in flames now ended in darkness and blowing sand. When the wind settled, they would have to face the Black Mine—and the demands made in return for promises of freedom.[^arc02-16]
