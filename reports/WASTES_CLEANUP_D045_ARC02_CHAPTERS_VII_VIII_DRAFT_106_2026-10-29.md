# Chains and Bargains — Arc 02, Chapters VII–VIII

## VII. The price of a soul

The tear in the ritual chamber widened. **Quizquaz** had pulled a dark gem from the fallen beholder and opened a way through it, but the enormous figure now stepping into the Black Mine was not the oily demon the companions had fought beneath Dusthill. A **goat-like being** called himself **Catoblepraithe**, later giving his title as **the Soulbroker**. He spoke easily to the four battered travelers, as though an invitation could follow naturally from the ruin around them.

**Vorel** was still suffering from the beholder's magical influence. The newcomer calmed and healed him, removing the immediate danger of his turning on his friends. The relief was real; the reasons behind it remained the host's to explain. Catoblepraithe proposed food, rest and information, then sent Quizquaz ahead to arrange the welcome. After the company had survived the dungeon, the offer of a bed was difficult to refuse.[^arc02-27]

They crossed through a portal onto a bridge over impossible darkness. **Château Weir** rose around them amid distant lights, with a vast whirlpool beneath it filled with struggling forms. Their host described these as **souls**. The sight of suffering was plain enough, but its machinery, the identities of the trapped and the trustworthiness of its keeper were not proven by his explanation. In a carriage through the strange city, Vorel apologized for the blows he had struck while charmed. He had not chosen the enchantment, yet his companions still bore its consequences.

At their guest house the staff treated hospitality as an elaborate profession. **Baba** attended to the accommodations; food, baths, music and attentive servants awaited. The guests were asked for papers concerning consent and dietary needs, written in **Infernal**. They studied what they were being asked to accept. Those service forms were **not** the binding soul compact the host would later propose. They spent the night, slept and recovered, although a good meal did not turn the place into a safe or disinterested refuge.

**Hope dreamed** that she returned to the **Haven** and met her mother. In the dream her mother spoke of a cost paid for their reunion and claimed that Hope's **father had survived**, only to be taken by a woman with a **red bandolier** and the **Dead Men**. Hope awoke with a new possibility, not independent proof of anyone's survival or whereabouts. Elsewhere in the guest house, **Vorel heard a childlike voice** pleading through a door. He did **not** open it, and the voice's identity remained unknown. In the morning Hope recounted her dream, and the party connected its red-bandolier figure to the woman who had approached Vorel about a bank robbery in Slavetown. That resemblance made a lead, not a confirmation.[^arc02-28]

The Soulbroker received them in his throne room. He asked what each hoped to change: **Hope** wanted to heal the Wastes, **Nash** to confront the oppression of the Solar Mines, **Stanley** a fairer world, and **Vorel** strength without surrendering himself. Catoblepraithe presented **the Principal** as the great obstacle and offered knowledge and power in exchange for a task.

The task was immense. They were to **kill the Principal within one thousand days and one thousand nights** or, under the proposed compact, lose the **souls bound by it**. In return he promised an immediate boon and leads toward artifacts that could make the attempt possible. **Hope signed. Nash signed. Stanley signed. Vorel refused.** He had been healed, had shared the rest and had advanced in ability with his companions, but **he did not enter the soul bargain** or receive its signatories' contractual gift. The other three accepted a debt whose terms they had heard from the very being who meant to profit by it.[^arc02-29]

The Soulbroker began probing the secret within Nash, and pain drove him into a private ordeal. Nash seemed to return to the **Solar Mines**: guards hunting him, a terrified girl, vats holding suspended bodies, the brutal **Bulger** and a controlling **Warden**. He struggled with the nail he called Freedom. The images were given to him by the Soulbroker; whatever truth they held, the others were **not present inside the experience**.

Then Nash changed **before their eyes** into a **black dragon**. He flew and spoke while the others watched a power that could no longer be dismissed as an uncontrolled flash of fire. The game's contemporary account identified a **dragon-metal heart** within him, an extraordinary relic tied to a black dragon's lineage and dangerous experimentation. That explanation carried more weight than guesses made about Nash's earlier symptoms, but the names and precise workings still called for contemporary records. Even here the change came with **exhaustion**, rather than the unlimited freedom his new wings seemed to promise.[^arc02-30]

Catoblepraithe offered more leads. He spoke of paired firearms called **Fox and Scully**, an elusive red-bandolier keeper, a route to a far-off elven destination, and **Celestium** that might seal **Esophageal Pass** if ignited by dragon breath. These were **his claims and proposed strategy**, not artifacts recovered or defenses already destroyed. He gave Nash **keys to Château Weir**, with restrictions on where a portal could open. When Hope asked about her father, he admitted that he relied on agents and offered only a qualified report about possible transfer toward **Principal City**. As for the missing mine deed, he claimed it had already been consumed in the ritual that brought him forth.

The companions had no deed to bring back, no certainty about Hope's father and no liberated Dusthill prisoners. They had, instead, Nash's visible new form and a compact binding **three** of their souls to a mission none could yet see how to complete. **Vorel remained outside the agreement**. When the Soulbroker's audience ended, the four still had to escape the Black Mine where this bargain had begun.[^arc02-31]

## VIII. The Heroes of the Wastes

The return portal deposited the companions back in the depths of the **Black Mine**. Nash's dragon shape could carry him, but it drained him; the others began measuring the danger of another transformation. **Vorel fashioned a shield from the defeated beholder's teeth**, an improvised piece of protection rather than a newly proven magical relic. They cleared fallen rock from their path, passed the chapel and again found the chamber where the woman calling herself **Catherine Speckles** had died.

They looked for something to bring to **Bertrand**, who had cared about Catherine and had asked them to find the deed. They still had neither the woman nor the deed. The heavy chest they had failed to open earlier had not disappeared: **only now** did they force it open, finding money, gems and two potions that Stanley identified, **not** the missing title document. The party would have to decide what these discoveries meant after reaching the surface.[^arc02-32]

Beyond the chapel, the speaking door **Dorothy** stood between them and the route they thought led out. Distrusting her warnings, they **set the wooden door on fire**. Smoke filled the narrow tunnel, and as they struggled to see a safe way forward **Dorothy's voice continued to speak**. Setting the wood alight did not prove the being behind the voice dead or destroyed. A claim that weeks had passed in the mine came from what characters believed Dorothy had said, not from any calendar they had checked. They ended that stretch of their ordeal **still belowground**, with smoke and a hidden hazard blocking their way.

The hazard caught **Nash**. He fell into a **spiked pit** but survived when the others hauled him up using rope and the equipment they carried. The company retraced its way through the old passages toward the great elevator shaft. They had seen the lift smashed far below; nobody proposed riding it back to daylight. **Nash became a black dragon once more**, and with spells and makeshift supports the companions climbed aboard him for a perilous flight. When he reached the **upper guard hall**, the dwarven overseers were still waiting.[^arc02-33]

The **Oilbeards** met them with guns, including a wheeled weapon they called **Bessie**. Vorel leapt among the guards; Nash's dragon breath tore across their position; Hope and Stanley fought through the answering fire. The weight of weapons and wounds forced **Nash out of dragon shape**, leaving him hurt and exhausted while the others continued. At least one dwarf escaped during the fighting. The company had reached the top of the mine, not ended the Oilbeards' power everywhere in the Wastes.

Their victory made prisoners of the guards who remained. The overseer **Scrolly Oilbeard** was held alive and interrogated. The companions **injured him, healed him, then injured him again** while making threats. Finally they **threw him down the elevator shaft**. Whatever the dwarves had done to the enslaved miners, this was deliberate coercive cruelty by the protagonists, not a peaceful exchange or a mercy they could claim afterward. They searched the hall and found a locked prison room. From behind its door **Vorel heard Bok Bok calling**. The other captives were not yet identified, and the room was still closed when that moment ended.[^arc02-34]

The lock gave way only afterward. **Bertrand, Trigger and Bok Bok** emerged from the cell. Bertrand's explanation was bitter: he said he had come to return the **repaired Fate 4**, aided by Bok Bok, and had been captured by the guards. The party had not witnessed his journey or the repairs; what they could now see was that their familiar vehicle was available again. Bertrand learned that Catherine was apparently dead and the deed gone. He spoke of Tylander having been executed years before, and speculated that the lost deed might have concerned **dragon metal**. His grief and his theories were his own, no substitute for the missing document.[^arc02-35]

A stranger in **powered armor** arrived at the mine in a dune buggy and went into the workings. The companions observed him but did not establish his identity or purpose. They took the buggy's **keys** and left in **Fate 4**, with their freed companions aboard. On the road Bertrand told them of the **Lactid Cartel** and suggested that forces were moving toward a nearly empty Slavetown, perhaps while the Principal's soldiers redeployed. It was useful intelligence from a man who had his own interests, not verified control of the city.

Rather than rush toward another uncertain battle, the travelers headed for a **Liberators camp** near the **Deadfingers**. The camp offered shelter and the possibility of allies, though no army had yet promised to march at their command. **Vorel and Bok Bok hunted a bear**; Vorel killed it and brought meat back. **Nash**, angered by an encounter at the camp, discharged a public **Fire Bolt**, alarming friends who feared he might reveal the supposedly concealed settlement. Their growing power made protection and recklessness harder to separate.

Then they encountered **Slippery Brent**, the nervous map seller from Slavetown, now disoriented and possibly caught in the grip of **Pixie Blush**. Some of the companions coerced him and **violently assaulted him**, injuring him as the interrogation became an attack. **Nash objected**, troubled both by the harm done and by what the party was becoming. Brent's confused claims were not automatically reliable, and the episode did not establish his final fate clearly enough to pronounce him dead. A company prepared to face tyrants was beginning to imitate some of the violence it condemned.[^arc02-36]

At the camp they met a **sightless orc** who said he had abandoned the teachings of the **Koromukishi**, even setting aside his old name. He argued for opposition to the Principal and spoke with Bertrand about freeing captives and building resistance. The company weighed an assault on the **Salivating Fields**, stronger alliances, the far-off artifacts and the proposed sealing of Esophageal Pass. No plan had yet been carried out. **Dusthill's enslaved people were still waiting**, and three travelers carried a bargain that would not wait forever.

They chose to call themselves the **Heroes of the Wastes**.

The name described a promise, not a completed victory. They had rescued friends, survived the mine and found allies who might help. They had also left captives in chains, made a pact with an interested power and inflicted cruelty of their own. Whatever they were to become would depend on what they did with the name after this night.[^arc02-37]
