# Chains and Bargains — Arc 02, Chapters V–VI

## V. The Black Mine and the long fall

Wind scoured the hollow carcass in which the companions waited. Their **station wagon** was buried somewhere in the sand, and the fate of the burning town behind them was still unknown. The small cloaked attackers had retreated, leaving the four to wait for a break in the storm. In that uneasy shelter, **Nash** spoke about the **Solar Mines**.

He told his companions of captivity and beatings, of finding a **nail** and using it to fight his way out. He had named the nail **Freedom** and fixed it to his staff. Escape had not erased his memories, or his doubts about people hurt along the way. They were hearing **Nash's account**, not watching an independently witnessed past. The name could be confused with another possession: the **firearm called Freedom** found in Thorn Row, which was not this nail. Hope and the others also wondered whether the storm might quench Slavetown's fires. Nobody knew that it had.[^arc02-17]

When the weather eased, the companions abandoned their damaged vehicle and continued on foot. At the **Black Mine**, a vast quarry and guarded shaft opened into the mountainside. **Oilbeard dwarves** claimed enslaved miners had risen against their overseers. They had blocked the only lift, they said, to starve the workers below. Their account explained the guards' policy, not the true condition of everyone trapped beneath them. One of the guards kept a **human prisoner called Tickles** on all fours in a helmet, treating him like a pet. The party recoiled at the cruelty but argued over whether to fight the slavers at once or get down to the miners first.

Claiming authority from the Principal's magistrate, the companions agreed to descend. The guards insisted the lift was stuck and the mine was almost impossibly deep. Numbers concerning **nine hundred and ninety-nine levels** circulated as rumor or reported property records; none of the four had reached such a place. **Stanley** cast **Feather Fall**, and they leapt together into the shaft, landing on the stranded elevator far below the surface. A marker put them near an early numbered level. Their attempt to restart the lift brought something worse than uncooperative machinery: **undead workers surged from the dark galleries** as the platform began rising.[^arc02-18]

The fight filled the elevator with clawing figures, gunfire and spellwork. **Vorel's fiery rage** and Stanley's moving sword cut paths among the attackers, while Nash and Hope tried to keep the party from being overwhelmed. As they neared the upper guards, the dwarves saw the undead crowding their lift and **sent it downward again** rather than help. In the confusion **Tickles jumped onto the platform**. He was still a human captive, however his captors had forced him to behave. For a time he survived beside the four as the struggle resumed.

A heavy **Thunderwave** and the chaos of fighting damaged the lift. A support **chain snapped**. The platform tilted, then fell, rushing beyond the numbered galleries. The group passed at least one distant level marker before darkness and speed made any precise count meaningless. Shortly before impact, **Stanley slowed their fall with another Feather Fall**. Wreckage and undead bodies plunged into the chamber below, but the living travelers survived. **Tickles also survived that fall**. It would be another danger, not the elevator's descent, that finally took his life.[^arc02-19]

Far beneath the worked mine, the party followed a rocky passage to a chamber filled with crushed dwarf skeletons. A skull-ended lever promised a possible way onward. **Hope pulled it**, and the ceiling came down. She escaped the worst of the rockfall, but the others and Tickles were buried or injured under stone. The session ended with their rescue unresolved.

Hope and her companions dug out the survivors. **Stanley** was in severe pain; **Tickles** lay beneath a dented helmet and did not respond. Only now did they conclude that the captive had **died**. They rested among the broken rock and, testing the trap again, found a **second control**. By operating the levers together, they opened a hidden passage rather than causing another cave-in.[^arc02-20]

Beyond it lay a sealed study where fragile books crumbled to dust. A journal survived. **Tylander Sullivan** had written of lost family, the days of his suffering, and a **voice beyond a door at the bottom of the Black Pit** that, he claimed, offered vengeance. His handwriting was evidence of his beliefs and intentions, not proof the unseen speaker had described the past truthfully. A skeleton lay on the bed, but the diary's presence did not identify whose remains they were. Other pages shifted into unreadable symbols. The party took the journal and found a trapdoor leading deeper.

The new cavern was crowded with **mushrooms**, a chasm and **glyphs** whose effects they could not safely predict. A badly wounded, tentacled **displacer beast** attacked among the ledges. It seemed to slip from place to place; Vorel finally killed it after a dangerous struggle over the pit. When the party used part of the creature to test a glyph, a cloud of **magical darkness** spread across their route. They went into it holding on to one another and following the wall.

While they searched through that darkness, **Hope alone heard whispers**. One voice pleaded that she **find it**. The others could see nothing and had not heard what she heard. She did not yet know whether the plea came from a prisoner, an enemy, or something wholly different. Their path forward was still hidden.[^arc02-21]

## VI. Ritual, deception and the opening of the rift

The company emerged from darkness into choking mushroom beds beside another hazardous ledge. Some of the growth was only an obstacle. Other **large purple fungi** moved, reaching with tentacles and attacking the travelers. The party fought them off and picked a cautious way past further glyphs. When the crossing seemed too dangerous, **Vorel's enlarged body became a living bridge** for his companions. They were making their way by ingenuity through defenses no one had completely explained.

At the end of the passage waited an extraordinary door. Its metal face **spoke**, complained of being neglected, and called itself **Dorothy**. She offered riddles and warnings of danger, as if being a door had never prevented her from forming opinions. **Stanley** argued that she deserved more independence than simply obeying whoever approached. Whether Dorothy was a sentient creature, a speaking enchantment or some other trick remained uncertain. She nevertheless let them pass, and they crossed another gap by rope and magic to reach a ruined chapel.[^arc02-22]

Within stood **Tylander Sullivan**, seemingly returned after the companions had killed him near Orp. Eight thin, hooded followers labored among mutilated remains. Tylander offered explanations for his hatred: he said he had once been the mayor of **Pristine**, before agents of the **Principal** and the **Oilbeards** destroyed his position and murdered his family. Cast into the Black Mine, he claimed, he had received a new body and power from a patron. The journal had prepared the companions for parts of this confession; neither the journal nor his speech could prove all the accusations.

He also said he had turned enslaved miners into **undead**, blaming them for failing to fight their oppressors. Whatever he had suffered, the cruelty of the creatures already encountered gave the companions reason to take the confession seriously. Yet his account of motives and allegiances remained his own. To unsettle them further, Tylander assumed the appearance of **Catherine Speckles**, a demonstration of disguise or transformation—not evidence that every woman bearing that name was merely him.

An attempted shot passed through the figure on the raised chair. His voice came from elsewhere. **The visible Tylander was not a solid, defeated enemy**. He set his eight followers on the party; they changed into grotesque forms and attacked. The companions fought a bitter battle against the things Tylander called the **Fingers** of his patron. Victory over them did **not** mean Tylander had been killed again.[^arc02-23]

In a chamber piled with bodies, they found another horror: a gravely mutilated woman who identified herself as **Catherine Speckles**. She asked them to tell **Bertrand, the Jeweler**, that she loved him and was sorry. Then she ceased responding, and the party believed her dead. She was a prisoner who **claimed Catherine's identity**, not the same demonstrated impersonation they had just seen from Tylander. Whatever the real history of those two encounters, collapsing them into one person would erase her final words.

No mine deed emerged from the chapel. A locked chest offered another uncertainty, but they could not establish what lay inside. Ahead, a ritual chamber pulsed with strange light. **Tylander** appeared near a cauldron, beside an enormous **winged black figure** with luminous features. The figure spoke to them. Only as the confrontation continued did the companions recognize a familiar adversary: **Quizquaz**, the oily creature they had fought beneath Dusthill. He was **not** automatically the patron Tylander had promised to summon.[^arc02-24]

The way back closed when **Vorel's tail touched a glyph**, bringing stone down behind the party. Strange fields and vibrating projections guarded the central platform. Hope fired into its defenses; Vorel's size and strength helped test the barrier, while eye-like stalks attacked from the platform. The floor broke open to reveal that these were parts of a single monstrous body: an enormous **beholder**, its central eye and many beams turning the battle into a struggle against fear, sleep and stolen control.

**Vorel fell under a charm** and struck out at his companions. They fought to subdue him rather than pretend he had freely chosen to betray them. When the beholder was finally defeated, Vorel was incapacitated and the enchantment's consequences remained for his friends to confront. Quizquaz was still present, a separate being from the many-eyed monster and from Tylander himself.[^arc02-25]

From the beholder's remains **Quizquaz took a dark gem**. He used it to open a whirling tear in the chamber, a passage through smoke and impossible space. From beyond it began to emerge an immense **goat-like face and grasping fingers**—the figure Tylander had invoked as **Catoblepraithe, the Soulbroker**. The companions had won a terrible fight only to find that something more powerful was coming through.

The arrival was not yet a welcome, a bargain or even a completed crossing. What the newcomer wanted, and what it would demand, belonged to the next part of their story.[^arc02-26]
