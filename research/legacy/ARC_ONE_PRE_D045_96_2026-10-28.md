# Arc One — Becoming the Heroes of the Wastes

**The Wastes · first story arc · Full spoilers.**[^one-balance][^one-archive][^one-1]

## Prologue — A world that survived the dragons

The world calls itself the Wastes for good reason. Ancient wars, ruined machines, scarce water, and powers much older than the settlements shape the lives of people who have learned to rebuild among wreckage. Old accounts tell of dragons arriving through gates, a disastrous human war against them, and the persistence of dragon-engine technology after the creatures themselves vanished. The travelers will discover that the meaning of dragons, engines, divine powers and remembered history is far from straightforward.

Four travelers converge on **Dusthill**: **Nash Maggard**, burdened by the Solar Mines and injuries he does not fully understand; **Hope**, a tiefling engineer searching for a way to pursue an extraordinary and dangerous purpose; **Stanley Billings**, a persuasive would-be reformer; and **Vorel**, a lizardfolk warrior whose outlook is often at odds with human settlements. They are not yet an inseparable company. Their histories, ambitions, temperaments and skills only gradually acquire meaning together.[^one-2]

## I. Beneath Dusthill — The broken waterworks

Dusthill's survival depends on machinery most of its inhabitants have never seen. Appointed as a new **Hill Guard**, Nash, Hope, Stanley and Vorel follow the endangered **Hillmaster** beneath the settlement, where a broken water supply has become entangled with the violence of his former guards. Across a precarious bridge, they confront **Thomas, Holga and Dharma**. The dispute draws them into a buried history of authority, secrecy and competing loyalties before they have even reached the pumps.

In the machinery chamber, the Hillmaster reveals an ancient **dragon engine** coupled to Dusthill's waterworks. He identifies a skeleton as the first holder of his office and asks the newcomers to keep the installation secret. The engine is only part of the problem: an intake lies farther below. Hope's mechanical knowledge gives the company a reason to continue down the twisting shaft, fighting spiders along its narrow stairs. Before they reach the bottom, a body plunges past them. The Hillmaster's severed head leaves no doubt whose protection they have failed to secure.

At the underground river, Hope works on the damaged plumbing. Their rest is interrupted when **Softhead** attacks; Vorel knocks him into the current, and he vanishes downstream. Once the lower pump is repaired, an opening across the river tempts them into a dwarven ruin. Skeleton residents carry on ordinary activities as though the living visitors scarcely exist. A surviving medical automaton offers remnants of an older world, but the strangest discovery waits in a throne room.

An emaciated queen sits connected to an oil-black being called **Quizquaz**. He speaks of dreams and offers a bargain. Stanley instead cuts the tube joining him to the queen, provoking a fight with the creature and its guards. Hope's close-range shot helps destroy him in oil and fire. The queen dies, and the animated residents collapse. Among the abandoned rooms Hope finds a wrench whose head changes shape to fit her work.

On the return journey, a crowned skeleton draws Vorel's attention and a spectral figure appears. Calling himself **Peter**, the original Hillmaster, he disputes his successors' legitimacy and says the dragon engine runs on blood. Dusthill's water can be restored, but its hidden foundations offer the new guards little comfort.[^one-3]

## II. Leaving Dusthill — Captives and a home on wheels

The companions return from the waterworks to find **Dusthill under goblin attack**. Vorel fights a formidable raider as the assault tears through the settlement. When the survivors begin counting losses, repaired machinery offers scant consolation: people are dead, homes damaged and townsfolk missing. Stanley comforts a child named **Bruce Bane**, separated from his mother, while the Hillmaster's death leaves the settlement without its accustomed ruler.

That evening brings more trouble. Visiting the widow of former guard Thomas, Stanley becomes entangled in a knife attack and kills **Aaron**, whose own widow will demand an accounting. The next morning's public meeting must address both the raid and the violence surrounding Dusthill's rescuers. **Shun'ti** offers to serve as interim townmaster; defense, trade and the search for the abducted all need decisions. When Aaron's widow accuses Stanley, the gathering turns dangerous. A gun is fired, and Nash erupts in pain and uncontrolled flame, haunted by images of the **Solar Mines**.

There is at least one tangible success. Nash opens the surface valve and water flows. Beneath the arguments, Dusthill now has the means to survive, though its people must learn what to do with the secret engine below. Hope's recovered wrench speaks in Dwarven, urging repair rather than destruction. The advice comes as the company prepares to leave fresh damage behind it.

Vorel follows the raiders' tracks and captures a stray goblin who submits to him as a lizard king. **Bok Bok** begins his place beside Vorel as a frightened captive, an unlikely attachment amid the hunt for other captives. Hope unveils **Fate 4**, the heavily modified vehicle she built with **Jimster**. With supplies, a compass and binoculars, the travelers set out after the warband.

The road immediately tests their intentions. Hope insists on helping a terrified old man who fears lights in the desert. At night, luminous beetles circle the camp; Bok Bok returns dragging the battered stranger, and the company maneuvers away without a needless battle. Farther on, goblin vehicles intercept them. A bus with a catapult and armed trucks turn the pursuit into a violent chase. The attackers are beaten, but Vorel is badly hurt and Fate 4 overheats. The main warband and Dusthill's abducted people remain beyond their reach.[^one-4]

## III. The buried laboratory — A race to escape

While Hope repairs **Fate 4**, Nash approaches a metal antenna protruding from the sand. His strange eye reacts painfully, and the ground swallows him. Stanley follows after hearing a voice through the radio. Beneath the desert, their rescue pursuit gives way to a sealed facility, where two gnomes, **Chance and Eddie**, have arrived by a similarly inexplicable route. Cooperation begins uneasily: every passage promises a way forward, but none offers a clear road back to the surface.

The voice directing them eventually names itself **Leopold**. It insists it is no computer, describes the installation as a containment complex and makes clear that its visitors may not leave. Badges, elevators, skeletal crew quarters and recycled food suggest systems that have survived their original inhabitants. Leopold's assurances do little to make them safer. A green equipment case attacks with a tongue, another unnatural creature stalks the rooms, and Nash is left unconscious in the fighting. His companions keep him alive until they can rest.

Beyond the guarded corridors grows an enormous **tree**, its branches forcing their way through the buried structure. A mental voice begs for release. It claims to have been an elf, changed and imprisoned during ancient experiments against dragons, and offers escape if the company destroys its prison. The travelers are caught between two hosts who each demand control over their fate. Leopold wants them confined; the tree wants the machinery brought down.

They reach a control chamber and interfere with the warden's settings, then begin a self-destruct sequence. A countdown chosen to allow ample time becomes a frantic struggle through failing systems. A gibbering creature attacks on the return journey, and reaching the tree no longer means reaching safety. Branches become their last ladder as the facility collapses around them.

Nash and Stanley climb into daylight. **Chance and Eddie fall back into the wreck and disappear beneath the sand**. There is no time to establish what has become of them before a huge **black dragon** bursts from the buried complex and climbs into the sky. Hope, Vorel and Fate 4 are waiting above, but reunion brings no chance to rest: a large convoy is approaching on the horizon. The underground escape has delivered the company into another danger.[^one-5][^one-6][^one-7]

## IV. Slavetown — Freedom with limits

The vehicles closing on **Fate 4** are better organized and more heavily armed than the goblins the company has been hunting. Attempts to escape or negotiate give way to another running battle. An imposing orc warrior boards the roof with a katana and firearms, demanding obedience to the laws of **the Principle**. The companions fight until injuries and overwhelming force leave surrender as their remaining choice. Their equipment is confiscated, their wrists manacled, and their journey continues inside a windowless transport.

After days with little food or water, they reach **Slavetown**. Among its prisoners are people taken from Dusthill. The rescuers can tell them that water flows at home, but cannot open their chains. Instead, a magistrate accuses the newcomers of attacking protected property and orders a trial by combat. In the **Rusty Cage**, the four and the old man they brought from the desert face armed orc initiates. Weapons fall into the arena, and survival depends on the same mixture of magic and brutality that brought them here.

They win. A ceremony declares them innocent and free, their possessions are returned, and **Lady Saltbubble** and **Sir Chickenfinger** invite them to seek a place among the **Koromukishi**. None of this frees Dusthill's other captives. Slavetown can celebrate the victors while keeping the institution that made them prisoners intact.

The company retrieves Fate 4 and begins exploring a city of foundries, garages, saloons and sharply divided neighborhoods. **Celibus Stump**, who profited by betting on their victory, offers money and hospitality. At his establishment Stanley recognizes **Talia**, Bruce's mother. Her fear and restricted freedom give the rescue mission a familiar face. He arranges temporary shelter for her at a hotel, but a night away from her owner is no lasting release.

Nash meanwhile quarrels with **the Jeweler**, loses a drunken fight and wakes without much of his property. Recovering it leads to competing offers over a missing **mine deed**. Stump ties his help with the captives to obtaining the document; the Jeweler proposes using it against the Oilbeards and the slave trade. He also alleges that Dusthill was betrayed by its own leaders. The adventurers have won their freedom only to become useful pieces in other people's struggles for power.[^one-8]

## V. A city in flames — Murders and the mine deed

The bargain with **the Jeweler** becomes dangerous almost at once. Gunmen gather at the **Sapphire**, a spellcaster accuses its owner of withholding a promised item, and shooting breaks out. The companions fight through the building and into the street. The Jeweler survives with healing, but his ally **Donk** is dead. Whatever the missing deed is worth, others are willing to kill for the interests surrounding it.

A more conspicuous threat arrives outside their hotel: **Orp**, an enormous cyclops demanding his stolen necklace. His fascination with magic points toward **Tylander Sullivan**, a trickster whose trail leads to a house on **Thorn Row**. There the party finds mutilated corpses, explosive magic, a ruby necklace and a hidden ritual complex. Among the dead is **Randall Owen**, the garage proprietor who employed Hope. Her anger draws her deeper into the hunt.

Tylander demands the necklace and claims to serve **the Soulbroker**. Animated bodies and traps delay pursuit, while a parchment he presents as the deed offers another lure. He escapes the basement, but is caught again near Orp. Hope and Vorel strike him down despite his attempts to yield; the supposed deed turns to smoke. His death provides no clean ending. Later, Hope and Stanley find a severed arm moving among dead soldiers at his ruined house and burn it.

The company splits. Nash and Vorel send Orp toward trade traffic with a false promise that an ordinary dagger can carry messages. Vorel follows a lizardfolk summons to a priestess and a fiery trial invoking **Fuxi**. Returning toward town, Nash sees the black dragon overhead and suffers another eruption of scales, flame and the voice calling for its mother. The others still cannot explain what lives inside him.

Slavetown is already emptying when the party prepares for an assignment at the **Black Mine**. Seeking relief, Nash takes **Pixie Blush** and falls into hallucinations. While Hope, Stanley and Vorel break into a foundry, its owner shoots Stanley and is killed by Vorel. Elsewhere an imagined companion urges Nash toward the explosives shop. The figure may be unreal; the dynamite he ignites is not. Fire spreads through the town as the company gathers its wounded and escapes in a station wagon. The captives remain unfreed, and the road to the mine begins in the light of a city burning behind them.[^one-9]

## VI. The Black Mine — The descent

The journey to the **Black Mine** nearly ends beneath the sand. A violent storm buries the company's station wagon, forcing the companions to dig their way out and cling together in the wind. They shelter inside the carcass of an enormous desert creature. When small cloaked figures approach with crude weapons, Hope's threatening Infernal voice drives them away. The travelers wait for visibility, unable to tell what the storm may have done to the fire behind them.

In that shelter, **Nash tells of the Solar Mines**. He describes finding the nail he calls **Freedom**, fighting his way out and carrying memories of violence he cannot easily judge. The weapon fixed to his staff has become a reminder of both escape and suffering. His account offers the others a glimpse of the captivity behind his pain, though it cannot explain his recent transformations.

Leaving the damaged car, they reach the quarry on foot. **Oilbeard guards** claim enslaved miners have risen below and that the blocked elevator will starve them into submission. One overseer keeps a human captive called **Tickles** restrained and treated as a dog. The adventurers argue over whether to attack the guards or descend first, then leap into the shaft under Stanley's **Feather Fall**.

Undead crowd onto the lift from the galleries. When it nears the top, the dwarves send it down again instead of rescuing the company. Tickles jumps aboard. The fighting continues until a suspension chain breaks and the platform falls into depths beyond the worked levels. Stanley slows the final plunge, saving the survivors from the shattered lift and the bodies raining past it.

A hidden lever offers hope of a passage but instead brings the ceiling down. Hope helps free the others; **Tickles is dead beneath his dented helmet**. A second control finally opens the way into an old study. There they find a journal belonging to **Tylander**, full of lost family, vengeance and a voice behind a door. Beyond lie glyphs, a wounded tentacled beast and magical darkness. The companions survive by rope, spells and Vorel's strength. Inside the darkness Hope alone hears whispers asking her to find someone. Their search for a deed has drawn them far below the reach of Slavetown's ordinary cruelties.[^one-10]

## VII. Château Weir — The price of a soul

Beyond the darkness, hostile mushrooms and glyph-marked ledges bar the path. The company improvises crossings, even using Vorel's enlarged body as a bridge, before reaching a talking door called **Dorothy**. Stanley argues with her about obedience and freedom. She admits them onward to a ruined chapel, where **Tylander** waits among followers preparing human remains.

He claims to have been mayor of **Pristine**, before the Principle's servants destroyed his family and threw him into the mine. A patron, he says, gave him a new body and the power to avenge them. He also admits making enslaved people into undead because they failed to resist. His account turns suffering into a justification for inflicting it on others. The figure on the throne proves insubstantial, and his transformed followers attack while he remains beyond their grasp.

In a chamber of bodies, the companions find a mutilated woman who names herself **Catherine Speckles**. She asks them to carry her love and apology to the Jeweler, then falls silent. Farther on, **Quizquaz** stands beside Tylander's ritual. A collapsing floor exposes a huge **beholder**, whose eye beams turn the fight against the company's own strength. Vorel is compelled to attack his friends and must be subdued. When the monster falls, Quizquaz takes a dark gem from its remains and opens a rent in reality.

The goat-like **Catoblepraithe**, the **Soulbroker**, emerges, restores Vorel and offers hospitality. Through a portal lies **Château Weir**, a city above a whirlpool of struggling beings the host calls souls. Baths, elaborate meals and comfortable rooms make an unsettling welcome. Hope dreams of her mother, who tells her that her father survived their home's destruction; the meeting leaves a hope she cannot yet verify.

The Soulbroker offers power and information if they destroy **the Principle** within a thousand days and nights. **Hope, Nash and Stanley sign. Vorel refuses.** Nash is then plunged into a private vision of abuse and experimentation in the Solar Mines. Before his companions, he becomes a **black dragon**, exposing the dragon-metal engine that serves as his heart. Their host supplies artifact leads and proposes sealing **Esophageal Pass** with Celestium and dragon breath. The deed, he says, has already been consumed in a ritual. The original errand has failed, while three souls now depend on a far greater undertaking.[^one-11]

## VIII. Heroes of the Wastes — The way out

The return from **Château Weir** leaves the companions deep beneath the mine with a bargain, new powers and no easy way out. Vorel makes a shield from the beholder's teeth while Nash discovers the exhaustion his dragon form demands. Their route back passes Catherine's remains and the talking door **Dorothy**. Distrusting her, they set her wooden body alight. Smoke fills the tunnels, her voice continues through it, and Nash falls into a spiked pit before the others pull him free.

At the ruined lift shaft, Nash's transformation finally becomes their road to daylight. Enlarged and burdened with his companions, the black dragon climbs through the immense drop that nearly killed them. They emerge into the upper guard hall, where the **Oilbeards** are still armed and waiting. A wheeled gun rakes the room. Nash breathes acid across the defenders, but concentrated fire forces him back into his injured human body, leaving the others to continue the fight.

Victory brings another ugly choice. The company captures the overseer alive, wounds him, heals him and hurts him again during interrogation. They finally throw him into the shaft. The violence is their own, however vicious the slavers they have defeated. Behind a locked door, Vorel hears **Bok Bok** calling. The rescued prisoners include the **Jeweler** and **Trigger**, captured while trying to return the repaired **Fate 4**. The news of Catherine and the lost deed costs their ally dearly.

The travelers leave in their recovered home on wheels, choosing a **Liberators** camp near the Deadfingers over an immediate return to Slavetown. There Vorel hunts a bear with Bok Bok and brings meat to the settlement. A sightless former Koromukishi speaks of rejecting the Principle and preserving a resistance force. The possibility of allies gives their enormous new obligation a foothold in the Wastes.

Yet safety does not settle what they are becoming. Nash's public magic risks exposing the hidden camp. When the disoriented **Slippery Brent** appears, some companions coerce and brutally assault him; Nash objects. Stanley's disquiet grows alongside arguments over the captives, the pass and the use of power. At this camp they adopt the name **Heroes of the Wastes**. The promise in that name is larger than anything they have accomplished, and they leave its meaning unresolved between them.[^one-12][^one-13]
