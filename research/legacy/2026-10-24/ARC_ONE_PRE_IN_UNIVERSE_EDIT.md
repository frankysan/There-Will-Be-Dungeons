# Arc One — Becoming the Heroes of the Wastes

**The Wastes, first story arc — corrected E000–E026, plus the E013.5 supplemental**

**Spoilers:** Full spoilers through E026, including the E013.5 supplemental. **Status:** An editorial retelling synthesized from existing **processed-SRT episode dossiers**, **not an audio-verified transcript**. The setting's more uncertain past, characters' private memories and retrospective GM statements are **attributed, not presented as indisputable history**. Chapters link to the source-first dossiers for detailed timelines.

## Prologue — A world that survived the dragons

The world calls itself the Wastes for good reason. Ancient wars, ruined machines, scarce water, and powers much older than the settlements shape the lives of people who have learned to rebuild among wreckage. The campaign's introductory narration describes dragons arriving through gates, a disastrous human war against them, and the persistence of dragon-engine technology after the creatures themselves vanished. Those are **the prologue's account of ancient history**; the party will discover that the meaning of dragons, engines, divine powers and remembered history is far from straightforward.

Four travelers converge on **Dusthill**: **Nash Maggard**, burdened by the Solar Mines and injuries he does not fully understand; **Hope**, a tiefling engineer searching for a way to pursue an extraordinary and dangerous purpose; **Stanley Billings**, a persuasive would-be reformer; and **Vorel**, a lizardfolk warrior whose outlook is often at odds with human settlements. They are not yet an inseparable company. Their histories, ambitions, temperaments and skills only gradually acquire meaning together.

## I. Beneath Dusthill — The broken waterworks

Dusthill's first crisis begins with a deceptively local need: its **water supply**. An invitation from the Hillmaster draws the adventurers together, then sends them beneath the settlement to investigate a system of tunnels, traps and ancient machinery. The lower passages expose secrets around the settlement's old guards and its **dragon-engine water infrastructure**. The Hillmaster dies during the descent. Beyond a broken intake and underground river, the group discovers a dwarven complex, its eerie inhabitants, and an oil-associated being named **Quizquaz**. The party's intervention helps put old systems back in motion, but the region's tangled claims to ownership and old violence remain.

## II. Leaving Dusthill — Captives and a home on wheels

Their return to the surface is no simple victory. **Goblin raiders attack Dusthill**, killing and abducting townspeople. The ensuing town meeting leaves temporary civic leadership in place; a surface test confirms that water flows again, but it cannot replace the people who have been taken. The group chooses to pursue the raiders beyond the settlement rather than settle permanently into its politics.

It is also here that the party acquires some of its most enduring attachments. Vorel captures the goblin **Bok Bok**, who, against nearly everyone's early expectations, will continue alongside him. Hope unveils the vehicle that becomes their unreliable home on the road, **Fate 4**. Nash's strange Solar Mines experiences intrude on apparently ordinary journeys, foreshadowing a connection to ancient machinery that neither he nor the others yet understand.

## III. The buried laboratory — A race to escape

A pursuit through the desert turns into a vehicle fight, then a detour no one planned. Nash and Stanley enter an enigmatic underground facility, temporarily joined by **Chance and Eddie**, while Hope and Vorel are away from that part of the story. The facility's systems speak in the name **Leopold**. Its trapped rooms, unnatural inhabitants and countdown become a race to escape. Nash and Stanley emerge alive, while the fate of their temporary companions is not securely established at the cutoff. A **black dragon** bursts from the collapsed facility and rises into the sky.

## IV. Slavetown — Freedom with limits

The four regular adventurers reunite only to encounter a much larger force. Overwhelmed by armed vehicles and soldiers serving **the Principle** (frequently called the Principal in later dialogue and documents), they are taken captive to **Slavetown**. There, a trial by combat and the town's layered slavery, patronage and martial orders introduce a different sort of peril. Winning their freedom does **not** free the Dusthill captives. Stanley encounters **Talia**, whose circumstances make the moral limits of local deals painfully tangible; offers from figures such as **Celibus Stump** and the **Jeweler** draw the party into competing claims surrounding an elusive **mine deed**.

## V. A city in flames — Murders and the mine deed

An investigation of ritual murders, a one-eyed giant named **Orp**, and a magician called **Tylander Sullivan** further destabilizes the town. Vorel finds connections to his own traditions, while Nash grows entangled with danger and hallucinogenic **Pixie Blush**. A foundry confrontation and Nash's reckless use of explosives help turn Slavetown into a firestorm. The adventurers leave as the blaze spreads. Whether every rumor about the slaves, the deeds or the town's unseen power brokers was true remains a different question from the destruction they saw.

## VI. The Black Mine — The descent

Desert storms and dwindling options lead the group to the **Black Mine**, where the Oilbeards, human prisoners and an apparently endless vertical shaft expose a pitiless industrial order. The characters fight undead on a moving lift, fall into deeper galleries, and search through caves, glyphs, strange fungus and a diary associated with Tylander. A captive named **Tickles** dies after a ceiling collapse; this is one of the ways the journey refuses to behave like a clean rescue mission.

[E019](../../../episodes/E019.md) · [E020](../../../episodes/E020.md) · [E021](../../../episodes/E021.md)

## VII. Château Weir — The price of a soul

Deeper still, the mine's supernatural machinery and Tylander's ritual culminate in a fight with a **beholder**. Defeating it opens the way to an encounter with **Catoblepraithe**, the **Soulbroker**, an otherworldly power associated with the Weir. In the disconcertingly hospitable **Château Weir**, the Soulbroker offers a contract: the party must destroy the **Principle** within the specified span of **one thousand days or nights**, or suffer the consequences for their souls. **Hope, Nash and Stanley sign. Vorel refuses.**

Nash receives an especially disturbing revelation. A private experience of the Solar Mines and an observable **black-dragon transformation** expose the metal engine that has been functioning as his heart. The party also learns about enchanted artifacts, political constraints and possibilities far beyond the mine. Not everything the Soulbroker asserts is independently demonstrated; nor is the full history of Nash's captivity resolved by what he sees.

## VIII. Heroes of the Wastes — The way out

Escape is almost as violent as descent. Nash's dragon form helps lift the party through the shaft; conflict with the Oilbeards leaves more blood behind. Reunited with Bok Bok and allies, they recover Fate 4 and visit the **Liberators**. Their arguments over the treatment of captives, prisoners and enemies begin to expose a deeper fracture: they may be companions, but they do not agree on what victory is for.

At the Liberators' camp, the companions adopt the name **Heroes of the Wastes**. Their local mission has grown into a wider struggle, even as Stanley's disquiet exposes how little they agree on the use of their power. This closes Arc One; their departure and the consequences of the Soulbroker bargain begin [Arc Two — The Road to the Crown](../../../narrative/ARC_TWO.md).
