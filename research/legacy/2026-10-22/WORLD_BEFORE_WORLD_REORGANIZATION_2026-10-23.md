## The world and its ancient catastrophe

### The surviving Earth

In the opening GM description, the Wastes are **a far-future Earth**, mostly dry, dusty and resource-poor, with no oceans or winter as described in the first episode. Isolated protected settlements depend on **scavenged machinery, limited farming, military force, organized mutual aid and magic**. The scarcity of working water systems drives the first adventure, and competition over the conversion or transport of scarce resources later drives politics in Tietkalla. This is **setting exposition**, not a claim that the explorers have surveyed the entire planet. [E000](../../../episodes/E000.md) · [E001](../../../episodes/E001.md).

The dramatic prologue describes **dragons arriving through gates** in an earlier, more prosperous age. Humanity resisted with advanced machines and dangerous **pacts with monsters or demonic forces**; a protracted conflict ravaged the world. Surviving societies use **dragon engines**, powerful devices associated with dragon remains and **mana emissions**, even though the fate of the dragons themselves is not fully reconstructed from the first-year episodes. The exact dates, length of the war, identity of the gates' builders and the accuracy of poetic prologue language **remain open to audio and source review**. [E000](../../../episodes/E000.md).

### Uneven records of the past

Different civilizations remember the ancient age differently. **Hope's introductory tiefling account** connects her ancestry to a dark pact; **Nash** remembers the Solar Mines through experiences that later seem inconsistent; an apparition of **Peter** disputes Dusthill's own inherited leadership; **Tylander Sullivan** offers his own account of past rule and forced labor; and Tietkalla's **Nobiery, Kefeli and Monsoon** disagree over the kingdom's destruction and who deserves to rule it. Their stories should remain **speaker-attributed historical claims** where the party cannot verify them. An ancient-looking skeleton, artifact or wall inscription is not by itself proof of the narrative supplied by the person describing it. ## Settlements, societies and political power

### Dusthill — shared survival and disputed inheritance

The party meets in **Dusthill**, a defensible settlement enclosed by rock, with farms, a garage, public water distribution and a succession of **Hillmasters**. Its reliance on a **subterranean pump and dragon-engine complex** demonstrates a society organized around a finite shared resource. When the current Hillmaster dies during the expedition, a returning apparition called **Peter** alleges that later leaders were impostors; this is **his testimony**, not an automatic retroactive invalidation of every previous ruler. A goblin raid kills and abducts residents, and an emergency meeting appoints **Shun'ti** to a temporary civic role while **Tetsuo and Jimster** help organize defense. By E006 the repaired water outlet is **tested flowing**, distinct from the earlier moment at which it remained untested. [Dusthill reference](../../../world/places/dusthill.md) · [E002](../../../episodes/E002.md) · [E005](../../../episodes/E005.md) · [E006](../../../episodes/E006.md).

### Pristine, Slavetown and the Principle

**Slavetown**, the slaveholding city where the adventurers become captives, grew from a settlement that the GM in an E013 **out-of-character briefing** calls **Pristine**. He describes a shift over roughly **10–20 years** involving the **Oilbeards**, slave traffic and arrangements with agents of the **Principle**. This is stronger evidence for the briefing's intended setting context than a dealer's or magistrate's sales pitch, though the numerical interval should still be checked against audio. The name **Principle** appears in source dialogue alongside `Principal`; neither spelling choice establishes a different person or institution without referent review. In the [June 2019 bonus](../../../episodes/BONUS-2019-06.md), **Beau retrospectively says** he conceived the Principle in episode one, describes him as **over one thousand years old** and familiar with life and technology **before the Scorching**, and warns that **Quizquaz's lich comparison is insufficient**. This is **GM creator testimony with deliberately withheld details**, not the Principle's independently proven species or the party's knowledge from E013. [E013](../../../episodes/E013.md) · [Slavetown reference](../../../world/places/slavetown.md).

Slavetown's political economy includes **coercive trials, slave labor, competing government and military agents, the Koromukishi, Oilbeard interests, the Lactid Cartel, criminal middlemen and rival claims to a mine deed**. The protagonists gain their own freedom but **do not free every Dusthill captive**. A supposed agreement or bank heist is not a directly witnessed fact merely because a faction spokesman describes it. The city suffers a substantial **real fire in E017–E018** while Nash is also experiencing **Pixie Blush hallucinations**; those are two different kinds of evidence. [E010](../../../episodes/E010.md)–[E018](../../../episodes/E018.md).

**E115 — a live appearance and unverified ascension claim:** The Principle **physically enters Elhandar through a portal**, visibly grows, changes his face, telekinetically draws Stanley close and leaves again. **He claims responsibility for the city’s explosion and demigodhood**, offering a future order under his leadership; **these claims are not independently verified by the display**. The High Elf identifies him as **Vladimir**, criticizes his choices and describes earlier collaboration/refused therapy. The station and its [living biodome](../../../world/places/elhandar.md) are observed, but their ancient restoration mission and corrupted-soil/elven-afterlife explanation remain **elven testimony**. [E115](../../../episodes/E115.md) · [comparison](../../../reports/E115_ASR_COMPARISON_2026-10-23.md).

### Tietkalla, Trashburg and the keep

**Tietkalla** is the project display for a historic tiefling city/kingdom supported by **Jon's cast-written spelling**, with ASR aliases `Tiat Cala` and `Tiat Kala`. **Teet/Teet keep** often refers to its fortified seat; **Trashburg** is the poorer, heavily governed settlement associated with it. These labels are **related places**, not necessarily identical administrative boundaries in every scene. By Arc Two, the fortress is ruled through the **Guzzle Gang/Suck Master Din**, opposed or exploited by **Monsoon Cabbage's Twenty-Sixers**, **Toe Rippers** and other local interests. Jolt's planned **royal wedding** promises political advantage; a **Blood Ball** match is prepared and trained for but **not actually played**. [Tietkalla reference](../../../world/places/tietkalla.md) · [E031](../../../episodes/E031.md)–[E039](../../../episodes/E039.md).

In E054, after ruin and siege, **Nobiery actually arrives with hundreds of armed tieflings**, **publicly crowns Hope Akmenos**, and brings house representatives into a war council. The returned troops and the acclamation are **observed played outcomes**; they do **not independently establish** the earlier exact claim of **one thousand soldiers from twelve houses** or the legality of an ancient royal succession. **Vorel executes captive Suck Master Din at Hope's signal**. Named figures such as **Melgor of Poxis** (security), **Fula Dentipilius** (food/farms) and **Relium Vanch** (construction) organize or propose work; those orders are **not evidence** that the sweeps, harvests, clinics, citizenship protections or city rebuilding have already succeeded. Some officers openly distrust non-tieflings; Hope admits Nash, Stanley and Vorel to her council despite opposition. **Monsoon escapes as mist** and the city's future remains contested. [E054](../../../episodes/E054.md).

### Other communities, orders and rival obligations

The **Golden Land** and **Diptak** connect Vorel to lizardfolk politics. After Diptak **yields alive** in E028, he pledges clan support, but a future military commitment is not the same as a fully observed army joining later battles. Vorel's visions and **Fuxi** traditions reflect lizardfolk cosmology without proving every divine claim. The **Liberators** and **Umbilical Order** appear along the journey away from the Black Mine; their allegiance, charitable or coercive actions and institutional capacity should be judged episode by episode. ## Dragon engines, machinery and altered bodies

**Dragon engines** link Old-World craft with present-day magic and resource economics. Beneath Dusthill the party distinguishes an **engine**, pumping machinery and a **much lower river intake**; separate work on those components precedes the successful **E006 outlet test**. A specter suggests that dragon engines are **blood-fueled**—a specific supernatural witness claim, not a confirmed universal engineering law. Hope's ambition to use a dragon engine to change the past is **her objective**, not proof that a practical time machine has been built. [E000](../../../episodes/E000.md) · [E002](../../../episodes/E002.md)–[E006](../../../episodes/E006.md).

**Nash's heart** is a metal dragon-engine-like organ revealed during the **Château Weir** encounters. It can emit powerful mana and helps explain his transformation into first a **black dragon wyrmling** and later a **young black dragon**; the transformations carry costs such as **exhaustion**. The [Year One Q&A](../../../episodes/BONUS-2019-03.md) adds Beau's **retrospective design explanation**: he developed the heart concept after Scott improvised uncertainty about Nash having a normal heart. This tells us how the fiction was constructed, not which Solar Mines memories were literally true. The identity of Nash's former betrayer is also **partially unshared player backstory**, not a settled NPC in Arc One.

**Fate 4**, Hope's repaired and improved vehicle, holds the companions together on the road; it is **not already a successfully time-traveling dragon engine**. The hidden **Leopold laboratory** uses access badges, surveillance, programmable containment and a powerful imprisoned tree. The subterranean dwarven complex beneath Dusthill includes **animated machinery such as C4N-D13**. These installations are different places with different functions; the E008–E009 facility should not be confused with the **Black Mine** because both are deep underground. In the [June bonus](../../../episodes/BONUS-2019-06.md) ~00:00:33–00:02:43, Beau recalls an **unclaimed magic staff** he placed among the laboratory loot as **tree- or druid-associated**, but says he **cannot recall its name or exact powers**. Do not invent a staff inventory entry, assign it to Nash or conflate it with Stanley's **Immovable Rod**. [E006](../../../episodes/E006.md) · [E008](../../../episodes/E008.md)–[E009](../../../episodes/E009.md).

**Body alteration and medical technology** range from Vorel's lizardfolk ritual experience and Nash's engineered heart to **Hope's amputations and mechanical prostheses** after the rebounded rocket in E038–E039. These are actual character-altering consequences, not simply costume changes. Hope later refuses a proposed **vampire transformation**. A separate homebrew procedure using a previously killed **basilisk** and old preserved eyes **restores the petrified Hope and Vorel in E051**; the scene does **not validate a universal alchemical recipe** or create remaining stock. [E038](../../../episodes/E038.md)–[E039](../../../episodes/E039.md) · [E051](../../../episodes/E051.md).

In E023, after the **Black Mine beholder** confrontation, the party enters **Château Weir** and meets **Catoblepraithe/Catobelpraithe** (spelling unresolved), the **Soulbroker**. **Hope, Nash and Stanley willingly sign** a soul-related compact obliging action against the **Principle** within a stipulated **one thousand days/nights**; **Vorel refuses**. Hospitality waivers, signed bargains and descriptions of the Weir are **different source types**. A claimed afterlife consequence is part of a supernatural contract and is not automatically witnessed later. ### Drug and pixie exploitation

**Pixie Blush** is a drug associated with **pixies' exploitation and crushed bodies**, powerful hallucinations, and in large concentrations **involuntary monstrous enlargement or other unstable effects**. **P0D**, a pixie close to Nash, describes captivity/harvesting around Trashburg. **Monsoon's Order 26** causes her monstrous transformation in E049, followed by **Diontalus killing her E050**. Other enormous pixies later appear and some return to small size; those are not resurrected P0D. In E050 Hope experiences intense **drug-induced imagery**, including a vision of Tedna rising toward a **steel heaven**; the death is real, but the imagery is **not observed metaphysical geography**. The [Q&A](../../../episodes/BONUS-2019-03.md) confirms an **unchosen pixie-rescue route** and GM preparation of Monsoon's transformed troops, not a liberation already carried out. ## Religions, souls, curses and uncertain realms

The **Weir** is the Soulbroker's domain or claimed destination for souls, but the precise metaphysical nature of the place, its rules and its relationship to other realms is **not fully verified** by an actual-play journey alone. The ancient scholar claims to be a **Dead Tongue** guiding tiefling souls to a **Night Pasture** rather than the Weir. That description, the alleged royal line, and claims about a vampire-cursed council should be kept as **the scholar's testimony** unless independently supported. The direct arrival of his armed forces in E054 validates **some of his concrete promises**, not every cosmological or historical explanation. [E048](../../../episodes/E048.md) · [E054](../../../episodes/E054.md).

**Kefeli**, the serpent-haired figure encountered under Tietkalla, truly **petrifies Hope and Vorel**. She says **Andreeste/the Nourisher** enabled her transformation to defend tieflings against a curse; **Monsoon** tells a partially competing story, later warning of Andreeste's coming claim when Kefeli is killed. Kefeli is destroyed as a statue in E051 and Monsoon escapes E054. **Andreeste does not arrive within the documented Arc Two ending**; claims about her divine authority and future intervention remain **NPC forecasts**. [E039](../../../episodes/E039.md) · [E051](../../../episodes/E051.md) · [E054](../../../episodes/E054.md).

The **Fuxi** traditions of Vorel's people, claims of **tiefling savior gods**, Hope's privately experienced maternal voice or vision, and Nash's sense of a **“Mother”** near dragon activity are all important to how characters understand themselves. They should not be flattened into a single confirmed pantheon, family relationship or afterlife. In the Year One Q&A, **Beau explicitly says he left the setting's gods and the “steel heaven” uncertain**. No later encyclopedia should promote Hope's pixie-blush imagery to a GM-confirmed excursion into heaven. [E015](../../../episodes/E015.md)–[E016](../../../episodes/E016.md) · [E023](../../../episodes/E023.md) · [Q&A](../../../episodes/BONUS-2019-03.md).

## Third arc — political authority, reform and its limits (E055–E066)

Three months after Hope's coronation, **Tietkalla reforms** inherited servitude under a queen with limited raw material, while **Vorel and Bok Bok build the Golden Sands**. **Softhead's return as envoy**, **Hope's mutilation of him**, the **stonefall killing civilians** and **Nobiery's confession of Soulbroker dealings** show how fragile royal legitimacy remains. The metallic **Principle** proposes **rail trade, an embassy and paid labor**, but the plan is **not a completed railway**. He visibly **extracts Diontalus into a crystal E059**, leaving open its subsequent custody. Actual **Dusthill captives arrive alive**; the full census remains unknown.

At **Razak Val**, cyclops **Orp** rules by eating human captives. **Vorel challenges and kills him**, but **Hope's redirected rocket kills Mirion, Typalla and many bystanders** during the feast. **Zercil dies after Hope's interference**. Her offered Tietkalla refuge **did not occur before capture**. **Sira actually escapes the Drive beam E081**, but no later secure resettlement is shown.

## Third arc — ancient science, weapons and the living walls (E067–E078)

Beneath **Kervalon**, the heroes physically encounter **CHIDS**, a pre-Scorching facility identifying itself as the **Center for Human-Demon Interdisciplinary Studies**. It contains rail lines, access-card gates, projection-like employees, frozen laboratories, dangerous corporate defenses, **brain-backed spiders, lizardfolk bodies in vats, neural-behavioral programming menus and connected living tissue**. The observed conditioning equipment substantiates **local abusive experimentation**; it does **not establish the origin of every lizardfolk species**.

The company encounters **Cerinthala**, a withered elf who claims to need **Elhandar** to die peacefully. Her **protective-looking amulet is demonstrably cursed** and binds Stanley to her wishes. She says old humans engineered lizardfolk to fight dragons, later **crushes in a selective living corridor**, **revives E075**, betrays her supposed allies, and escapes. Her **visible body disintegrates in the E079 Drive beam**, but earlier return prevents proof of permanent death. **Nash's staff is broken**, and the party suffers **red-creature memory-drain penalties**, whose later cures remain unverified. A secured chamber yields **one red/green portal device**; a separate setting emits a **lethal necrotic beam** which hurts **both Lethanphius and friendly Vorel** in E077. The device is not itself the **Traversal Drive**.

In containment, the **Eye of Groxl** responds to two special vessels, one intact with **Lethanphius**, the other broken and marked **G-R-0-X-1**. Lethanphius's defeat **severs Vorel's fingers and hand**, whose preservation and **Monster Mender reattachment are actually played E078**, not accomplished solely by E077's GM epilogue. **The same singular Eye of Groxl** is later used as a flame sword in E080. A hive spokesperson claims **GR0X1 was an anti-dragon prototype with divine power** and that CHIDS engineered soldiers from **already existing lizardfolk**; these are uncorroborated or only partially corroborated historical assertions. The **hive's physical transport of the heroes toward engineering E078 is demonstrated**, however.

## Third arc — competing accounts of the Drive and the Principle

An **E067 spectral dragon** alleges humans built a machine to **move the entire planet** away from dragons. In a convincing **E070 illithid office simulation**, an employee calling himself **Charles Dickens** says he can delay the same **Traversal Drive** by uploading false progress figures; the upload is played, but its practical effect is **not measured**. **Alvert**, a self-styled investigator from Hell, alleges the **Principle was once a CHIDS human scientist** maintained by awful human-processing facilities. **Cerinthala** claims direct knowledge of ancient bioengineering. The **hive council** describes stored human minds and an anti-illithid biological shield. A **wall-shaped purported future Nash** forecasts the friends' betrayal and the end of the world. These explanations come from distinct interested or uncertain speakers, **not interchangeable omniscient narrators**.

The party physically **enters the transformed Drive chamber in E079**, so the old status “machine never seen” is no longer current. After defeating the commander and the manifested **Dolo Popio**, they explore controls that visibly affect the central beam. **Quizquaz** appears and argues the apparatus channels energy from the **Void**, proposing planetary destruction as a way to kill the Principle and fulfill the **Soulbroker pact**, or alternatively a powerful person absorbing the beam. The party **rejects letting Earth die**. Some controls behave as he explains; **his cosmology, contract law, future threats and machine permanence remain interested testimony**.

## Third arc — observed finale and continuing world state (E079–E081)

The group saves **Chance and Eddie** from illithid captivity in E078. **Hope fires both found rockets E079**, throwing several illithids and **Cerinthala's present body** into the beam. **Nash kills the large illithid commander E080**, after which **Dolo Popio** creates a strange star-filled battlefield. **Chance drifts away in E081 and is not recovered**. **Vorel removes his CHIDS nanoweave suit and uses it to bind Dolo's mouth shut**, while **Hope reaches a drifting fire truck and kills the demon's manifested body with radiant darts**.

Back in engineering, **Sira and Eddie actually leave the central beam**; **Sira is alive beside Eddie** and he promises to protect her. **Stanley petitions Moha by Augury**, receiving a favorable yet final omen. **Vorel proposes four-person rather than single-person absorption**. Stanley, Hope, Vorel and Nash **voluntarily enter the crimson beam**, take massive damage, dissolve into white light, and the **GM explicitly declares all four dead at the E081 cutoff**. Their permanent fates, **Chance's destination, Earth's eventual safety, the Drive's enduring state and the Principle's demise are not shown**.

**Later GM preparation account:** In the [August 2020 public-release Q&A](../../../episodes/BONUS-2020-08.md) (~11:34–12:30), **Beau says the four-person shared sacrifice surprised him and forced him to rethink the galactic/planar aftermath**. One sacrifice or maneuvering **Quizquaz into the beam** were possible alternatives he had considered, **not actions the party performed or guarantees those routes would succeed**. He distinguishes already developing **Nash time material** from the unplanned shape of the group's journey. This retrospective account does not change E081's actual cutoff.

The third arc is **complete as a played CHIDS/Drive confrontation**, not as the final resolution of every political, cosmic or soul-contract mystery. ## Later March 2020 GM authorship clarification — original Weir versus borrowed Planescape

In the [March 2020 public-release cast Q&A](../../../episodes/BONUS-2020-03.md) (~04:30–07:00), **Beau explicitly says the Weir, its people and Kitobol Prith/Kitobalpraith (ASR spelling variants) were created for *The Wastes***. The GM contrasts this with **published D&D/TSR planes, Sigil, Planescape, Manual of the Planes and the Far Realm**, from which he borrows planar infrastructure. He remarks that **the Weir has been *alluded to* as connected to the Far Realm**, but **does not declare that the campaign's Weir is definitively identical to or governed in full by the published Far Realm**. This **is GM retrospective provenance**: it does not add a new E081 event or make any Wastes character suddenly aware of out-of-fiction sourcebooks. Also, **Sigil's availability of resurrection options after the beam sacrifice** is Beau's setting-specific justification, not evidence that revival is automatic anywhere.

The [August 2020 bonus](../../../episodes/BONUS-2020-08.md) (~21:42–24:29; 26:18–27:05) adds **Beau's explanation that a sudden planar turn made borrowing a published pantheon practical**, while much of the **Planescape cant** proved hard to remember during play. He had intended to use more of it. **This is authorship provenance, not a complete import of published cosmology, proof that every glossary term names a Wastes place, or new character knowledge**. The linked glossary itself was not independently inspected; obscure ASR words remain provisional.

**Editorial identity control:** A homebrew realm can take inspiration from published cosmology without being identical; **“Kitobol Prith”, “Kitobalpraith” and ASR-like forms should be audited against original audio/handouts before silently imposing new canonical spelling**. ## Later April 2020 GM commentary — inert Eye and rare good-aligned orders

In [BONUS-2020-04](../../../episodes/BONUS-2020-04.md) (~24:45–26:20), **Beau says that the one Eye of Groxl is artifact-like rather than an ordinary decaying organic part**. He depicts the **White Tower's followers of Euphemius as intended good-aligned**, while acknowledging dishonest figures in Sigil. **Tietkalla** remains one of the few hopeful communities on Earth, alongside rare **good-aligned/paladinlike orders** such as **Paxus, associated with Melgor** (ASR spellings provisional). This is **retrospective creator characterization**, not a new event within the E081 world-reference cutoff or a guarantee that any future negotiation succeeds. The source also **speculates that one hive-linked future Nash could survive if the Kervalon hive persisted**, a conditional claim that requires live episode corroboration.
