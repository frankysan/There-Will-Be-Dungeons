# Arc 07 — The City of Doors

## I. Strangers in the City of Doors

The light of the **Traversal Drive** swallowed four friends, and the world they knew vanished. **Vorel** found himself without the familiar weight of his body, falling through stars that seemed to surround him on every side. Somewhere in that impossible distance stood—or hung—an immense white-haired face, unmoving and silent. A black speck tumbled beside him. Vorel could not name the face or measure how long he drifted before the stars rushed inward and returned him to flesh. When he opened his eyes, he lay naked and exhausted in a foul **brick sewer**. The Eye of Groxl had split open in his chest, its former contents spread across his scales like a shedding protective skin. Whatever lived inside it was no longer answering him. He had survived, but the protection had cost something he could not recover by wishing.[^arc07-1]

The black speck struck his snout, then rattled on the floor. It was a **dark metal-and-stone heart** bearing a little gauge and a protruding, newly made eye. From behind that eye **Nash Maggard** could see and think, but not speak. No arms or legs remained for him to move. He scraped along the stones and slowly discovered he could **float**. When a hooded rat-man approached the sleeping lizardfolk with a fork and knife, Nash struggled to warn his friend; the stranger slashed Vorel awake and fled. Vorel took the strange living object along, naming it **Speckle the Cosmic Speck**, not yet understanding that Nash himself looked through its eye. The two had been changed differently by the same sacrifice.[^arc07-2]

The sewer offered no clothes, weapons or direction home. Vorel's old camouflage still let his scales shift against the shadows, but the **Eye of Groxl** was silent and his belongings gone. The hungry rat-man, calling himself **Jarver**, returned with a promise of food. Following him through rubbish and side passages, Vorel heard machines and voices ahead. Four masked collectors were lifting bodies onto a **hovering slab**, examining them with a many-lensed instrument as if the dead were merchandise. Vorel hid, struck a collector unconscious and seized his dark-seeing goggles. Then he saw whose bodies lay on the slab: **Hope**, missing her metal arm and leg, and **Stanley**, cold, swollen and motionless. Neither stirred when he reached them.[^arc07-3]

The collectors fought to keep their find. One drove electricity through the shallow water, stunning Vorel for a moment; Jarver killed another man and made clear that he wanted the corpses for himself. Nash, unable to shape a spell in his heart-form, **rammed a collector instead**. Vorel struck another down without killing him and held the last active man fast. The survivor spoke of the **Dead Book**, of bodies registered for collection and paid for by the **Dustmen**. Vorel refused the trade. His friends were not a fee. A grudging bargain left him with both bodies and the use of the hovering slab, while the collector **Binch Lory** remained to investigate the refuse heap where Vorel had awakened. Binch drew a route to the surface and gave their strange new place a name: **Sigil, the City of Doors**.[^arc07-4]

Above the sewer, **the city bent around its own sky**. Streets, roofs and lights curved overhead instead of stopping at a distant horizon. Travelers of every shape pressed past the half-clothed newcomer pushing his dead friends. As they went, a single human **finger began growing painfully from Speckle's heart**, letting Nash signal with more than a flicker of the eye. At the **Welcome Center**, a tiefling clerk registered Vorel as an impoverished refugee and granted **three days of food and lodging**. In describing his companion to the clerk, Vorel finally gave the heart its proper identity: **Nash Maggard of the Solar Mines**. The clerk suggested the Dustmen for the bodies, but Vorel asked for someone who might restore them. She directed him to an inn called the **Soggy Feather**.[^arc07-5]

**Bill** took the Welcome Center's vouchers without flinching at a floating heart or a cart bearing the dead. **Pam** brought food, and Vorel, who had once kept an inn himself, found something like ordinary hospitality amid the city's impossible shapes. Bill mentioned work, fighting and other ways to pay for a cleric, but none yet offered enough help. He also spoke of a feared **Lady of Pain** and Sigil's competing factions, describing powers of the city that Vorel had not witnessed. Two strangers sat close enough to listen: a small black-feathered **kenku** in a beaked mask, repeating other people's words, and a kobold whose safety-pin armor and makeshift weapons looked assembled from household scraps. At first, neither offered a trustworthy account of who they were. **Hope and Stanley remained on their slab**, and Nash could still manage only the noises and signals of a heart trying to become a person again.[^arc07-6]

The kobold soon named herself **Squirts**. Her infant, **Chunks**, rode in a little shoe fastened to her clothing. She said she knew a church capable of bringing back the dead, and the kenku, now called **Nothing**, joined the expedition as a guide. Their route crossed several districts and ended in an alley where a shabby building concealed a much larger sanctuary. Its door opened to Squirts' absurd little performance; within, a feathered cleric examined the motionless bodies through an enchanted crystal. He called **Hope and Stanley petitioners**, manifestations bound to the power he saw marked on Stanley's neck: the **Weir**. He spoke of the Far Realm and of obligations too deep for an ordinary healing spell. Vorel had seen the crystal reveal writing, but the priest's account of the wider cosmos remained his own.[^arc07-7]

Vorel insisted that his friends deserved another chance. The cleric, who gave his name as **Kidigree**, valued **a monumental public humiliation** more than coin. If Vorel helped engineer a sufficiently cruel and surprising joke against someone proud, Kidigree would **attempt** to restore the two petitioners. He had not yet selected a victim or completed a ritual. Vorel agreed to hear the terms. Attendants took **Hope and Stanley into protected crypts**, leaving Vorel to walk away from the only bodies he had managed to save. When the party turned back, the church's alley no longer looked the same; even its entrance had vanished among ordinary houses.[^arc07-8]

At the Soggy Feather, the companions slept in an executive suite. Nash woke from his rest with **a grasping hand and a mouth**, additions to his eye and little finger. Words finally came out of the odd heart, though the shape of his old body was still a distant hope. Three charitable visitors serving **Pelor** arrived with fruit and advice about work and learning a trade. Squirts distrusted them; Vorel rejected the thought of settling into a new profession while Hope and Stanley lay in a temple crypt. He needed money and a way to make Kidigree keep his promise. Bill showed him a notice for **Big Time Smack'em Brothers**, a tournament fought by **controlled mechanical marionettes** rather than by the contestants themselves. Its winner would receive a substantial prize.[^arc07-9]

**Vorel, Nash and Nothing registered** at **76 Mulberry Lane** and were assigned to compete at **Royce Gymnasium**. Squirts did not enter. They examined the puppets and their complex controls, but could not afford practice. A famous champion's strategy book might help. At a small sports library, Nash spotted the desired treatise in another reader's hands; the stranger would not lend it. The companions left with only the sight of its title—until the next day's schemes began. Nothing and Squirts had already shown Vorel that Sigil was a place where a person could get what they needed by means more dangerous than honest work.[^arc07-10]

The following morning, Nash persuaded the book's halfling reader outside. **Vorel took the volume**, and when its owner chased them, struck him **unconscious without killing him**. They carried him to the hidden church, where Squirts administered a vile-looking **forgetfulness mixture** supplied by Kidigree. The man's pulse was checked; whether the concoction truly erased any memory was not. Kidigree now revealed his target: **Kanja**, the celebrated, apparently unbeatable champion and author of the stolen treatise. Vorel was to beat him before a crowd, then humiliate him further. The cleric would interfere with the tournament's bracket, while Squirts carried **sickness-causing needles** to disadvantage opponents. Vorel recognized the dishonor but chose to proceed for his friends' sake.[^arc07-11]

That night Nash grew another arm and a foot, still floating as an incomplete creature. Nothing, meanwhile, accepted a **private commission** from a fixer named **Pelvis**: abduct an important Sigil resident and deliver him through an Abyss-bound portal. She told the others little of the assignment, and she **did not perform the kidnapping**. At Royce Gymnasium the next day, Vorel and Nash passed the magical screening, while Squirts entered by Kidigree's covert help. In the first bouts **Vorel's Kung beat a sickened gnome's Rubles**, and **Nash's Rubles defeated an older orc woman's Bland**. **Nothing and another kenku waited for one another to strike until both forfeited**. The matches were real, and so was the cheating already underway. **Kanja remained undefeated, Hope and Stanley remained motionless, and no championship prize had yet been paid**.[^arc07-12]

## II. A champion bought with tricks

At **Royce Gymnasium**, victory made Vorel a contender before it made him a hero. Several qualifying rounds carried him and Nash into the final four, alongside a quiet elf and the local favorite **Ventro**. Ventro bullied the others, and Squirts secretly **jabbed him twice** with her needles before he caught her. When Vorel and Nash were paired, Squirts tried to poison Nash too; **his strange heart resisted the injection**. The friends then fought honestly with marionettes. **Vorel's Kung defeated Nash's Bland**, and Nash, eliminated from the qualifier, congratulated him. Ventro had beaten the elf, leaving one final game. The local champion took Kung to deprive Vorel of his favored fighter. Vorel chose **Bland**, chained a succession of high kicks and won. He was the **Royce qualifier's champion**, not yet the winner of Sigil's larger contest.[^arc07-13]

Ventro would not accept the defeat. He raised part of the controller station to strike Vorel, and his followers turned the arena into a brawl. Nothing fought to stop them; Squirts cast spells that humiliated or calmed attackers; Nash found that his own attempted spells failed to take shape, though Squirts' magic worked. The room blinked, and a monumental **green female figure** stood among the crowd, seeming to face everyone at once. Her warning needed no words. Ventro protested, dropped his weapons and was **drawn through a pinhole in space**. No one saw where he went. Those who watched associated the apparition with Sigil's Lady of Pain, but could not prove its identity or that Ventro had died. His entourage fled. The crowd celebrated Vorel's win and the stranger who had survived an intervention by something greater than the arena's guards.[^arc07-14]

For one night Vorel was famous. Reporters and sponsors pressed close; he refused binding offers for regular endorsements. A young lizardfolk admirer even asked him to become his father, and Vorel urged the child to find strength in himself. At the **Soggy Feather**, a victory crowd filled the rooms, and **Nash and Nothing earned money serving patrons**. Squirts took trinkets and potions whose effects she did not know. The attention was overwhelming, and Vorel damaged a curtain and window while escaping unwanted followers. Eventually the inn went quiet. **Nothing's warning sword then stirred**. Armed intruders forced their way into the shared suite. The night ended with masked assassins coming through the window, before their attack had run its course.[^arc07-15]

The killers came in numbers, wearing a mark that resembled **a wolf facing a red moon**. Their bodies dissolved into dust when they fell, leaving clothing without answers. Nash's heart could still release **a dragonlike breath of flame** even while his own sorcery seemed obstructed. He burned several attackers, including a leader whom **Vorel had pinned alive for questioning**. Vorel was furious at the lost chance to learn who had sent them. Nothing recognized the emblem privately and, through mimicry and gestures, helped the others work out the name **Order of the Crimson Moon**. It was an expensive, secretive guild. **Who had paid it remained unknown**. Bill demanded compensation for the ruined room, and no appearance of the green woman saved the companions from the consequences of violence this time.[^arc07-16]

After a rest Nash gained more of one leg; he was still a floating heart, not a restored man. He learned of merchants who claimed to sell **spell keys** permitting magic in Sigil, but could neither afford their terms nor qualify for credit. That account seemed to explain his failed spells for the moment. Kidigree, disguised as an older man, helped Squirts make a grotesque **performance-enhancing broth** from plants, mushrooms and worse ingredients. **Vorel drank it**, although the larger championship banned such help. The bargain was beginning to demand more of him than a clever joke. When the lizardfolk child returned to seek Vorel's approval, Squirts struck him with **Sacred Flame**. He fell motionless. She hid him in an alley, then returned and used magic to stabilize him; **he woke and fled alive but burned**. Witnesses believed a child had been murdered. Vorel lost an **Arcane Spice** endorsement before its promised money was paid, and the rumor followed him.[^arc07-17]

The championship brought him to the **Great Colosseum**, where the spectators shouted the false accusation and pelted him with vegetables. A physician suppressed the delayed **poison from an assassin's dart**, but warned the remedy would wear off. Vorel first faced **the Mind Master**, a boastful halfling whose female companion had eyes Vorel recognized from the masked attackers. The resemblance suggested a dangerous connection, not proof the halfling had hired the assassins. As the mechanical fighters exchanged blows, Kidigree's potion took effect: Vorel heard a voice anticipating the **moves his opponent intended to make**. He still had to fight through dazes, failed strikes and near losses, but his Kung won. The crowd's hostility did not lift simply because he advanced.[^arc07-18]

The surviving contestants demanded a doping test. In the private stall, the attendant revealed himself as **Kidigree in disguise**, then **discarded Vorel's sample and replaced it with his own**. Vorel passed the official test. Another contender, the ghost **Tubbs 9K**, tested positive and was disqualified; there was no proof Kidigree had tampered with that ghost's result. The disqualification gave Vorel a **bye into the final**. He did not defeat Tubbs in a played match. His opponent was the undefeated champion **Kanja**, whose image adorned the stolen strategy book. Kanja chose **Bland**, Vorel **Kung**. Near the end the fighters had almost no strength left. Vorel ducked Kanja's last powerful attack and landed the decisive kick. **The championship was his**, won by genuine plays assisted by cheating.[^arc07-19]

Kidigree had given Vorel a small disc for the moment after victory. Vorel slid it toward Kanja and activated it. Purple light revealed what looked like **tiny people, ladders and workstations moving inside the champion's body**. The crowd interpreted it as an entire team masquerading as one competitor and turned upon the fallen idol. Kanja fled beneath the boos. Whatever the device's full secret, **the public humiliation had happened**. Vorel received the **championship trophy and five thousand gold**. He paid Nothing for protection, gave Nash money toward a spell key and settled the Soggy Feather's damage and lodging bill. The celebration lasted until dawn; when the doctor's temporary treatment faded, the dart poison returned, until **Squirts cured it with Protection from Poison**. The money and the trophy were real. **Hope and Stanley had not yet awakened**, and Vorel demanded that Kidigree deliver the more important part of the bargain.[^arc07-20]

Squirts guided them back to the trickster temple. In a deep chamber lined with mirrors, **Kidigree watched Kanja through an enchanted reflection**. The **Mind Master**, now accompanied by men wearing the Crimson Moon's marks, threatened the disgraced champion over an immense claimed debt and the small lives within his body. The sight showed a relationship between the halfling and the guild in this confrontation, but still did not establish who had ordered the earlier assault at the inn. Kidigree collected **Kanja's tears** into little vials and admitted that he had considered tricking the grieving Vorel by never returning his friends. Instead, he said, an inconsistent prank would be to keep his word.[^arc07-21]

The original bodies of **Hope and Stanley** were brought from the crypts and fixed to stone slabs before the mirrors. Kidigree poured out his gathered tears and began the ritual. For Hope and Stanley, time had been a formless darkness. Each found an opening of light and **chose to pass through it**. The figures who emerged from the mirrors could stand, speak and recognize their friends. **Hope was wholly flesh again**, with her old scars but without her metal arm and leg or the weapons incorporated into them. Stanley, too, was a living companion once more. Their **old corpses stayed on the slabs**; the priest refused to release them, saying they would become discarded husks. Vorel rejoiced but could not stop wondering whether the reflected people were truly the same friends. The companions' responses and shared memories offered comfort, though no ritual had made their larger obligations vanish.[^arc07-22]

In the temple's corridors Nash complained that his spells had been blocked by Sigil's strange rules. Then, **without buying any key**, he made a **Fire Bolt** shine in the passage. The supposed blanket suppression had been mistaken; the earlier failures and merchant's offer had happened, but they were no longer the rule under which he must live. **Squirts stayed with Kidigree and her child**, while **Nothing escorted the reunited company** toward the Soggy Feather. Neither was dead, and Nothing's private Abyss commission remained unperformed. Hope and Stanley saw the curving city overhead for the first time and borrowed what clothing they could. At the inn, Bill and Pam gave them food, warmth and a place to hear what had happened while they were gone.[^arc07-23]

Over a meal the companions considered their next road. **Bill** spoke of Sigil's hidden doors and suggested the **Clerk's Ward** might hold records of routes to **Earth**, but **no specific Earth portal had yet been found**. Vorel borrowed a merchant's **ethereal spectrometer** and saw luminous forms like those of living people, together with **Infernal writing around Stanley's neck**. The Weir's marks had survived the return from the mirrors; whether the bargain itself could be broken remained unknown. Vorel **bought a professional marionette cabinet** for the Soggy Feather, and **a skull-shaped focus for Nash**. The machine was installed, while a tailor supplied new clothes for Hope, Stanley and Vorel. Nash's rebuilding body remained too changeable to fit. They discussed allowing **eight days** for recovery, but **his full regrowth had not yet happened**. With four friends together again and a city of possible doors before them, the search for home could begin.[^arc07-24]

## III. The dragon's price and the stolen key

Eight days passed beneath Sigil's curving streets. **Nash Maggard** no longer floated through the Soggy Feather as a heart with a collection of limbs. His body had **grown back**, two arms and legs, a familiar red eye and the scars that had been his long before the Traversal Drive. He could stand, dress and argue with the others as himself again, although his new clothes made him look like an uncomfortable schoolboy. A bothersome bird found its way into their room, and Vorel's suspicions that every bird might be a spy gave the reunion an absurd edge. When the daily **M Sports Weekly** arrived, the news was more serious: **Kanja had been found dead**, hanged and burned, with smaller bodies strewn around his home. The **Harmonium** was investigating. The Mind Master's threats in Kidigree's mirror made suspicion natural, but the article **named no killer**. A championship victory had not ended the trouble in Sigil.[^arc07-25]

The reunited friends needed a route to **Earth**, not another contest. **Stanley** wondered whether they ought to go home at all: the Wastes had taken so much, while this strange city at least offered a warm inn and a chance to begin again. **Hope** remembered her people and the responsibilities of a queen; **Nash** thought of his wife and expected child, and **Vorel** of the friends and promises he had left behind. No one knew how much time had passed in their own world. They hired a rickshaw into the quieter **Clerk's Ward**, where Hope endured a halfling clerk's demand that she, as a tiefling, be escorted by armed guards. The agency's official **Duerto** treated them more courteously, with help from **Fax**, the metal-fitted illithid Vorel had met as a tournament competitor. Even a mind flayer could hold an office here, although that did little to calm those who remembered CHIDS.[^arc07-26]

At the **Portal Regulatory Agency**, the officers searched a registry for **Earth**, carefully distinguishing the planet from the Elemental Plane with the same name. One **recorded stable portal** appeared. Its reputed steward was **Tobacho Fleur**, based in the impoverished **Hive**. The official supplied **neither the portal's exact door nor its key**, and no one demonstrated that this was the only passage to Earth in existence. It was a name and a neighborhood, not a way home. Before descending into the city's dangerous underlayers, the four returned to the sewer where Vorel had first found his fallen companions. **Stanley recovered Hope's pistol Scully** from the filthy water. He also found a workable pair of **ethereal spectrometer goggles**. Their light revealed names and **Infernal writing on Hope and Stanley**, as if someone still claimed their souls for the **Weir**. The words were a warning, not a court's judgment on the pact.[^arc07-27]

**Nash** found a metal oddity that unfolded into an umbrella and christened it **Barbara**. While he repeatedly snapped it open, a **purple opening appeared in the sewer wall**. He threw objects through and cautiously put his head across, glimpsing a hot, dark enclosure crawling with oily many-legged creatures. He pulled back before the aperture faded; **no one had crossed into a new world**, and the opening did not identify a route to Earth. In the nearby arrival chamber the scavenger camp lay wrecked and bodies were scattered among broken equipment. A rat-man contact, **Jarver**, claimed the Rat King's people had destroyed the collectors who tried to claim the site, and that the original arrival portal was gone. For a bribe, he named the broker who had bought **Hope's other missing pistol**: a man connected to **Tobacho**. That made the dragon's steward both a chance of reaching home and a lead to what she had lost. The group argued over tiny sewer passages into the Hive, but no workable journey for four full-sized travelers emerged.[^arc07-28]

They chose **the streets above**, not the ratfolk tunnels. The orc tailor **Lohar** fitted them with threatening leather disguises—spikes, buckles, skull designs and boots with hidden steel points. Two days passed while the costumes were prepared and Vorel tried to work with the marionette cabinet he had purchased. An expected illithid pupil failed to arrive, for reasons unknown. Nash discovered another odd consequence of his regrowth: the cooling underwear he had worn at CHIDS had **fused with his new body**, leaving him a vent-like control and a little of the ability to **hover**. Dressed as a gang, with Stanley magically aging the fresh leather, they approached the **Harmonium checkpoint** at the Hive's boundary. Soldiers were building a gallows beside cages of prisoners. Their captain let the strangers **enter** only after warning that they would **never be permitted back out**. A caged person begged for help; the four did not free him or witness his threatened execution. The ward swallowed them in smoke, ruined pavement and shouting.[^arc07-29]

**Hope** entered a grim establishment called **Daddy Long Legs** to seek the dragon's whereabouts. There, amid armed patrons and performers, a hooded tiefling with scarred dark-red skin and gold teeth introduced himself as **Spatulo**. He pointed her toward a frog vendor near the center of the Hive and a concealed cantina door beyond. His directions proved useful; **his loyalties did not**. Nash spoke with a tiefling woman who seemed unhappy with her work and wished for a different life. He offered to try to help her someday, but made **no rescue** before leaving. The party found the vendor, followed his subtle sign into an alley, and descended beneath an unmarked burgundy door. The room beyond bristled with weapons and guards. Its master was no ordinary broker: **Tobacho Fleur was an immense blue dragon**, reclining with a pipe, beret and monocle, his ancient dialect interpreted by a stone golem.[^arc07-30]

A man called **Jelver** arranged an audience in exchange for a payment. He advised respectful honesty, even as the companions clung to names invented for their disguises. **Stanley presented Scully to Tobacho as a gift, with Hope's approval**. A servant carried it away beneath the dragon's couch. She had barely recovered her pistol before surrendering it again. Tobacho acknowledged that he knew of a route to Earth but would reveal its location and means of use **only after the party retrieved a valuable object** for him. At this first meeting he **did not name the object**. Mention of **Spatulo** enraged the dragon: the apparently helpful informant was an enemy who interfered with his business. Tobacho even offered a separate reward for capturing him, which the companions did not undertake. **Jelver led them out through a guarded passage**, promising the details of the bargain in private.[^arc07-31]

Through a briefly opened door in the private passages, the four glimpsed **the Mind Master**, their old tournament rival, talking with another figure. The door shut before they could confront him. Jelver then took them across a glowing circle into what looked like a **desert canyon**. Four enormous trolls came forward; the creatures struck hard enough to injure the companions despite the uncanny setting. **Stanley killed one with his blade**, **Vorel used the new spiked boots** to dispatch a two-headed foe, one troll destroyed itself with its own poisonous blood, and **Hope's crossbow killed the last**. Stanley fell unconscious beneath a rock. **Nash stabilized him with Stanley's healer's kit**. The canyon dissolved around them, revealing an arranged magical trial rather than a proven journey into a real desert. Jelver declared that they had passed, **healed Stanley**, and had his people restore the wounded company to full strength.[^arc07-32]

Only then did Jelver tell them the price of Tobacho's aid: **Zorander's Fist**, a powerful gauntlet said to be held by holy warriors somewhere in the **Outlands**. They were shown a kidnapped **bishop of Euphemius**, while a changeling occupied his place in Sigil. **Stanley entered the cell alone and charmed the captive with a performance**, making the bishop believe the strangers served the same sacred cause. The bishop said the Fist lay within the besieged **White Tower**, in a **sealed chest** whose key was a pendant worn by **High Cleric Thrun** in Sigil. He claimed the tower's walls admitted only souls judged good and that its defenders hoped to move the relic to **Mount Celestia**. The companions had seen neither chest nor tower. Stanley left the bishop bound, with his information and an uneasy promise to look after a relative. Their own mission required them first to steal a holy man's key, then to fall into a siege and take the relic from people who believed they were protecting the world.[^arc07-33]

Jelver proposed using a crewed airship to approach the tower and **Dimension Door** spells to leap through its defenses. The company asked for scrolls, weapons, provisions and a means of returning to the ship; these were **requests**, not yet supplies in hand. The broker did give them **a sealed travel writ** and an authorization for new disguises, and **loaned Vorel a greatsword called Shatter**, insisting it be returned. The next day a Harmonium checkpoint **honored the writ**, allowing them out of the Hive despite the earlier captain's threat. At Lohar's shop they collected white acolyte garments and later rested in a safehouse with the **changeling posing as the stolen bishop**. With temporary altered appearances—**Nash as a handsome human, Vorel as a large Goliath, Hope as a human named Candice, Stanley still himself**—they approached the **Unwavering House** of Euphemius. This was no longer a plan: they were about to try the first theft.[^arc07-34]

A very old **High Cleric Thrun** agreed to perform an unusual blessing for the supposed pilgrims. **Stanley hid the words of Telekinesis inside his prayers**, loosening the clasp of the sun-and-fist pendant. As it fell, he **swapped the real ornament for a prepared imitation**. The genuine piece showed briefly from his sleeve; **Hope's extravagant display of devotion and Vorel's tears** distracted the cleric until it was concealed. Thrun accepted and wore the false key. But Hope's fervor impressed him too well: he ordered **Candice to remain behind** for spiritual examination. Stanley returned, used **Modify Memory** on a sister of the temple, and induced her to send Hope out with the others. She escaped unharmed. The companions now had the **actual stolen pendant**, though none had yet tried it on the Fist's chest. No paladin pursued them out of the gate.[^arc07-35]

Jelver's supplies were finally waiting in the carriage: **Dimension Door and Reverse Gravity scrolls**, a **Ring of Spell Storing for Nash**, a magic dagger for Hope, food and climbing gear, and **two pistols with ammunition described as Fox and Scully**. **Scully had truly been given to Tobacho earlier**; it now returned as part of the equipment shipment. The name of its companion conflicted with earlier accounts of Hope's missing second gun, and no third pistol could be assumed. Nash stored several **Chromatic Orbs** in the ring. Far below Sigil, an immense hangar held an **airship crewed by gnomes** and commanded by a towering elephantlike captain. An enormous portal ring stood along its launch route. They toured the vessel and heard that departure would be soon. **The ship had not yet launched**, and Zorander's Fist remained beyond the siege.[^arc07-36]

## IV. Four leap, three enter

The vessel gained its name before it flew: **Traveler's Twin**. As the party prepared to depart, the **Mind Master** appeared at the dock accompanied by men dressed in the unmistakable **Crimson Moon** insignia. He claimed to have helped Tobacho procure the airship. He also offered Vorel a dragon-headed jeweled amulet, its inscription professing friendship. **Nash examined its magic** and learned that it was an **Amulet of Health**. Vorel distrusted the gift and would not wear it. **Stanley put it on**, feeling his health strengthen. The Mind Master's public dealings with Tobacho and these escorts were unmistakable; whether he had ordered the earlier inn attack or Kanja's death was still unproved. With the gift stowed on Stanley's person, the ship went to the launching rails.[^arc07-37]

An immense **pink-purple gate** flared open. Traveler's Twin passed through and dropped into **real open sky** before its engines caught the hull. Beneath them spread the **Outlands**—distant mountains, colored forests and plains, and a colossal central spire whose summit seemed to hold Sigil. The captain welcomed the travelers to the wider planes, then showed **Stanley** the besieged **White Tower** through a telescope. Black ranks crowded the earth below; trebuchets struck its pale sides. The bishop had spoken of a siege, and at least this part of his report was true. The captain gave them a **sending stone** for requesting extraction after their drop. No one could verify from that distant view whether the Fist lay inside, nor whether the wards would permit all four friends to enter.[^arc07-38]

The attack came before their planned jump. **Grey winged warriors and two wyverns** swarmed the ship, smashing into its decks and throwing gnome sailors into the air. **Nash became a young black dragon** and flew against the boarders, his breath spilling acid through the attackers. **Stanley turned one wyvern into a little rat**, which fell out of sight; whether it survived the fall was unknown. **Vorel fought with Shatter**, the sword loaned to him by Jelver, while **Hope fired Scully** across the heaving rails. A blow knocked her over the stern, leaving her hanging by one bloodied hand. When another attacker slashed that hand, **she fell**. Nash saw her and **caught her in dragon form**. She climbed onto his back and resumed shooting as they flew around the wounded vessel. A wild shot even struck Nash before her next shot killed an enemy.[^arc07-39]

Belowdecks, a wyvern broke through timbers into the crew's quarters. **Vorel followed**, still raging, and killed it with Shatter despite its venomous sting and tearing bites. The defenders cleared the immediate swarm. But the ship **continued to shudder**, grey smoke thickening behind its engines as the bow began to fall. The wyverns' assault had damaged her severely; nothing proved Nash's own acid was the sole cause. The captain struggled to keep her aloft. At the end of the fight **Traveler's Twin was descending dangerously**, not yet a confirmed wreck. The White Tower still stood within sight, and the company had very little time to decide whether to risk the leap.[^arc07-40]

The next moments brought a decision. With the mast failing and crew still trying to save the ship, **the captain urged the four to leave**. **Nash returned to human form**, and the companions leapt in two pairs, **Stanley holding Hope** and **Nash holding Vorel**. In freefall, the spell readers cast **Dimension Door** from their scrolls. The damaged ship vanished behind a ridge, a plume of smoke rising beyond it. **Stanley, Nash and Vorel emerged inside White Tower**, cramped among cleaning supplies. **Hope did not**. The tower's ward had **thrown her against the outer wall** as the spell worked. She tried to hold on with the magic dagger delivered before departure and with her spiked boots, but slid loose. She struck the ground below, **badly hurt yet alive**. Whatever rules governed the white stone, she had not met them.[^arc07-41]

Winged doglike soldiers with tridents seized Hope and brought her before **General Carthax** of the besieging forces. A smaller creature called **Ulvert** recognized her from the old UBI facility; he claimed that **devils and demons**, usually bitter enemies, had formed a temporary alliance to breach the tower. Hope admitted that she had come for **Zorander's Fist** at Tobacho's direction, but argued for the safe passage of **all four friends to Earth** as the price of any further help. The besiegers wanted her to enter, steal the relic and return it to them. A strange caster **changed Hope's alignment to lawful good**, allowing her to pass through the ward that had rejected her. The alteration was real but **conditional upon her behavior**, not a permanent conversion or a loss of every choice. Ulvert instructed her to act wickedly after acquiring the Fist so the wall would expel her back to the siege. He could not show how her companions would be recovered. **Hope was sent inside with a second, incompatible bargain**.[^arc07-42]

Meanwhile **Stanley** opened the closet door and told the paladins they were reinforcements. **Sir Samson**, another knight and a small robed acolyte called **Walnut** accepted the story only cautiously, especially with a tiefling missing. **Hope appeared in the room**, newly permitted through the tower's defenses. The knights almost attacked her on sight. Vorel supplied a ludicrous claim about a divine child, but **Stanley persuaded them to stand down**. Then **Hope disclosed the pact she had just made with the besiegers**. The knights demanded their weapons. **Nash gave Walnut his strange spellcasting ball**, while **Vorel refused to surrender Shatter**, headbutted Samson and sparked a confused fight in which another paladin accidentally wounded his ally. **Stanley healed the knight** and stood between the combatants. Nash tried to recover his ball, but the acolyte escaped his grasp. None of the companions or paladins died in the struggle, and the four eventually submitted to judgment.[^arc07-43]

The **Knight Commander** sat beside a **hexagonal chest** that she said held Zorander's Fist. She understood that the strangers had entered with an intention to steal it, and now Hope admitted a second promise to the tower's enemies. **She pronounced a death sentence over all four**. Before it could be carried out, **Stanley proposed a different use for their skills**: escort the relic to **Mount Celestia**, the safe destination the paladins themselves desired, rather than take it for Tobacho. The commander granted **a stay of execution**, contingent on finding a workable plan and with her defenders still entitled to accompany the relic. She returned their confiscated belongings, **including Nash's ball and Vorel's Shatter**. She examined **Thrun's genuine pendant key**, then gave it **back to Stanley**, insisting it remain separate from the **still-closed chest** in her care. Stories of the Fist's terrible power were her account; no one opened the box or tested the hand within.[^arc07-44]

The sending stone came alive with the captain's voice. **She had survived a bad landing**, but **Traveler's Twin would need about a week of repairs** before carrying anyone away. Extraction by airship was no immediate solution. **Walnut** led the companions to quarters and a meal served by **Gregor**, the tower's cook. Behind closed doors the four argued over the promises they had made. **Vorel** wanted their road home and feared Tobacho's retaliation. **Stanley** would not murder the paladins for a prize he had agreed to carry into their sanctuary. **Nash** trusted Hope but longed for his family. **Hope's altered sense of lawful duty** made a simple theft harder even while the besiegers' demands weighed on her. Stanley left **the medallion in his bag**, not in the Fist's chest, and retired. Hope heard **a private voice pressing her to bring the Fist** to an unnamed master. She could not identify its speaker and received no answer to her silent reply. **No one had yet stolen the gauntlet, escaped the siege, delivered it to heaven or found a way home**.[^arc07-45]

## V. A cure in a besieged tower

The walls of **White Tower** had admitted the four companions only to hold them within a siege, under a sentence delayed rather than forgiven. By morning, another enemy had crossed those walls. **Hope** woke rested and apparently untouched, while **Stanley, Nash and Vorel** rose feverish, aching and more exhausted than when they had lain down. Nash's distress produced an alarming heat within his body; he locked the results of one especially miserable episode in a spare storage chest, keeping its key apart from every object connected to **Zorander's Fist**. Stanley tried **Lesser Restoration** on himself and Vorel. The spells eased their symptoms but failed to end the illness. They were not merely weary after the flight from the airship. Something was spreading through the tower.[^arc07-46]

The **Knight Commander** was ill too. Her name, **Alentris Vayne**, was finally spoken plainly as she reported dead defenders and entire companies weakened overnight. When she noticed that Hope alone remained well, suspicion turned toward the tiefling who had fallen among the besiegers and been allowed back through a change in her very nature. Hope admitted that a strange caster outside had blown dust into her face while altering her alignment. Alentris ordered her arrested. **Stanley persuaded the commander to place Hope in his custody while they searched for a cure**, not to pardon her or establish her innocence by decree. Nor did the suspected dust prove what had caused the plague. With more people dying, there would be time to apportion blame only if anyone survived.[^arc07-47]

An ancient, nearly blind cleric named **Crudmund Hordweld** listened to the symptoms. He recalled an old battlefield sickness, **Incubatrix**, allegedly borne by people who remained untouched while their victims died and returned as a curse. His account was memory and suspicion, not a diagnosis. He sent the travelers down to the tower library to find his journal. On the stairs a frightened knight begged them to let him escape to his family, but Stanley would not help someone potentially carrying the illness spread it beyond the walls. The lower rooms were crowded with **thick black clouds**. Spells failed to disperse them, and fire vanished into their darkness. A suffering man staggered from one cloud, bled, died, and **became a cloud himself**. There was no need to take Hordweld's entire history on faith to know the danger was real.[^arc07-48]

**Hope walked into the darkness without falling sick**. While the others hesitated, she heard the same unseen voice that had demanded the Fist, this time urging her to abandon her friends and bring the relic to a new master. She did not know who spoke or whether that speaker had created the plague, and her companions could not hear the words. Hope searched through shelves and personal journals, throwing volumes toward the light. Stanley used **Dimension Door** to reach her, and together they found **Musings of Hordweld**. Its diagram gave instructions for a cure to **“shale disease”**—a different name from the one the cleric had told them to seek. The method required **cradling and rocking the patient, words in Celestial, and broth**. Stanley and Hope tried the rocking alone. **It did not cure him**. The journal offered a possibility, not yet a success.[^arc07-49]

Stanley took the pages upstairs while Hope continued looking for more answers. When clouds blocked the stair, the other three used further **Dimension Door** scrolls to get out; Hope remained below, protected from the immediate sickness but still alone with the voice. Hordweld, now close to collapse himself, suggested that **Walnut** could help pronounce the Celestial words. Meanwhile **Vorel found Gregor**, the cook, and began making broth from chicken and vegetables. The apprentice had not yet prepared the words, the soup was not ready, and the last sick had not been healed. White Tower still shook under distant attacks. Their search had produced a recipe while the disease continued to claim its victims.[^arc07-50]

The broth was finally ready, and Walnut made **a phonetic guide to the Celestial incantation** so that Stanley could speak words he did not otherwise know. They went first to **Alentris**, barely strong enough to sit. Stanley held her and rocked her while chanting; Vorel poured broth **over her face**, not into a cup for her to drink. A **black cloud fled her mouth**, flashed blue-white and dissolved. The commander's breathing eased. She embraced Stanley, then **performed the same ritual for him**, and another cloud left its host. Walnut took his turn with **Vorel and Nash**, learning that the broth had to accompany the chanting rather than be omitted. Both men were freed. The cures were witnessed, not merely promised by an old book.[^arc07-51]

The friends and defenders spread the treatment through the tower over the following hours. The fever broke in the remaining sick; the soldiers who could still be saved were **actually cured**. Some had already died and **did not return**. Neither the black mists still lurking below nor the identity of the disease's maker had been fully explained. **Stanley and Alentris**, drawn close by the ordeal and the trust he had shown her, became lovers. She was still commander of a besieged fortress; he was still a traveler desperate for Earth. The intimacy did not settle their conflicting obligations, but it made his promise to protect her people more than a tactic for postponing death.[^arc07-52]

A new voice came through the **sending stone**: **Captain Waltz**, sent by the same interested powers who had supplied Traveler's Twin, spoke of a replacement ship that might arrive near White Tower the next day. He wanted the **Fist** carried aboard as its cargo. The ship was not yet there, and his report that the earlier captain's vessel was missing did not erase **Meart's own message that she lived** and needed repairs. Stanley and Alentris discussed using a vessel to take the **still-closed relic toward Mount Celestia** instead of surrendering it to Tobacho. The commander proposed keeping the **pendant key** apart from the chest, perhaps entrusting it to Walnut, but **no handoff had yet occurred**. Stanley would consult his friends before pledging all four. The new transport was an opportunity, not an escape already secured.[^arc07-53]

For much of the healing Hope was still in the lower library. She found a restricted collection containing a promising **compendium**, but its gate stayed shut; she had **not retrieved the book** when the tower's residents were pronounced cured. The unseen voice returned, accusing her of ruining its plan and warning that the **lawful-good change** which allowed her to remain inside would not last forever. Hope refused its demand to sacrifice her friends. When she finally climbed upstairs, defenders knelt in gratitude for the journal she had helped discover. She later told **Stanley, Nash and Vorel** what she had been hearing, sharing a threat they had not known while she searched alone. The four considered investigating the restricted collection together, and Walnut agreed to help them descend. **The speaker's identity was still unknown**, and Hope had made no pact to hand the Fist over in exchange for her friends' lives.[^arc07-54]

The next expedition reached the lower library **before Walnut joined them**. With the plague's obstructing stair-mist now gone, Hope, Nash and Vorel combined their strength to raise a barred **portcullis**, breaking its restraining mechanism. Hope read the **Monstrous Compendium**, a catalogue of devils, demons and other powers. It offered **no name for the voice**, no entry that identified **Carthax or Ulvert** as her tormentor, and no proof that a particular ruler of hell stood inside the tower. Vorel found no firm history of the Fist in the religious manuscripts. Later he fetched Walnut, whose stories about the relic's terrible power and a journey to Mount Celestia were earnest but uncertain. They **left the books behind**. They had learned the limits of the library rather than found an enemy they could strike.[^arc07-55]

Over breakfast, the companions argued once more about how the Fist should leave. **Vorel** still feared losing Tobacho's promised route home; **Stanley** would not deliver a dangerous weapon to a dragon merely because they had made a bargain; **Hope** begged the others **not to let her touch the gauntlet**, fearing what her private voice might compel. Before the tower's council she heard that voice promise they would meet **that day**, but the words remained a threat, not an appearance. **Alentris formally withdrew the death sentence**, welcoming the four as allies rather than prisoners on reprieve. She had received word of **celestial knights on pegasi** approaching to carry the relic away. At first she considered opening the gates to meet them. **Stanley proposed bringing a small escort to the roof instead**, where teleportation could avoid the army below. **Alentris accepted the rooftop plan**. No rider had yet taken the Fist, the chest remained unopened, and the siege had not ended. The party could only prepare to trust one more promise in a city of broken ones.[^arc07-56]

## VI. The rooftop siege and the impossible moon

The planned rendezvous accelerated when two messages arrived almost together. **Alentris** learned that the celestial riders were **less than an hour away**, while **Captain Waltz** told **Stanley** that his ship was following their flight. The commander expected the main celestial force to draw the besiegers away while a smaller party collected **Zorander's Fist** from the roof. Waltz wanted the relic for the interests behind his ship, and Stanley did not hide that danger from her. He floated the notion of misleading Waltz with a false report of the travelers' deaths. **Alentris refused to tell that lie**. The companions now faced a real choice between a holy escort they could see approaching and a merchant's ship whose promised destination was still unproved. **Neither had received the Fist**.[^arc07-57]

In the tower's deep workshop a red-haired dwarf smith offered the friends weapons from his stores. **Vorel** chose a flame-bearing greatsword and ignited it with the words **“Fangs Inferno”**; the phrase awakened the blade but was not shown to be its formal name. **Stanley** received a **Corpse Slayer dagger**, **Nash** a staff topped with a white-winged ornament, and **Hope** a **Broom of Flying** that lifted when she tested it. Each took a healing potion. In a dusty hidden chamber Nash discovered a **working old teleportation circle** and copied its sigil sequence. He recalled other destinations, including **T'ietkalla**, but **no spell was cast that transported the party to Earth**. Scrolls and spells were passed between companions as they tried to devise a way to lift paladins and a sealed chest above a tower under fire.[^arc07-58]

The **main gate opened**, and White Tower's defenders met the army on the ground. An idea involving **Reverse Gravity** had helped persuade them to try an exterior route, but the spell was **not cast** for that ascent. Instead **Stanley brought Alentris by Dimension Door**, and with her came the **still-closed hexagonal chest**, floating beside its sworn guardian. **Nash carried Vorel through with another scroll**, while Hope rode her new broom into the sky. For a moment all five stood together on the roof. Beyond the battlements, dark ranks and siege machines stretched away. The companions had made it to the rendezvous without giving the Fist to Tobacho or taking it from Alentris. **They had not yet passed it to the celestial riders either**.[^arc07-59]

The first enemies to reach them came from the other direction: **green, bat-winged fliers**, some bearing huge weapons, with a smaller sorcerous commander. **Nash took his young black dragon form**, Vorel's new sword burned, and **Stanley turned one enemy mount into a mouse** that dropped from the air. Hope fired from her broom. Stanley used **Telekinesis** to wrench attackers down against the roof until their bodies burst on impact. The defenders of the tower fought below as great stones and spells passed overhead. Nash's acid failed to harm two of the enemies he breathed upon, so he turned to teeth, claws and magic. He **banished one opponent**, holding it away while the fight spread along the parapet. **Vorel caught a flier by the leg with a rope**, dangling beneath the creature as it tried to carry him off.[^arc07-60]

As the celestial pegasi drew nearer, **Hope** rose to help Vorel and **Stanley** struck at another attacker. Nash, still in dragon form, chose to cast **Enlarge on Vorel**. The new spell **ended Nash's concentration on Banishment**. The banished enemy returned at the very point where Nash's great black body now occupied the air. Their forms **intersected grotesquely**; the fiend was neither killed nor safely expelled by the overlap. The creature's wings and limbs seemed trapped in Nash's flesh. **The battle ended that moment unresolved**, with Alentris still guarding the unopened chest and the celestial couriers close but not yet entrusted with it.[^arc07-61]

The next movement of the fight revealed that **both Nash and the fiend had survived**, bound into a horrifying living conjunction. The hostile creature projected sideways from the dragon's torso, still able to strike. Nash could carry it wherever he flew, but not shrug it off. A smaller winged enemy cast a **Fire Storm** across the roof, burning the defenders. At last the **pegasi-mounted celestial knights** entered the struggle. Their leader, **Knight Commander Thayle**, reached Alentris and **took custody of the closed floating chest**. For a brief, hopeful instant the transfer the paladins had promised was real. But it had happened only above a battlefield, not at Mount Celestia or safely beyond the attackers' reach.[^arc07-62]

A second enemy commander rode a **black flaming horse** and cast a compulsion that sent those who failed to resist moving **south**. **Vorel and several celestial knights**, including Thayle, were caught in its pull. Still fighting and still clinging to a rope, Vorel leapt onto the celestial's Pegasus and **kicked Thayle from the saddle**. **The chest dropped onto the tower roof**, not into the depths below. **Stanley cast Feather Fall on the falling commander**; she was not confirmed dead. The spell's hold on Vorel later broke as the fliers attacked and disrupted the coerced procession. There were real deaths among mounts and riders, but the Fist remained **sealed in its box**, and no side could claim a completed delivery.[^arc07-63]

**Alentris recovered the chest** and mounted an unoccupied Pegasus, trying to fly it away. Above and around her, Hope picked out the real enemy from false winged copies and **shot it dead**. Stanley drew another sorcerous attacker down to the roof with **Telekinesis**. Nash fought desperately against the thing fused to his body, then slammed past a battlement so hard that **the fiend's head tore away**. The headless torso **remained attached and moved**, an injury to the opponent rather than a separation from Nash. Strange surges of magic distorted the battle. Nash became **convinced he would someday drown in a painting**, a conviction planted by magic rather than a death witnessed or a prophecy already fulfilled. Elsewhere enormous figures doubled in apparent size, and a wall of fire closed around Stanley until Vorel leapt through it to help him.[^arc07-64]

The battle fractured into falls and desperate rescues. **Stanley and Vorel** used magic to reach the black horse and its rider, then fell safely under **Feather Fall** when their foothold vanished. Nash attempted to banish another enemy while the headless torso still hung from him. Above them, **Alentris's Pegasus was struck down by arrows** as she carried the Fist away from the roof. **She and the still-closed chest fell outside the tower**, beyond Stanley's immediate reach. They were falling at the end of that moment; **neither Alentris's death nor the recovery of the chest had been witnessed**. The holy escort had failed to depart with its burden. As if the sky itself could bear no more of Nash's unstable magic, daylight vanished. **A vast moon appeared unnaturally close**, a **city visible upon its surface**. It was there—no illusion had been established—but no one knew its name or origin. The companions remained in the besieged tower's air, under an impossible new night, with their commander and the Fist still falling.[^arc07-65]

## VII. The Fist over the battlements

Under the impossibly close **moon**, White Tower's battle continued without offering anyone time to understand the city shining on its face. **Alentris** was still falling outside the tower, and the **closed chest of Zorander's Fist** had dropped with her. **Vorel** lunged at a huge winged fiend, only to lose his grip when it vanished from his teeth. A second giant seized **Hope** and lifted her from her flying broom. She sent the broom toward the falling commander, but it could not reach her in time. Then **three airships emerged from the clouds**—one large vessel flanked by smaller escorts. Armed figures descended from them into the fighting. Nothing yet identified the newcomers as Tobacho's crew, Waltz's promised help or allies of the celestials. Their arrival meant another armed force wanted something in the tower's keeping.[^arc07-66]

**Stanley** refused to let Alentris disappear below the walls. He used **Dimension Door** to reach the air close to the ground, found her still falling, and cast **Feather Fall** upon them both. She landed alive. Above, the winged creatures drove **Nash** from his black-dragon form with accumulated wounds. As he became human again, **the decapitated fiend's torso tore free** of his body and fell away. The strange union had not become permanent, though its physical consequences had been real. Nash slowed his own descent by the enchanted flight still woven into his rebuilt body. At the tower's base **Alentris recovered the unopened chest**, intact and hovering where it had fallen. She clutched it and urged Stanley toward safety, but the air remained crowded with hostile figures.[^arc07-67]

Hope twisted out of the giant's grasp on a second attempt and fell hard enough to leave her badly hurt, though alive. Stanley drank the healing potion he had received from the dwarf smith; it was now spent. The people descending from the airships reached the ground: **two orcs, a robed spellcaster and a towering minotaur with four arms and four swords**. Their leader demanded the chest in exchange for the defenders' lives. A green winged caster sent **fear** through the melee, briefly forcing Alentris to let go of the relic. Stanley held his ground while Nash hid himself with **Greater Invisibility** and struck an enormous enemy with cold magic. The arrivals were attacking the keepers of the Fist, but whether they acted for Tobacho or some other power remained unknown.[^arc07-68]

Stanley regained the chest and took Alentris by **Dimension Door** back to the roof. For a moment, the relic and its commander were again within White Tower's battlements, as they had been before the fall. The defenders fought to hold that height. **Vorel's Flame Tongue** felled a gigantic winged attacker; Stanley smashed another against the stone with **Telekinesis** and healed Hope with a word. Nash's **Fireballs** tore through false doubles and real enemies alike, though some survived the flames. Hope fired into one giant flier's head, then finished it with another shot. Alentris, wounded and exhausted, urged retreat. The celestial extraction had failed, and every attempt to keep the Fist on the roof was drawing yet more enemies to it.[^arc07-69]

The four-armed minotaur climbed onto the battlements with the hooded caster and an orc. **Nash abandoned his invisibility to Haste Vorel**, whose wounds were still bleeding. Hope killed one of the attackers with her gun. Stanley broke a hostile **Reverse Gravity** spell, but the minotaur struck Alentris until she fell **unconscious**. He seized the **still-closed chest** and leapt over the parapet with it. For the moment, the enemy had taken the very prize the paladins had hoped to deliver to heaven. The companions followed into the air rather than concede it. Another caster's **Mass Suggestion** made Hope and Nash cling to their friends; Vorel carried them over the edge while Stanley sheltered the descent with **Feather Fall**. Stanley pulled unconscious Alentris toward him and followed. No one had reached the safety of the tower's interior.[^arc07-70]

The falling battle reversed direction as the hooded mage unfurled a scroll of **Reverse Gravity**. Combatants rose again in tangled groups, still protected from the ordinary plunge by Feather Fall. **Vorel struck the caster** while Hope and Nash held on. His hood fell, exposing scarred red skin and **gold teeth marked with little skulls**. Hope recognized **Spatulo**, the informant at **Daddy Long Legs** who had sent her toward Tobacho's lair—and whom Tobacho had called an enemy. **Only Hope had met him there**. Vorel forced a grip across the tiefling's mouth, frustrating further spoken magic, and the spell's hold broke. They resumed their slow fall amid demons and armed strangers. Spatulo's appearance explained who one combatant was, not why he had joined the fight or who ultimately commanded his companions.[^arc07-71]

Stanley drew **Alentris** back from unconsciousness with **Healing Word**, but her reprieve did not last through the chaos. Nash saw no useful way to invoke a teleportation circle while drifting amid enemies, and instead used **Thunder Step** to carry **Hope** down to the ground-level approach to White Tower. The burst hurt combatants nearby, including some it was not intended to strike. Stanley and Alentris were left in the falling group. A winged caster unleashed another **Fire Storm**, and **both of them fell unconscious**. Vorel survived, bleeding and still moving with Nash's Haste, while the besieged garrison shouted for those outside to get behind the walls. The commander who had been saved twice was again in immediate danger, and the Fist was still contested in the open air.[^arc07-72]

The minotaur became occupied fighting the fiends, and the levitating chest floated close enough for **Vorel** to reach. With no time to ask permission, he released Spatulo and searched **Stanley's unconscious belongings**. He found the **genuine pendant** stolen from High Cleric Thrun and carried throughout their approach to White Tower. Vorel pressed it into the chest's receptacle. The **key snapped into place**, and its luminous surfaces unfolded. Inside lay **Zorander's Fist**, not empty but fitted around **a severed hand**. Vorel lifted the entire gauntlet with that hand still inside. He **did not wear it**, separate the hand from it, or give it to the minotaur. The chest remained **open**, its pendant key seated in the mechanism. For the first time, the artifact all these powers had fought over was exposed in a companion's hands.[^arc07-73]

Something reached toward **Hope's thoughts** as she became aware of Vorel carrying the prize. An unseen voice invited her to meet it at last. The summons was **hers alone**, unheard by her companions; it did not establish that everyone knew the speaker or understood its plan. **Vorel** felt a different pressure from the object in his own grasp, **dark whispers** that pulled at his judgment without proving that he had yielded. Stanley and Alentris remained unconscious amid the fall; Nash and Hope had reached the approach to the tower. Demons, the minotaur and the newly revealed tiefling caster still fought around them. The Fist lay open to the air, its promise and its danger no longer separated by enchanted panels. **Nothing in that moment had decided who would wear its hand—or what it might demand**.[^arc07-74]

## VIII. The Hand and the parting road

There were stories about **Zorander's Fist** that made the glittering gauntlet more terrible than an ordinary treasure. One history told of **Zorander**, a holy warrior who had begged **Euphemius** for strength against an invading army, failed, and later returned from an encounter with a mysterious voice bearing a changed Fist. He won his war and turned against those who had once been his own. Centuries later the relic was said to have resurfaced, bringing new conflicts wherever it passed. The names of far worlds and the account of Zorander's motives had become uncertain in the telling. These were **claims about the past**, not things the four companions had witnessed. What they could see now was a severed hand housed in a gauntlet and an army willing to kill for it.[^arc07-75]

**Hope and Nash** stood near the entrance to White Tower. Above them, **Vorel** held the opened Fist while **Stanley and Alentris** drifted unconscious under Feather Fall. Hope sent her **flying broom** to push the two downward, bringing them almost within reach of the ground. At the edge of death, **Stanley opened his eyes**. He had survived the blow that felled him, and with his first breath of strength he cast **Healing Word on Alentris**. She too awoke. He carried her inside, where defenders still held a precarious refuge from the siege. Their survival did not end the battle or place the relic in safekeeping. Hope could see the friends she had nearly lost and the Fist still suspended beyond the threshold.[^arc07-76]

A mounted fiend struck **Vorel's mind** and left him stunned. The **gauntlet slipped from his hands**. Hope sent her broom beneath it and commanded the broom to bear it through White Tower's entrance, but **the tower's ward rejected the loose relic**. The magic that had admitted the friends would not admit the exposed Fist. Hope dodged beneath the minotaur, was wounded again, and **caught the gauntlet herself**. The voice that had troubled her since her capture among the besiegers spoke where no one else could hear. It instructed her to **discard her own right hand and join the severed stump within the Fist to her arm**. Whatever spoke knew the object better than Hope did. She could not ask the others to judge words they had not heard.[^arc07-77]

At the threshold Hope made the choice. She set the Fist beneath her foot, drew her dagger and **cut away her own right hand and part of the forearm**. Pain and blood overwhelmed her; she fell to the ground. But she reached again, guiding the exposed stump to the place where her hand had been. **Flesh joined flesh**. A violent shockwave flung combatants away, and the torn places in her body closed. She rose restored in strength, with the **Hand of the Voice** now part of her arm. It was no longer merely a severed thing enclosed in an enchanted glove. The act had happened, and there was no pretending it was only a threat from an unseen patron.[^arc07-78]

For Hope, the battle seemed to stop. In a stillness beyond the noise, a **hooded figure whose face remained hidden** told her she was its **champion** and asked what she wanted most. **She answered that she wanted to become a god**. The words were private to her. Vorel glimpsed the stranger only as a misty presence, yet saw it perform something unmistakable: the cavity left by the lost **Eye of Groxl** was filled again. **The Eye returned to Vorel's chest**. The figure loosened the **gauntlet** from Hope's newly joined arm, leaving the **Hand** attached but the **Fist's outer casing separate**. It had restored one companion's missing power while binding another to something no one fully understood. Nash and Stanley had not heard the bargain Hope was offered.[^arc07-79]

Hope's body changed. Her skin grew cold; her horns became more pronounced, her hair turned pale, and old scars traced unfamiliar lines upon her. The Hand lent her new strength and dreadful magic, though the price and limits of its power remained uncertain. She raised it against a green winged fiend and cast **Finger of Death**. The creature died, then **rose again under her command**. Hope climbed onto its back and rode above the fighting. The surrounding demonic ranks began to avoid her rather than rush to strike. The transformation had won her fearsome authority on this battlefield, but it had not answered whether she could refuse the patron who had brought it about.[^arc07-80]

The others tried to salvage something from the disaster. **Nash** poured a healing potion into **Vorel**, easing the bleeding that had threatened him through the long fight. Vorel recovered the **separate Fist gauntlet**, looked at the unsettling, toothlike growths inside it and **placed it back in the floating, open chest**. He tried to close the mechanism with a kick, but it **sprang open again**. The **Hand of the Voice was no longer inside**; it belonged to Hope's living arm. Vorel could now summon the **Eye of Groxl's blade** again, proof that the hooded being's restoration had been real. The paladins watched Hope with alarm. Even the prize Tobacho had ordered them to bring back was no longer the same object the party had been sent to steal.[^arc07-81]

The figure called to Hope again, beyond the hearing of the others, commanding her to leave White Tower and walk into the hostile army. It allowed her to take companions, while warning that those left behind might be absent from her life for a long time. **Hope chose to go without Stanley, Nash or Vorel**. She looked back at them, told Vorel to return to the tower, and stepped into a path opening among the lesser demons.
