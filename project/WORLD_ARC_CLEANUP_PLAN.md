## 1. Goal and non-goals

Make `/world` a **compact, cross-linked, subject-centered encyclopedia** where recurring people, places, factions, artifacts and major events can be understood in the order they develop **through each completed story arc**. Long accumulated episode additions should be moved into the appropriate chronological section, condensed without silently losing real changes, and cross-linked to evidence. Readers should first meet the subject **as it was broadly presented at introduction**, then follow meaningful changes chronologically through the arcs, and only at the **end** see the latest known Season One status and unresolved questions. Do not disclose later revelations or final outcomes in the introductory description.

**New presentation directive (D-037):** [World narrative style](WORLD_NARRATIVE_STYLE.md) now governs ordinary `/world` main text: **in-universe narrative prose**, introduction-time viewpoint, chronological arc development and latest status last. **Episode numbers, transcripts/ASR uncertainty, exact source links and research commentary go in Markdown footnotes**, with naturally attributed in-world claims retained. Specialized registers (Nash's spellcasting, Vorel's titles) and functional index/ledger/timeline/schema pages are purpose-based exceptions. **Nash and Elhandar have completed their follow-up in-universe presentation passes** (see [migration checkpoints](WORLD_ARC_MIGRATION_MANIFEST.md)); **Eltanfawr remains the only second-pass pilot pending**. No bulk conversion is claimed.

**Keep the existing top-level subject taxonomy**—`characters/`, `places/`, `technology/`, thematic indexes, historical studies and one main page for each subject. **Do not create `world/arc-one/` through `world/arc-five/` trees** or duplicate a biography for every arc. Arc is an **internal ordering attribute**, not a new competing owner of identities. Major creature-category research in [WORLD_CREATURES_PLAN.md](WORLD_CREATURES_PLAN.md) is independent and must not be swept into this cleanup or silently published.

This pass is **not** new ASR analysis, audio verification, a reorganization of `episodes/`, a rewrite of `narrative/`, wholesale entity/schema migration, or a license to invent connections. **Season Two is not yet sourced**; do not add future events or empty placeholders for it. Avoid forcing every short page to have five sections.

## 3. Reader-facing page contract — in-universe prose with source footnotes

1. **Narrative opening:** Introduce the subject naturally, as first encountered. Put the first episode, page spoiler horizon, source quality, ASR alternatives and editorial metadata **in footnotes**, not the main text or an opening banner.
2. **Introduction — first encounter:** Describe the subject's broad nature, role, location and condition when originally encountered. Do not front-load future deaths, revelations, transfers or transformations. Mark the relevant claim with a **Markdown footnote reference** to the introductory dossier.
3. **Development by story arc:** Use chronological, **episode-number-free** headings such as `### Arc One — The early journey`. Only include arcs with material changes. Tell the story in natural prose; keep source episodes, ASR disputes, mechanics and chronology corrections **in footnotes**. Retain each character's knowledge boundaries and place revelations when they occur.
4. **Enduring reference (optional):** Add relationships, factions and powers not already clear from the story, before current status. Distinguish actual uses from alleged abilities in-universe. Keep **retrospective cast/GM claims and exact item mechanics in footnotes**, not a separate out-of-world narrative section.
5. **Current status last:** Finish with `## Current status`, narrating the latest known in-world fate, whereabouts and relevant unresolved story questions. Exact episode cutoffs, source disagreements and review-queue links belong **in footnote definitions beneath this final substantive section**, not in a new `## Notes` section.
6. **Links and exceptions:** Preserve canonical slugs and incoming anchors. Source-keyed research registers such as **Nash's spells** and **Vorel's titles**, ledgers, timelines, indexes and technical schemas retain essential episode/cue metadata in their bodies. Follow the binding [world narrative directive](WORLD_NARRATIVE_STYLE.md).

**Additional character-only directive (D-038):** [World narrative style](WORLD_NARRATIVE_STYLE.md) now treats recurring-character pages as **studies of personality, motivations, attributes, relationships, loyalties, revelations and changes of identity**, not abbreviated event chronologies. In each substantive arc, explain **who the character is becoming and why**, while referencing battles and journeys only as evidence. **Balance similar-impact arc sections** using a flexible ~150–300 main-prose word planning range and review those exceeding ~2× the median of similarly substantive sections. Do not apply this range mechanically to minor appearances, short entries, location histories, item reference pages or specialist registers. Preserve genuine character complexities, in-world knowledge boundaries and all provenance in footnotes.

**All-domain subject-focus directive (D-039):** Extend D-038's principle to **places, artifacts/technology, factions and powers, historical events and thematic setting essays**, changing the questions rather than copying the character biography length target. Places emphasize setting character, residents, social and physical evolution; items emphasize identity, ownership and capabilities; factions emphasize purpose, leadership and alliances; histories emphasize causes, transformations and legacy; thematic pages emphasize how the world works. **Balance comparable significant changes inside each subject**, reducing retold adventure scenes and repeated episode chronology, but **do not impose 150–300 words on non-character pages**. Short entries, quiet arcs and one-off events need not be padded. Follow the [domain-specific checklist](WORLD_REFERENCE_PAGE_TEMPLATE.md) and [world style directive](WORLD_NARRATIVE_STYLE.md).

**Page size is not the organizing principle alone.** An entry with no meaningful arc progression may remain short. When a long page blends several *different* subjects, consider splitting along **subject identity** (not arc) and leaving a stable redirect-style summary at the old path, per [Stanley/Diontalus precedent](../world/characters/stanley-diontalus.md).

## 6. Non-negotiable regression examples

1. **Arc boundary E101:** Hope exits White Tower *within* E101; both Arc Four and Arc Five may cite the episode, but opposite parts of its played event clock.
2. **Two deaths and a return:** Squirts is killed **E111**, separately embodied under Brent in **E125 Hell epilogue**; E128 “Squirts in tow” remains **a disputed roster statement**, not silently corrected to Chunks.
3. **Diontalus and Stanley:** Stanley's body, Diontalus's presence and subsequent spectral agency are distinct. Diontalus falls motionless **E126**, Stanley **heals him E128**. Henatar falls limp in E126 but **Nash kills him after surrender E128**.
4. **Evidence tier:** The dragon's sealing-device advice and Soulbroker's claims of the Principle's history/Weir's motives are **attributed claims**. The E128 **purple rift, burrower and storm** are witnessed. Stanley's private interview becomes group knowledge **only after he reports it**.
5. **Custody/technology:** The extracted **natural dragon heart** is later found by Elhandari and leaves for Mars; Nash's **engineered heart remains inside him**, and **Ruby is not built**. The spelljammer mask is worn but **not fully attuned**; the villa does not fly in Season One.
6. **Distinct people, items and storylines:** **Hand vs Fist**, **Eye of Voice vs Eye of Groxl**, **Dr. Spider vs Professor Spider**, historical *Dunebreaker* vs Arc Five, and E127 one-off vs E128 finale.
7. **Cutoff semantics:** A later revelation updates a canonical latest-status field, **but does not make the earlier witness/party know the future**. An earlier wrong belief must be contextualized, not deleted as if never spoken.
