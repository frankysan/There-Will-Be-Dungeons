# Arc 10 — Across the Stars

## I. The ship that remembers

Three days passed beneath the wreckage of Elhandar. **Nash, Hope, Stanley and Vorel** remained beside **Villa Or'Nath** on Earth, waiting for the last day of the mask's attunement. Their families and the surviving refugees had flown toward Mars; the enormous burrower that had risen from the desert had not returned. Hope gathered food from the wastes. Vorel buried those killed by the station's fall, grieving Omnom and the people he had left at Razak Val. Nash sought Stanley's company while Stanley shut himself into the rooms his father had hidden. None could say what the cottage would become.[^arc10-1]

A disturbance crossed the dunes. Vorel watched the ground for a predator; Hope took the shape of a brown-scaled female lizardfolk with her appearance necklace. The purple scar above them seemed closed. The figure approaching the house was **Monsoon Cabbage**, a vampire they had met before, and he carried **Softhead** in a cage. The prisoner was alive, hurt and kept as a source of blood. When the vampire asked to enter, Stanley denied him an invitation. Monsoon could speak to the companions across the threshold but could not cross it himself.[^arc10-2]

Monsoon raised a **book-and-quill crest** Stanley remembered from his family. Stanley allowed him inside. The visitor said he had once traveled with **Delit Or'Nath**, **Groxl**, another Nash and the man now called **the Principal**. He told an uncertain history of an ancient fellowship, a war against the Weir and the Principal's theft of **Diontalus's** secrets. According to Monsoon, the Principal had become a lich and survived the explosion of **Principal City**, though the full cost and cause of that ruin remained his account. None of the four had witnessed the supposed transformation.[^arc10-3]

Yet the vampire proposed a tangible service. He sensed, he said, that **Stanley alone** still owed a soul-bargain to the **Soulbroker** through the Weir. He could draw the curse out without removing the benefits it had granted. Stanley asked his friends to share the risk. Hope and Nash consented; Vorel agreed with distrust. They ate together before the ritual, mixing salt, ink, blueberries, a lock of Stanley's hair and stranger ingredients. Nash drew the protective signs and repeated Infernal words supplied by Monsoon with Hope's help. Vorel felt the **Eye of Groxl** recognize power in the visitor, but that sensation offered no guarantee of good faith.[^arc10-4]

**Hope cut a rectangular wound high on Stanley's back**. The ointment burned as Monsoon called the thing within him into the open. A smoky, struggling shape emerged and immediately tried to crawl back into Stanley. Hope's cold dagger and Vorel's shield drove at it while Stanley fought through the pain. Nash's first icy spell missed. Even the companions' devotion did not spare them error: Hope's weapon struck Vorel by accident, and his shield caught her in turn. The old curse had become an enemy that could fight back.[^arc10-5]

The thing swelled into a many-limbed horror with an **antimagic maw** and smaller one-eyed demons around it. **Vorel threw Softhead's occupied cage into its mouth**, blocking the cone long enough for the others to act; the trapped man remained alive. Stanley shook the room with a destructive wave, Nash found his mark with cold magic, and **Hope drove her blade into the curseborn's neck**. It disintegrated. The briefly visible face of the **Soulbroker** threatened revenge, then vanished with the demons. The real Soulbroker had not been killed in the fight.[^arc10-6]

A judgment stronger than Monsoon's story followed the victory: **Stanley's remaining Weir soul obligation was gone, while the powers of his original bargain remained**. For the first time in years, he could ask whether he wanted to follow the others without a debt deciding for him. Monsoon left with **Softhead still caged**, speaking as he went of the **Solar Mines** as a possible route to the hottest depths of Earth for the unbuilt **Rubistic Triangulosphere**. It was only a lead. Nash knew that name too well to take comfort from it, and the Weir's threat remained beyond the cottage.[^arc10-7]

On the **fourth day**, Villa Or'Nath changed. Great wooden spars rose from the sand; sails and a new deck unfolded around the surviving house. Spectral elves took their places aboard a magnificent airship with the cottage woven into its stern. A tall, peg-legged ghost approached Stanley and called him **nephew**. The vessel had awakened above the wreckage of Elhandar. It had not yet flown away, been named or revealed the sailor's identity to its astonished passengers.[^arc10-8]

The peg-legged sailor introduced himself as **Sever**, Stanley's uncle and the ship's first mate. He showed the friends an elaborate crew of dead elves who tended guns, ropes, supplies and helm. Ghostly **Crooked Hand Row** steered; **Doctor Daffodil** had a medical room; cooks and riggers answered the new captain. **Gorgeous George**, a huge one-eyed shipmate, was among them. The sailors belonged to the vessel, and while they could handle its mechanisms, living bodies were another matter. **Vorel**, already distrustful of supernatural visitors, found little comfort in a house crewed by obliging spirits.[^arc10-9]

Sever led them down to an impossible wooden chamber beneath the engine. In the wall they found a carved **face resembling Delit Or'Nath**, and it answered Stanley in the voice of his father. Delit said his mortal body had been lost and his remaining life given to the ship to guard his son. He said also that **Stanley's mother had died**. Stanley had heard a voice and seen his father's likeness respond, but not witnessed the old sacrifice or recovered a father in flesh. The encounter was an inheritance too strange to settle his grief.[^arc10-10]

**Stanley offered the companions a choice.** With the Soulbroker's claim broken, he could no longer pretend that any pact compelled them to remain together. Nash wanted Angelashi and their children safe; Hope had survived too many terrors to abandon the struggle; Vorel still feared for **Razak Val**. They agreed to visit the lizardfolk first, then reach **Mars**. Vorel joked that their succession of vessels called for the name **Fate Six**. They adopted it in conversation but did not yet proclaim it before the ghostly crew. Their voyage would be a decision made freely rather than one more command handed down by a patron.[^arc10-11]

With **Crooked Hand Row** guiding the controls, the vessel made its **first true flight**. From above the ruined plains the companions glimpsed **T'ietkalla**, damaged but still standing. Its streets looked quiet from the sky; they did not land to learn who remained alive. They descended instead at **Razak Val**. The canyon was battered, yet lizardfolk emerged from its shelters and recognized **Vorel as Great Fang**. A wounded warrior described enemies who had abruptly died in the earlier fighting. It was his report, not a survey of every hostile creature on Earth.[^arc10-12]

More people gathered than had first appeared beneath the ship. **Orp's bones** marked one of their losses, but Vorel's kin were not gone. When their leader prepared to leave, they asked whether they might follow. Vorel consented, and a sizeable company **actually boarded** the haunted ship. The exact number was not counted. The wounded survivors were not a restored nation, but they would not be left entirely to face the next catastrophe alone.[^arc10-13]

The airship climbed past the last breathable sky under an enchantment that held the vacuum away. The lizardfolk stared into the void in fear and wonder. Sever explained that the helm knew only a few destinations, among them **Earth, Mars and Planet Nash**. No rumored giant space hamster appeared merely because the sailors told stories. The ship crossed interplanetary space in roughly an hour and descended toward a green pocket of life on the red planet. Within the **Elhandari terraforming refuge**, it landed among trees and unfamiliar wet earth. Here, before the cheering crew, **Stanley formally christened it Fate Six**.[^arc10-14]

**Amanthalodar**, the High Elf, and Admiral **Chrysorius** welcomed them. Vorel's followers might find shelter under Elhandari protection; no promised new habitat was yet finished. **Nash embraced Angelashi and their four children**, alive together after the evacuation. She feared that Mars could be caught in the same war and Nash told her of **Planet Nash** as a possible refuge, a proposal rather than a journey they had made. The companions still had one urgent task: find **Tungsten Wheel**, who might finish the machine meant to heal Earth.[^arc10-15]

They rode through the Martian jungle to **Dunglestar Ravensbread's Theatre of Mortal Bodies**. Strange anatomical exhibits surrounded an eccentric healer who spoke of **Tungsten Wheel** as a lover long absent. Dunglestar's grief and bitterness made his young assistant **Twaddle** miserable, but Stanley and Vorel won their way into the engineer's abandoned workshop. There hung a vast, intricate and **unfinished Rubistic Triangulosphere**. Something called **Gluptwarp**, or a name near it in the surviving record, was missing from its working parts. No one had found the engineer, started the device or proved that it could yet close Earth's wound.[^arc10-16]

Dunglestar showed them a message attributed to Tungsten, delivered by a **laser pigeon**. It suggested **Blast Belly Rock** and a possible material lead at **Pentel Weiss**, with glyphs resembling the ship's navigation controls. Resemblance was not a journey already planned or tested. **Twaddle** wrote a farewell and went with the four toward the elder tree, considering work as a living helper to their ghostly surgeon. He had not yet boarded Fate Six. Beneath great branches, the companions joined a gathering facing a map of Earth. The **council opened**, but its war strategy would be decided only when the meeting continued.[^arc10-17]

## II. Council and many Nashes

The **Council of Earthly Defenders** assembled beneath Mars's towering elder tree. The **Nameless One** represented the Liberators; **Nobieri** waited among the survivors, and **Bok Bok** spoke with a confidence and Common speech he had lacked on Earth. A new cyclops, **Hargomoon**, stood beside birdfolk nobles and the Elhandari leaders, not to be mistaken for dead Orp. Vorel proclaimed his greatness with the ceremony of a man who never needed a herald. At the rear, **Monsoon Cabbage** slipped in under a hood. Stanley noticed him before the vampire declared his presence to the room.[^arc10-18]

**Amanthalodar** spoke of the Weir and the duty for which his people had studied Earth since before the Scorching. His account credited **Delit Or'Nath** with a remedy and a **future Nash** who had traveled by strange adjacencies to help him. The present Nash had no memory of the alleged journey. Yet the council could see the diagram before them: a device to be carried to **Earth's core** and set against the corruption beneath its surface. The machine existed unfinished on Mars. The secret history of its design was testimony; finding a way into the planet was their immediate problem.[^arc10-19]

The admiral named three possible ways downward: the **Firelands**, the Black Mine and the **Solar Mines**. Nash recoiled at the last. He described a childhood spent beneath a system that tested children in clean upper halls, then sent those deemed less valuable into forced labor below. Crowded miners dug dangerous material under the gaze of enormous metal **Slow Peters**. He remembered collapses, heat, an obscured ruler called **the Emperor With No Name**, and his escape inside a hollowed-out construct. Others had died making it possible. The council heard what Nash remembered, not a fresh reconnaissance of who held the mines now.[^arc10-20]

The **Solar Mines** became the council's selected route to the core. Nash considered slipping an infiltration party into a tunnel from behind while a larger force distracted the defenders at their gates. No tunnel had been dug and no assault was under way. The admiral described likely units serving the Principal: **Slow Peters**, soldiers of the **Umbilical Order** and other armored factions. The estimates drew on fears and reports, not a count of a garrison the party had inspected. Nash refused to let their planners forget that **prisoners might still be alive** beneath the machines.[^arc10-21]

Their proposed coalition was a thin one: elves, Vorel's lizardfolk, Liberators, birdfolk and a handful of giants. **Hope refused to summon exhausted Harbor refugees into another war without their choice**. Nobieri offered only his own service. **Bok Bok** proposed challenging the goblin king and returning with thousands of followers if he won; the promise was not an army already assembled. **Monsoon** rose to volunteer as a vampire knight, offering himself rather than a legion of vampires. The hall was making alliances from individual pledges and debts, not from soldiers already standing in formation.[^arc10-22]

Stanley asked them to seek **Diontalus**. The archfey had occupied his body and endangered their friends, and the Elhandari were slow to welcome such a being. Stanley argued that the coming danger would leave no comfortable set of allies from which to choose. The council agreed only to **attempt** obtaining Diontalus's aid. Then the High Elf unveiled the **World Breaker**, presented as a monstrous **Tarrasque changed by the Weir**. The threat was somewhere beneath Earth; no hero had slain it. Saving the planet with a machine, even if that worked, would not necessarily destroy the creature already growing there.[^arc10-23]

They drew routes for dropships and ground transports, talked of false assaults, tunnels and people who might still help from distant towns. **Vorel** urged the Elhandari to approach ordinary Earth settlements by ground vehicle rather than appear above them in unfamiliar spacecraft. **Nash** feared indiscriminate destruction of mine laborers. Stanley considered leading part of the battle from **Fate Six**. Many roles remained undecided; soldiers, material and the engineer were all missing from their plans in different ways. The council dispersed with work to do, not a completed strategy already carried to victory.[^arc10-24]

Before the hall emptied, Amanthalodar announced that the elves had chosen **Stanley — Valiir Or'Nath** — as their new **Selle Prime**. The exact office name was indistinct in the testimony that survived, but the choice was not: their long-lost kinsman, a half-elf, would lead them. Stanley accepted. The roots and living branches of the elder tree closed about him, leaving the others watching the leaves. Whatever words were spoken inside were not addressed to them.[^arc10-25]

Within the tree Stanley heard a private warning: **Nash was necessary, yet Nash must die**. It was the tree's prophecy, not an execution or a bargain Stanley had accepted. When he emerged his appearance was changed—more elven, with silver eyes and an almost unbearable majesty. The Elhandari knelt and he asked them to stand. A young spear-bearing guardian took position by him and said that **Tungsten Wheel was her uncle**. Stanley had gained authority, a dangerous private question and a companion to guard him. He did not share the prophecy with Nash.[^arc10-26]

The new guardian was known as **Union Wheel**. She admitted she did not know Tungsten's location, but vowed to protect Stanley on the search. His changed face could seem less overwhelming when he spoke with his companions; he could not simply discard his new office. **Amanthalodar** agreed to govern the Mars colony while Stanley traveled. Hope and the others saw the awkward distance between the man they knew and the leader hailed by strangers. The ship waiting for them still needed a captain more than a king.[^arc10-27]

At Fate Six **Twaddle actually boarded**, intending to help ghostly **Doctor Daffodil** with living patients a ghost could not touch. **Vorel** constructed an elaborate bed of Martian sand in a barrel, as though he could carry a little familiar ground across the stars. The ship's dead sailors could move its doors, ropes and stores even while their hands could not work directly upon living flesh. The vessel was said to be powered by Delit's continuing life essence. Stanley asked to be addressed as **Captain**, but the ship's name remained the one chosen on Mars.[^arc10-28]

Before they left, **Hope visited Nobieri** among the refugees. He could not tell her who the Voice attached to her Hand truly was. He offered a speculation: a strange soul might endure in an object, and perhaps extraordinary shielding could contain the magic if her Hand were severed. No such operation took place. Hope hugged her friend and promised to return, her dangerous **Hand of the Voice** still alive and attached. Their farewell was a tenderness in a world where too many proposals had become demands.[^arc10-29]

The voyage changed direction before the ship sailed. **Angelashi and all four children came aboard Fate Six**, and the companions chose to bring the family to **Planet Nash** rather than attempt Blast Belly Rock first. The later conversation identified **Nash Junior as a daughter**, in tension with some earlier attempts to assign the newborns' shapes and sexes; a correction could not make the conflicting older descriptions unspoken. Hope entered the known coordinates at the helm, and this time the ship passed into **rift space**. Color and motion swallowed the view. After several hours Hope guided Fate Six through the final passage and **actually landed at Planet Nash**.[^arc10-30]

The **Tungans** greeted the travelers, and **Gorgeous George** answered them with surprising familiarity. At the central tower a stone face of Nash awoke. The living Nash listened while **Tower Nash** recounted a different life: a failed attempt to reach the Solar Mines with only his companions, a strange vortex of Nashes, a new meeting with **Delit Or'Nath**, and the choice to give his old self to the tower. Those were memories the speaker claimed, not adventures the four had lived or a guarantee that their future would follow the same path.[^arc10-31]

The tower presented **seven other Nashes as colored projections**. Their stories contradicted and frightened one another. **White Nash** had lost his ship to the terrible aerial defenses of the Solar Mines. **Green Nash** recalled a failed attempt carrying the Triangulosphere through its guards. **Red Nash** had lost Stanley to a poisoned vegetable in a life the current Stanley had not led. Other Nashes argued for tunnels, dinosaurs and paths not yet tried. Their images could speak and quarrel, but no alternate soldier had physically joined the army. They were warnings from different histories, not verdicts against the companions' own choices.[^arc10-32]

The current Nash told the projections of his wife and children, a distinction none could easily match. **Vorel** summoned projected alternate Vorels and plunged the meeting into boasts of greatness. White Nash described the Principal's heavy defenses in his own world, useful intelligence only if the histories remained alike. No one could promise that they did. The tower withdrew its visions without sending any extra Nashes into the room. **Angelashi agreed to stay with the children and investigate** whether willing versions of her husband could be brought here to aid Earth. It was a task she accepted, not a solution already discovered.[^arc10-33]

Upstairs, Nash found **another Pod** who lived with her pixie husband **Roger** and their children. She embraced him, but the warmth could not undo the loss of the Pod he remembered from Earth. He promised to visit again. Elsewhere **Hope met a scarred alternate Tedna** calling himself **Badass T**. He insisted that she allow him to accompany the coming journey; at last she agreed to a possible future place beside them, not an immediate boarding. He returned **Smokey's belt and its special ammunition** to her hands.[^arc10-34]

As they prepared to decide what came next, **Angelashi** saw the most important mystery in the room. Badass T had reached Planet Nash **from another reality**. If he had crossed between histories, perhaps the tower's projections were not the end of the road. The idea offered a possible means of finding allies, not an army yet summoned. **Nash, Hope, Stanley, Vorel and Union** remained on Planet Nash, with Angelashi and the children still beside them. **Tungsten Wheel was missing**, the device was unfinished and the Solar Mines remained a dreadful destination far ahead.[^arc10-35]

## III. Planet Nash and the old bank

The talk in **Tower Nash** had scarcely turned toward an army of other Nashes when a dispute among cooks demanded Vorel's judgment. A **Tungan** and a blue **Triton** each claimed responsibility for **Vorel's Sandburgers**, and neither would yield the kitchen. Vorel proposed a cook-off. Meanwhile the talking tower admitted that bringing travelers across realities was not work it had done alone. A bright signal flashed into space, summoning someone who might explain how **Badass T** had crossed from a different history.[^arc10-36]

The stranger arrived upon a surfboard of shining white light, green-skinned and flecked with darkness. He offered Nash an orange and gave a name heard roughly as **Zolfin Sol**. He called himself a herald of **Chronos**, a god of time who, he said, was dying. The wounds of Earth threatened all creation, and countless versions of the fellowship had already sought to save it. The herald claimed to have helped Tower Nash draw another Tedna into this reality. The friends could see the stranger and his impossible board; his vast account of time's sickness remained a claim they could not verify.[^arc10-37]

Nash imagined gathering two hundred versions of himself to march upon the Principal. Hope suggested that even Nashes who had already lost their own worlds might still fight for this one. Their visitor cautioned that assembling so many counterparts could carry a danger of its own, and brought none forward. His gaze paused upon **Hope's Hand of the Voice**. In a private exchange, the Voice advised Hope to follow his lead for the moment. The herald called the hidden being a **Lord of Secrets**, an ancient wizard raised to divine power. That was one more troubling explanation, not an identity proved to everyone present.[^arc10-38]

Vorel sampled two extraordinary recipes—one built around seafood, one around cat food, both seasoned with sand. His companions were appalled. He declared neither universal victor and recommended letting customers choose. He would not run the business, though the herald speculated that another Vorel might someday be brought to manage it. Stanley urged restraint about summoning two hundred Nashes without anywhere to house them. **Angelashi** wanted no crowd of alternate fathers around the children. She kissed Nash, went back into the tower and arranged shelter for their family. For all the plans discussed that day, neither an army nor an alternate Vorel had been summoned.[^arc10-39]

**Fate Six** finally left Planet Nash. **Badass T** now boarded for the voyage, alongside the four old companions and **Union Wheel**. Hope steered into rift space for **Blast Belly Rock**. In their conversations the scarred Tedna insisted he was no longer the sheltered youth they remembered; battles and years had made him a man. Later Stanley, Hope, Nash and Vorel shared elven mead in the captain's quarters as ghostly **Gorgeous George** served the cups. They had gained little certainty about the coming war, but could still find a small measure of comfort in traveling together.[^arc10-40]

After a day's passage they docked at the pitted asteroid of **Blast Belly Rock**. Its nearly abandoned mining town was called **Burp**, and its tavern the **Wet Whistle**. Stanley warned Badass T against reckless violence, speaking harshly of the original Tedna's death. The dwarf innkeeper, **Ailboon Hardbottom**, distrusted the strangers until Stanley took up his guitar and enchanted the room with a song of their travels. The keeper remembered an elf who had waited at the tavern decades ago and was said to work at the **Bank of Burp**. He could not swear the elf had been **Tungsten Wheel**. It was an uncertain memory, but enough to guide their next search.[^arc10-41]

Badass T agreed to watch the Wet Whistle while **Nash, Hope, Stanley, Vorel and Union** crossed a deserted district to the bank. A peculiar rock dog watched from outside. Vorel kicked in the locked doors, and Hope tied them shut from within. Pale **yellow oozes** glimmered on the abandoned counters, floor and walls. Nash could not resist touching one, smelling it and finally tasting it. The first contact seemed harmless. Then a blob clung to his boot and took something no thief should have been able to steal.[^arc10-42]

One ooze drained **Nash's intelligence**, acquiring speech as he lost the power to reason. Another stole **his dexterity** and became preternaturally swift. Nash fought through bewilderment, while Hope and Vorel struck the creatures and Stanley's blasts destroyed the clever and quick thieves. They killed the oozes, but nothing flowed back to their wounded friend. At the end of the fight he could hardly explain that the blobs had taken his brain. Their search for Tungsten had brought them to the right bank and left Nash in need of more help than any of them knew how to give.[^arc10-43]

In the rooms beyond the teller cage, Nash remained clumsy and bewildered, though his unusual **Charisma-driven magic** still answered him. He spoke an awkward rhyme he had used before and **reduced Vorel's size**, allowing him to slip through the bars and work the doors from inside. Nash even tasted the charred remains of a dead ooze, hoping to regain his lost wit. It accomplished nothing. The bank's deeper offices held the evidence their original search required, but reaching it now depended on the companions Nash could scarcely follow.[^arc10-44]

A signed contract named **Tungsten Wheel** as a bank teller earning ten gold each week. A ledger indicated his desk, from which Stanley took a small key. Three strange words—**tapioca, iron and plow**—emerged from books and possessions scattered among the offices. **Union Wheel** had found a place where her uncle truly worked in the past. It did not tell her whether he still lived or where he had gone, but the records were stronger than the tavern keeper's uncertain tale.[^arc10-45]

Then another ooze clung to **Stanley's face** and robbed him of **wisdom**. He killed it, but his judgment did not return. Straining to lift a horned statue injured his back; healing the injury changed nothing about the theft. Vorel broke an old celestial globe and uncovered a cord. Pulling it moved the statue aside and revealed a stair descending to the vault. Now two friends suffered invisible losses, and the bank still held its locked secrets.[^arc10-46]

The three words opened an ancient security challenge. Stanley passed the barred gate by magic, entered the treasury and found **deposit box 724**, matching the key from Tungsten's desk. The vault also held tempting gold and jewels. Stanley tried to carry far too much and spilled some amid his efforts. **Hope opened Tungsten's box** and retrieved a parchment of **coordinates for Fate Six**. They had found a real instruction left behind by their missing quarry. They had not discovered where it would lead them or settled exactly how much treasure they could take.[^arc10-47]

Their departure from the bank brought them face to face with **orc marauders** who claimed the asteroid and intended to seize Fate Six. The leader displayed the head of a dead dwarf lookout. Stanley's destructive magic broke through the raiders; Hope moved among them disguised as an orc, and Vorel leaped into the fight. **Nash changed into an adult black dragon**. Within that form his mind was clear again, though his ordinary body remained impaired. He crushed attackers and breathed acid as Vorel jumped from his back and struck down their leader with the **Blade of Groxl**.[^arc10-48]

**Three surviving orcs surrendered**. Stanley accepted them as crew, and they pointed out cages where the raiders had held **Badass T**, the tavern keeper and others. Hope opened the locks and freed them. The townspeople trembled at the great dragon until she called out that Nash was a friend. The company had acquired sailors and rescued prisoners, but the orcs' future aboard a ghost ship was still unwritten.[^arc10-49]

Fate Six remained docked at Blast Belly Rock. In their keeping were the signed evidence of Tungsten's old employment, the key, the coordinate parchment and a chest of uncertain riches. **Tungsten himself was still missing**. Nash's ordinary intelligence and dexterity remained diminished, and Stanley's stolen wisdom had not been restored. They would need the rest of their company, and perhaps the passage of time, before those wounds could be properly understood. Ahead lay an uncharted destination and another danger they could not yet name.[^arc10-50]

## IV. Beyond the nebula

The three orcs who had surrendered in Burp brought treasure aboard **Fate Six**, only to draw their swords when its ghost sailors appeared. Stanley talked them into laying the weapons down. **Badass T** and **Union Wheel** were aboard again, and **Hope** entered the coordinates recovered from Tungsten's deposit box. Crooked Hand Row warned that this destination lay beyond the vessel's familiar routes. The ship left the mining asteroid and passed into rift space, the four companions carrying their injuries and their first real clue toward a prison they had not yet seen.[^arc10-51]

**Nash** kept his dragon shape for a time because his mind worked more clearly inside it. He asked Vorel to help him sleep when he changed back. In private, Vorel told Stanley that the loss of wisdom had made him fearless and perhaps need not be repaired; his view was not an agreement among the party. Belowdecks Stanley and Vorel spent hours gambling with the ghost crew, the stakes coming and going without much profit. Nash tried to rest, confused and miserable, until **Sever** returned him to bed. Even friendship could be careless when none of them understood how to help.[^arc10-52]

**Union Wheel** confronted Stanley over his reckless behavior, and he cast **Lesser Restoration** upon himself. The spell restored the wisdom taken by the bank ooze, although the drunkenness and poor choices of the evening did not immediately disappear. An entire long rest followed. When Nash woke, he found that his ordinary **intelligence and dexterity had returned** as well. His abilities had not come back merely because he killed the thieves, or because his dragon body had remained clever. Here, at last, both men could think again without the bank's magic clouding their minds.[^arc10-53]

They left rift space facing an immense **purple and pink nebula** veined with lightning. Neither planet nor prison could be seen. Stanley sent a **laser pigeon** into the storm with a greeting, but no answer came. **Hope** steered the ship between scattered pockets of safer space as monstrous whales with **three eyes and three mouths** moved through the glowing clouds. They did not seem intent on hunting the ship, yet the lightning that surrounded them struck Fate Six when the great bodies drew near. Their apparent curiosity was dangerous enough.[^arc10-54]

The storm killed the newest sailors. One of the three orcs was battered fatally in the violent passage; **the other two fell overboard and were lost**. All three who had surrendered at Burp were gone before they had made a second landfall. Stanley drove one whale away with a shattering shriek from his megaphone, and the survivors took a brief rest between hazards. The ghosts and the living had no way to retrieve their missing companions from the electricity and empty distance behind them.[^arc10-55]

Hope recalled the strange **jack-in-the-box compass** the clowns had once given her. It pointed toward the northeast through the worst of the nebula, guiding Fate Six until even the whales withdrew from a dark pocket among the clouds. A blue-edged sphere floated there, hardly large enough for a person, with the image of an enormous **black pyramid** distorted within. **Nash** wore a breathing helmet and flew through alone to investigate. In the space beyond, the pyramid towered miles high. He approached, felt the danger and returned rather than enter without his friends.[^arc10-56]

The portal's apparent size was deceptive. **Hope actually piloted Fate Six through the sphere**, the whole vessel bending through its impossible surface before recovering its shape. Crooked Hand Row helped record fresh **return coordinates** aboard the ship, distinct from Tungsten's original directions. They docked at the vast gray pyramid of **Pentel Weiss**. The ghost sailors stayed to guard their way home while **Nash, Hope, Stanley, Vorel, Union Wheel and Badass T** walked into the prison. The engineer might have passed this way, but no one had yet found him.[^arc10-57]

Geometric stone figures lined the passage to a deep circular **intake shaft**. The **Voice** inside Hope's Hand spoke to her alone, describing a prison holding the cosmos's most dangerous inmates. Its account was private testimony, not knowledge the others possessed. A stone keeper named the place **Pentel Weiss** and demanded credentials from authorized **collectors** before it would disclose anything. Hope's use of Tungsten Wheel's name did not make her one. The guardians offered neither an inmate's location nor permission to enter.[^arc10-58]

They asked whether **Badass T** could be treated as a prisoner. He reluctantly supplied a surname heard as **Garfield**, but the keeper found no record of him as a wanted criminal. Hope asked him to try the shaft. With knives ready, he leaped into the darkness and disappeared. No sound answered from below. The five standing on the edge did not know whether their companion had survived or where the passage led. The episode closed with a risk taken, not a death confirmed.[^arc10-59]

Stanley tried to contact Badass T using **Sending**, but the magic failed painfully in his throat and its power was spent. The keeper warned that spells were forbidden. Vorel dropped a burning lantern down the shaft, and Stanley followed with two ordinary swords. The surrounding golems raised their arms and warned against further detritus; none had attacked yet. Rather than force their way past these massive guardians, the five withdrew and began looking for another entrance.[^arc10-60]

**Fate Six** circled the pyramid for hours, searching immense sealed walls. Hope's compass pointed only toward the building itself. When they returned, a gaunt **warden** with gray robes and a scarred eye actually appeared, declared the prison closed and rejected entry without collector credentials. **Hope wondered** whether his damaged eye might matter to the Voice. She did not know that it was any lost artifact; indeed, the boundary between what she had learned privately and what she could plausibly know was uncertain. The warden vanished, leaving the keepers to order the visitors away.[^arc10-61]

The companions changed their clothing and weapons aboard ship. **Vorel** took a plain sword and an officer's coat, **Nash** removed armor ill suited to his magic, and **Stanley** chose studded leather. Hope visited the carved face of **Delit Or'Nath** beneath the ship's engine. The voice said he had helped recruit **Tungsten Wheel** for the Triangulosphere, and knew Pentel Weiss as an ancient and perilous place, but could offer no secret door. When they returned, **Hope's human disguise fell away** as she passed the entrance. The suppression of magic was real; it did not render every enchanted object useless.[^arc10-62]

This time **all five** jumped into the shaft. They fell through winding darkness and struck a pool of icy water. Nash caught the glowing peel he had enchanted before entering; its old light still shone dimly even when new spells failed. A gigantic creature with tentacles, many eyes and a circular toothed mouth rose to meet them. **Vorel's plain blade could not injure it**. Stanley's blasts, Nash's new offensive spell and Hope's attempt at darkness failed as well. But the flames of Vorel's **rage** still burned, and Nash could still float through the water. The prison's prohibitions were powerful, not absolute in every form.[^arc10-63]

A shout came from a ledge. **Badass T was alive**, surrounded by a halfling and other prisoners who threw down a rope. Vorel borrowed Stanley's magical blade **Diplomacy**, which could pierce the creature's hide where his plain sword had failed. The monster **swallowed Nash**. **Hope** climbed toward its teeth, caught hold of him and pulled him out alive. Vorel fought his way back through the water while the people above hauled on the ropes. They escaped together, leaving the creature undefeated beneath the surface. Vorel returned Diplomacy to Stanley.[^arc10-64]

Their rescuer, a talkative halfling whose name sounded like **Zipty**, led them to a ragged camp. Its residents claimed they had been wrongly imprisoned, had lived for ages without aging and needed no food. Yet they eyed the heroes' provisions with unmistakable hunger, discarding bread and vegetables while reaching eagerly for **meat**. Hope offered rations. Badass T complained that his companions had taken hours to follow him into the shaft. The long search above, not any proven shift in time, could explain his wait.[^arc10-65]

When Stanley asked about **Tungsten Wheel**, the strangers' answers became evasive. They promised scraps of information but demanded more food and offered no dependable route to the engineer. Hope challenged them. Someone pulled a lever, and **a metal gate slammed shut behind the company**. The hungry prisoners transformed into terrifying animal-human shapes: a rat, a boar, a bear, a tiger and an elephant-like giant among them. The precise match between every name and beast remained uncertain. The heroes and Union faced a trapped camp of hostile werefolk, with Badass T beside them. **The fight had not yet begun; Tungsten was still missing.**[^arc10-66]

## V. The prisoner's price

The metal gate had scarcely locked when the hungry prisoners of **Pentel Weiss** shed their human faces. **Grandma Tilly** became a wolf, **Ziptyree** a rat, **Morton** a boar, **Torbeer** a hulking bear, **Daniel** a tiger and **Rugrish** an elephant-like giant. Their names remained uncertain in the spoken record, and even the keeper of the encounter briefly confused his tokens. But the trap was unmistakable. **Nash, Stanley, Hope, Vorel and Union Wheel** faced their former hosts with **Badass T** at their side, the exit closed behind them.[^arc10-67]

Badass T charged Torbeer. Union's ordinary spear cut the tiger but the wound closed without harm. Hope's enchanted dagger and Stanley's **Diplomacy** could hurt these changing bodies, while Stanley's encouragement strengthened his companions. **Nash tried a fiery Chromatic Orb**, only for the prison's suppression to swallow the spell and consume its power. **Vorel** raged, trying to keep the largest attacker away from Nash. They had learned that not all magic failed here, but choosing the wrong kind at the wrong moment could be fatal.[^arc10-68]

Hope killed the rat **Ziptyree**. Stanley brought down **Morton the boar**. Vorel seized Grandma Tilly and held her beneath the water of a small stone pool while Rugrish battered him. His attempt to drown her never succeeded. Torbeer meanwhile mauled Badass T, then hurled him against the chamber wall. The scarred warrior lay unconscious. The family of werefolk was losing members, but the friends were in danger of losing the companion they had only recently found again.[^arc10-69]

Tilly broke free, returned to an old woman's shape and ordered the survivors to surrender. Daniel had already yielded; Torbeer and Rugrish followed. The dead were Ziptyree and Morton. **Stanley bandaged Badass T and stabilized him**: he was breathing, but still unconscious. Fur began growing visibly from beneath his mask. Tilly said Torbeer's bite had passed their curse to him, and promised that the strange hunger might be trained rather than allowed to rule him. Her explanation was not proof of his eventual form, his cure or his safety.[^arc10-70]

Their search for Tungsten could not end in that camp. After difficult discussion, the companions left Badass T in **Tilly's care**, meaning to return when they could. **Torbeer** led the four heroes and **Union** through a concealed passage past silent golems to a domed junction. A raised altar stood there with circles and an empty triangular recess. They had neither the right object nor the knowledge to operate it. The werebear guided them toward another settlement and then returned to his own family, leaving the friends to pass onward without their wounded companion.[^arc10-71]

An old bearded gatekeeper, whose name sounded like **Rolston**, let them into a crowded community of mages and other prisoners. Stanley used his megaphone to call for **Tungsten Wheel**. A tall, white-haired elf appeared, and **Union ran to embrace her uncle**. The missing engineer was alive. At last their clues had led to the person who might repair the Rubistic Triangulosphere; yet the walls of Pentel Weiss still stood between them and Fate Six.[^arc10-72]

Tungsten took them to a cramped cell covered with calculations and papers. He heard of **Stanley's new office** among the Elhandari and the unfinished machine awaiting its maker on Mars. He believed the prison suppressed newly cast spells while some inherent power in enchanted objects and bodies remained. Stanley tested **Diplomacy's Parley command** without success, although the sword had wounded real enemies here. Tungsten spoke of collector **rings** said to bypass the suppression, but no such ring had been found, worn or tested by any of them.[^arc10-73]

He also remembered being taken from his bed in **Burp**, and feared that someone among the few who knew his hiding place had betrayed him. His suspects were not culprits proved by evidence. A terrible being in a distant ward might, he said, have overcome one of the old ring-bearing collectors. He agreed to go with the rescuers despite all that remained unknown. Stanley instructed Union to put her uncle's protection first. Together they left his cell, still inside the prison.[^arc10-74]

Rolston and other inmates barred the exit, demanding that the newcomers surrender their possessions. Stanley's warning persuaded most to stand aside, but Rolston persisted. Stanley gave Vorel an **Elhandari empathy grenade** and Vorel struck the magistrate with it like a club. The grenade did not explode. Rolston fell, his precise condition unconfirmed. The six companions emerged into the hallway with **Tungsten**, leaving angry voices behind. They had found him, but they had not escaped Pentel Weiss, secured a ring or retrieved Badass T.[^arc10-75]

The passages ahead held only motionless towering golems and galleries of ancient dead. Tungsten thought a collector's ring might not only restore spellcasting but open levels and mechanisms in the prison. This was theory, not experience. Vorel questioned how the engineer had come to be imprisoned, and Union defended him. The companions considered giving the first ring to **Nash**, who could float with the strange power fused to his body even though the magic of his implanted heart could not transform him into a dragon here.[^arc10-76]

Vorel crept ahead among dusty crypts. Nash found a bandaged corpse in a sarcophagus and, after an unkind joke about its resemblance to him, took an old **finger bone**. Later he placed his already glowing vegetable peeling in a skull's eye socket to make a lantern for Vorel. The strange light was moved, not recast. Ancient skeletons, motionless demonic statues and empty tombs offered no ring and no trustworthy explanation of those who had died in a prison whose residents claimed not to age.[^arc10-77]

The other path was carpeted. Vorel argued cheerfully over what furnishings the dead would prefer before the carpets rose around the living. One trapped Nash against the floor and another engulfed Stanley. Vorel tore and bit at the rugs, while **Hope's cold-infused dagger** froze their fibers until the animated cloth burst apart. Eventually every attacking carpet was destroyed. **Tungsten saved the fragments** to study how something apparently magical could remain active where new spells failed. The party had won a dangerous fight without finding a collector's ring.[^arc10-78]

Nash later found decorated **leather sandals** in a corpse's crypt and put them in his bag, without wearing them. At the end of the burial passages stood **two seated, withered figures**, one crowned with rotten finery. From farther east a separate voice entered the companions' minds, promising friendship and answers. **Tungsten** began to follow it without responding until Stanley caught and restrained him. The seated dead had not yet spoken; the persuasive mind-voice came from beyond their chamber.[^arc10-79]

At that doorway **Vorel saw a glorious desert** full of creatures worthy of hunting. It was the world he wished to see, and he could not turn away. The two seated corpses awoke and warned him to stay back, their voices distinct from the unseen inviter. Hope caught his tail but could not stop him. For a heartbeat the vision opened onto figures bound in black tentacles and a vast **eye set into the wall**. Then the desert returned. Vorel walked into the eastern chamber while **Hope, Nash, Stanley, Union and Tungsten** remained outside. No ring had yet been found, and no one had rescued him.[^arc10-80]

## VI. Song, ring and rebirth

Within the eastern chamber **Vorel** inhabited a dream of everything he had ever wanted: a dragon-skull throne, a nation of lizardfolk beneath his rule, abundant food and contests he could always win. To everyone else he was lost among motionless captives bound by black tentacles. **Stanley** handed the entranced Tungsten to **Union**, who moved her uncle away from the dreadful light until he recovered his senses. Then Stanley went through the doorway alone with **Diplomacy** in hand, determined to rescue Vorel and search the bodies for the collector's ring they still needed. He could see the tentacles without immediately believing their promises.[^arc10-81]

The searching cost Stanley his freedom. Tentacles closed around him while he examined the captives' hands and found nothing. His will finally failed, and the chamber gave him another life: a peaceful home, **his father and mother at dinner**, his friends and children gathered around a table where none of the old griefs had happened. It was everything he had lost, and none of it was there. Vorel's imagined kingdom and Stanley's family meal were prisons made of desire. **Hope and Nash** remained outside, unable to reach their friends by simply calling to them.[^arc10-82]

The two ancient figures on their thrones began speaking as **lich rulers**, offering names that sounded like **Egnails the Maggot Lord** and **Archeloft the Obtuserizer**. They desired a ring as urgently as the companions did. They promised rewards and future dominion if one were brought to them; even offered that the heroes kill their bodies so their hidden **phylacteries** might let them reform elsewhere. **Nash and Hope refused** to give dangerous beings a route out of their prison. The liches guessed that the monster beyond them might have some secret weakness, but none could say they had found it.[^arc10-83]

**Hope and Nash** covered their eyes and entered the chamber holding a line for safety. The alluring voice still reached their minds, but without seeing its dreadful light they did not surrender to the same fantasies. They felt their way among bodies that barely moved and found no ring. The tentacles caught them anyway: Nash was dragged up toward the ceiling, while Hope fought free and crawled closer to the warm, pulsing source. When Nash finally escaped he returned to the safer chamber. **Union** entered blindfolded and found Stanley, but covering his eyes did not awaken him from a dream already in progress.[^arc10-84]

Hope's hands encountered a great slippery membrane. She stabbed it with the **enchanted dagger and cold carried through her Hand**, drawing a scream that was not sound at all. Psychic agony struck the living throughout the room. **Union fell unconscious**, and later struggled against death while the others were still fighting to understand what they faced. Hope struck again, then felt within the wound and found only fibrous flesh. In desperation she embraced the creature rather than stab once more. It spoke of forgiveness, yet kept urging her to remove the blindfold. Its claims of benevolence had imprisoned people for who knew how long.[^arc10-85]

In private, **the Voice attached to Hope's Hand** demanded that she seek another portion of its power at the **top of Pentel Weiss**. It threatened her and claimed authority over her life. Hope asked whether deadly magic could end the creature, but no **Finger of Death** was actually cast here. Instead the Voice suggested an almost absurd remedy: **sing to it**. Hope began a lullaby, and for the first time the creature reacted with fear. She called Nash to join her. **Tungsten** joined the song as well. Music, unlike their failed spells, reached a place the monster had not been able to defend.[^arc10-86]

The singers gave their friends another chance to resist. **Stanley woke from his dream**, saw Union lying near death and stabilized her with ordinary care. She was breathing but remained unconscious. Vorel still wandered within his imagined kingdom. Stanley took up his guitar and sang the song he had written of love, shelter and finding one another. The voices joined. The magnificent dream-light collapsed, and the **great eye and its tentacles melted** into a pool of warm flesh. Vorel woke among the others, his kingdom gone, while the captives once suspended within the monster fell lifeless into the ruin. The victory was real, and its aftermath was terrible.[^arc10-87]

They searched the dead and the wreckage at last. **Nash discovered a single ring** on a hand near where the great eye had been and put it on his own finger. The oppressive grip of Pentel Weiss seemed to loosen, and strange information crowded his senses, but he **had not yet cast a spell** to prove what the ring could do. The two liches approached demanding the promised treasure. The companions claimed the prize was merely a wedding band, seeking time while refusing to free the undead rulers. As the liches pressed forward, Hope moved to block them and Stanley drew Diplomacy. **S2E11 ended at the start of that confrontation**, not after a fight or a prison escape.[^arc10-88]

The next clash began when **Archeloft** retreated and pulled a lever, dropping a stone partition across the passage. He escaped to the far side, leaving **Egnails** trapped in the crypt with the adventurers. Nash gave their **only collector ring** to Stanley. Once Stanley put it on, he **successfully cast Healing Word** and restored consciousness to Union. The ring's power was no longer merely a sensation or a rumor: a spell suppressed elsewhere in the prison had worked. But the sarcophagi around them started shaking, and bandaged dead rose to fight beside a gold-adorned **mummy lord**.[^arc10-89]

Stanley's waves of destructive light and thunder tore through the mummies. He gave Vorel the **Corpse Slayer Dagger**, which the lizardfolk used first against Egnails and then against the undead threatening his friends. The mummy lord's dreadful glare stopped Nash in his tracks. Stanley was stricken with **mummy rot**, unable to recover health while the curse endured. **Union was knocked down again** after her earlier healing. The company battled among the open tombs, with Tungsten still vulnerable and the way out sealed. Each new wound made the ring's small restoration of magic feel less like salvation.[^arc10-90]

**Hope actually cast Finger of Death** through her Hand against the mummy lord, unlike the earlier discussion in the eye chamber. The dark power manifested, but the creature was **immune to its necrotic damage** and took none. Stanley's spells and the magical steel continued to cut through the rising dead. **Vorel killed the mummy lord**, breaking the crowned creature down into sand, while **Nash lay dying** after a series of failed death saves. The moment could not be softened into a narrow escape: **Nash was declared dead in the crypt**. The ring remained on Stanley's hand at the instant of death.[^arc10-91]

The last mummies fell. **Tungsten** stabilized his injured niece, and Stanley later healed her awake again. **Vorel** seized Egnails, pushed the still-living lich into a sarcophagus and kept the heavy lid shut with his own weight. They did not kill him, and **Archeloft** remained beyond the partition. Stanley placed the ring on **dead Nash's finger**. The smallest motion seemed to follow, but there was no immediate return to the friend who had joked and fought beside them. All of them were still trapped within the stone prison, and Badass T was not among them.[^arc10-92]

**Hope gathered the companions around Nash's body** and used the power of her Hand to **Teleport** them to **Fate Six**. The spell succeeded: Hope, Vorel, Stanley, **Tungsten, Union and Nash's dead body** arrived together on the ship still docked at Pentel Weiss. Badass T remained behind in the werefolk camp, unconscious when they last saw him. The rescue had brought Tungsten to freedom, but at a terrible cost. Hope took the helm and prepared to leave, choosing the friends she could still save over a return to the prison's unexplored heights.[^arc10-93]

As Fate Six left the pyramid dock, the **Voice** pressed Hope privately for another artifact it believed waited above. It threatened to withhold its favor if she abandoned that demand. She refused to return while her friends needed her. **Tungsten** recognized Stanley's mummy rot and went to the ghost surgeon's workspace, intent on treating it. The ship passed back through the strange sphere toward the purple nebula, with **Mars intended as the next refuge**. No one could say they had reached it yet, and there had been no reunion with Dunglestar.[^arc10-94]

Belowdecks, **Stanley and Vorel brought Nash's corpse** before the carved face that spoke as **Delit Or'Nath**. Stanley confronted his father about the tragedies he had endured and what his family might have known of them. Delit spoke of the Principal's aims and the Weir, defending sacrifices made in the name of survival. These were his explanations, not a verdict on all creation. He also claimed Nash had been made in the **flesh pits of Clench** and carried an engineered, regenerative **dragon-metal heart**. So long as that heart survived with Nash's own nature, the body might grow again.[^arc10-95]

The ship left the prison's sphere and entered the nebula. At **Doctor Daffodil's workstation**, **Tungsten actually cured Stanley's mummy rot**, lifting the black marks and the injury's lasting power. The crew rested before steering into more dangerous lightning. In the darkness of that rest Nash's consciousness encountered a fierce inner image of his dragon self, urging him to push outward from death. It was an experience inside Nash, not proof that Delit's entire history was correct. Something did move within the body left below decks.[^arc10-96]

A tiny **eyestalk** emerged through Nash's chest from the living **metal heart**. It blinked, looked at his companions and responded to them. He had become conscious again, but he had no limbs, voice or ordinary body. **Vorel read the frantic blinking**, opened the chest and drew out the heart with its new eye. It floated unaided. The friends saved the old body's equipment as best they could; the **collector ring's precise custody after this extraction was not established**. Nash was not whole, but he was present, and even managed to conjure a spectral hand for an impudent gesture.[^arc10-97]

At last Vorel carried the **empty corpse**, not the living heart, onto the deck and cast it into the nebula. Lightning tore the discarded flesh apart. Nash's small floating heart and eyestalk remained aboard **Fate Six**, conscious and beginning a slow regeneration. **Season Two ended with Tungsten rescued, Union alive, Badass T left behind and the ship still short of Mars.** The Triangulosphere was unfinished; the Principal, the Weir and the Voice's demands remained. Nothing about a season ending required the companions' story to stop. Their voyage into **Season Three** was already waiting.[^arc10-98]

## VII. Chrome in the void

**Fate Six** had escaped Pentel Weiss but not the purple nebula. **Nash** hovered above its deck as a living **dragon-metal heart with a single eyestalk**, without hands, feet or speech. His old body had been discarded. Mars remained a destination, not a shore in sight. Stanley watched the small survivor and resolved not to wait for his friend's body to grow at its slow natural pace.[^arc10-99]

Stanley lifted his megaphone and invoked **Regenerate**, touching Nash's eye. A pink mark spread across the metal heart. Flesh formed in strange shapes: teeth before a mouth, lips before a face, then the first signs of limbs and a voice. **Vorel fetched a cloak** to shield his friend from curious eyes. The enchantment promised faster restoration, but Nash was **not yet fully human** when the crew called them back to their duties.[^arc10-100]

In the ship's medical quarters, **Tungsten Wheel** watched over the recovering **Union**. Hope and Stanley asked whether he could finish the **Rubistic Triangulosphere**. Tungsten **claimed** that he had concealed its missing rare material upon his person throughout his imprisonment. He hoped **Dunglestar** would help retrieve it when they reached Mars. No ingredient was produced for inspection, and no part of the machine was repaired there aboard Fate Six.[^arc10-101]

At the helm, **Crooked Hand Row** had charted a safer route between the nebula's lightning fields. **Hope** followed it past wandering space whales until Fate Six emerged into open darkness. With Mars still ahead, enormous **harpoons** suddenly struck the vessel. A hostile enchantment enclosed it in a shimmering sphere, and a vast dark airship drew alongside. Black-armored **Hell Knights** surged across the chains to fight the ship's ghostly elves.[^arc10-102]

Four figures led the boarding: **Squirts**, whom an infernal patron had brought back to life; **Blood Skunk**, the clown; **Steerious Business**, a minotaur; and the arachnid surgeon **Doctor Spider**. They came in Hell's service to punish **Hope** for defying its demands. Their arrival did not prove that the Principal had arranged or approved the assault. The company had enough enemies before any further alliances could be guessed.[^arc10-103]

**Stanley** answered with thunderous magic while **Vorel** met Steerious in a furious clash of blades and flame. Doctor Spider spread **Sickening Radiance** across the deck, harming friends and foes alike until Stanley dispelled it. **Squirts** struck Vorel with cruel magic, was wounded by Hope, then recovered under Spider's healing long enough to aid her comrades again. **Blood Skunk** fired arrows into the fight. Between living intruders and ghostly sailors, Fate Six had become a battlefield with no clear front.[^arc10-104]

As **Regenerate** worked, Nash's new body grew a head and legs but not functional arms. The battle reached him before he could stand and fight as himself. He changed instead into an **adult black dragon**, whose claws and wings served where his half-grown limbs could not. Doctor Spider caught the dragon in an illusory **straitjacket**. The bindings existed only in Nash's mind, yet they caused genuine pain. He struggled free at great cost, a dragon once more but still not a fully regenerated humanoid.[^arc10-105]

Hope pressed the attack. She struck down Squirts, and Spider brought the kobold back to her feet. Vorel beat Steerious into submission for a time, though the minotaur would rise again. **Blood Skunk died** in the fighting, and Squirts was left burned and lifeless on the deck; what Hell might later do with her remains was unknown. **Doctor Spider** climbed the mast and conjured a ring of fire, harming allies as well as foes, before **Nash's acid breath** struck him down. Hell Knights still fought the ghostly crew, but the defenders began gaining ground.[^arc10-106]

Then a **new portal opened** on Fate Six. A giant figure of polished chrome stepped through, blue light crossing his frame and scarlet rings flashing upon his fingers. His face rose into a towering **crowned skull**: **the Principal**. He proclaimed grand names and titles whose exact form and history the companions could not verify. The Voice bound to **Hope's Hand** warned her privately to **flee**. She would not abandon the others.[^arc10-107]

With a spoken word the Principal **stunned Stanley**, who could no longer act. Hope struck at the chrome with her enchanted dagger. Its blade could wound him, but the cold that had defeated lesser enemies made little impression. Nash, still a black dragon, lunged with tooth and claw and found the metal almost impossible to pierce; only a trace of acid burned through. Vorel battled the remaining Hell forces as the vessel shuddered beneath its harpoons. The Principal was unvanquished, and Stanley had yet to shake off the spell.[^arc10-108]

The Principal demanded that **Hope surrender the Hand of the Voice**. He claimed he had once worked through **Tobacho** to arrange its retrieval, and that he had expected Hope to recover another part of its power within Pentel Weiss. It was an alarming story told by their enemy, not a past event they had witnessed. **Hope refused** and held out the Hand, telling him he would have to take it. The Hell ship still held Fate Six, Stanley remained stunned, and the struggle was unresolved. **The Principal had not yet taken the Hand.**[^arc10-109]

## VIII. The Hand taken

**Hope** stood before the Principal on Fate Six's battered deck and struck at him again, the enchanted blade cutting chrome while the cold bound to her Hand was partly resisted. **Stanley** remained stunned, unable even to catch **Diplomacy** when the blade came back toward him and fell at his feet. **Vorel** chose another path into the battle: he ran across one of the immense harpoon chains to the enemy vessel, scattering Hell Knights into space, and made for the ship's guns. Nash, still in dragon form, wheeled above the fighting ghosts.[^arc10-110]

The dragon's **Frightful Presence** shook the enemy ranks, allowing the elven sailors to press their advantage. On the Hell ship, Vorel turned its black cannons against the Principal and drove shrapnel into the chrome figure. **Doctor Spider** and **Steerious** lay down but remained alive, their struggles not yet ended. The Principal was a far greater danger than the exhausted boarding party, and no ordinary charge seemed likely to break him.[^arc10-111]

The Principal caught **Hope by the neck** and wrapped his other hand around the **Hand of the Voice**, chrome flowing over it like liquid metal. Hope tried to change into the shape of a slender blond elf, but the disguise could not release either grip. **Stanley** finally shook off the spell that had stunned him, too late to reclaim the moment he had lost. Their enemy held the Hand fast, and the choice Hope had made to resist him was about to carry a terrible cost.[^arc10-112]

From the sky Nash poured **acid** over the Principal, melting part of the shining surface. To restore himself, the chrome giant ejected two barely formed, faceless bodies whose origins no one could identify. Then the metal enclosing Hope's Hand flashed with strange power. **The Hand of the Voice was severed and absorbed into the Principal's chrome.** Hope collapsed with her life in immediate danger, yet she was not declared irretrievably dead. What the Voice had claimed as hers was now in their enemy's possession.[^arc10-113]

The Principal turned to Nash and called him **his son**. He told a story of finding Nash broken in a desert, lifting him onto a silver road and preparing him to serve a coming world of steel as **Jesus Chrome**. Nash rejected the story and the offered place beside him. They were extraordinary claims of parentage and destiny, not proof of blood or creation. **Stanley** answered with **Disintegrate**, tearing away part of the enemy's chrome arm. Even that blow did not destroy him, and Vorel continued firing from the other ship.[^arc10-114]

The Principal opened a portal and withdrew **alive**, carrying the stolen Hand. He told Nash he could be found at the **Solar Mines**, offering him a place among the generals of the metal world he imagined. Vorel destroyed the device maintaining the magical enclosure around Fate Six and returned across the wrecked link. At Stanley's command, **Crooked Hand Row** sent the vessel into a rift, leaving the Hell ship and its remaining warriors behind. The dragon Nash caught hold of the departing ship and climbed aboard. The company had escaped its enemy, not beaten him.[^arc10-115]

**Stanley's Healing Word revived Hope**, who woke without the Hand. She felt an unexpected warmth in her body, a sensation she had scarcely known while carrying the Voice's cold power. **Doctor Spider** and **Steerious** survived and were bound in Fate Six's hidden warded brig, while the remains of **Squirts** and **Blood Skunk** were brought below at Spider's request. Hope could not know whether any promise made by her former master would follow her across the void. Crooked Hand Row had deliberately steered for **Blast Belly Rock**, not straight to Mars, hoping to shake pursuit.[^arc10-116]

As the ship traveled, the magic Stanley had cast upon Nash finally **completed his humanoid regrowth** when he returned from dragon form. The newly formed body was not quite as simple as it seemed. In a shared cabin with Vorel, Nash heard a thin voice telling them to put out the light. An unexpected bite drew his attention to a tiny **eye and mouth growing from his shoulder**, opposite his familiar extra baby hand. The little creature spoke, blinked and wanted food, but knew almost nothing of the world. Nash promised not to cut it away. It was alive and connected to him, yet its origin and relation to him were a mystery.[^arc10-117]

**Tungsten** came to Stanley with another desperate plea. **Union Wheel** had suffered a devastating injury during the fighting and now lay barely alive in the ship's medical quarters, her damaged body sustained by **Doctor Daffodil's** magic. Tungsten feared that she would not survive. Stanley refused to surrender her to that fate and brought **Doctor Spider** out of the brig under guard. The enemy surgeon agreed to help if he could use his own medical instrument, and the ghosts watched him carefully as he began.[^arc10-118]

With a spectral nurse assisting, Spider's many hands worked through the damage to Union's spine and organs, using silk and healing equipment while Stanley and Doctor Daffodil lent their own skills. When Union faltered, Stanley's magic and Spider's healing kept her with them. **The operation succeeded. Union survived**, sleeping and recovering rather than being returned from death. Stanley promised that Spider and Steerious would be released at **Blast Belly Rock**. Tungsten stayed by his niece, whose survival had required the aid of someone who had only just been their enemy.[^arc10-119]

After a real rest, **Hope tasted breakfast** and felt ordinary warmth and comfort without the Voice's Hand. Nash introduced his shoulder companion to the others and gave it the name **Sprout**. The eye-and-mouth accepted the name, enjoyed grapes and wondered whether Nash might be its father. Sprout was not a second full-sized Nash, a mere illusion or the same thing as his old baby hand. Even its own first memories were uncertain. The friends allowed themselves to welcome a peculiar new life while considering what the Principal had taken from them.[^arc10-120]

Stanley offered Hope the gift he had given Nash. He cast **Regenerate** upon the stump where her supernatural Hand had been taken. Pink light gathered into a new **ordinary living arm and hand**. Hope embraced him. She had recovered a limb, not the **Hand of the Voice** or any power it had held, and her skin no longer carried that artifact's strange cold. The new warmth was hers to live with.[^arc10-121]

**Fate Six docked at Blast Belly Rock**, now emptied of the dwarves who had lived there. Doctor Spider had sewn the dead heads of Squirts and Blood Skunk onto the shoulders of the living **Steerious**, making an unsettling three-headed figure. The added faces neither spoke nor returned to recognizable life. Hope gave Spider her old **Hell-issued appearance necklace** and **teleportation ring** to carry back to their former patron. That ring was not the **Pentel Weiss collector's ring**, whose whereabouts after Nash's old body was discarded remained unknown. Spider and Steerious were **released alive** on the asteroid, while Vorel's uncertain recognition of the damaged Squirts head settled nothing about her identity.[^arc10-122]

The companions considered other destinations, including **Planet Nash**, but **Tungsten** repeated that the rare component he claimed to possess could be made useful with **Dunglestar's** help on Mars. They chose **Mars first**. **Union** appeared on deck on crutches, her chest bandaged, alive but far from uninjured. She reached for **Vorel's hand**; he accepted it without speech. Fate Six departed Blast Belly Rock through the rift toward the red planet. They had not yet arrived. **The Principal remained alive with Hope's stolen Hand, Sprout's beginnings were unexplained, and the work of saving Earth still lay ahead.**[^arc10-123]

## IX. The journey home to Mars

A green wood waited beyond a dream's bright silence. **Nash** wore old bandages and struggled to split logs before a cottage, where **the Principal** scolded him like an impatient father. The work became a shift behind the counter at **Vorel's Sandburgers**; familiar faces appeared wearing impossible lives. Vorel lounged in strange casual clothes, Hope inspected the restaurant as a severe officer, and Stanley made the demands of a small child. Even **Softhead** appeared, speaking and carrying an invitation although the companions remembered what had happened to his tongue. A door to the restroom opened instead upon the **Hillmaster's tent in Dusthill**, prepared for a feast. Nothing followed the ordinary rules of waking history, but the four friends were together within it.[^arc10-124]

The **Hillmaster** greeted the former Hill Guard and asked whether they regretted the road that had brought them there. Hope would walk it again. Stanley would change some choices but was glad of the people he had become close to; Vorel cared, as ever, about the promise of a feast. Nash would not trade away the life that had given him **Angelashi and their children**. Their strange host said he had reached them through a shared realm of spirits and dreams because Nash's soul was troubled. He also claimed that **Dharma** had brought about his death in Dusthill's engine-room pit. The companions had not returned to watch that old moment, and even their host's presence could not prove he was truly the dead man rather than a dream's convincing likeness.[^arc10-125]

The Principal entered the feast bearing burgers and offering Nash an unsettling version of paternal kindness. A scene played before them like a home film, showing a young boy with vegetables subjected to cruelty. The Principal claimed the boy had been an earlier **Jesus**, later a wandering preacher betrayed by violence, and that a dead body had been combined with many others and the Principal's own essence to make the Nash they knew. He said a **dragon-metal heart** and mysterious radiation had multiplied Nashes, and that a final transformation at the **Solar Mines** would allow Nash to become **Jesus Chrome**. His vision of a metal world without gods or demons sounded as much like a threat as a rescue. Yet this was a speaker inside a dream that could put the lost Hillmaster at their table. Nash and the others argued with him; no one could establish the film, the claimed parentage or the full history as fact.[^arc10-126]

Nash struck his own face, determined to wake. The scene erupted in **flame**. The feast and its troubling guests vanished into heat and noise, and what had seemed an argument about his first life was revealed as something far more dangerous than an ordinary dream. None of the strange family's words settled the past. A fire was coming from somewhere the companions could reach, but not from a place their dreaming eyes had understood.[^arc10-127]

**Vorel** awoke aboard **Fate Six** as **Union Wheel** pulled a wet green, tentacled thing from his face. The others were infested too: the creatures probed ears, noses, mouths and necks, and one reached into **Sprout's shoulder-mouth** on Nash. Vorel dragged the parasite from Stanley; Stanley helped free Hope. As they struggled with Nash's, his dream-fire became an **actual burst of flame**, hurting the companions and burning the remaining parasites. All four remembered the same shared nightmare. **Sever** suspected the little horrors had slipped aboard during the recent battle, but no one had seen them enter. The waking ship was already leaving rift space above **Mars**. Their dreams might have shown a genuine fear or a clever imitation; they had not physically revisited Dusthill or recovered the stolen Hand.[^arc10-128]

Fate Six descended through the Martian sky and **landed** in the Elhandari terraforming refuge. Soldiers welcomed Stanley home. **Tungsten Wheel** came down alive to meet those who had feared him lost, and **Dunglestar Ravensbread** rushed to embrace and kiss his long-absent lover. There was little time to remain at the landing platform. With Stanley's consent, Tungsten began assembling a team of at least **sixty-four** Elhandari assistants to finish the **Rubistic Triangulosphere**, and **Union** received permission to work alongside her uncle. Dunglestar asked for his former apprentice **Twaddle**, but Stanley insisted the young man's own wishes should decide where he served. The machine was still unfinished, its strange ingredient unproduced, and a **war council** was planned for the following morning rather than convened that day.[^arc10-129]

An elven speeder carried the companions to a nearby canyon, where an astonishing sight awaited them: **ninety-nine other Nashes** were physically present on Mars. They were no longer the tower's colored projections. Robed and bandaged in different ways, they passed the time with dangerous games, sending fireballs into the rock; one brought down a rain of chickens. The original Nash tried to impose order, but an alternate with a patched eye challenged him. Nash turned into his **black dragon** form and scorched a cliff face with acid to command attention. His opponent became a **giant hamster** and charged. A war that needed allies had instead found a crowd of unruly men who shared a face and not necessarily a purpose.[^arc10-130]

The giant hamster bit the dragon, and Nash struck back before a white-robed counterpart intervened with **Calm Emotions**. The challenger quieted, and Nash let the quarrel end. The peacemaker soon became known as **Holy Nash**, accepted for the moment as an organizer of the alternates, while the original Nash would speak for them at the councils of Mars. The crowd went back to games of rocks and vegetable peelings, hardly a disciplined army. How the ninety-nine had made the journey from **Planet Nash** was explained by others rather than witnessed by the four friends. They were here, and their help was possible, but no one had yet persuaded them to follow a battlefield plan.[^arc10-131]

In the green of the terraforming forest, **Nash found Angelashi and their four children** once more. One of the children had hidden in plain sight as a **hammer**, then changed into a piece of wood and back into her ordinary appearance with a delighted giggle. She was **Nash Junior**. The moment was a welcome interruption to the talk of war, even though earlier accounts of the children's forms had not always agreed. **Sprout** spoke to Angelashi from Nash's shoulder, prompting new questions about its identity that no one could yet answer. At an open-air restaurant they found another familiar face: **Charlemagne**, who had once cooked in T'ietkalla and now served food beneath the Martian trees.[^arc10-132]

Around **Charlemagne's table**, the friends spoke of more than the next battle. **Vorel** described the closeness he had begun to share with **Union**, but admitted he was unsure what to make of it. Stanley tried to explain trust and the courage of allowing another person to know him. Vorel preferred to recite the titles he had given himself after their exploits—**Victorious Dreamer**, **Entomber of the Maggot Lord** and **Cannoneer of the Principal's Face** among them. Charlemagne suggested that even the mighty Great Fang might be uncertain about companionship. Vorel agreed only to think on it, and no new promise was made. The four travelers had already become a kind of family, whether or not they knew what future awaited them.[^arc10-133]

**Stanley** placed the enchanted blade **Diplomacy** on the table before **Hope**. He believed she could make better use of it. She took the hilt and thanked him. He had carried the weapon throughout their travels; now he entrusted it to her. Hope's arm had regrown, but its ordinary hand was not the same supernatural artifact the Principal had taken. The transfer of Diplomacy was real and complete, a quiet expression of friendship before the challenges to come.[^arc10-134]

**Admiral Chrysorius** led the four companions beneath the roots of Mars's great tree to a hidden Elhandari laboratory. Within it stood an **augmentation cylinder** salvaged from their old work on Earth. The elves offered surgery that could change some learned spells and abilities, alongside a selection of enchanted weapons and armor. Those were choices the company would have to make, not improvements already performed. Each of the four received a **rejuvenation syringe** capable, they were told, of restoring the benefits of a long rest almost instantly; there might be a cost in years of life. None used a syringe. A higher measure of strength was held out to them, but the procedures and new equipment remained possibilities rather than completed preparations.[^arc10-135]

The offers left questions no instrument could answer for them. **Vorel** considered whether the **Eye of Groxl** still bound him too closely to the Principal, but did not remove it. **Nash** refused to part with the dragon-metal heart that had carried him through death and back. The others looked over the Elhandari options without committing to any operation. **No augmentation surgery had begun, no new weapon or armor had been selected, and the next morning's war council had not yet met.** The heroes were finally on Mars, reunited with family and allies and surrounded by possibilities. Saving Earth would demand choices far harder than reaching this refuge. For one evening they stood together before those choices, their journey across the stars complete but their struggle still ahead.[^arc10-136]
