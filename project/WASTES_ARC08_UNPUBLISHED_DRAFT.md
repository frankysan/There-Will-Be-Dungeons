# Arc 08 — Separate Paths

## I. Two roads after White Tower

**Hope was gone.** Beyond White Tower, the stone gateway had sunk out of sight, taking her, **Spatulo** and the hidden patron who had called her away. **Nash**, **Stanley** and **Vorel** could only watch the broken demon army withdraw. The great moon, its surface glittering with what looked like a city, still hung impossibly close. Three airships remained in the distance. **Stanley** bound Vorel's bleeding wounds and urged the battered defenders back through the gates. Nash saw Hope's departure as abandonment, a choice to leave them stranded when they still had families to find. Vorel, feeling the restored **Eye of Groxl** in his chest, refused to believe she was lost to them forever. Hope had been asked to choose and had chosen; neither man's interpretation could change where she had gone.[^arc08-1]

The treasure left behind was a hollow promise. Hope had taken the living **Hand of the Voice** on her own arm, leaving the ornate **gauntlet of Zorander's Fist** in its floating, open chest. **Alentris** called the loss of the Hand a failure, even though the siege had been broken. Vorel tried to send the loose gauntlet through White Tower's entrance, but the ward threw it back; the artifact itself could not cross unprotected. **Nash** studied the intricate case, fitted its panels over the empty glove and **locked it with the genuine pendant key**. He removed the key and **returned it to Stanley**, who put it in his bag. Only the sealed chest could pass inside. For now they had an object to offer **Tobacho Fleur**, but no certainty that a dragon who wanted the Fist would accept a shell without its Hand.[^arc08-2]

On the other side of the closed portal, **Hope** found a road among gardens and infernal fires, running toward a dazzling casino. The hooded figure who had attached the Hand to her arm reached for the **Infernal writing on her neck and shoulder**, pulled away a marked piece of flesh and threw it into the flames. The act removed something visible from her body. The figure said a former soul-holder could no longer claim her, but Hope could not measure how many other obligations remained. A black motorcar took her and Spatulo past a lake where a fiery serpent leapt. Inside the **Kaiser Casino**, elderly patrons fed coins into bright machines beneath a roof that made damnation look like leisure.[^arc08-3]

A well-dressed host invited Hope upstairs, sent Spatulo away from the private table and introduced himself as **Asmodeus**, saying she could call him **Brett**. He knew of the **Hand** and spoke cautiously of the presence that had given it to her. According to Brett, that mysterious being wished to bring all creation under its rule. These were the words of an interested host, not knowledge Hope could confirm. He offered ways to visit **T'ietkalla** and perhaps check on the friends she had left behind; Hope spoke of her kingdom and of the power she wanted to wield. She insisted she would make **no formal pact**. Brett turned the conversation toward music, extravagant drinks and company. She remained with him for the evening. No signed contract, return to her city or contact with the three companions followed from that first invitation.[^arc08-4]

At White Tower, a troop of **Pegasus-mounted paladins** arrived to take stock of the fallen defenders and the relic. Their stern captain challenged the right of three strangers to possess what his order had guarded for years. **Walnut** helped them reach their rooms, and the night's rest restored enough strength to think of escape; it was the same recovery already granted after the siege, not a second miraculous night. The locked chest floated into a closet under **Vorel's watch**. **Stanley** used a sending stone to tell Tobacho's representatives that they had the requested item and intended to return. The answer promised descending airships and asked after **Spatulo**. Stanley gave no reply about the tiefling's whereabouts. Their silence left the dragon's people ignorant of where he had actually gone.[^arc08-5]

Then **Nash's moon** sent visitors. Two yellow-furred, many-legged spheroids with shifting eyes descended in a little silver flying saucer, repeatedly making a sound like **Tunga**. Nash tried speaking back, then climbed into their ship with a nervous **Walnut**. The saucer soared toward the luminous city above. Neither passenger learned the visitors' purpose before the craft failed and plunged earthward. Nash opened its hatch, seized Walnut and floated them toward safety, deliberately dropping faster before catching their fall with the strange flying ability still integrated into his body. They landed alive; the saucer smashed against White Tower and exploded, **without visibly damaging the fortress**. The two visitors' fate was unknown. When Walnut could not stop shaking, Nash conjured an image of **Euphemius** to comfort him. Walnut knelt before it, believing the god had appeared. The others saw the magic for what it was: **Nash's illusion**, not a divine visitation.[^arc08-6]

**Alentris Vayne** met Stanley with an offer harder to dismiss than an enemy's demand. She expected to be called before her temple to account for losing the Hand, and she wanted to leave years of war behind. She asked whether he would remain with her in the Outlands and begin another life. Stanley cared for her, but the promise to bring **Nash and Vorel** home mattered more. He refused. Alentris then insisted the **Fist's sealed chest** be returned to her vault. Stanley, furious at the gods and unwilling to surrender the last bargaining piece they carried, did something worse than stealing it: **he changed her memory**. Her mind accepted a false account in which the relic had been left safe within the tower and the companions carried only an empty case. She walked away without knowing that her last argument had been erased. Stanley had betrayed the trust of the woman who had saved them, and the deception remained real even if she could not remember it.[^arc08-7]

The three left without a final battle. Stanley carried **Vorel and the locked chest** out by magic, while Nash walked past the guards separately. They reunited beside three grounded airships, where armed orcs wore familiar-looking badges. The men expected news of Spatulo. The companions gave an account that treated him as dead, though he had gone with Hope through the portal. The orc leader said **Tobacho awaited them** and hurried them aboard. Their ship **actually rose from the ground** with the chest on board. It did not yet reach the blue dragon. Overhead the moon seemed even closer, and whatever had summoned it remained entirely beyond Nash's control.[^arc08-8]

In Brett's casino, Hope learned what kind of help the apparent lord of Hell preferred to offer. He led her into a cold room where a goblin called **Pumpo** was held and beaten. Brett claimed to feed the goblin's poor village in exchange for its strongest warrior each year, and said Pumpo had tried to cheat him. His servants **tore off Pumpo's arm**, only for Brett to join it back again as easily as if cruelty could be canceled by repair. Hope suggested punishments of her own. Brett instead weakened Pumpo with magic so someone else might qualify as his village's strongest warrior and sent him away with a warning. The goblin **survived**; no substitute was delivered before Hope left. She had seen Brett both punish and spare a life, but nothing in that room made his claimed bargains trustworthy.[^arc08-9]

Brett's gifts were more immediately useful. He placed a jeweled choker around Hope's neck, a **Necklace of Infinite Appearances** that concealed her altered body beneath whatever face she chose to show. In a mirror she saw herself unmarred; then she took on the appearance of a birdlike stranger and became herself again. The **Hand remained physically attached**, however beautiful the disguise. Brett first offered a ring limited to selected familiar destinations. The hooded patron, visible to Hope but not announced to the room, urged her to demand better. She **returned the first ring** and received instead a rough, copper-colored one. Brett helped her test it: she pictured another room of the casino and **actually appeared there**. That demonstrated one journey, not limitless command over all the worlds.[^arc08-10]

The host also gave her companions who would be useful and watchful. **Steerious Business** was an immense, black-furred minotaur in a tailored suit, carrying a blade as imposing as himself. **Blood Skunk Hendrickson** wore the unsettling face and colors of a clown; **Dr. Spider** was an arachnid physician with equipment whose purpose was not yet clear. Brett described them as his employees and acknowledged that they reported to him. They were **not replacements for Nash, Stanley and Vorel**, nor were they secret allies already proven loyal to Hope. With their introductions complete, the hidden patron instructed her to go home. Hope used the stronger copper ring to open a **skull-framed gateway** into her own royal chambers at **T'ietkalla**. She stepped through; the three new servants followed. Brett remained at the casino, and the gateway closed behind them.[^arc08-11]

**T'ietkalla was not empty.** Its keep seemed familiar and yet strange after Hope's absence, with voices gathering beyond the secret royal passage. **Spatulo** emerged through the fading gate after her new companions, alive and indignant at almost being left behind. He caught her transformed arm; she forced him to release it, but did not banish or kill him. Beyond the passage, guards faced petitioning citizens. On **Hope's throne** sat **Celibus Stump**, a businessman she had known before, now serving as **acting chancellor**. Local leaders disputed rail deliveries and accusations against the **Liberators**. The flow of water outward and the disappearance of supplies inward were arguments in the court, not facts Hope had independently investigated. Her people were still there to argue over their future.[^arc08-12]

Hope entered that court with the minotaur, clown and spider-man at her side. Celibus greeted her as a returning queen and reached to embrace her. She answered with the **Hand of the Voice**, seized his neck and **killed him with Finger of Death** before the assembled citizens. The courtroom broke into terror. Armed guards rushed the strangers; civilians fled. Hope drew **Scully** and fired, while Steerious fought with an enormous sword and a strange beastlike tail. **Dr. Spider** called forth a glittering, infant-filled guardian that struck the attackers with radiant force. Blood Skunk lost control of his blades before summoning an immense serpent. The fighting was chaotic, with real wounds and deaths. The chancellor did not rise again as a living man when Hope let him fall.[^arc08-13]

One of the remaining warriors was taken alive for questioning. **Steerious killed the captive beneath his foot**, extinguishing that chance. Dr. Spider then used a spell to make **Celibus's corpse speak**, questioning it about what had happened in the months of Hope's absence. The dead chancellor said he had ruled for about **three months** and made allegations about the **Liberators**, the **Jeweler** and the reach of **The Principal**. A dead man's summoned words were not sworn testimony, and the charm of the spell did not oblige honesty. Outside, the city still lay beyond the throne-room windows, with its ruler slain and its political arguments unresolved. Hope had returned with fearsome power, but she had not yet learned what her people had lived through, nor what the unseen Voice would demand of her next.[^arc08-14]

## II. The burning city and the vanishing moon

The air outside **T'ietkalla's** keep carried a different warning from the violence Hope had left behind. A **blue-white crack** stretched across the distant sky, above low purple clouds. Repairs to the old stonework remained unfinished. Near the garage **Jimster**, familiar from her Dusthill days, recognized her and spoke of rain falling regularly since the crack appeared. He said Celibus had taken the chancellorship after the houses quarreled and their former leadership failed to hold them together. None of that meant Jimster had witnessed every agreement by which a stranger came to sit on her throne. In the warmth of the daylight, Hope felt heat upon the skin she had thought forever cold since the Hand joined her. It was a moment of sensation, not the breaking of her new bond.[^arc08-15]

Wearing the appearance of an old human beggar she called **Greg**, Hope looked in on the **Golden Sands**. The inn was damaged around the edges but open and serving guests; **Bok Bok** was working there. The kingdom had not become an empty ruin. At the **Fishbird church**, Hope put aside her disguise for **Emenil**, who welcomed her as queen. The priestess described disputes over public duty, absent officials, the railway and supplies said to have been raided by Liberators. She spoke of missing investigators and of political opponents allegedly taken in the night. These were worries and reports, not proven executions by any particular faction. Hope had to hear them as a ruler, though she had returned as a killer.[^arc08-16]

The hooded **Voice** appeared to Hope within the church and asked whether she had enjoyed killing Celibus. It required another act of evil if she meant to keep enjoying the Hand's powers. **He told her to kill Emenil. She refused.** When he suggested the first stranger she met outside, she refused that too. The Hand grew heavy, its granted abilities withheld until she met his demand, though nothing established that she had lost all her other spells or strength. Hope stepped back into the street to confer with the three people Brett had assigned to her. Their suggestions were ugly. Together they settled on destroying the **railway station**, which stood **temporarily closed and apparently empty**. **Blood Skunk** fired a flaming arrow into it and **Dr. Spider** walled it with magical fire. The building burned as citizens fetched water. Still disguised as Greg, Hope misdirected a witness and watched the destruction begin. No victim had been seen inside, and the Voice had **not yet declared the deed sufficient**.[^arc08-17]

High above the Outlands, the other three companions had a different view of catastrophe. Their chartered airship carried the **locked Fist case** toward Tobacho, with armed orcs guarding it and an operating crew of other peoples. The moon with the city on its face looked larger each time they saw it. The captain, whom the travelers called **Walls**, feared it would strike the world; Nash could offer only the explanation that some uncontrolled spell of his had summoned it, not a way to send it back. **Vorel challenged Stanley** about the memory he had stolen from Alentris and wondered whether their own memories could ever be safe. Stanley admitted what he had done to her and denied having done it to his friends. No new spell or proof settled the argument. Before they could decide what to do about the moon, **silver saucers came out of it**, and a larger linked machine opened fire on the airship. The danger was no longer only an approaching light in the sky.[^arc08-18]

Back in the city, the railway station crumbled to ash. The Voice returned and told Hope the fire was crude but **acceptable**. She could rest before their next journey. It was the first direct answer to whether the station's destruction satisfied his demand; she still had not tested exactly which Hand powers he had restored. **Omnom Cinderborn**, alive and angered by what he had witnessed, confronted the strange company in the street. He spoke of his failed attempt to reclaim **Razak Val**. Hope abandoned Greg's face and publicly declared that **T'ietkalla would no longer do business with The Principal**, blaming the railway agreement for the city's troubles. People bowed to their returned queen, but their questions about trade and safety did not vanish with the disguise.[^arc08-19]

**Alarm bells** drowned their gathering. A floating castle emerged over T'ietkalla, and winged attackers streaked into the streets. Hope called for civilians to seek shelter in the keep; her voice carried to those nearby, not miraculously to every household. **Steerious** lifted two tieflings and ran with them, while Blood Skunk followed and **Dr. Spider** scaled the walls to convince guards to retreat. Amid the confusion **Celibus's body moved again**, a lurching undead remnant of the man Hope had killed, not the living chancellor miraculously restored. Then meteors struck the keep. One tower fell as people crowded toward safety. **General Carthax** came toward the courtyard with an escort of monstrous warriors, and buildings burned around him. The Voice's smile did not prove he had ordered the invasion. Hope could not protect the entire city with the small company she had brought.[^arc08-20]

She used Brett's **copper ring** and called upon **Asmodeus**, opening a skull-framed gateway before Carthax could seize her. **Hope crossed**, followed by **Steerious carrying the two rescued tieflings**, Blood Skunk, Dr. Spider and **Spatulo**. The portal closed with Carthax still breaking into the keep behind them. **No destination was shown on the far side**. The citizens who had turned toward their queen were left in a city under attack; neither their final survival nor Omnom's fate could be known from the closing gate. Hope had gained a way to flee, not a way to undo what happened to her home.[^arc08-21]

Above the Outlands, **Stanley** and **Vorel** fought a different assault. With two smaller support craft flying alongside their main ship, the trio faced a massive linked saucer and clouds of smaller machines. Stanley used **Dimension Door** to carry **Vorel and the closed relic chest** directly into the enemy's control room. **Nash stayed aboard the main airship** and watched the enemy batteries fire. Vorel attacked the operators with fire, claws and the very chest they were trying to keep safe; Stanley used magic to throw defenders from the bridge and turn its weaponry on the smaller saucers. Their attack disabled the forward controls but did not give them a working enemy vessel. On the main ship Nash investigated a noisy orange crate and found **Squirts**, the little kobold cleric, hiding with **Chunks** among its fruit. How they had boarded remained a mystery, but the friends were no longer fighting alone.[^arc08-22]

Three heavy machines launched from the moon and landed upon the ships, brandishing swords, guns and an enormous charging cannon. They displayed **Nash's face marked as a target**. The reason for their pursuit was not yet known. Nash became a **young black dragon** and breathed acid across their armor, shattering smaller craft as he fought. Stanley teleported with Vorel from the crippled saucer to one of the attackers' great machines. Vorel fought to break its cockpit while Stanley redirected still more metal through the sky. They were battling a fleet, a world-sized object and a clock they could not see. Yet the answer did not come from killing every pilot.[^arc08-23]

**Squirts** looked upon the moon and recognized that mere dispelling would not undo a real body brought from another place. She grasped a small holy symbol and called on **Olidammara** for a miracle: let the moon and the hostile things that had come with it be taken away. **The divine intervention succeeded.** The city-bearing moon vanished. The surviving enemy craft, immense walkers and their occupants vanished with it. Broken wreckage, damaged hulls and the losses already suffered **remained**. The destination of the moon and its people was not revealed. **Stanley** slowed his and Vorel's fall back toward their ship. They lived, not because Nash had learned to command his wild magic, but because Squirts had made an appeal that was answered.[^arc08-24]

The orcs on the main vessel credited **Nash** with their deliverance. Their admiral, **Walls**, had gone overboard and was reported lost, though no body was recovered. One officer, **Farco**, led the survivors in acclaim. Nash accepted the title **Captain Dragon Lord** and named their ship **The Great Angelashi** for his wife. The ceremony brought pride, a claim to command and obligations Nash had not yet understood. **Squirts and Chunks** remained aboard. With the moon gone, the craft still needed to enter **Sigil**, and its new captain needed the oddest piece of seamanship in any pilot's manual.[^arc08-25]

A servant of the missing admiral, a monkey-faced man called **Snatch**, knew the necessary ritual. He described a sequence of fruit and a spoken phrase: put out **orange, banana and apple**; **smash the banana**, throw away the orange, eat the apple and speak the charm. Nash instead **threw the orange first**. A gateway opened anyway. The crew sent **The Great Angelashi** toward it as Stanley realized Snatch had said the order mattered. With almost no time, Stanley took **Vorel and the still-sealed Fist case** by magic to the remaining small support boat. **Squirts** carried Chunks by her own teleportation, and **Nash** flew **Farco**, renamed **Samuel or Sam**, to safety. The great airship went into the wrong opening, where huge tentacles seized and **crushed it into pieces**. The exact fate of Snatch and every crewman left aboard was unseen, but the vessel was lost. The title Nash had just accepted already carried a terrible cost.[^arc08-26]

The smaller craft had fruit left, but Sam was almost senseless with fear. Stanley restored his ability to help, and the orc remembered the right order. There was no open window in their little boat, so **Sam cut and framed a square opening**. Nash placed the fruit before him, this time **smashed the banana first**, cast the orange through the makeshift window, ate the apple and spoke the charm. A new gateway appeared, swirling in several colors. Sam recognized them as promising. The survivors—including the three original companions, **Squirts, Chunks, Sam**, the remaining orcs and **the sealed case holding the separate gauntlet**—passed through aboard their boat. **The portal closed.** Where it had carried them was still unknown. Hope was elsewhere, and neither road had yet brought anyone home.[^arc08-27]

## III. The empty Fist and the false road home

The second fruit ritual had worked, though no one aboard the little boat could say where it would leave them. Their craft slipped through the colored membrane into an immense industrial hall and emerged coated in **translucent slime**. Beneath the dock stretched a dark drop instead of an ocean. **Sigil** had received them again. **Stanley** cleaned as much of the mess as his magic allowed, while the surviving orcs struggled to berth the small vessel they had salvaged from **The Great Angelashi**. A weary official had been expecting a much larger ship and an admiral named **Walls**, not a boat full of frightened survivors. The admiral had last been reported lost overboard; no one had recovered his body. The arrival resolved one uncertainty: Nash had brought the companions back to the City of Doors, **not to Earth**.[^arc08-28]

Being **Captain Dragon Lord Nash** was less pleasant at a harbor desk than it had been before cheering orcs. The official said the destroyed main airship had never been insured, and that its last captain's responsibilities had passed to the man who had accepted command. **A hundred thousand gold** was the sum now claimed from Nash. He named the **Soggy Feather** as a place to reach him, but neither paid the demand nor received a final judgment about its enforceability. **Vorel** had a smaller debt to settle. A powerful rickshaw driver called **Fambo Joe** recognized him from an earlier failed appointment and confronted him on the dock. Vorel offered an unconvincing apology; Fambo slapped him, and the exchange dissolved into insults instead of a fight. They parted still angry, unaware that they would soon need one another.[^arc08-29]

The party needed Tobacho's address and the shortest route through the Hive. A stranger rising from a refuse bin offered guidance, changed his price and received a **nonlethal slap from Vorel** that left him unconscious. A company of rickshaw drivers proved more useful. **Squirts**, whose intervention had already saved them from the moon, charmed a friendly lizardfolk driver who lifted her as if she were an adorable infant, with **Chunks** safe beside her. Two women and Fambo joined the same traveling group. The driver knew the Hive, but getting there meant taking a great **freight lift** through Sigil's stacked structures. Nash's unease worsened with each stop. Ogres crowded the car, an old gnome tended its controls, and armed strangers boarded around the travelers until the air smelled of sweat, iron and danger.[^arc08-30]

The lift halted in a shaft that seemed **sealed by solid concrete**. Nash pried at a roof hatch, but found no open route above. A little messenger brought a threat whose sender remained unknown, and one of the huge passengers let slip that there was to be a killing. Darkness came suddenly; knives followed. The rickshaw drivers fought beside the companions, as did their surviving orc escort. **Stanley** took a storm of cuts and kept his concentration on **Heat Metal**. **Vorel's Eye of Groxl** flared around those he tried to protect, while Nash used his odd hovering ability to evade the crush of bodies. Squirts spent powerful healing magic to restore Stanley. Neither the bandana-wearing ringleader nor the unseen sender of the warning identified who had ordered the trap or even whom it was principally meant to kill.[^arc08-31]

The fight turned the trapped carriage into a furnace. **Nash cast Fireball** from near the ceiling and tried to spare his allies, but there was no way to keep every innocent passenger outside the blast. **The elderly gnome lift operator died**, along with attackers and other victims. The floor buckled and opened into a widening hole. **Fambo** was stabbed badly while defending the group, yet **Squirts stabilized him and restored enough strength for him to survive**. Stanley used **Feather Fall** when the lift finally broke apart. Nash remained suspended by his own flight, while Vorel and the drivers forced a lower shaft door open. The named travelers escaped into Sigil's curved streets, leaving a ruined elevator and dead bystanders behind. Vorel and Fambo clasped arms afterward, their grievance displaced by a battle fought on the same side. Their reconciliation did not undo the dead.[^arc08-32]

The surviving group hurried through the Hive. The familiar frog-on-a-stick vendor was missing from his corner; they could not know why. At **Tobacho's hidden bar**, dancers moved before the massive **blue dragon** and his stone interpreter. **Vorel still carried the closed floating case**. He had not surrendered it during the elevator fight or on the road. Tobacho looked toward his visitors, and the long pursuit of the Fist seemed about to end. **It had not ended yet**: at this first encounter back in the club, the dragon had neither seen the contents nor offered a route to Earth. The company had only reached the room where their bargain would finally be tested.[^arc08-33]

**Stanley** performed for the dragon and told the tale of their journey. When the question of opening the box arose, he searched his belongings and discovered the **genuine pendant key oddly lodged in his boot**. He had last been given it by Nash after the siege, but no one had seen another person move it into that hiding place. He fitted it to the chest and opened the panels. The **outer gauntlet of Zorander's Fist** lay inside. **The living Hand did not**. A golem carried the gauntlet to Tobacho, who turned it in his claws and examined the toothed hollow where a severed hand had once rested. Stanley dissembled when the dragon questioned the absence. The object was genuinely delivered; the part Tobacho most wanted had gone away with **Hope**.[^arc08-34]

Tobacho did not strike them down. Through his interpreter he agreed to count the retrieval toward their bargain, but admitted that a **direct portal from Sigil to Earth** was not available as they had been led to expect. Instead he would supply a contracted guide, **Badass T**, a bald, masked man whose wandering eye and battered metal armor made him look like a survivor of several unrelated catastrophes. Tobacho then displayed a moving likeness of **Hope** and announced enormous **bounties on her and especially on her right arm**. The sums varied according to what was brought back; this was an offer to hunters, not proof anyone had captured her. Nash's separate harbor debt had not thereby been adjudicated away. **Vorel left the chest with Tobacho's attendants** and physically **returned the borrowed sword Shatter** to **Jelver**. They were leaving with a guide and without the gauntlet, not with a confirmed passage home.[^arc08-35]

Outside, a swordsman calling himself **the Mangler** offered to join a hunt for Hope. **Badass T killed him with a machete** before Nash, Stanley or Vorel could accept or reject any agreement. The abrupt violence made the guide's urgency more alarming. He led them to the home of the familiar **frog seller**, who had been absent from the street because he was away from his usual stall. The old man was alive and, persuaded by Stanley, allowed the party into his basement. Badass T produced a peculiar travel instruction: Stanley was to **stand on his head and recite a Dwarvish-sounding poem**, while Nash ate a green-tipped plant. They **performed the acts**, and a purple opening appeared in the brick wall. The party crossed with **Squirts and Chunks** to a strange spaceport. Badass T came after them with a **bloody knife** and would not plainly say whether he had killed the man whose doorway they had used. No body was observed. They boarded a gnome-crewed vessel as the guide ordered its guns turned back toward the portal.[^arc08-36]

Far away from that journey, **Hope** and the three employees supplied by Brett emerged from their escape gate into the **Kaiser Casino**. **Spatulo** had followed them out of T'ietkalla, and the two tieflings carried away by Steerious were briefly present before their later whereabouts became uncertain. Brett introduced another guest: **The Principal**, a towering metal-bodied man also called **Vladimir**. He claimed that Principal City was under demonic attack and sought cooperation from the woman who now bore the Hand of the Voice. Brett showed Hope elaborate **visions of the world before the Scorching**, of dragons and laboratories, and of a younger Vladimir entering an installation linked to **CHIDS**. The visions were selected and presented by men with interests of their own. Hope could listen without treating every scene as a witnessed, impartial history.[^arc08-37]

The most dreadful image was of a facility where human bodies were cultivated in rows, limbless and helpless. **The Principal called it the Lich Project** and claimed that **a hundred living souls a day** were consumed to preserve his life. Even after hearing this, Hope indicated that she would **work with him** against the greater threat he described. They did not sign a written pact or fight a joint battle. He spoke of another relic, the **Eye of the Voice**, and directed her toward Tobacho for a lead. It was **not the Eye of Groxl** restored in Vorel's chest. **Spatulo** then challenged Hope's claim to the Hand, saying that it should have been his. Steerious wounded him; he begged for mercy. At **Dr. Spider's infernal laboratory**, the doctor trapped him in **Mental Prison**, and **Spatulo actually died**. His removed brain spoke by necromantic means from a jar, then **Blood Skunk shot it with an arrow**. The brain's answers did not restore Spatulo to life or authenticate his boasts about the dragon.[^arc08-38]

Hope sought Sigil through the stronger copper ring. After the killing, its magic was usable again, and she opened a gate into the city's streets. **Hope, Steerious, Blood Skunk and Dr. Spider** crossed together, carrying the doctor's strange preserved specimen. **The two rescued tieflings and Spatulo did not join them**; the refugees' precise location was unknown. Hope had not yet gone to Tobacho or reunited with Nash. Meanwhile, the gnome-crewed ship carrying **Nash, Stanley, Vorel, Squirts, Chunks and Badass T** made a wild jump through impossible space and arrived beside **two linked asteroids**. A crooked tower rose there, watched over by a statue resembling Stanley. Its skull-mouth entrance opened on a room larger than the building seemed capable of containing. Inside, a great **stone likeness of Nash Maggard** spoke to the approaching man and called him **Junior**. Nash asked who it was. **No answer came before the scene ended**. They had been promised Earth, yet stood before a face in the stars.[^arc08-39]

## IV. First Nash and the ruined T'ietkalla

The face in the asteroid tower finally answered. It called itself **First Nash**, an older Nash Maggard who said he had lived another course of the world and become part of the building itself. The strange face knew too much about the companions for Nash to dismiss it as a mere carving. But its familiarity was not proof that it was his father, nor that every assertion about time and other worlds should be taken as law. **First Nash** said his own **Hope, Stanley and Vorel had died** and that he had kept memorials to them. Those were deaths in **his** remembered history. The three who stood beside Nash were alive, though frightened by what another version of their friendship might have cost. Saving **Hope's soul**, the stone face insisted, mattered beyond any one life.[^arc08-40]

First Nash told the younger man to use the damaged **right eye** that had troubled him since the Wastes. Nash tried awkwardly to dislodge it, but the faculty was not in tearing himself apart. Through pain and repeated effort, the eye found another kind of sight. He saw **Angelashi**, heavily **pregnant**, fleeing through ruined **T'ietkalla** with **Bok Bok**. Blue light traced the handprints of the unborn child through her belly. A **nycaloth** lunged at them; she held it in magical restraint while the goblin attacked from within its jaws. The creature burst apart, and the two survivors fled toward **the Golden Sands**. Nash had not touched his wife, warned her or appeared in her path. The sight was a vision, but it was enough to make him demand a way to reach her immediately.[^arc08-41]

The tower contained possessions that belonged to someone else's history. **Stanley** found a counterpart of his floating sword **Diplomacy**, an English dictionary and a megaphone. **Nash** recovered a familiar staff with no exceptional magic. **Vorel** discovered **Hope's guns and holster**, held them for a time, and later **handed them back to Badass T** before leaving. Their resemblance to lost equipment did not establish that every object was numerically identical to what once existed in the companions' own world. Nearby stood a miniature house where **Pod** lived with a pixie partner and children. She embraced Nash as though her own friend had returned. He had to explain that the Pod he knew had died and that he was not the Nash she remembered. Though she wished to accompany them, she **stayed with her family**, trusting his promise to return.[^arc08-42]

**Badass T** finally removed his mask. The disfigured face beneath it belonged to **another Tedna**, his lips torn away in an ordeal he attributed to a powerful enemy. He insisted on his chosen name and did not claim to be the original Tedna who had died in the party's history. He and First Nash hoped that these visitors could return to help Hope. For now, they supplied ingredients and the arcane circle required for another crossing. Nash inscribed the known glyphs for **T'ietkalla**, even decorating the design as he worked, and **actually completed the Teleportation Circle**. **Nash, Stanley, Vorel, Squirts and Chunks** stepped through. **Pod and Badass T remained at the asteroid tower**, along with the guns Vorel had returned. Nothing in that departure rescued Hope or restored the earlier dead.[^arc08-43]

They emerged beneath the ruined keep in **T'ietkalla**, in a teleportation chamber the three men knew. Broken passages and an old body-hoist shaft offered a path toward the surface. They climbed with Squirts riding Vorel; Nash floated and helped Stanley when the chains and rollers defeated his footing. In the supply rooms a terrified tiefling in armor recognized the travelers. He spoke of thousands of demons, ruined crops and desperate resistance, but his numbers were a frightened survivor's estimate. When he ventured into the corridor, **an invisible nycaloth struck and killed him**. The fiend was real, wounded and armed with trophies of its earlier fights. Hiding nearby, the companions moved together: **Stanley dropped Diplomacy onto the creature and heated its metal**, **Squirts silenced the hall**, and **Vorel drove a barrel into its path**. Nash's acid magic failed against the demon's immunity. Then Vorel seized Stanley's burning sword, **killed the nycaloth** and gave **Diplomacy back to its owner**.[^arc08-44]

The company moved under **Pass without Trace** through the devastated keep. Demons wheeled above fallen roofs and dead streets. From a ruined vantage they saw **the Golden Sands**, its flag raised to signal survivors inside. That was not yet reunion. They slipped across broken approaches and a splintered drawbridge rather than draw the patrols into another battle. The inn's public rooms were smashed and deserted, but the kitchen had recently been used. Vorel led the way into his old basement room and uncovered the hidden passage he had once prepared. He called for **Bok Bok**. A familiar goblin emerged from the earth, alive, shouting with joy, and Vorel held him close. Then **Angelashi** came out behind him, heavily pregnant as Nash had seen. She embraced her husband. The vision had brought him to her, but this was their first **physical reunion** since his long absence.[^arc08-45]

Angelashi and Bok Bok had survived by staying hidden. They begged for a safe way out before the next attack found them. Nash began drawing another **Teleportation Circle**, intending to return to First Nash's tower. Before he completed it, **Stanley** received a magical message from **Riddle Nobieri**, who said a rescue force had arrived. **Elhandari elves** were intervening from the sky, and the sender wanted the party to remain. Vorel distrusted the offer and wanted to escape with the people already in their care. Stanley believed the new arrivals might save other survivors as well. They argued about Hope's absence and the invasion, but no one in that cellar had witnessed her issue an order to Carthax. **Nash interrupted the spell without casting it**, remaining with Angelashi while the others went to examine the supposed rescue.[^arc08-46]

Above the ruined district, massive silver ships hovered while dark-clad elves descended among the demons. Some did not kill their opponents: a bespectacled **elven therapist** used an empathy device to calm a nycaloth, and a small mechanized trap drew the creature inside. The claim that such monsters might be rehabilitated was an elven hope, not a result the companions could yet verify. **Nobieri** and **Melgor** approached with accounts of their search for the missing queen. Melgor was angry, especially with Vorel, and the friends disagreed about Hope's choices. Their elven captain promised sanctuary in **Elhandar**, a place he described as peaceful and civilized. Vorel questioned whether a society that subdued and caged enemies was as free as its representatives claimed. **Angelashi**, considering the baby, wanted to go. Nash agreed to trust her judgment, not to certify the elves' assurances as fact.[^arc08-47]

The rescue began to fail at its most dangerous point. **General Carthax** towered over a throne of tiefling corpses, surrounded by shattered elven efforts to subdue him. **Omnom Cinderborn**, exhausted but alive, was near the general. Nash carried **Angelashi into an elven ship for safety** before joining the fight. Elven soldiers supplied **empathy grenades** meant to calm even a mighty enemy. **Stanley and Nash** took strange fast-rest pills that restored their strength but threatened severe exhaustion later; **Vorel and Squirts refused**. They had bought a little power with consequences not yet paid. Vorel and Melgor advanced together. **Squirts healed Omnom**, while Nash accelerated Vorel with magic and Stanley burned the demon's armor with a spell. The soldiers fired from the surrounding roofs, and the giant fought on.[^arc08-48]

Then **Carthax raised his hands**, and red beams swept across the field. **Squirts was disintegrated into ashes**. **Melgor** and several elven soldiers perished in the same blast. **Chunks fell free and survived**. There was no moment in which Squirts simply lay unconscious awaiting a healing spell; she was gone. Vorel's rage became terrible. Nash and Stanley fought beside him as the elves continued to fall. Vorel forced a bandolier of **empathy grenades into Carthax's eye**, blasting it and leaving the giant **pacified rather than dead**. The elves deployed a trap that took the subdued general alive. Their success ended the immediate fight, not the grief of those who had watched Squirts vanish. Omnom had been alive after her healing, but his final route through the ruined city remained unseen.[^arc08-49]

**Bok Bok** had entered Carthax's mouth carrying explosives. The blast wounded the general, but did not kill him, and the goblin seemed lost inside. **Stanley commanded the pacified demon to vomit**, and **Bok Bok came out alive**, terribly burned and missing a hand. Vorel gathered him up. Nearby the elves' descending ship sent a wind through the pile that remained of Squirts. Nash held his cloak around it, and Stanley offered a small clean container. **Vorel collected her ashes and kept the vial**. **Chunks** was carried safely with the company. No magic reversed his mother's death, and no promised future resurrection could make the absence less immediate. They could leave with their injured and their dead, or remain among the bodies that filled the city.[^arc08-50]

Nash chose to go with **Angelashi** and the child still unborn. Vorel boarded with **Bok Bok**, wounded but living, and **Squirts's remains**. **Stanley** carried **Chunks**, who would need care beyond a journey between planes. **Carthax remained alive in elven custody**, while the other rescuers worked among T'ietkalla's ruins. There had been no reunion with Hope. The huge **Elhandari vessel** shut its doors and climbed past the damaged towers, turning from the broken city toward the stars. **They had departed**, but they had **not reached Elhandar**, and Angelashi's child had **not yet been born**. The companions were together again in one ship, except for the friend who had chosen another road and the small cleric whose sacrifice would travel with them in a vial.[^arc08-51]

## V. The Eye beyond the Styx

The gate from Brett's casino had left **Hope** in Sigil with **Steerious Business, Blood Skunk Hendrickson and Dr. Spider**, far from the three companions who had once traveled with her. She had a Hand that could change her fate, a lead toward a second relic and an enormous bounty on her right arm. The streets offered no safe welcome. When an older resident hesitated to give directions, **Steerious seized and struck him**. A neighbor threatened to summon the **Harmonium**; an elderly woman intervened and was struck in the face as well. Both were **alive when the group left**, and Hope refused the doctor's suggestion that they eliminate witnesses. A smith eventually directed them to a rickshaw rather than risk their threats. With a paid driver and an additional fee for his **writ at the Hive checkpoint**, they passed the guards and descended into the district where Tobacho Fleur kept his bar. The minotaur's violence had purchased them no better knowledge of the city.[^arc08-52]

The **blue dragon** was playing an electric guitar above a crowded dance floor. A red-haired man backed by knife-wielding companions recognized Hope and announced his interest in the bounty. Before the gang could carry off their prize, **Steerious cut its leader in two**; the other hunters withdrew. Unlike the elderly neighbors, this man was truly dead. Tobacho cleared the room, dismissed his interpreter and drew the four into a private field where they could understand his speech. He examined the bargain that had brought Hope's old companions before him. **The separate Fist gauntlet had already been delivered by Stanley and the others**; **Hope had kept the living Hand of the Voice on her own arm**. No one in the room could pretend the two objects were still joined. Hope spoke of surviving and becoming powerful enough to shape her future. The dragon listened, then offered a new price for knowledge rather than demanding the glove back.[^arc08-53]

Tobacho said he wanted more than a hoard. **The Lady of Pain** ruled over Sigil beyond the reach of ordinary kings and dragons, and he wished to replace her. He would offer **a lead toward the Eye of the Voice** and withdraw **the bounty he himself had issued** against Hope. In exchange, she must someday help remove the Lady and establish **Tobacho as Sigil's ruler**, without keeping an independent base of power in the city. These were extraordinary promises from an interested creature, not proof that the Lady could be defeated or that every hunter across creation would honor his withdrawal. The hooded **Voice** appeared to Hope alone and warned her away from contracts. Nevertheless she **read and signed the written compact**. Tobacho signed as well, and **a copy passed into her possession**. It was neither an unsigned proposal nor a completed coup. Hope had acquired another obligation precisely while seeking freedom from those already upon her.[^arc08-54]

The dragon gave her a place to begin looking. The **Council of Clowns** in **Pandemonium**, he claimed, might know where the Eye had been hidden. He did **not know or disclose its actual location**. A direct portal into that plane would not guarantee arrival before the Council, so he described a crooked route: a house of pleasures in wealthy Sigil, a passage into the **Gray Waste**, a settlement by the **River Styx**, and a ferryman who could carry them to Pandemonium. The four had not reached even the first of those destinations when they left the bar. They sought an inn in the prosperous district but failed to secure rooms after threats, confusion and the release of four temporary animals Blood Skunk had conjured. A vacant-looking mansion seemed easier. Inside they found a man hanging dead in an upper chamber. The dead man was an accountant, called **Davies Reese** in uncertain accounts of the scene, and there was no evidence that the travelers had killed him.[^arc08-55]

**Dr. Spider** questioned the corpse through a spell that borrowed its voice; the man did not return to life. The travelers held a grotesque dinner and **slept in the mansion**, completing a real rest. In the morning Hope borrowed the clothing of its absent family and used her appearance magic to look like an elderly human woman. Before leaving, **Dr. Spider wrote a false suicide note and ignited the house with Wall of Fire**. The already dead accountant was not a casualty of that blaze, and the fate of the building and surrounding residents remained unwitnessed. At the **House of Delights**, a suited proprietor accepted the reference from Tobacho and led them below the rooms of the living. A narrow closet hung with whips contained the ritual doorway. All four **ate portions of a carrot**, grasped the whips and waited as a huge mouth descended through the dark. The chamber disappeared. They stood under a flat gray sky on lifeless, colorless earth. **They had reached the Gray Waste, not the Council**. Blood Skunk and Dr. Spider were already draining into gray despair, while Hope and Steerious initially retained more of themselves.[^arc08-56]

In that land direction seemed to lose its meaning. A motionless gray orc lay almost at their feet, offering only the unhelpful advice that they were going the wrong way. He reluctantly named **Pointless**, a settlement beside the Styx, but neither magic nor persuasion made him a willing guide. **Steerious punched him, dragged him along and eventually cut off his head**. The head **kept blinking and speaking**, even after Blood Skunk shot an arrow through it. The orc had not been shown to die, and the others could not know whether every soul trapped on that plane shared his strange endurance. After hours of walking, the miserable huts of Pointless appeared beside black water and fog. By then **Hope too had lost her color**, though Steerious's golden ornament still shone. The minotaur brought the severed head to the wharf, as though he expected a ferryman to charge nothing for a passenger who could no longer walk.[^arc08-57]

The cloaked boatman was **Hesterbrook**, with a blue, skeletal face and a rule none could evade. Passage required something each traveler **truly cared about**. Hope surrendered **Fox**, her pistol—not **Scully**, whose history had followed another path. **Steerious gave up his greatsword**. Blood Skunk refused to part with his bow and instead **cut away his own left little toe**, which Dr. Spider healed without restoring the missing part. The doctor surrendered **his beloved lab coat**. Each payment disappeared into Hesterbrook's keeping, and **all four were admitted aboard**. The severed but responsive orc head had nothing to give; Steerious **left it and its body behind at Pointless**. The ferryman pushed out onto the Styx, speaking of old protections and of a guide who would await them beyond the river. None of them recovered the surrendered possessions by asking again.[^arc08-58]

A black-scaled **leviathan** rose from the river, dwarfing the boat. It recognized something of an old master in the power attached to Hope and threatened to disregard the ferryman's ancient protection. Hope changed her appearance to resemble the **hooded Voice**, but the monster knew she was not the being it sought. The Voice itself appeared beside her, **visible only to Hope**, while the creature begged to hear a word from the absent master it could not see. Hope offered extravagant praise and promised a visit to the leviathan's underwater domain when her business was finished. Whether that promise could be enforced was unknown, but it was enough for the beast to let the boat pass. **Hesterbrook delivered them to Pandemonium**, where the gray exhaustion of the waste lifted and their colors returned. The river crossing was no longer a promised route: they had paid its price and survived it.[^arc08-59]

An imp led them through a concealed opening and a wild ride in a **mine cart**, then left them before the entrance to the **Council of Clowns**. A spring-loaded guardian demanded a password, which the imp supplied. **Dario the Impresario** welcomed them only to announce an absurd appointment thousands of years away. The travelers protested, persuaded him, and were **actually admitted that day**. A vast auditorium of heckling faces surrounded the Council's seats. **Bobo**, its leading clown, insisted on examining Hope's Hand and **smelled it** before declaring that he recognized the power of the **Voice**. His account of a being that wished to displace or control the gods was the Council's testimony, not an independent proof of the patron's every intention. Hope spoke of her abandoned friends, her ruined kingdom, her ambitions and her grief. Some answers were veiled or exaggerated. The clowns mocked her seriousness until her anger amused them more than a prepared joke ever could.[^arc08-60]

At last the Council granted the request. **Bobo claimed** the **Eye of the Voice** lay hidden in a prison whose name sounded like **Pentel Weiss**, built to hold dangerous beings and concealed somewhere in **phlogiston** near the **Corinthian Nebula**. Neither Hope nor her companions saw the prison, and the precise spelling and location remained uncertain. What they **actually received** was a small **jack-in-the-box**, its springing clown head tilting as if pointing a direction. Bobo said this **compass was keyed to Hope** and could guide her toward the hidden place. He advised obtaining passage aboard a spacefaring ship from the Prime Material Plane. No ship was booked, no Eye seized and no Lady of Pain deposed. Hope turned Brett's copper ring once more and **opened a Gate back to Tobacho's bar in Sigil**. All four crossed, carrying the compass and Hope's signed compact. The portal closed on Pandemonium behind them. They had won a lead and lost precious things for it; **her original companions remained elsewhere, unaware of what she had learned**.[^arc08-61]

## VI. Four children under the stars

The ship carrying **Nash, Stanley and Vorel** had left the ruins of T'ietkalla under Elhandari protection, with **Angelashi**, badly hurt **Bok Bok**, infant **Chunks** and **Squirts's ashes** aboard. The vessel rose into space, its artificial gravity returning after a moment of weightlessness. An elven host spoke of a council that governed **Elhandar** and asked the refugees to pass through a medical scanner. Injured passengers were taken for care. **Squirts had not returned to life**; Vorel showed her ashes and hoped someone might someday help, but no resurrection was performed. The strange restorative pills Stanley and Nash had swallowed before the Carthax fight still exacted a price: **four levels of exhaustion**, not a harmless renewal. The crew thought docking might follow soon. That was a forecast, not an arrival.[^arc08-62]

An explosion shook the ship, and a screen on the bridge showed a vast **purple-gray cloud rising from Earth**. The elves ventured terrible casualty estimates, but no one aboard had counted the dead or established the cause. **Angelashi quietly told Nash that her water had broken**. Another tremor nearly threw him to the floor. **Lyrielle**, a newly arrived elven physician accompanied by a small white mink, hurried to the medical bay. She was a living doctor, **not Squirts reborn**. A scan appeared to show **one baby beside a fork, a boot and a smoking pipe** within Angelashi. Neither the doctor nor Nash knew what to make of it. An attempt to treat the strange shapes as ordinary animate objects failed. Angelashi had told Nash privately that she was **a changeling**—and asserted that he was one too. He protested that the only change he understood in himself came from his dragon heart. The claim about his own ancestry remained unresolved.[^arc08-63]

While Nash stayed with Angelashi, **Stanley and Vorel** sought the bridge. Guards resisted admitting them until Stanley's performance won their trust. An unfamiliar vessel had appeared nearby, refusing ordinary hails. Its captain presented himself as **Toesalt**, a ghostly pirate, and sent a monstrous creature called **Fluffy** against the elves. Explosions tore gaps in the hull. The ship's air began escaping into the dark, pulling crew toward the broken walls. Lyrielle gripped a corner of the medical bay and placed a **Wall of Force** across one breach. Angelashi was dragged from the bed but caught herself. **Bok Bok**, still unconscious and missing a hand from Carthax's fight, was swept into danger; he was **not pronounced dead**. **Chunks hid safely in a cabinet**. Far from the controlled serenity of the Elhandari rescue, the spacecraft was suddenly a battlefield with no floor or sky beyond its wounded shell.[^arc08-64]

Boarding tubes clamped against the vessel. **Stanley healed Bok Bok enough to wake**, and Vorel gave the goblin a red fire-retardant canister to guard the medical bay. Bok Bok stacked more canisters into a barricade rather than knowing how to operate them. Fluffy smashed into the corridors, a huge, many-mouthed creature whose limbs and teeth tore through defenders. Ghostly pirates followed with blades and crossbows. **Vorel's fiery sword** burned their approach, while Stanley struck with **Diplomacy**, dagger and spells. Elven energy weapons did little against parts of the assault. The ship's defenders gradually closed blast doors and secured the medical room, though other breaches still threatened them. A welcoming elf called **Sensate Philanthalar** was killed during the attack. Not every crew member pulled into space was recovered, and nothing in the chaos established that the pirates' captain had died.[^arc08-65]

The fighting split the travelers. **Nash** gripped Angelashi's hand while she labored, sometimes taking the hurt of her contractions with good humor and sometimes making it worse by speaking. **Vorel** was seized by Fluffy and refused another of the dangerous fast-rest pills Stanley offered him. Inside the medical bay the first strange delivery was **a fork**, caught and placed carefully in a warming bed. A **smoking pipe** came next. The medical attendants did not throw them away as debris; Lyrielle's earlier magic had failed because they were not ordinary objects. A giant skeleton appeared among the boarders while Fluffy grappled and battered Vorel. Stanley animated **Diplomacy** against the creature, heating its metal until Fluffy's jaws melted shut. **Fluffy died before it could swallow Vorel**, and the warrior was freed from its grip.[^arc08-66]

There was still a ship to save. **Vorel grappled the giant skeleton and forced it backward into a boarding tube**, holding the opening while the elves prepared to disengage. In the medical bay a **boot** was delivered, and Nash tried to interfere before Lyrielle stopped him. Poisonous vapor rolled along one of the tubes, its caster unseen. **Stanley used Telekinesis to pull Vorel back** toward a safe handhold. The Elhandari vessel then tore itself free, sending the skeleton into the void but also reopening the airless gap in its own hull. Several elves were swept out. An impact caused Stanley to **lose concentration** before he could complete a rescue he had attempted. He seized a fire-retardant canister and sprayed foam across the breach while other crew secured the remaining opening. The ship **repressurized and escaped the boarding contact**. The pirates were not all killed or captured, but they no longer held the vessel.[^arc08-67]

Amid the sudden hush of the restored medical room, Angelashi gave birth once more. **Nash caught a gray-skinned newborn**, a living baby with strange eyes and small fangs. Lyrielle identified him as a boy. When the attendants placed the **fork, boot and pipe** beside Angelashi, the three shapes began to change. **The pipe hiccupped**. In their mother's arms they became **three more infants**, living and breathing. Four children had survived the ordeal: **two girls and two boys**. No individual names had been given, and no reliable account yet assigned the sexes of the three that had first resembled objects. They were not tools mistakenly deposited during childbirth, nor children lost when the ship breached. They were **four living changeling babies**.[^arc08-68]

Angelashi let her elven appearance fall away, revealing **gray skin, pale hair and pointed ears**. She embraced Nash and their children, renewing her claim that **Nash was a changeling too**. Nash had no demonstration of that ability to answer her with and still trusted the explanation he knew for his dragon shape. The revelation did not erase his love for her or settle the mystery of his own blood. Elsewhere **Vorel still kept Squirts's ashes**, **Bok Bok was conscious but injured**, and **Chunks lived** through another ordeal without his mother. Stanley and the elves had saved enough of the ship to continue, though dead defenders and those swept away would not return merely because the air pressure had been restored. **Hope was not aboard**; her separate search for the Eye of the Voice had brought her back to Sigil, not to this family.[^arc08-69]

Beyond the viewport, the route to **Elhandar** remained open. The ship had escaped the pirates and turned toward the refuge the elves had promised, carrying the survivors of T'ietkalla, the wounded, a bereaved infant and four new lives. Nash held his family as the others recovered around them. The names of the children, the truth of Angelashi's claim about Nash, and the welcome awaiting them at the elven station lay beyond what anyone aboard could yet know. **They had not docked**. Their story in this stretch of the Wastes ended aboard a damaged but living ship, traveling through the dark with one companion absent and the future no longer quite as empty as it had seemed.[^arc08-70]
