# Arc 05 — Kings and Consequences

## I. A queen after the war

For three months after her coronation, **Hope Akmenos** had tried to make a country out of the wreckage of **T'ietkalla**. What had been promises made amid the ruins was now the work of governing. Walls and vehicles had been repaired, plumbing ordered, the old **Guzzle Gang money** pushed aside, and inherited restrictions on people without noble houses challenged. Each change met shortages or resistance. The court could order freedom more readily than it could supply stone, timber or willing cooperation. The new queen lived among advisers who had returned to the city in her name, but who did not always share her idea of justice.[^arc05-1]

The others had begun lives that were more than an endless chase through the desert. **Vorel** and **Bok Bok** kept the **Golden Sands**, an inn that also served as an adventurers' guild. Vorel had given the place rules drawn from his **Pillars of Decency**: honesty, fair dealing and some loyalty between neighbors. **Stanley** studied medicine and the rites of the **Tidebearers**, adherents of the Fishbird, while trying to live with the persistent presence of **Diontalus** in his body. He had recovered books and a map from the cat Buttons's possessions, though none could tell him how to be free of the archfey. **Nash** performed puppet shows with the small extra hand left to him by the froghemoth's strange magic. He lived with **Angelashi**, and for the first time their shared future seemed capable of taking shape.[^arc05-2]

Yet P0D's death still followed Nash into sleep. In one nightmare she appeared before a great war rig crushed him and an explosion left only impossible remnants of his body behind. He awoke alive in Angelashi's care. She told him that **she was pregnant with his child**. The terror had been a dream, not another death or resurrection; the pregnancy was the real news on the other side of waking. Nash could scarcely find a way to hold grief for the childlike pixie he had lost and hope for a child not yet born.[^arc05-3]

At court, two tiefling houses disputed a coveted position making clothing and armor. A younger noble's son had been favored over a more experienced applicant. **Hope ordered an impartial test of ability** rather than automatically rewarding lineage. An engineer reported substantial repairs but warned that the city was running out of building materials. Trading oil or water with settlements under **the Principal's** influence might solve the shortage, though such a trade risked dependence upon an old enemy. Hope asked for time to consider it. Her guards also brought in a defiant survivor of the **Twenty-Sixers**. She ordered interrogation and a public execution, a harsh contrast with the freedoms she meant to establish. A prisoner's claim that **Monsoon** had already died could not outweigh the companions' own sight of him escaping in mist.[^arc05-4]

The next visitor was **Softhead**, the gnome who had once been associated with Dusthill and now professed to serve **the Principal**. Beneath a disguise and a borrowed name, he offered peace talks, trade and the return of the people taken from Dusthill. He claimed that a subordinate had ordered that raid without his master's permission. **Those were diplomatic claims, not evidence that the prisoners were free**. Hope did not allow the envoy to leave unhurt. At her command his tongue was cut out and the wound cauterized. He survived, unconscious, while she instructed that her willingness to meet be conveyed when possible. Even an enemy's plea for negotiation could become another occasion for sovereign violence.[^arc05-5]

**Nobiery** gave Hope a sending stone with which they could exchange a message each day. She then retired to prepare for an evening away from court. In her private chambers **an assassin attacked with a poisoned blade**, invoking a name associated in the party's books with an obscure demonic power. Hope fought with the detachable horn she sometimes used as a weapon, recovered **Scully** and fired radiant ammunition. Guards reached her and killed the intruder. Hope was injured but alive. The assassin's words did not yet establish who had hired him or which of the city's many enemies had gained access to her rooms.[^arc05-6]

She joined the others at the Golden Sands without the full ceremony of a queen's entrance. A visiting dwarf captain from the great traveling vessel **Diamond Stroke** boasted of cargo and roads far beyond their familiar desert: meat, stone, timber and wealth that T'ietkalla badly needed. The captain's account of remote ruins, passes and **Principal City** was a merchant's tale, not a journey the companions had made themselves. Bok Bok worked among the food and guests while Vorel enforced his house rules, even ejecting a regular patron who had grown disruptive. In that crowded room, the friends could talk about the Principal's offer without holding a formal council.[^arc05-7]

The peace of the inn was fragile. A group of tieflings confronted its non-tiefling proprietor and customers with open contempt. The quarrel became **a rough tavern brawl**, and Hope, Nash, Stanley and Vorel were all drawn into it. The sight of Hope among the fighters changed the aggressors' behavior, but it did not resolve the prejudice that had survived a change of rulers. The city the companions had helped free could still turn on people like them.[^arc05-8]

Then an immense **light crossed the sky**. Thunder rolled over T'ietkalla, followed by falling stones. People fled into the streets as masonry shattered around them. The companions had no reliable answer for what had fallen, why it had come or whether the danger was directed at their kingdom. **The stones were still falling** when the night's fragile normality ended.[^arc05-9]

## II. Skyfall and ancestral debts

The Golden Sands became a place to leave behind rather than defend. **Angelashi**, carrying Nash's unborn child, reached the companions with a young tiefling helper, and Vorel sent them to shelter with **Bok Bok**. Hope hurried back toward the keep while **Vorel, Nash and Stanley** moved among the people caught beneath the stone rain. Vorel raised the **Eye of Groxl's burning shield** over endangered residents. Others were not so fortunate. A man was smashed by a rock, a woman cradled the dead partner struck inside her home, and a child died even as the party tried to direct families to safety. The three friends helped mothers and children reach **sewers and underground covers**, sometimes lowering frightened people with magic. They saved lives; they did not save everyone.[^arc05-10]

The companions regrouped beneath the keep in **Nobiery's laboratory**, passing the corridors and rooms in which so many of their earlier losses had occurred. The elder had animated attendants, including a corpse called **Carnage** and another named **Theresa**. The identity of the former was not secure merely because an executed Twenty-Sixer had borne a similar name. Nobiery proposed a dangerous way to learn what had struck the city: **Hope would enter an ethereal ancestral circle** while her three companions protected her body from hostile spirits. He warned her not to step outside the prescribed boundary.[^arc05-11]

Hope found herself before **four tiefling spirits who claimed an ancestral right to judge her**. They put questions about punishment, hunger, illness and loyalty to her, testing what sort of queen she meant to be. Their ritual authority could be experienced without making every assertion about ancient bloodlines historically certain. In the room of the living, the defenders faced a more familiar accusation. **The ghost of George Miller**, the guard **Diontalus had murdered** beneath the Pit, came against the companions in anger. He terrified **Vorel and Stanley** and aged Vorel into a frail elder. Stanley's magic and animated sword helped drive the spirit back, and **Nash's fire finally dispersed it**. George had not returned to life, nor did dispersing the apparition erase the death they remembered.[^arc05-12]

A woman the companions recognized as **Martha Miller** appeared as another anguished spirit calling for George; Stanley drove her back as well. The terrible keepsake they still intended to return to George's family had not found its way home. Elsewhere in the ethereal realm, **P0D appeared to Nash** and invited him to follow her into play. He did not. The voice and appearance were the encounter he experienced, **not proof that the pixie killed in the keep had bodily returned**. Nash remained with the living companions who needed him.[^arc05-13]

After the ancestors' questioning, **Quizquaz** approached Hope with an altogether different offer. He claimed legal trouble before a **Grim Council** over the Soulbroker pact, described **Andreeste** as possessing some ancient claim over tieflings, and named a supposed demonic contractor in the events overtaking them. He also asserted that a dragon had been destroyed on entering the atmosphere, near the impact. These were the oil-demon's statements; **the companions had not seen the dragon die**. Quizquaz proposed another bargain involving **Melgor and fifty tiefling souls**. Hope refused it and remained within her protective circle. She returned to the living with warnings rather than an explanation she could yet verify.[^arc05-14]

Vorel, meanwhile, was still unnaturally old. The independent will of the Eye of Groxl pressed against him, but **he retained control of it**. Stanley sought the Tidebearer priestess **Emenil**. Using diamond dust prepared from a jewel taken from the treasury, she performed **Greater Restoration** and **reversed Vorel's aging before the day was over**. Stanley demonstrated the new water-making prayer he had learned through the Fishbird faith. Emenil spoke sincerely of powers she called saviors; the water she produced or witnessed was real, while her wider religious explanation remained her belief.[^arc05-15]

The restoration could not repair the mistrust underground. When Nobiery referred too casually to the Soulbroker and the companions' dealings with demons, **Emenil realized how much the old man and her new allies had concealed**. She threatened to tell the people they had entrusted to Hope's rule. Stanley tried to reason with her, but Nobiery chose force. He ordered his animated **Carnage** to attack. The corpse lunged at Emenil; at that moment **her death was not established**. The companions had survived a storm and a haunting only to confront the violence of their own supposed protectors.[^arc05-16]

The attack landed. **Carnage bit Emenil**, but **Stanley struck with Diplomacy and healed her**, while Hope commanded Nobiery to stop. The remaining undead obeyed the halt. **Emenil survived** and demanded an account of the pacts that had been hidden from the court and its citizens. Nobiery claimed his people had misunderstood their long absence and described sacrifices bound to his own bargain; Stanley admitted the party had made a separate agreement with the Soulbroker. Emenil did not immediately denounce them to the city, although neither obedience nor forgiveness followed from her decision to keep talking.[^arc05-17]

The storm had stopped by the time the company set out to investigate its source. They chose a **rust-brown interceptor** from the Royal Truckery, passing the still-intact **Fate 4** as they departed. Beyond the repaired city lay a strange field of fallen stones. At the principal crater, damp green growth and **banana-bearing plants** rose incongruously from the desert. Something large had been **dragged away**, leaving wheel tracks and many heavy footprints. They had evidence of removal, **not a witnessed dragon corpse** or proof of exactly what had been taken.[^arc05-18]

A glowing clover drew them to a buried little man who called himself an unlucky **leprechaun**, deprived of his pot of gold. He wanted freedom, not to become their magical benefactor. **Diontalus took control of Stanley's body**, killed the man and absorbed the energy released. Hope took the leprechaun's **Lucky Coin**, which offered a limited chance to change fortune. Nash and Vorel confronted Diontalus over the killing, while he promised not to harm Nash's unborn child. His promise was his own, not a demonstrated guarantee; **the archfey remained active inside Stanley**, and nothing like his later separation had yet taken place.[^arc05-19]

They were intercepted on the way out by **bugbears speaking of a Wormfather**, led by a robed figure who sent a winged, stinging beast into the fight. Nash assumed his **young black dragon** form, Vorel fought among the raiders, Hope fired **Fox and Scully**, and Stanley struck with **Diplomacy**. The winged creature was brought down by **Stanley** as the fighting continued. There were still bugbears on their feet, and the hooded leader's fate was not clear. The mystery of the crater had yielded tracks, strange growth and more enemies—but the company had **not yet found the black worm that would later deepen it**, and the battle was unfinished.[^arc05-20]

## III. A metallic peace

The raiders around the green crater had not finished fighting. Their winged beast lay dead, but **the remaining bugbears** pressed the attack while Nash fought in dragon form and Hope, Stanley and Vorel struggled to hold their ground. The last attackers fell or scattered. A robed opponent disappeared in a manner the companions could not explain, leaving possessions behind but no body they could identify. What the raiders called the **Wormfather** still had no confirmed face, and victory gave the party no safe account of who had sent them.[^arc05-21]

It was **Vorel** who found something harder to dismiss than a name. Investigating the head of a fallen bugbear, he exposed a **living black, leechlike worm** embedded near its brain. The little creature moved across the sand after removal and seemed drawn toward him. **Nash destroyed it with the acid of his dragon form**. The companions now knew that at least one enemy had carried a parasite. They did not know whether every raider was infested, who had implanted the worm, or whether the thing explained the strangers' earlier violence. It gave disturbing weight to stories of minds being controlled without proving every account of a demonic conspiracy.[^arc05-22]

The journey home became a danger of its own. **Nash flew apart from the truck** to scout or distract anything following them, and the distance between him and the others widened. **Vorel's bananas**, taken from the strange growth near the crater, became unlikely markers dropped along the road to guide him. Nash found the trail, but his dragon shape expired before he reached the city. He was not left to walk for days: **Hope sent a search party that found him and brought him safely home**. They returned to the Golden Sands, to family, and to the memorial raised for **Tedna**, with more evidence of an unknown enemy than of its purpose. Stanley apologized to Hope for having compared her to a demonic ruler during the confrontation with Nobiery; she did not deny that the old man's conduct needed restraint.[^arc05-23]

Hope still had a damaged kingdom to supply. She directed **Relium Vanch** to inquire after stone, steel and timber from the visiting **Diamond Stroke** trader so the new court would not depend entirely on the Principal's goodwill. Days passed while she organized a formal reception. **Emenil**, alive after the attack beneath the keep, attended alongside **Nobiery**. The companions were dressed for diplomacy rather than the desert when **the Principal himself appeared**: a towering dark metal figure traced with blue markings, carrying chains and **ten red rings**. His arrival was real. The truth of the history he intended to tell was another matter.[^arc05-24]

The Principal claimed that a magistrate, not he, had ordered the seizure of **Dusthill** and promised the remaining captives would be returned by nightfall. He praised the party for their deeds in the mines and T'ietkalla and spoke as if **Buttons** had been an informant. He offered resources and trade, a **railway**, an embassy and a place for the worship of the **Steel God**. **Vorel challenged him over slavery**. The metallic ruler described old wars, necessary harshness and a mission against the Weirlords, but those defenses remained his own. Nor had a single length of promised track been laid or a captive yet walked through the gate simply because he said they would.[^arc05-25]

The friends argued in private over whether to fight him immediately. **Stanley's augury foretold woe** if they chose that course; it did not name an unavoidable death. They returned to negotiation instead. **Hope and the others insisted that construction use paid, willing workers**, and that any railway and embassy respect their city's security. They discussed a prayer building, guarded access and reciprocal visits to the Principal's places of learning. The agreement was conditional, made by people who had fought too long to mistake the ruler's politeness for innocence. A new route to trade might feed their people; it might also become a road along which an empire could reach them.[^arc05-26]

Then the Principal asked for **Diontalus**. He said **Buttons** had told him what lived inside Stanley and called the archfey a danger on a scale the companions scarcely understood. He raised a crystal, and **the ten rings shone as he drew Diontalus's presence out of Stanley's body and into the stone**. Stanley remained himself, alive and no longer actively sharing his flesh with the voice that had killed George and the leprechaun. **The Principal took the crystal away**. He promised confinement, perhaps even exile beyond the world, but the party had witnessed **capture, not destruction or that future exile**. His claims about cities ruined by Diontalus in a distant age were no more independently proved than his other ancient histories. Freedom for Stanley had come at the cost of trusting another dangerous power with the prisoner.[^arc05-27]

At the gate, the promise made in court began to take on substance. **Dusthill captives really arrived**, including **Talia, Bruce Bane, Jimster, Shun'ti and other familiar faces**. The reunions were painful, joyous and incomplete: not everyone taken in the raid had been accounted for. The survivors spoke of the **Salivating Fields**, where they had farmed under coercion and seen the punishments given to those who tried to escape. Hope arranged for them to be cared for and screened at the **Sand Sanctuary**; Nash's inability to detect a clear magical trace on an initial examination did not rule out every kind of hidden harm. The Principal had done something tangible, but he had not made the enslavement disappear.[^arc05-28]

Another duty called Vorel away before the city could settle. **Diptak**, the lizardfolk who had pledged himself to Vorel, urged him to challenge the ruler of **Razak Val**. The companions took **Fate 4**, while Vorel and Diptak rode swift lizard mounts beside them. A vast dark swarm threatened the desert crossing. They sheltered themselves and their mounts inside **Stanley's Tiny Hut**, with Nash shaping sand around it, rather than testing whether Diptak's dreadful description of bloodsucking insects was exact. At last they reached the valley of hunting clans and caves. Diptak spoke of honor and of the **Great Fang** as if an old title could answer what had gone wrong.[^arc05-29]

Instead the Great Fang was a familiar horror. **Orp the cyclops**, once encountered by the party in the Principal's lands, presided over the lizardfolk court with **living human captives chained to him as food**. He killed at least one captive in the companions' presence, even while seeming pleased to recognize his old friends. The lives taken here were not mere rumors, whatever numbers Orp boasted of eating. Vorel faced the impossible welcome of a friend who had become the instrument of systematic slaughter. **No leadership contest had yet begun** when the travelers first stood before him.[^arc05-30]

## IV. The Great Fang and the missing egg

**Diptak** had brought Vorel to Razak Val to put an end to Orp's reign. The lizardfolk spoke of a formal challenge called **scaling**, a contest through which a warrior might claim the title **Great Fang**. Vorel demanded that a society prizing strength stop feeding helpless prisoners to its ruler. Orp seemed bewildered that someone he considered a friend would fight him. When Diptak spoke out in fury, **Orp struck him a single crushing blow and killed him**. There was no rescue after that blow and no later uncertainty over whether the challenger had only been wounded. Vorel's return to his people had already cost the life of the one who summoned him.[^arc05-31]

A remarkably small but influential lizardfolk, **Mirion Fengar**, stepped forward to restore courtly order. He explained that his own father had once challenged Orp and died, but that the **Fengar family** had then helped make the cyclops the kingdom's adopted ruler. Behind the arena stood clans, priests, soldiers and advisers who depended on the Great Fang without themselves bearing his crown. Vorel had hoped to find a people who understood honor without politics. Mirion's smooth explanations showed him a court where **politics controlled the terms of honor**. Even Stanley's indignant attempt to strike the little adviser **missed**, while Mirion calmly distinguished honorable challenge from assaulting someone far weaker.[^arc05-32]

The party were invited to **Cave Fengar**, where **Bramie the Tyrant Slayer** and **Zercil** held considerable influence. Priests spoke of dragon gods and the **Council of Twelve**, of a founding warrior named Razak and of the **Tailway** shrine. Their stories were traditions told by interested custodians, not a history the travelers had inspected. The feast itself was alarming: hornfuls of fermented drink, live snakes and a nearly raw creature presented as a **chicken lord** from the **Demon Vold**. Vorel listened to the warriors' proud claims while knowing that human prisoners might still be killed outside the banquet hall.[^arc05-33]

At the gathering **Mirion persuaded Orp to accept Vorel's scaling at sunrise**. The challenge was scheduled, not fought. Vorel spoke before the assembled clans about his life beyond the valley and what he believed strength should protect. Meanwhile **Nash**, uneasy and seeking privacy beneath the hall, encountered a hooded stranger who looked astonishingly like him: **a scarless face, a red eye and an assertion that he was Nash Maggard from the future**. The visitor's knowledge and likeness made the claim unsettling, but Nash had no independent way to prove it. **The meeting was private**; Hope, Stanley and Vorel could not yet know what he had heard.[^arc05-34]

The strange visitor was joined by **another apparent Nash**, differently dressed and marked. They spoke of time as something that could accumulate and go wrong, and pressed Nash to help steal an **ancient Fengar egg**. Their explanations about future disasters and their own origins were claims, not a dependable map of what lay ahead. They demanded secrecy. **Nash cast Sleep on two guards** and led the visitors along the restricted passage to the heirloom chamber. He was not an innocent passerby in what followed; he had deliberately helped them enter a guarded place.[^arc05-35]

Inside a locked metal chest lay **a preserved red lizardfolk egg**. One stranger opened the chest with magic. **Bramie entered alive**, displaying his immense dragon-tooth weapon and speaking of his family treasures. Nash kept him occupied with questions about the cave while the two lookalikes **took the egg and vanished**. Nash remained with Bramie for a short while and **left while he was still alive**. Whatever became of the egg, the theft did not itself show that Nash had witnessed or carried out a murder.[^arc05-36]

Elsewhere, Vorel sought the **Elder of Families** for records of his own clan and the **Eye of Groxl**, which the people called a god-killer. The elder's stories of that relic did not create a second artifact separate from the eye in Vorel's chest. A visitor from the northern **Zark** clans warned that defenders near **Khaervilon** faced a breach called the **Demon Vold**, perhaps connected to the demonic name the companions had heard before. **Hope promised that T'ietkalla would offer help even if Vorel lost his contest**; her words were a diplomatic commitment, not an army already marching. The priestess **Typalla** began a legend about **Closthi** and **Telestar**, but could not finish it. Nash returned to the gathering and faced questions about his unexplained absence. The priestess's approval of Vorel's extraordinary fire remained her interpretation of a blessing.[^arc05-37]

The interrupted story gave way to an accusation. **Bramie had been found dead in the heirloom chamber**, impaled from behind with his own great weapon, **Tyrant's Bane**. **Zercil** blamed the strangers in her hall. Orp, still disposed to trust his companions, **appointed Vorel to investigate as a sheriff**, and Zercil entered the pending scaling herself. What had been a duel scheduled between Orp and Vorel was now a **three-way succession contest**. No one had yet fought it. The scene of Bramie's death raised questions sharper than the house's grief alone could answer.[^arc05-38]

The guards' interrupted watch and the markings in the dust gave Vorel pieces of a story, not its ending. **Three matching human-sized sets of footprints** led through the chamber, beside tracks belonging to Bramie and a larger lizardfolk. The great polearm was heavy enough to make the manner of its use uncertain. **The egg was missing**. Vorel considered whether theft and murder might be separate crimes; Zercil's ambitions might furnish a motive, but a possible motive was not proof. He accompanied Nash into a private place, where Nash admitted **helping two lookalikes take the egg**, yet insisted he had **neither killed Bramie nor seen him killed**. Vorel told him not to alter his tracks, and the comparison implicated Nash in the visit without identifying the killer.[^arc05-39]

The preserved egg carried a **Fengar prophecy**, according to Mirion: an unhatched heir might one day surpass even the figures of their oldest stories. Neither its powers nor its destination could be established from the family's tale. At last **Nash confessed the strange visitors and the theft aloud**, speaking before his friends **and nearby lizardfolk who could understand him**. He denied Bramie's murder. The strangers had warned him not to tell outsiders; a **sudden chill** followed his confession, but no witnessed catastrophe explained its cause. **Orp still lived, Bramie's murderer was still unknown, the egg was gone, and the three-way scaling remained ahead.**[^arc05-40]
