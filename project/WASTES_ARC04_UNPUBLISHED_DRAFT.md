# Arc 04 — The Crown of T'ietkalla

## I. Beneath the Pit

The **spotlights of the Teet** were still following the companions when they reached the **Pit of Despair**. Behind them lay a street battle that had yielded no lasting safety; ahead, the vast barred opening promised only darkness. **Hope's new mechanical leg** slowed her, and **Vorel** had carried her through the pursuit. **Stanley's body**, at times disguised by **Diontalus** as a Guzzle Gang fighter, was no easier to recognize in the confusion. They slipped between the bars and descended by rope into a shaft deep enough to make the last fall perilous. Magic broke that fall. Their pursuers came down after them.[^arc04-1]

Below, the guards were no longer simply an approaching patrol. One frightened **Teet Guard**, who gave his name as **George**, begged the heroes to let him live; a wounded **Guzzle Gang** pursuer was someone else. The others argued over whether to use the wounded man as a distraction while **P0D** tried to scout the dark. She could not see reliably without light. From beyond piles of chewed bones and slick stone came the breathing of something enormous. The spotlights above still searched the floor of the pit, and George's dependence on the people who had just fought his comrades made every choice more fraught.[^arc04-2]

The creature emerged into view: a towering, **three-eyed froghemoth**, matching the kind of monster **Monsoon Cabbage** had described but now frighteningly present in its own right. **Vorel fired a shotgun to draw it away** from the others and then met its charge with his chainsaw-edged sword and flame. **Nash** raised earth for shelter. **Stanley** conjured several apparent Vorels to bewilder the beast; the doubles existed in the monster's perception, not as fresh allies. While Vorel risked being caught, the others sought the hidden route Monsoon had promised.[^arc04-3]

In a narrow crevasse, amid clusters of glowing, slimy spheres, they found **an ancient metal grate**. An old key seemed to fit but could scarcely move its corroded lock. Hope's mechanical strength and Vorel's help finally forced the way open. A ladder led deeper into the oil-clogged rock. **Nash secretly took one of the small luminous spheres**, despite warnings; no one had established that it was an egg. They brought George down the ladder and shut the hatch against the creature above. **The keep itself was still nowhere in sight.**[^arc04-4]

Once below, **Stanley spoke as himself**. The others discussed killing **Buttons**, the cat they feared had exposed them to the Teet authorities. Stanley protested. He knew he had used magic to coerce Buttons into silence and reconnaissance; whatever the cat had done afterward, that did not make murder a settled or righteous answer. The argument revealed how badly the company still differed over what it owed a former companion. Nash's hidden sphere soon betrayed itself by its smell. George thought it might fetch a price, but even its nature remained uncertain.[^arc04-5]

The new caves were damp, oily and filled with insects. Somewhere ahead, a distant voice sang and called itself a **king**. Vorel wanted to meet the singer. The others had reason to distrust unseen powers offering themselves as guardians of the underworld. For now they stopped short of him. Nash examined the sphere, whose translucent membrane enclosed a dark center, and cut it open. The outer substance collapsed; **he kept only the black, pliable core** in his cloak. No creature had yet been identified inside. The group's pause was a **short rest**, not a full night of renewed strength.[^arc04-6]

Their search took them through caverns where danger did not always attack. A pale, tentacled worm-like animal watched from a webbed chamber, then burrowed away when they withdrew. Elsewhere they found rigid, stone-like boots that suggested petrification without proving whom they had belonged to. In a room marked by old clay tables, **Hope alone received the full meaning of an Infernal inscription**; the others could not read it, and her private message gave them no public text the company could repeat with certainty. A ruined supply bag yielded a ruby, old tools, a medical kit, weapons and a strangely well-preserved wooden stick. Before they could settle its significance, the dark passages began to move.[^arc04-7]

**Two black puddings** pressed toward them from different ways. Blades split the oozes into further pieces; their touch corroded weapons, damaged barriers and hurt Hope despite her new metal limbs. The confined passages turned retreat into a fight for room to breathe and stand. Vorel resorted to his fists, Nash and Stanley used magic, and Hope brought tables and gunfire to bear until the last fragments were destroyed. The victory did not bring them to the keep. **George accidentally shot his own foot**, badly wounding himself just when the party might have thought the worst was over. Then the unseen singer fell quiet. **Vorel called out to the self-proclaimed king** in the darkness, and the company waited without knowing whether an answer would come.[^arc04-8]

## II. A king's bargain and a borrowed body

There was no answer that led them immediately out of the caves. **George was bleeding**, and the first-aid supplies they had found were old and inadequate for such an injury. **Stanley cleaned and bandaged the guard's foot**, using what help he could. The others were tired, hungry and still dependent on an exit none of them had seen. The polished wooden stick from the ruined cache proved to be a **Wand of Scowls** when Stanley examined it; he handed it to Nash. They could not simply claim a restorative night's sleep whenever they wished, so the question of where to go next remained urgent.[^arc04-9]

**Vorel carried George** when they followed the singing west toward the sound of water. Across a fast-running underground river stood an enormous humanoid with pale, scarred hands and feet, attended by a floating light. He declared himself **king of the cave and guardian of the river** and demanded rent. Vorel answered with poetry; the company offered a shotgun and stones of value. The giant spoke of a dangerous **“headache man”** beyond the water, one he said he had blocked with boulders. He accepted further gems as payment but **would not grant their passage until the threat was dealt with**. Neither his account of the enemy nor the reach of his royal authority had yet been tested. The companions stayed on their own bank.[^arc04-10]

They searched for another route. Bats supplied an unappealing possibility for dinner. In a green-lit sinkhole, **Nash disturbed luminous mushrooms**, whose spores made the company sneeze; a great spider appeared among them and tried to trap him. **Vorel severed its legs**, and the creature fell into the pit, releasing still more spores as it crushed the growth below. The party took some of the legs for food. In another chamber they discovered enormous discarded limbs and old carcasses, suggesting an unsettling connection to the cave king without proving how he had acquired or lost them.[^arc04-11]

From the mushroom area they recovered **a small bag containing seven strange beans and a skeleton-marked key**. These were not part of the earlier supply cache. The beans held unpredictable magic, while the key's intended lock remained unknown. The companions returned to the lower hatch chamber, blocked an entrance with earth and prepared to rest. Before they slept, **Stanley spoke about the name he had chosen for himself**. *Billings* belonged to a life he had tried to make, not the name he had been born with. He asked his friends to be better than the cruelty into which they so often fell, and not to accept Diontalus's ruthlessness as wisdom. **George, still alive**, thanked him and spoke of his family. Nothing in that night suggested that Stanley's plea had won control of his body forever.[^arc04-12]

When the company woke after their **long rest**, **Diontalus was directing Stanley's body**. George was relieved to have survived the caves and hoped to take home something useful. **Hope tried to offer him a handgun**. Dion instead drew a dagger. George had pleaded for his life in the pit, endured the march and spoken kindly to Stanley the night before; now **Diontalus stabbed him in the throat and killed him**. Hope's attempt to stop the murder failed. The others recoiled or argued, not all with the same horror. **Stanley had not chosen the blow**, yet it was his body Diontalus had used, and his words about compassion had scarcely survived the morning.[^arc04-13]

The travelers searched George's belongings, finding a **loving note signed Martha Miller** and a stuffed rabbit among the meager reminders of a home beyond the pit. They spoke of what was owed to him and covered or buried his remains. **Hope took his padded guard armor** as protection against hazards that had already damaged her own equipment. No act of salvage could undo the murder. They still needed to travel through caverns in which a hostile voice, now silent again, might be waiting.[^arc04-14]

Seeking the danger the cave king had named, they fought an **eight-legged basilisk** whose gaze threatened to turn flesh to stone; Vorel finally severed its head. During a brief rest afterward, Nash learned what his supposed prize from the froghemoth's chamber really held: the **black core bit his hand**. A small toothed mass moved within it. He pinned it down and **burned it apart**. The sphere had never been securely identified as a froghemoth egg, and now nothing living from it remained in his keeping. Elsewhere a human-shaped **shadow peeled from a wall**, weakening Vorel before Hope's shots broke it into retreating fragments. It was another threat, not a spirit proved to be George.[^arc04-15]

Beside an old **tiefling skeleton**, the companions found jeweled earrings and a fragile writing. **Dion received its words privately**; those who heard only fragments could not truthfully supply the whole letter. A stone-like form resembling the earlier pale cavern animal raised further questions about the creatures below T'ietkalla, but no witnessed transformation answered them. Farther on the air became cold and lifeless. Hope felt dread, and even P0D hesitated before scouting toward a broad river with scattered rocky islands.[^arc04-16]

A dark, almost motionless figure waited on one of the rocks. Its hollow eyes and silent face seemed to bring pain and a deep buzzing into the air around it. The companions fought across the water, **Nash choosing magic that would not endanger P0D**, while Diontalus and the others struggled against the creature's unnatural effects. **Dion's blasts finally broke the figure apart**. It might have been the enemy the cave king meant, but the claim had not yet been reported or accepted. Worse, **more similar figures began pushing through the cavern walls**. The king's river was still uncrossed, the keep unreached, and the company faced a new danger before it could seek any reward.[^arc04-17]

## III. The book beneath the city

The destruction of the dark figure on the river rock had not ended the attack. **More hollow-eyed creatures pushed through the cavern walls**, bringing the same dreadful pain and fear that had followed the first. The company fought in the shallows and among the stones, with **Nash briefly taking the shape of a black dragon wyrmling** and **Diontalus** hurling magic through Stanley's body. **Vorel fell unconscious** during the struggle. The others stabilized him before the last of their visible attackers finally dissolved under Nash's acid. Surviving this fight was not proof that every such creature beneath the Teet had been destroyed.[^arc04-18]

Near the water, blue fabric buried in sand proved to be a **Robe of Useful Items**. Its sewn patches could become ladders, doors, ropes, poles and other useful objects, but each detached patch was a **finite possession**, not a spell to be cast without limit. **Nash took the robe**. They also learned that the elaborate earrings recovered beside the old tiefling skeleton were enchanted merely to enlarge a wearer's ears. Even a treasure found beside ancient bones could turn out to be a cruel joke rather than an heirloom of power.[^arc04-19]

They found stones blocking passages near the underground river and still heard the self-styled cave king singing, but **did not return to pay him a second visit or win passage**. Instead, a patch of worked earth and an **Infernal riddle** offered another possibility. **Hope**, able to read the writing, relayed its clues about green growth and a sack of beans. The unusual beans found beneath the mushroom pit were planted in the prepared soil; their precise number was not something the others could confidently recover afterward. A **glowing green beanstalk** surged upward to a metal hatch. The **skeleton-marked key** remained with the company for whatever waited above.[^arc04-20]

Before climbing, the adventurers doubled back to check the **grate beneath the Pit of Despair**. Daylight and the voices of guards overhead suggested that their pursuers believed them dead. The **froghemoth was still alive**, guarding the region of those mysterious glowing spheres; the party resisted the temptation to attack it again. They closed the grate and withdrew, marking an underground route before returning to the beanstalk. **Hope used the skeleton-marked key** at the upper hatch, and the travelers entered old masonry rooms filled with **horned tiefling remains**. They had discovered an ancient level beneath the fortress, not yet its inhabited prison.[^arc04-21]

Four arranged **suits of armor** radiated magic along their path. The companions tested and negotiated the obstacle rather than claiming to understand its maker's purpose. Beyond lay a chamber marked by a pentagram and a remarkably preserved **book**. Its illustrations changed as they turned the pages: scenery gave way to the figure of a tiefling who seemed to draw closer, pleading for help. **Diontalus's attempt to Identify the book met resistance**, and he shut it before any figure emerged. The others feared that opening it might release something they could not put back.[^arc04-22]

The book continued to move even when closed. **Vorel tied it to the podium**, and they explored an adjoining old library rather than destroy it. **Hope found poetry and writings about T'ietkalla's irrigation works**; those books showed what earlier inhabitants had known or believed, not that the current city's machinery still functioned. When they attempted to pass a magically protected doorway, **flying books attacked**, marbles scattered across the floor, and fire spread among shelves of paper. **Hope struck the last attacking volume** as the company struggled to keep the library blaze from trapping them.[^arc04-23]

**P0D** passed through narrow ceiling passages with a light and found a way to operate a neighboring door. Beyond it lay a strange **bureau**, filled with preserved remains, hidden drawers, instruments and concealed routes. **Vorel found a protective helmet made from a large lizardfolk skull**; its existence told them nothing certain about any legendary figure to whom the skull might later be attributed. **Hope examined a book of house lineages privately**. Among the office's papers was a scroll to an initial **M** from someone signing **N**, promising work on a book to protect T'ietkalla. Neither initial had yet been established as a full name. A secret passage curved back to the original ritual room.[^arc04-24]

There, **Hope unbound the book** and discovered that the figure within was forming **Infernal words** with the changing illustrations. He instructed them to place a preserved head in the pentagram. They used remains from the bureau, repeated the prescribed phrase, and watched the object disappear into the ritual. The book opened like a passage, and **a living elderly tiefling struggled to climb out**. **Nash cast Reduce** to help the party pull him free. Weak and disoriented, the old man looked toward Hope and exclaimed that **the queen had returned**. They had saved a person, but the identity he thought he recognized—and the history he might tell—still awaited examination.[^arc04-25]

## IV. The prison and the betrayal

The rescued elder was hungry and frail. **Hope gave him food and water**, and he slowly realized that she was **not the same queen** he had first believed he saw. In his former bureau he examined her horns using a reagent or magic and **asserted that she belonged to House Akmenos**, descended from T'ietkalla's old royal line. His demonstration was something the travelers could witness; the genealogy and political rights he claimed it established were **his interpretation**, not a coronation. Hope had entered the ruins seeking answers and a way to save her friends, not a kingdom already waiting to obey her.[^arc04-26]

The old tiefling spoke in **Infernal** of betrayal, a cursed council and the **Night Pasture**, where he said his order of **Dead Tongues** guided souls rather than letting them pass into the Weir. He distrusted the name associated with **Monsoon Cabbage** and suggested **Kefeli** and earlier rulers had reshaped the kingdom. He also described what he believed he could perceive about the spirits accompanying **Hope, Nash, Vorel and Diontalus**. These accounts offered troubling possibilities, not independently verified ancient history. The group debated whether any promised restoration of tiefling rule could be just to those already living in the city.[^arc04-27]

After resting, **Stanley regained control of his body**. He said he had witnessed Diontalus kill George and believed the killing was meant to hurt *him*. He and Nash discussed a future attempt to return **George's stuffed rabbit** to the dead guard's family; they did **not** reach that family. Stanley urged Hope not to let a claim of royal blood excuse cruelty. The elder, meanwhile, examined **Nash's dragon-metal heart** and called him something like a **crystal of arcane energy**. That was the elder's diagnosis, not proof that Nash had become an entirely different people or creature.[^arc04-28]

Pressed for the substance of his offer, the old man claimed he had hidden loyal people of **twelve houses** beyond the world, reachable through a **Soulbroker bargain**. He said **twenty newborns** had been the price. The company recoiled at the enormity of the claim. He described a force that could reclaim T'ietkalla, yet **no such army was present**. He reasoned from what the adventurers told him that **Kefeli might have become a petrifying Medusa-like being**; he had not seen her transformation. Finally he offered two passages, one toward an old **prisoner lift**, another toward a hidden crypt and the **Gem of Tides**. At their urging, he stepped through a door opened using **Nash's Key to Château Weir**, met there by **Baba**, to attempt to fetch his promised followers. Whether he would return remained unknown.[^arc04-29]

The companions chose the **prisoner lift** first. From Nash's newly acquired robe they detached the **twenty-four-foot ladder patch**, turning it into a real ladder and **using up that patch**. Chains, pulleys and a difficult ascent brought them into a higher level with guarded cells. **P0D** scouted the corridors and watched two guards playing cards. **Nash cast Sleep** and entered while they lay unconscious. He then divided a recovered bag of **Pixie Blush** between the men without their consent. Their bodies convulsed and hallucinated; the party feared the consequences, but **neither guard's death was confirmed**. This was not a harmless arrest or clean rescue tactic.[^arc04-30]

Using the prison keys, they opened neighboring cells and discovered **Tedna and Bok Bok alive**. Tedna embraced **Hope**; Bok Bok rushed back to **Vorel**. Their relief was immediately complicated by news about **Angelashi**. Tedna said the singer had been taken away as a traitor and supposedly fed to the beast in the pit. **He was reporting what he had learned, not recounting a death he had witnessed**. Nash feared for her, but the party could not yet know whether she had survived. The wedding, the pixie prisoners and the Gem of Tides all remained beyond their reach.[^arc04-31]

As the rescued companions caught up, Tedna described his work inside the keep, the letter he had sent and the role of **Softhead**, whose earlier disappearance into a river had never established his death. **Stanley went ahead alone** into a food storeroom and saw Softhead there, but the man fled. Armed guards emerged from concealment. **Jolt**, waiting for the intruders, confronted Stanley. He called his companions and **yielded control to Diontalus**. Then **Buttons appeared**, alive and openly opposed to the group. His account of a competing arrangement with **Monsoon**, **Jolt** and the **Principal**, involving control of the Gem and the city, came from the cat himself; the party had not independently watched the alleged bargain being struck.[^arc04-32]

A **radio message from Monsoon** offered the companions a last chance to change sides. When they refused, he issued **Order Twenty-Six**. **P0D was forcibly transformed** into a towering, violent version of herself. She struggled and lashed out, while **Nash tried to preserve her life** rather than treat her as a willing traitor. The fight spread among grain sacks and barrels. Guards fell, **Jolt fought with guns and magic**, and the bodies and loyalties of the combatants were confused by illusion and spells. **Vorel** was briefly turned into a chair; **Hope**, wounded, seized Jolt and fought for control of the room. None of the chaos proved P0D had consented to become an enemy.[^arc04-33]

**Buttons used spells and concealment**, injuring and briefly blinding Hope as the combat widened. She eventually **held him with her mechanical grip**. He struggled, and **Vorel struck the fatal blow**, killing the cat Stanley had earlier pleaded to spare. His reported plans and the violence of the ambush could not make his death disappear from their own account of the day. **Jolt was still alive, though badly hurt**, when the fighting slackened. The giant **P0D remained alive too**, distressed and confused, and Nash moved toward her in hope of calming her. **Tedna and Bok Bok had survived the prison rescue**, though the battle had placed them in fresh danger. The deaths and losses of the next episode had **not yet happened**; here the terrible choice had been forced upon the pixie, not made by her.[^arc04-34]

## V. The cost of the Gem

The battle in the keep's storeroom had slackened, but it had not ended the suffering. **Buttons lay dead**, slain by Vorel in the struggle that had begun the previous episode. **P0D still towered over her companions**, altered against her will by Monsoon's Order Twenty-Six. **Nash tried to calm her**. Nearby, **Hope held the wounded Jolt**, while **Tedna**, freed from prison only hours before, was gravely hurt. **Diontalus drew arcane energy from Buttons's corpse** and treated the pixie's distress as a danger to be eliminated. He briefly stabilized Tedna's injuries, but the young man still needed care beyond that spell.[^arc04-35]

Nash begged the others not to kill P0D. He even attempted to use magic against Diontalus as the dispute sharpened. **Diontalus attacked the transformed pixie**, and her enormous body burst into dust. She had not chosen to betray them, and her death was not an escape from a freely accepted bargain. The room filled with particles of the friend who had begged them to save the other captives. For Nash, who had sheltered and protected her since she appeared at Charlemagne's, the loss was immediate and irreparable.[^arc04-36]

The carnage spread before the companions could gather themselves. A gunman known as **Dashington** appeared with a bag and sought to take the injured Jolt away. He pressed a revolver to Hope's head and **shot her when she resisted**. Amid the pixie dust and near-blind confusion, **Diontalus and Nash fought him**, and Dashington was killed. **Vorel**, who believed the gunman had dealt honorably with him before, turned his rage upon Diontalus. The companions were now struggling not merely against the keep's enemies, but against one another's incompatible judgments.[^arc04-37]

**Hope's exposure to pixie dust and Pixie Blush distorted what she saw.** Fantastical figures and impossible journeys crowded her senses while real lives slipped away around her. **Tedna**, who had repeatedly asked to see her, spoke with her as his failing condition returned. He died despite the temporary stabilization. Hope's vision of his passage into a gleaming metal afterlife belonged to her altered perceptions, not to any verified road the dead had taken. Still under the drug's influence, **Hope killed Jolt**. Whatever else was hallucination, Tedna's and Jolt's deaths were not.[^arc04-38]

The survivors withdrew with what bodies and possessions they could gather, moving through the old tiefling ritual passages. **Nash sealed their route with earth**. When **Stanley returned to control of his shared body**, he used **Speak with Dead** on Buttons's recovered corpse, seeking answers about the Gem and the alleged bargain. The corpse's brief replies were constrained by the spell and by Buttons's own hostility; they could not reveal an infallible history. The company discovered that a recovered bag contained the **glowing blue Gem of Tides**. It was finally **in their custody**, though it had not yet been returned to the ancient waterworks.[^arc04-39]

Grief left them searching for something more reliable than their own recent conduct. **Vorel proposed the Pillars of Decency**, a code named in memory of P0D. It was an attempt to put words around how they ought to act, not proof that they could already live by those words. **Hope took possession of Fox and Scully**, Jolt's paired enchanted revolvers, with **Smokey's ammunition belt**. They were the weapons promised under the Soulbroker's arrangement, with powers and limited ammunition rather than limitless force. The company considered a hidden listening device that might explain Monsoon's knowledge, but could not establish precisely who had overheard what.[^arc04-40]

Following the elder's earlier directions, they passed a concealed way into a **tiefling mausoleum**, then descended beside abandoned pipes to an old **pool and throne chamber**. The empty place where the Gem belonged stood among figures turned to stone. A **serpent-haired woman wearing protective glasses** emerged. **Kefeli** demanded the Gem and called herself the rightful protector of T'ietkalla. She told them that **Andreeste** had helped her acquire powers to petrify enemies who threatened the kingdom. Her living gaze and serpentine hair were real dangers; her story about righteous rule, ancient vampires and the cause of every statue was her own testimony.[^arc04-41]

**Hope refused to kneel or surrender the party's freedom.** Kefeli demanded their allegiance and the Gem. When negotiation broke down, water and metallic serpent hair became weapons. **Nash produced a steel mirror from one finite patch of his robe**, and the companions fought for positions where they could look away from Kefeli's eyes. **Stanley's magic** burned through her metallic protection; **Hope's revolvers** cut at her hair. **Vorel was turned to stone** while pressing the attack. **Stanley then used the mirror and a Command to make Kefeli look at herself**; she began to petrify, but **Hope also became a statue** during the struggle. By its end Kefeli, Vorel and Hope stood motionless, leaving **Nash and Stanley** to face what their victory had cost.[^arc04-42]

They destroyed **Kefeli's petrified body**, but the same violence did nothing to free their companions. **Bok Bok remained with Vorel**, guarding him and Hope after Nash and Stanley enclosed the two statues in protective hollow pillars. Recalling the **basilisk they had killed earlier in the caves**, the survivors retraced their steps and recovered material from its body. With preserved ingredients from the old bureau they prepared **two improvised restoratives**. The earth trembled around them, yet both returned to the hidden pillars, administered the preparations and **restored Hope and Vorel to living form before E051 ended**. Kefeli had fallen, but the **Gem's exact final emplacement** was still not securely established by their account.[^arc04-43]

The reunited companions climbed toward the battle raging above. **A fleeing guard died after Nash tried to trip him**, the fatal fall an unintended result of that attempt rather than a planned execution. Then **huge transformed pixies burst through the ruined walls**, attacking the keep's defenders. These were not P0D returned from the dead. They were other victims of the power that had destroyed her, and the larger assault was still unfolding as the company reached the fighting.[^arc04-44]

## VI. Through the beast

The upper rooms of the keep were becoming a ruin. Among the **giant transformed pixies**, a vastly enlarged and changed **froghemoth** surged through the fighting, swallowing the monstrous pixie bodies as readily as it struck at the adventurers. It bore more eyes and tentacles than the creature the company had seen in the Pit. **Vorel charged it with his chainsaw-edged sword**, while Nash and Stanley found their spells behaving unpredictably amid the chaos. **Hope sought a way through heavy doors**. The creature's violence soon turned their effort to escape the keep into a fight to escape the creature itself.[^arc04-45]

They were dragged through **teeth, tongue and throat**, struggling to hold ropes and blades against great muscular contractions. **Hope stabbed at living tissue**; Vorel tried cutting a way through from outside before the monster took them deeper. **Nash acquired a strange small additional hand** during one of the unstable magical effects, a limb he could move but not wholly predict. None of them knew whether this change would last. The enormous swallow eventually forced all four companions into the throat and digestive passages, amid other swallowed, transformed beings.[^arc04-46]

Above a **pool of stomach acid**, the companions considered magic, equipment patches and any route that might lead upward. **Nash changed his form to move through the fluid**, taking a light with him; the others followed as best they could. Strange **pink toothed worms** clung to or approached fallen pixie hulks and avoided his illumination. A different **white, thread-bearing creature** seized Vorel with a silvery filament. **Stanley's magic severed the line**, but the creature was not slain, and its later thread had to be avoided. Flushes of bile came from the host's body rather than as a confirmed power of that hunter. Ropes and a pole drawn from Nash's finite robe patches saved the travelers from sliding toward a lower, fouler outlet.[^arc04-47]

The deeper journey passed **valves, veins and chambers of pulsating tissue**. The party climbed rather than cut through an **electrified heart**. Eventually they reached a vast **glowing brain**, ringed by eyes and small tendrils. **Vorel laid a hand upon it** and heard a voice in his mind. He claimed to be its master and promised nourishment and glory, but the voice questioned what such mastery meant. His conversation was private: the other companions did not automatically hear every reply. By the end of that encounter, the creature had not been proved obedient, destroyed or safely landed.[^arc04-48]

The mind within the beast spoke of **transformation and a higher journey**, wanting more powerful nourishment. Vorel urged it to release those trapped inside before that ascent. In the event, **the froghemoth divided**. An **upper cocoon-like portion rose away**, its destination unknown, while the **lower half carrying the travelers' digestive passage dropped into the desert**. They were not entitled to call the whole organism dead: one portion had escaped. The lower body, however, was contracting and failing around them, making escape a race against its decay.[^arc04-49]

In its failing intestines, the companions passed more **pink waste-processing worms**, distinct from the earlier light-avoiding stomach creatures. Vorel, still troubled by the possibility that **Angelashi** might have been swallowed somewhere within, followed an unnatural sense of attraction toward a branch containing a vicious **roper-like inhabitant**. They fought it with spells, acid, special rounds and sword blows until **the first roper was killed**. Behind it waited **a huge living eye**, ancient and strangely familiar to Vorel. It beckoned, and when he took it from the wall, the eye **embedded itself in his chest**.[^arc04-50]

It was the **Eye of Groxl**—not the separate artifacts suggested by inconsistent renderings of its name. The eye possessed **a will of its own**, granting dangerous powers associated with protection, a flaming shield and endurance in combat, while imposing demands on the worthiness of its bearer. Its power was not a moral judgment the others had to accept. A later examination by **Stanley** confirmed the eye's independent presence and its apparent dislike of him. **Vorel could also change the color of his scales**, but that alteration arose separately from his contact with the beast and unstable magic, not as a power bestowed by the eye.[^arc04-51]

The dying body contracted around them. Another **roper** tried to catch them as they fled; the party escaped its last grasp, **without confirming its death**. When the passage became a deep stream of filth, **Nash used the rowboat patch from his Robe of Useful Items**, consuming another of its finite supplies. They rode the current out through the carcass's final outlet and emerged into the **desert**, filthy but alive. Birds circled the collapsing lower remains. The flying upper mass was still unaccounted for, and **Angelashi had not been found**.[^arc04-52]

The adventurers cleaned themselves and rested, discussing how they might confront Monsoon after the devastation. **Nash's small additional arm remained**, and **Vorel's eye stayed lodged in his chest**. Before returning to T'ietkalla, Vorel led them to the **treasure he had buried in the sand** after an earlier heist. The hoard was enormous, but they **carried away only a portion and reburied most of it**. They imagined a dramatic return rather than a furtive entrance. **Nash assumed his young-black-dragon form**, took the others onto his back, and **flew toward the damaged city**. What had happened to its inhabitants, who controlled the keep and whether Hope would ever claim the crown remained questions for their arrival, not events already witnessed.[^arc04-53]

## VII. A crown amid ruins

**Nash carried the others back toward T'ietkalla in the shape of a young black dragon.** From above they saw **Trashburg torn apart**: smoke, corpses and damaged streets beneath the breached walls of the keep. It was more ruin than rescue, but the empty spaces they could see from the air did not tell them that everyone in the city had died. **Enlarged pixies** rose to meet them. **Stanley tried to persuade them with a promise of freedom**, and several briefly seemed to listen. Before a decisive battle, the giants began shrinking back into smaller pixies and vanishing from sight. The companions suspected the magic that held their monstrous forms had expired; they had witnessed the reversal, not discovered the precise cause.[^arc04-54]

They landed in the shattered citadel courtyard, with Nash still dragon-shaped, and approached the throne hall. **Monsoon Cabbage** sat within, apparently untroubled by the devastation. **A prince lay dead** at his feet, while **Suck Master Din hung restrained and gagged** nearby. At Monsoon's side, a stone-limbed servant called **Lazlo** held a prisoner fast. It was **Angelashi, alive**. Tedna's dreadful report that she had been fed to the beast in the pit had been wrong. **Nash broke through an exterior wall with his dragon tail**, opening the hall to light and giving his companions an entrance; the breach was real, not merely a declaration that Monsoon's rule was finished.[^arc04-55]

Monsoon offered to **yield the throne to Hope**, but gave the offer the shape of a warning. He claimed that **Kefeli's destruction** would leave T'ietkalla exposed to **Andreeste, the Nourisher**, and to struggles that bound the Principal, demons and the Weir. Those were the hostile ruler's assertions about a future he wanted the company to fear; **Andreeste had not appeared before them**. When **Vorel attacked**, Monsoon dissolved into mist and escaped. The party could neither claim to have slain him nor trust that his relinquishment of authority meant he would never return. His servant's animation failed, yet **Lazlo's stone grip still imprisoned Angelashi**. Hope supported her, Stanley worked to loosen the fingers, and **Nash broke the stone arm with earth magic**. Only then was Angelashi actually free. She embraced Nash, exhausted but living, and he finally abandoned his dragon shape at the cost of exhaustion.[^arc04-56]

The company turned upon **Suck Master Din**, who remained alive and captive. They confronted him about the gangs, forced labor and deaths that had flourished under his authority. His son was already dead, and the question of what justice might follow the fall of the old regime had no easy answer. Then **a dark portal opened**. The elderly tiefling who had once been trapped inside the book **returned from Château Weir**, bringing **hundreds of armed tieflings** who formed ranks in the hall. His earlier claims of an army hidden away had acquired visible substance, though no one had counted out the precise number he had once promised or proven every detail of his ancient story.[^arc04-57]

The elder presented **Hope Akmenos** before the soldiers and placed **a jagged black crown** upon her head. **The tieflings knelt and acclaimed her queen.** This was no longer an unfulfilled suggestion made beside an ancient ritual book: **Hope had been publicly crowned**. Yet the ceremony did not erase her doubts about what the elder believed she owed to her bloodline or what the soldiers might do in her name. Her kingdom was a place of displaced people and broken buildings, with no assurance that the return of an old dynasty would make its power just.[^arc04-58]

Her first acts exposed the danger in that uncertainty. With **Hope's approving signal**, **Vorel executed the captive Suck Master Din**. Hope used the death in a blood-marked proclamation about rebuilding T'ietkalla. The judgment was real, not merely threatened, and it stood uneasily beside Stanley's earlier pleas for mercy and Vorel's own **Pillars of Decency**. An armored tiefling leader, **Melgor**, dispatched soldiers to secure the city and instructed them to kill resistance. Other representatives, **Fula** and **Relium**, proposed work on farms, food and damaged structures. Orders had been issued and plans offered; **the deaths those patrols might cause, the harvests and the repairs had not yet been witnessed**.[^arc04-59]

At the council that followed, some of the returning tieflings tried to exclude **Nash, Stanley and Vorel** because they were not tieflings. **Hope insisted that her companions belonged beside her**, refusing to let the new court deny those who had helped her survive. The discussion moved among Monsoon's escape, the rumored threat of Andreeste, the Principal's reach, surviving gangs, transformed pixies and the unresolved fate of the froghemoth's strange offspring. Ministers offered reconnaissance and further orders, but no report had yet proved that the streets were secure or every enemy gone. **The Eye of Groxl** remained lodged in Vorel's chest, its independent will no guarantee of good counsel; it was no separate artifact from the differently transcribed eye named in the earlier caves.[^arc04-60]

The new court spoke of old houses and an heir. **Hope said she could not bear one**; the elder proposed that ancient or spiritual methods might help, without demonstrating any remedy. The companions asked what sort of government could protect the living inhabitants of the city, including people the returned soldiers despised. They spoke of a **clinic**, of finding **George's family** to return his keepsake, of retrieving Stanley's property, and of someday scouting **Principal City**. Each undertaking remained a plan. **Bok Bok returned to Vorel**, and **Angelashi openly reunited with Nash**, the fear of her reported death finally answered by a living embrace.[^arc04-61]

The company gathered to celebrate the end of a harrowing stretch of their journey. Around them, **T'ietkalla was still shattered**. **Monsoon had escaped**, the flying part of the froghemoth had vanished into an unknown future, and the people under Hope's crown had yet to see what the new rulers would do. Hope's elevation was real; **months of rebuilding, journeys toward the Principal and the mercy or violence of her reign were not yet played deeds**. The friends who had argued over Buttons, George and P0D had been given a chance to build something better than the powers they had defeated. Whether they could agree on what that meant—and act on it—remained open when the celebration began.[^arc04-62]
